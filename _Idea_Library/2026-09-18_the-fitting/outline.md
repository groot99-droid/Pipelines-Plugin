# The Fitting — Outline

## Structural note
Escalation-by-catalogued-incident: each section is one dated/logged prosthetic-adjustment appointment, numbered as an entry in her own private reckoning (not the clinic's — the clinic never treats these as anomalies). Mechanism of alteration is never shown; only before/after states, her noticing, and the psychological terrain between appointments. Ending is cyclical, non-resolving: she completes the fit without resistance, and the technician schedules the next patient.

## Recurring apparatus (established once, referenced throughout)
- Setting: a fitting room/clinic for a prosthetic limb (routine adjustment appointments, recurring technician — calm, procedural, unfailingly courteous, never sinister on the surface).
- The technician takes measurements against a chart/spec sheet that is never hers to see fully.
- Between appointments: her interior monitoring of her own body, trying to rationalize the drift, delaying or nearly canceling, going anyway.
- Motif: numbers, calibration language, the small talk that stays exactly the same each visit while she changes.

## Incident 1 — Baseline appointment (establishing normal)
- Ordinary tone. The routine she's used to: measurements taken, socket adjusted, minor tightness noted, technician's script is friendly and fixed.
- One small wrongness surfaces at the end: a measurement read aloud doesn't match what she remembers being told last time — off by a fraction. She assumes technician error, or her own memory.
- Interiority: she clocks it, dismisses it. First seed of doubt, immediately talked down.

## Incident 2 — The chart doesn't match her
- The technician measures her residual limb's circumference, girth of the sound leg, shoulder height for balance — reads numbers that don't correspond to what she sees in the mirror after.
- Not wrong for a person — wrong for *her specifically*. The numbers are consistent, plausible, just not hers.
- She asks a soft question ("that seems different from before?"). Gets a calm, complete, unbothered answer that doesn't quite address it — technician's courtesy never breaks.
- She leaves telling herself it's tolerance drift, normal adjustment variance. Buys the explanation because the alternative is unthinkable.

## Incident 3 — Something in her gait, not her leg
- The adjustment this time changes how she walks — a corrected asymmetry she didn't know she had. Small comment from technician: "there, that's more natural for you." The phrase catches wrong — *for you*, as if there's a specific her being aimed at that isn't the one who walked in.
- She notices her own reflection cataloguing as slightly off to herself in ways she can't name — not the limb now, something in stance, in weight distribution.
- She tests herself against old photos/memory at home. The evidence is thin, deniable. She half-convinces herself she's imagining it, out of anxiety, out of the strangeness of prosthetic adaptation generally.

## Incident 4 — The chart has a name that isn't quite a name / a history that isn't quite hers
- Glimpses something on the clipboard/screen — an ID, an old measurement column, a note field — that references a trajectory of adjustments predating her own history with this device, or specifics (an old injury, a handedness, a stated preference) that aren't things she's ever told them.
- She raises it directly this time — not a soft question, an actual objection. Gets redirected smoothly, procedurally, kindly. Never denied outright, never confirmed — simply absorbed into the appointment's rhythm and moved past.
- First real fear here, articulated in interiority: not "is this wrong" but "who is this fitting for."
- She almost doesn't return. Books the next appointment anyway — inertia, need of the limb to function, unwillingness to be the kind of person who makes a scene over a feeling.

## Incident 5 — Her own hands help
- The fitting requires her participation — holding a position, adjusting her own grip, shifting weight on command — and she does it fluidly, competently, before being asked, anticipating what's wanted.
- The horror is located in her own capability: she is good at becoming this now. Interiority notes this with a kind of remote, exhausted clarity rather than panic — panic would imply she still expects to resist.
- Technician's script shifts almost imperceptibly to address her the way it would address a longer-standing patient — implying continuity, familiarity, as though this fit has been ongoing longer than she's aware. She lets the implication stand unchallenged.

## Incident 6 (closing incident) — The completed fit
- Final appointment. No more discrepancy to notice — the measurements now agree with what she sees in the mirror, because she has become what they agree with. The wrongness has resolved not by correction but by her arrival at it.
- She completes the fit herself — the last adjustment, the one that used to be the technician's alone, she performs without being asked and without hesitation. No resistance to narrate because there is no gap left between her and the fit to resist across.
- No externalized triumph or despair — flat, settled interiority. Something like relief, unnamed as such. She keeps her composure and her private awareness of what's happened to her; she is not spectacle, she is complicit and she knows it, and that knowledge is the last thing that's still fully hers.
- Technician closes the file, courteous as ever, and calls the next name from the waiting room — the same tone, same script, unbothered, already recalibrated toward whoever's next.
- Ending is explicitly cyclical/non-resolving: no rescue, no revelation delivered to an outside party, no consequence for the technician or the clinic. The loop is structural, not personal — this was never a story that was going to stop with her.

## Voice/craft reminders for draft stage
- Keep paragraphs short, fragment-stacked; concrete sensory nouns (numbers, vinyl chair, socket, strap, chart) over abstraction.
- Minimal dialogue tags — let the technician's lines carry their own flat courtesy without heavy attribution.
- Ellipse the alteration mechanism completely at every incident — never explain how the fit changes her, only that it has, and that she notices.
- High interiority delivered clipped: short declarative internal lines, not discursive rumination.
- No project/mode tie to *The Stuffed Ones* or any other vault work should be stated in the draft itself — it's a craft model for this outline only, per references.md's explicit note that no project tie exists or should be inferred.
