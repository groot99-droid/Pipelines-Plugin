# The Two Who Woke the Sea — Outline (short-story scale)

**Mode:** epic-fantasy — twin-bond soul-pairing structure, elemental-cosmic imbalance (a drowned god's rising memory-tide) as the stake. Compressed to a single short story (target_folder: 03_Stories) rather than novel scale — a broader ensemble/multi-act treatment was drafted first and cut down to this scope; see note at bottom.

## Compressed cosmology (world-bible fragments woven into the prose, not appended separately)

The god Sevrain drowned carrying a fire he could not let the other gods finish fighting over; his heart split at the moment of drowning into grief (which went to one shore) and hunger (which went to the other). On a long, uncertain cycle, the sea rises to give back what it remembers — not as flood, but as *un-becoming*: what the tide touches forgets it was ever separate from the water. It has risen twice. The second time, it swallowed a capital city and split one kingdom into two halves along the wound: a grieving western coast and a hungry eastern one. A third rising is due.

## Principal cast (trimmed)

- **Yrsa** — grief-bound, a mourner-for-hire on the western coast, wakes with a stranger's grief laid over her own.
- **Corvin** — hunger-bound, a dock scavenger on the eastern coast, wakes starving in a way no meal answers.
No other named companions — keep this a two-hander. A single unnamed antagonist function (a coastal cult that profits from the tide's imbalance and does not want it resolved) can appear as pressure/urgency, but does not need a named leader or extended confrontation scene.

## Structure (single story, compressed beats)

1. **Parallel awakenings** (alternating short sections): Yrsa wakes mid-mourning-rite weeping for a love that isn't hers; Corvin wakes emptying a larder and is still hungry. Each feels a pull toward the kingdom's center — toward a person-shaped absence.
2. **The pull, and a first tremor**: a small tide-tremor un-becomes something minor near each of them (a boat, a bell, a name half-forgotten) — enough to make the pull impossible to ignore. Each leaves home.
3. **Crossing, compressed**: brief, image-driven passages of each traveling toward the center — not a full journey-with-companions arc, just enough to establish grief and hunger as active forces arguing with each protagonist the whole way (grief wants to sit down and let the tide take what it wants; hunger wants to seize and hoard against what's coming). A moment of dream-contact where each starts silently addressing the other as "the other shore of me."
4. **Convergence**: they meet in person for the first time at the strait where the tide is due to rise a third time, on the falling tide before it turns.
5. **Climax**: in the drowned ruin beneath the strait, as the tide turns, they don't fight it and don't flee it — they finish what Sevrain couldn't finish alone, letting grief mourn its loss all the way through and hunger set down what it was trying to reclaim, together, in the same moment.
6. **Cost**: completing the rite draws off the bond that let them find each other — what exactly they lose (the dream-contact; the perfect memory of the crossing; possibly each other's names) should land as a real, specific cost, decided in the draft rather than hedged.
7. **Closing image (callback)**: the tide settles rather than rising a third time as catastrophe. Epilogue-in-miniature: Yrsa and Corvin, ordinary again, each independently drawn to stand near open water sometime after, with only a faint, wordless familiarity — enough to gesture at what passed between them without restoring it.

## Voice/craft reminders for draft stage

Third-person, past tense; simile-dense nature/light/water figurative language; codex-register cosmology folded into the prose in small doses (a line here, not a separate appended section) rather than an isolated info-dump block. No mid-scene POV head-hopping — alternate by section break, not within one continuous scene.

## Note on scope

An earlier pass outlined this at full novel/ensemble scale (7 named characters, 19 plot beats across 3 acts, multiple named factions and locations) — appropriate for `WrymWretch`-scale work, but out of proportion with this pipeline's other four pieces (a single essay, story, poem, and story) and with epic-fantasy's own `target_folder` (`03_Stories`, a single short story). This version keeps the same cosmology, cast, and ending but compresses it to a self-contained short story. The full-scale version isn't discarded information — it's simply not what gets drafted here.

## Note on originality

Sevrain, Yrsa, Corvin, and the un-becoming-tide cosmology above are original to this piece. No names, places, factions, or cosmological terms from the Wyrmreach project were reused. The twin-bond/elemental-imbalance convention and the memory-as-tide figurative language are shared mode conventions, not an asserted tie to that project.
