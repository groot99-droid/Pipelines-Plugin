# Outline: The Nightly Inventory

**Mode:** horror-prose
**Structure:** escalation-by-catalogued-incident → cyclical/non-resolving ending
**Project tie:** none. (References.md flags *The Stuffed Ones* and *Missing Campsites* as the closest structural kin — same mode conventions, not a shared world. No tie should be stated or implied in the draft.)

## Frame

First-person, present-tense-adjacent, told as/through the keeper's actual station log: numbered or dated entries with the keeper's marginal interiority bleeding into the "official" notation. The log is diegetic — the object the reader is holding is the trap. Voice: plain, clipped, sensory, fragment-stacked; the keeper narrates like a man trying to keep the entries small so the wrongness stays small.

Setting: a remote manned light, no relief due for a fixed stretch (isolation is structural, not incidental — no one is coming to rescue him before the log finishes).

## Opening line (to be echoed at the close)

Something plain and procedural that sounds like nothing — e.g. a variant of: *"Item one: the light dimmed a second early. Nothing. Logged it anyway."* (Exact wording is the drafter's call; what matters structurally is that it is bland, numbered, dismissive, and built to be repeated verbatim at the end in a different mouth.)

## Catalogued incidents (escalation)

Each incident = one short scene, noticed → explained away/shelved → closes on a one-line gut-punch or ominous fragment. The shelving logic gets thinner and more effortful as the list grows; by the back half the keeper is shelving things he no longer believes.

1. **The early dim.** The lamp's beam drops a beat sooner than the rotation calls for. Explained as bulb warm-up, filed as a maintenance line. Gut-punch: he writes it down instead of walking up to check — the first choice to log over look.
2. **The gull.** A gull that has landed on the same rail every evening for a season won't land tonight — circles, won't come down. Explained as a weather front. Closing fragment: the rail, empty, in a wind that isn't bad enough to explain it.
3. **The unlatched door.** The lamp-room door he double-checked is open again. Explained as his own forgetfulness — the first entry that indicts him instead of the station. He starts checking the door twice, then three times, and writing down the number of checks.
4. **The handwriting that isn't his memory.** He finds a page about the gull (incident 2) written in his own hand, with details he doesn't remember observing — the gull's exact wingbeat count, something he'd have had no reason to count. Shelved as fatigue, sleepwalking. Gut-punch: "Note to self: sleep more." — the log now has an entry with no memory attached to it.
5. **The counts stop matching.** He starts compulsively counting fixed things — stair treads, lamp panes, the interval between wave-sets — as a private check against #4. The counts disagree with the counts from the night before, for things that cannot change. Shelved as eye strain. He starts logging the counts too, "to be sure," which means the log is now bigger than the job it exists to serve.
6. **The predecessor's log.** In a supply chest, a logbook from a keeper decades gone. Same incidents. Same order. Some of the same sentences, verbatim. He does not report this to the mainland (radio's spotty, authority's indifferent, and — the real reason — some shelving instinct in him that has become an isolating instinct: reporting it would make it real to someone besides him). He burns the old log instead of the new one. Complicit act #1: destroying evidence rather than raising it.
7. **Entries with no gap for him.** He starts finding new lines in the current log written in the hours he was asleep or at the lamp, in a hand almost — not quite — his. He is no longer sure which entries are his. He starts checking the log more than he checks the light itself: the inventory has become the job.
8. **The log gets ahead of him.** He decides to stop — to burn this log too, end the list by ending the object. He goes to do it and finds an entry already there, past tense, describing the attempt before he's made it: written in the hand that isn't quite his. Shelving fails outright here; there is nothing left to explain it as. This is the last incident that gets a number — after this, the keeper stops adding headers, because the list has stopped needing his permission to grow.

## Ending (cyclical / hand-off, protagonist rendered complicit)

**Shape chosen:** loop-back to the opening line, delivered to a new victim, with the keeper revealed as unwitting *author* of the entry that traps his replacement — not merely consumed, but made complicit, per idea.md's own bifurcation ("becomes the next entry" / "log writes entries no one added") — fused rather than picked apart, because the draft can and should reveal these were never two different fates: the entries no one consciously added were his hand all along, and that is *how* he becomes the next entry.

- The relief keeper arrives (boat, replacement, whoever the mode calls for — no rescue framing; this person is a fresh, unwarned body, not a savior). The station is found in order. No dramatic scene of the vanished keeper — he is simply not there, or is there in some fixture-like, absorbed, barely-commented-on way the drafter can render obliquely rather than explained (avoid over-resolving what happened to him; the not-knowing is part of the non-resolution).
- The new keeper opens the log to start their own entries and finds the last page already occupied: a fresh numbered entry, first item, in handwriting that will turn out — though the new keeper has no way to know this yet — to be the old keeper's hand, describing the light dimming a second early tonight.
- Final line echoes the opening near-verbatim, now delivered as an entry the new keeper believes is their own first observation: *"Item one: the light dimmed a second early. Nothing. Logged it anyway."*
- No rescue, no explanation delivered to the reader beyond what the incidents already implied. The old keeper is gone, complicit (his hand wrote the trap forward), and a new victim has just made the first shelving choice that starts the cycle again.

## Notes for the draft stage

- Keep the "shelving" language literally present in the prose (logging, filing, filling in a maintenance line) so the reader watches denial-as-routine happen in real time, per idea.md's theme "routine as denial."
- Minimal dialogue — this is largely a one-man, log-form piece; if the relief keeper speaks at the end, bare "said," no more than a line or two, so the hand-off lands on the log entry, not on chatter.
- Do not name or gesture at *The Stuffed Ones* or *Missing Campsites* inside the piece itself or imply a shared world/project — those are craft references for the drafter only, per references.md's explicit caution that no tie exists or should be inferred.
