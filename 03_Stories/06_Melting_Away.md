 Melting Away

His phone chimed as a text popped onto the screen. Daniel glanced at the message; he was the new student at a prestigious college known for its exceptional education and… wild parties. The message read: “Don’t be boring, Daniel. We hate boring. Party this weekend. Be there.” Daniel let out a sigh. Parties and their associated activities were not his cup of tea. He had always been a bookworm, dedicating himself to maintaining good grades to secure a promising career like his father. His father had always discouraged him from reckless behavior and he did whatever his father told him to, but maybe this time it could be different?

If he stayed in his room, he was the creep. If he went, he risked his father’s disappointment. Either way, he was trapped. Finally, he decided: “What harm could it do? I’ll give it a try.”

The following days passed swiftly, and Daniel was getting ready for the upcoming party in his private dorm room. Thanks to his father’s extra payment, he didn’t have to deal with a lousy roommate who might “influence him negatively” according to his father. As he glanced at himself in the mirror, he couldn’t help but feel self-conscious. His scrawny frame, thin shoulders and ill-fitting glasses epitomized the image of a social outcast. Contemplating a change, he removed his glasses, hoping they might enhance his appearance and make him seem cooler. Though it meant sacrificing clear vision, Daniel reassured himself: “It’ll be fine. What’s the worst that can happen?”

The music could’ve been heard from 3 blocks down. Nervousness hit Daniel as he climbed the steps of the frat house, neon lights flickering like warning signs through dingy curtained windows. The booming music echoed disrupting his heartbeat. Suddenly the door flung open, a drunk student stumbles out the door and vomits over the railing, losing his balance and tumbling into the bushes. Daniel froze, ready to bolt at the sight —until strong hands grabbed him.

Matty, the lively girl who had texted him, smiled too wide. Her eyes lingered on him, measuring. Ralph her boyfriend, stood towering next to her, his hulking frame sending fear through Daniel. Ralph’s grip on his shoulders was uncomfortably tight but Matty’s words were worse, “You weren’t thinking about leaving, were you?” Matty teased.  
“No,” Daniel lied.  
“Good. We hate quitters. Now come inside and let’s get this night started.” Matty said with a blissful tone. 

Inside, the party was chaos. Blaring music. Students chanting “chug, chug, chug.” Lines of cocaine on tables. Smoke so thick it made Daniel choke. Dealers handed out handfuls of loose pills like candy. Daniel’s stomach turned. Report it, and he’d be a snitch. Leave, and he’d be a loser. Stay, and maybe he’d finally belong.

Matty shoved a red solo cup into his hand. “Drink.”  
He gulped, vodka, his throat burned but the warmth spread. Another cup followed—sweet, syrupy, strange. His body hummed, light as air, his mind started moving slower. 

Walls dripped like wax. Faces melted into grotesque masks, whispering his name. He reached for his glasses, but they were gone. How could he see without them, now the blur became monstrous—colors bleeding, shadows twisting. He was just trying to keep it calm, cool, collected. Then all of a sudden, Matty appeared again, a few blotter tabs in her palm, mixed with a few red and white pills. Matty didn’t take any. 
“Open up,” she said.  
He paused.  
“I said open up!” Her tone was menacing and Daniel was too far out of it to think for himself right now. 
So, He obeyed.

The world fractured. His heartbeat thundered, then slowed to a crawl. The floor rippled beneath him. His own hands looked alien, melting into the couch. His breathe seemed to hitch, the corners of his mouth felt locked, his stomached churning. 

Matty leaned close, her lips brushing his ear as her words cut sharp:  
“You’re done, Daniel. No books. No daddy’s money. Just you—foaming, choking, dying. I’m gonna watch as you suffer and I’m gonna enjoy it.” She almost giggled with this last part. Daniel was in horror, he thought Matty was his friend now he knew otherwise. 

He collapsed, convulsing, froth spilling from his lips. The room warped into endless corridors, each echoing his father’s disappointed face.

Dawn came and police lights flickered outside the frat house. Daniel’s body was hauled onto a stretcher, barely hanging on. 

“Officer, I found him like this,” Matty said, voice trembling with false concern. “Will he be alright, I would hate for anything to happen to him” she said faking a whimper of sadness. 
The officer just nodded, commending her bravery, and said “ there isn’t anything we can do, these kids aren’t going to listen, they’ll just keep doing this stuff. So trust me, he won’t be the last. Just stay safe and keep out of trouble.” The officer left and Matty stood there alone in front of the frat house stairs watching as they left on the ambulance. She smiled politely but inside, her sick satisfaction bloomed. As the ambulance doors shut, her phone buzzed again. Another name lit the screen. Another lamb.

She typed back:  
“Don’t be boring. We hate boring.”
