The Yellow Drop and The Plain Before Sound

In the first morning, the ear held only a pale, waiting surface — a blank stretch of nothing that felt more like potential than space.

Then a yellow drop of sound fell.

It struck the emptiness and rippled outward in warm, trembling rings, the way the first stir of wind might ripple across a still lake. The ripple brightened the plain, hinting at depth, hinting at direction, as if the world were remembering how to begin.

A second sound followed — a high, feather‑light note that fluttered across the ripples like a bird testing the air.  
Then a deeper tone arrived, slow and weighted, settling into the ground like packed earth.  
A bright, sharp chime cracked through the space, breaking open the horizon the way a wave breaks against stone.

They fell at irregular intervals, like a hesitant composer testing notes in an empty hall.  
Each one a different shade of sound — thin silver ticks, dull red thuds, pale blue hums — spreading outward, overlapping, interfering.  
Where they crossed, new patterns formed: soft oscillations, trembling grids, invisible architecture.

This was not light painting a scene.  
This was sound building one.

High notes lifted the air, giving it height.  
Low notes thickened the ground, giving it weight.  
Mid‑tones stretched themselves into long, trembling lines that became the first hints of structure — arches of resonance, pillars of vibration, corridors made of nothing but pressure and rhythm.

The soundscape grew more intricate.  
The air thickened with tone.  
The ground hummed with quiet intention.

And still, the yellow drop kept falling.

Each drip struck the forming landscape with a steady, patient rhythm.  
Each one added a new layer of motion.  
Each one deepened the sense that something was gathering itself, preparing to become.

The rhythm quickened — not frantic, but inevitable, like a tide rising toward shore.  
The whole sound‑world leaned forward, trembling at the edge of revelation.

Then the final drop fell — bright, sharp, impossibly clear.

And the body opened its eyes.

The sound‑architecture collapsed in an instant, folding back into silence.  
The ripples flattened.  
The spires dissolved.  
The trembling ground became nothing more than sensation.

The point of view slid outward, smooth as breath — from the ear’s inner creation to the dim room beyond the eyelids.

We see now through the body’s eyes.

The alarm is buzzing.  
The morning has begun.  
But for a heartbeat, the echo of that sound‑born world lingers — the memory of a universe that rose and fell in the space between dreaming and waking.
