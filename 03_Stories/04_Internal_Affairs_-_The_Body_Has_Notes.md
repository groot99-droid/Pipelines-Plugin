Internal Affairs: The Body Has Notes

1. The City Scene — “New York Should Come With Padding”

He walks through Midtown like everyone else: head forward, earbuds in, mind on autopilot.

But his left shoulder is already fed up.

Left Shoulder:  
“Oh fantastic, another elbow. Does no one here know how to walk? I swear this city is just a pinball machine with rent.”

Right Shoulder:  
“Calm down. It’s New York. We get bumped. It’s part of the charm.”

Left Shoulder:  
“Charm? That guy just used me as a turn signal.”

His ribs chime in, rattling with indignation.

Ribs:  
“He’s right, you know. We’re basically public infrastructure at this point.”

His knees, always dramatic, groan.

Knees:  
“Can we not fight? We’re doing all the work down here.”

His feet, exhausted and cynical, mutter without lifting their voices.

Feet:  
“Try being stepped on. Then talk to me about suffering.”

Meanwhile, the man himself?  
He’s thinking about whether he left the stove on.



2. The Love Scene — “The Heart Has Notes, The Stomach Has Opinions”

He sees someone beautiful on the subway.

His heart immediately leaps into a full orchestral swell.

Heart:  
“Oh. Oh. Oh. This is it. This is the moment. Everyone be cool.”

His lungs inhale sharply.

Lungs:  
“Why are we breathing like we’re running a marathon? Calm down.”

His hands start sweating.

Hands:  
“We’re nervous! We’re allowed to be nervous!”

His stomach, always the realist, grumbles.

Stomach:  
“Don’t get attached. We’re still recovering from the last crush.”

His brain, trying to maintain dignity, clears its throat.

Brain:  
“Everyone shut up. We’re going to smile politely and not embarrass ourselves.”

His heart, already composing wedding vows:  
“I think we should marry them.”

His brain:  
“We don’t even know their name.”

His heart:  
“Names are just labels.”

His brain:  
“Please stop.”



3. The Anger Scene — “The Body Reacts Before the Man Does”

Someone cuts him off in traffic.

His jaw clenches instantly.

Jaw:  
“Say the word. I’m ready to grind.”

His hands tighten on the wheel.

Hands:  
“Let me honk. Just once. A polite honk. A civilized honk.”

His heart spikes.

Heart:  
“Fight or flight! Fight or flight! I vote fight!”

His brain, exhausted:  
“We’re not fighting anyone. We’re going to breathe.”

His lungs, reluctantly:  
“Fine. In… out… in… out…”

His teeth, muttering:  
“Cowards.”



4. The Hunger‑While‑Dieting Scene — “Civil War in the Abdomen”

He walks past a bakery.

His nose swoons.

Nose:  
“Is that… cinnamon? Fresh bread? We should go in. For research.”

His stomach roars like a mythic beast.

Stomach:  
“YES. FEED ME.”

His brain, clutching a clipboard:  
“No. We’re on a diet. We agreed.”

His stomach:  
“I didn’t agree to anything.”

His mouth, already watering:  
“I mean… one bite wouldn’t hurt.”

His brain:  
“It absolutely would.”

His legs, traitorous:  
“We’re turning toward the bakery.”

His brain:  
“No we’re not.”

His legs:  
“We already did.”

His willpower, tiny and trembling:  
“Please… please… don’t…”

His stomach:  
“TO GLORY.”



5. The Quiet Moment — “Everyone Has Something to Say”

He sits on a park bench at the end of the day.

His back sighs with relief.

Back:  
“Finally. A surface that isn’t moving.”

His feet collapse dramatically.

Feet:  
“We’re retiring. Effective immediately.”

His eyes, softening:  
“The light is nice right now.”

His heart, calmer:  
“We made it through another day.”

His brain, for once, quiet:  
“Yeah. We did.”

And for a moment, the whole body agrees — a rare, fleeting peace treaty.

6. The Gym Scene — “Mutiny on Treadmill Row”

He steps onto the treadmill, determined to “get back in shape.”

His legs immediately protest.

Legs:  
“Oh no. Absolutely not. We did stairs yesterday.”

His lungs, already wheezing:  
“Why are we doing cardio without warning? I need a memo.”

His heart, trying to be supportive:  
“Guys, it’s good for us. We’re getting stronger.”

His lower back:  
“Speak for yourself. I’m about to file a complaint.”

His brain, pretending to be a coach:  
“We’re doing ten minutes. That’s it.”

His feet:  
“Ten minutes? On this conveyor belt of suffering?”

His arms, swinging uselessly:  
“What exactly is our role here?”

His legs:  
“You’re decorative. Stay out of it.”

Meanwhile, the man himself is thinking:  
“Wow, I’m actually doing great today.”



7. The First Date — “The Body Attempts Diplomacy”

He sits across from someone he really likes.

His heart is already playing a string quartet.

Heart:  
“This is romance. This is destiny. This is—”

Brain:  
“Stop. We’re focusing. We’re listening. We’re normal.”

His hands, sweating profusely:  
“We’re not normal. We’re melting.”

His stomach, nervous:  
“I swear if we laugh too hard I’m going to make noises.”

His mouth, trying to flirt:  
“Say something charming. Something clever. Something—”

His tongue:  
“I panicked. I said ‘I like your… fork.’”

His brain:  
“WHY WOULD YOU SAY THAT.”

His heart:  
“I’m still proud of us.”



8. The Job Interview — “Corporate Chaos Inside the Ribcage”

He sits in a waiting room, rehearsing answers.

His brain is in full crisis mode.

Brain:  
“Okay, we practiced. We’re ready. We’re confident.”

His hands, trembling:  
“We are absolutely not confident.”

His heart:  
“I’m beating too fast. Is that allowed? Is that legal?”

His stomach:  
“I’m going to growl. I’m warning everyone right now.”

His mouth:  
“Please don’t. Please. I beg you.”

His armpits, sweating aggressively:  
“We’re doing our best.”

His brain:  
“No you’re not.”



9. The Horror Movie — “Every Body Part Has a Different Threshold”

He sits on the couch watching a scary movie.

His eyes widen.

Eyes:  
“Why are we watching this. Why. Why.”

His ears:  
“I’m closing myself. I’m done. I’m out.”

His heart:  
“RUN. RUN. RUN. I DON’T CARE IF IT’S A MOVIE.”

His hands, covering his face:  
“We’re not looking. We refuse.”

His brain:  
“It’s fictional. Relax.”

His spine, shivering:  
“Tell that to the goosebumps.”

His feet:  
“We’re ready to sprint if needed.”

His brain:  
“You’re on a couch.”

His feet:  
“We’ll sprint anyway.”



10. The Grocery Store — “Diet vs. Desire: A Civil War”

He walks past the snack aisle.

His stomach gasps dramatically.

Stomach:  
“Look. Look. Chips. Cookies. Glory.”

His brain:  
“No. We’re eating healthy.”

His mouth:  
“But… the textures…”

His nose:  
“I smell artificial cheese. I want artificial cheese.”

His hands, reaching toward a bag:  
“We’re just holding it. Not buying it.”

His brain:  
“PUT IT BACK.”

His hands:  
“We can’t. It’s chosen us.”

His wallet, muffled from the pocket:  
“I beg you. Have mercy.”



11. The Argument — “Anger Has a Union”

Someone says something rude.

His jaw locks.

Jaw:  
“Say the word. I’m ready to clench.”

His fists tighten.

Fists:  
“We can throw hands. We’ve trained for this.”

His heart:  
“Adrenaline deployed. You’re welcome.”

His brain:  
“No fighting. We’re adults.”

His teeth:  
“Coward.”

His lungs:  
“Deep breaths. Deep breaths. Deep—”

His eyebrows, furrowing:  
“We’re sending a message.”

His brain:  
“Not that message.”



12. The Sleep Scene — “Everyone Wants Something Different”

He lies in bed.

His eyes:  
“Close us. We’re done.”

His brain:  
“No. Let’s think about every mistake we’ve ever made.”

His legs:  
“We’re restless. We want to run a marathon right now.”

His back:  
“I’m uncomfortable. Adjust. Adjust again. Adjust more.”

His stomach:  
“I want a snack.”

His mouth:  
“I second that.”

His brain:  
“No snacks. Sleep.”

His heart:  
“I’m beating weird. Is that normal? Should we panic?”

His brain:  
“No. Stop. Everyone stop.”

His body:  
“We refuse.”
