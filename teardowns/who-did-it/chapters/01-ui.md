# UI and frontend

The client is a Phaser game with a DOM overlay. Phaser draws the world and the scenes (Boot, Intro,
Landing, Lobby, RoleReveal, World, Night, Kitchen, Meeting, GameOver, Admin). The DOM draws every
panel, card and menu. In numbers: 179 source files and about 36,000 lines, 87 test files and about
576 test cases ([[area:client]]), 132 design tokens, 725 UI strings in `apps/client/src/ui/copy/`, and
53 screens across the Phaser, DOM and HUD layers.

In two weeks the UI has had two looks. The 2026-10-10 v0.1 look was bone paper with ink frames: the
screenshots in `docs/ui/` ([[sec:UI:12]]). On 2026-10-23, round 7 re-skinned the whole game to your
two Claude Design mockups: night glass, pixel type and a lantern accent ([[dec:A103]], [[dec:A106]]).
Your brief answers tell this chapter what the re-skin should keep, and in two places they disagree
with what round 7 actually built.

## One design system, enforced by tests

**Describe.** `apps/client/src/ui/theme/tokens.css` defines every colour, size, radius, shadow,
duration and font as a `--wdi-*` property. Every hex comes from `packages/content/data/palette.json`.
`theme.test.ts` fails the build in four cases:
- a hex outside the palette;
- a literal `rgb()`;
- a font family that is not a token;
- a text pair under its WCAG AA floor ([[sec:UI:10]]).

The night set (night, violet, indigo, lilac, snow) is a separate palette block for the DOM only, so the
world palette stays the art palette ([[dec:A106]]). Even the card frames are tokens: the frame tokens
hold inline SVG nine-slice images.

**Compare** (Claude's analysis). Few hobby games enforce their palette and contrast in CI. It is closer
to a product design system than to the usual jam-game stylesheet. Among Us shipped a deliberately
plain UI, and its identity lives in the characters. Who Did It puts much more of its identity in the
interface itself.

**Critique** (Claude's analysis). This is the strongest part of the frontend. It is also why the
teardown's explorer could wear the game's look by copying tokens alone. The one gap is that the
reference screenshots are stale. All 16 files in `docs/ui/` date from 2026-10-10, before both round 5's
isometric world and round 7's re-skin. The HUD and role card they show are not the game any more.
Anyone reading UI.md §12 sees a game that no longer exists.

## Glass for the world, paper for what you hold

**Describe.** You answered that the split is intended: glass for menus and the world, paper for what
you hold. Round 7 built something else. Session 7F moved the match screens to the night theme
([[sec:UI:14.27]], [[dec:A110]]):
- the role card keeps its frame and flip but takes an indigo fill with lilac text;
- the HUD, its log, the toasts, the notebook sheet and the settings panel all became glass over the world.

Today the only paper left is in components that 7F did not restyle.

**Compare** (Claude's analysis). Secret Hitler's physical cards and Town of Salem's will-and-notes
panels both use a "this is a document in your hand" material. The material itself tells you the
thing is private. Your rule works the same way, and it suits a hidden-role game: paper means *mine,
secret*, glass means *shared, the world*.

**Critique** (Claude's analysis). A110 is the newer written decision and points the other way. **On hold,
by your choice (final brief):** don't change it now. You haven't looked at the glass version yet, you
didn't like the old paper style, and if "what you hold" gets its own material again it will be a new style
you design. Until then A110 stands, and nothing here asks anyone to change it.

The teardown's explorer currently deals role cards on paper. It follows your answer, not the build.

## Pixel type: menus, not matches

**Describe.** You chose pixel type on menus and not in matches. In the code, A110 kept Inter for
running text (card text, goal, abilities, Beware, notebook). It still uses **Press Start 2P inside the
match** for short caps:
- the role name and the faction word on the card (`apps/client/src/ui/match.css:39`, `:51`);
- the night clock (`apps/client/src/view/hud.css:170`).

Pixelify Sans is used for the HUD chip's role line (`hud.css:154`). In total, `match.css` names a pixel
font 18 times and `hud.css` 4 times.

**Compare** (Claude's analysis). Pixel display faces read well large and short, as in title screens
and score counters, and badly small and under time pressure. Games that commit to pixel type usually
render it at whole-pixel multiples and keep in-play text short.

**Critique** (Claude's analysis). Your answer agrees with the "Readable in 20 seconds" pillar
([[sec:GDD:1]]), and A110 already did most of the work. What remains is a short audit:
- the role name, the faction word, the clock and the HUD role line move to Oswald, the existing
  `--wdi-font-display` token;
- the role reveal screen (RoleReveal) and the game-over screen can stay pixel, because they are
  ceremonies, not play.

## What you know about places you are not in

**Describe.** You chose to keep things as they are. Today that means:
- **directional sound lines:** "A scream from the {dir}", "A door to the {dir}", "Footsteps to the
  {dir}" (`apps/client/src/ui/copy/world.ts:58`-`60`);
- the bell and the train;
- the 3-second door swing that others see when you go through a door (`apps/server/src/sim/Portals.ts`;
  [[sec:GDD:2]]).

The v0.1 HUD mockup also shows a named presence line, "Wren entered The Drowned Lantern"
([[shot:hud-1280x720]]). No such string exists in the copy, so it was never built.

**Compare** (Claude's analysis). Among Us gives you nothing outside your vision cone: rooms are
silent. Here you can hear, which supports "dread, then suspicion", because the scream arrives before
the body does.

**Critique** (Claude's analysis). The current state agrees with "space is information". A sound
crosses a door only as a direction, never as a name. The named line from the mockup would break the
pillar: it hands out alibis the world should make players earn by watching doors. If "a little more"
meant that named line, it needs a decision, because it changes what crosses a door.

## Phone and desktop, equally

**Describe.** You want phone and desktop to be equal. Every v0.1 mockup has a 1280×720 and a 390 px
version. The HUD has a stick and four touch buttons (B bell, E use, Shift sneak, Tab card).

**Critique** (Claude's analysis). Equal support means every screen is checked at both sizes. Today
only the v0.1 screens have both shots. The two round-7 mockups are 1280-only ([[shot:menu-mockup-1280x720]]),
and so is the re-skin built from them. The Playwright specs in `apps/client/e2e/` (16 of them) could
take both shots on each run and replace the stale `docs/ui/` set. Then "equal" is tested, not just
intended.
