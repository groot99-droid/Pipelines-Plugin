# Screens, glitches and the paper card

The game has four stacked screens (home, loading, room, walk) and a glitch veil that cuts between
them ([[screen:shell/index]], `index.html`). Everything that sits over the picture is small and
mostly at the edges: a paper caption card, a flickering room title, a LOG tab, a SKIP tab, a sound
toggle, and in the walk an objective line, a readout and a use prompt. The owner's answers set the
frame for this chapter: a loading screen is "A room of its own", SKIP is for use "Only if something
breaks", and the owner plays it on a "Phone". Read against those three answers, the interface gets
the loading screen right and two other things slightly wrong. SKIP is offered more readily than the
owner wants. And the last room's button promises a restart before the last walk has begun.

## The loading screen as a room

**Describe.** While `body.phase-load` is set, the scanlines, the vignette, the sound toggle, the LOG
tab and its panel are all hidden. The clip is also not crop-scaled, so the frame holds the video
alone (`styles.css:152`). The clip runs for exactly `LOAD_SECONDS` (5.0) of *playback* time. The
count starts when the picture starts and pauses while it buffers (`script.js:111`,
`script.js:450`). Three guards keep the screen from ever hanging: it never started
(`LOAD_START_MS`, 6 s), the picture froze (`LOAD_STALL_MS`, 6 s), and an absolute ceiling
(`LOAD_CAP_MS`, 15 s). The stall guard re-arms only on a real advance of `currentTime`
(`script.js:442`). The room video starts downloading only once the clip is actually playing, so
the two never compete for the connection (`script.js:471`). The section's track starts with the
clip and runs on through the glitch and the room, so the clip and the room that follows are one
continuous piece of sound ([[sec:README:notes]]). This took four pull requests in two days to get
right: bare loading screens, then the 5.0 s trim, then the hang fix ([[pr:4]] to [[pr:7]]).

**Compare** *(Claude's analysis)*. Most games treat a loading screen as the thing to apologise for.
Here the loading clip carries the passages between rooms: the shaft after the grate, the vent into
the trampoline park, the sleeper on the couch ([[sec:README:scenes]]). That is the job P.T. gives
its doorway, which returns you to the corridor without a cut. The difference is that Right Door
*keeps* the cut and makes the cut the place.

**Critique** *(Claude's analysis)*. "A room of its own" is exactly what the code builds, and it is
one of the most carefully engineered parts of the game. The cost of that care is that the five
seconds are a promise the room videos then break: the room's video loops, but the loading clip
never does (`script.js:483`). A player cannot linger in this room, which may be the point.

## SKIP: a safety net with the door left open

**Describe.** SKIP is a white tab on the right edge. It takes the LOG tab's place during a loading
clip and during a walk, and is hidden everywhere else (`styles.css:166`, `styles.css:557`). On a
loading screen it calls the same `finish()` that the clip's end and the guards call
(`script.js:498`). In a walk it ends the walk as `'skipped'` and goes on to the next loading clip
(`walk/walk.js:139`). There is no confirmation. On a phone (`max-width: 640px`) the tab moves down
to the right edge, 16% of the screen height from the bottom, at 38 × 104 px (`styles.css:897`).

**Critique** *(Claude's analysis)*. The owner wants SKIP used only if something breaks. On a
loading screen the code already handles every break SKIP could fix, on its own: a clip that never
starts, that freezes, or that runs long is cut within 6 to 15 seconds. So on that screen SKIP only
ever does what the owner does *not* want, which is to cut a room short that is working. In the
walk the case is different and stronger. A broken room has no other way out, and a phone user
whose thumb joystick or look drag goes wrong needs one. Two conflicts follow from playing on a
phone. The right half of the screen is the look-drag area ([[input:touch]]), and the SKIP tab
sits on that half's edge, low, where a right thumb rests. And one tap ends the walk with no undo.
I have not tested this on a device, but an accidental skip would be easy to reach. Showing SKIP
only after a delay, or after the walk sees no progress, or on a second tap (as Ninety-Nine's EXIT
does), would match the owner's intent more closely.

## The paper card and the room title

**Describe.** Each room opens with a paper card that types the caption out one character at a
time, at 30 to 48 ms per character. It pauses longer after a full stop or a question mark, less
after a comma, and now and then at random, so the typing reads as a person and not a machine
(`script.js:348`). Each card is tilted by its chapter's own `rot`, from -1.7° to 1.6°
(`script.js:575`). A click on the card, Enter or Space finishes the caption. Five seconds after it
finishes, the card fades away and leaves the room on its own, while NEXT stays (`script.js:323`).
Above the card the room's name sits over its epithet. Each letter of the epithet flickers like a
fluorescent tube on its own random timing, and every five seconds one or two letters burst
(`script.js:275`, `script.js:290`). Under reduced motion the caption prints at once, the flicker
stops, and the glitch becomes a 260 ms fade (`script.js:340`, `script.js:384`, `styles.css:840`).

**Compare** *(Claude's analysis)*. The typed card and the LOG together act as found writing: the
caption is what the walker is thinking now, and the log is what they wrote down. The found-footage
Backrooms tells its story through the camera alone and has no narrator. Right Door gives its one
voice the paper. That makes the game quieter, and more of a walkthrough in the owner's sense.

**Critique** *(Claude's analysis)*. On the last room the button under the card reads **BEGIN
AGAIN ↻**, not NEXT (`script.js:369`). That label was written when the last room's NEXT went
straight home, before the walks existed (commit 987395f). Since the walks were added, pressing it
starts the grocery store's walk instead (`script.js:651`). So the game says "begin again" one room
too early, before the maze, the anomaly and the EXIT door. Against "Trapped: there was never a way
out" ([[answer:1]]) it also gives the ending away: the player is told the run will restart before
looking for the exit. If the label is meant for the trap, it belongs after the exit. If it isn't,
it should read NEXT like the other six.

## The phone

**Describe.** The phone layout narrows the log panel to 82% of the width, moves the card to 46%
of the height, and sets the objective, readout and tally in small boxed lines near the top
(`styles.css:856`). The use prompt sits at 65% of the height, and the hint near the bottom.
Touch gets a joystick on the left half and a look drag on the right ([[input:touch]]), and a
portrait screen gets a taller field of view ([[sec:README:built-for-phones]]). The walk test runs
a `--mobile` pass at 390 × 844 with touch and 2× pixel density ([[sec:README:testing]]).

**Critique** *(Claude's analysis)*. For an owner who plays on a phone this is good support, and the
test proves the phone path rather than assuming it. One gap: the browser test can tap the prompt,
but it cannot feel a thumb. The SKIP placement above and the gate veil's "CLICK ANYWHERE TO BEGIN"
both use desktop words and positions. On a phone it is a tap, and the veil's wording is the first
thing the player reads.

## Owner's word

- *Each loading screen is the clip and nothing else: no overlay, no LOG, no sound toggle, exactly
  5.0 seconds. What is a loading screen to you?* — "A room of its own" ([[answer:5]])
- *SKIP is offered during every loading clip and every walk, so no room is a dead end. Do you want
  people to use it?* — "Only if something breaks" ([[answer:6]])
- *Phone or desktop first: which one do you play it on yourself?* — "Phone" ([[answer:7]])
