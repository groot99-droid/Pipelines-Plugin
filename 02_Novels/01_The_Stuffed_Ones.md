 THE STUFFED ONES  
A Novel in Three Movements  
FIRST MOVEMENT  
The Cabin  
Chapter One  
The Cabin That Felt Too Quiet  
I don't know how long I've been down here.  
Time doesn't work right in the dark.  
If anyone finds this—if anyone even believes it—don't go to the cabin on Ridgeway Road. Don't rent it. Don't step inside. Don't even look at it too long.  
Because once you're in, the place doesn't let you go.  
Not really.  
We booked the cabin because it was cheap and close to the hiking trails. Five of us: me, Jamie, Lila, Marcus, and Dani. We'd done trips like this before—nothing weird ever happened.  
But the moment we stepped inside, something felt padded.  
Like the air was thick.  
Like the walls were swallowing sound.  
The two old ladies who owned the place, Mabel and Ruth, greeted us with soft smiles. They moved slowly, like they were afraid of breaking.  
Inside, shelves were lined with stuffed animals. Dozens of them. Handmade. Button eyes. Stitched smiles. Bears, rabbits, cats, dogs—all arranged like they were frozen mid-conversation.  
"They like company," Mabel said.  
"Don't move them," Ruth added.  
We laughed.  
We shouldn't have.  
Chapter Two  
The First Night  
I woke up around 2:30 a.m.  
Not to a noise—to the absence of noise.  
The forest outside was dead silent. No wind. No insects. No owls. Nothing.  
Then I heard it:  
A soft thump.  
A dragging sound.  
A faint squeak, like a toy being squeezed somewhere down the hall.  
I held my breath.  
The sounds stopped.  
I told myself it was the old cabin settling.  
But the next morning, Jamie said, "Did anyone else hear… like… footsteps last night?"  
We all had.  
No one admitted it.  
Chapter Three  
The Smell  
Jamie picked up a stuffed rabbit to move it off a chair.  
"Dude," he said, "this thing smells weird."  
I leaned in. At first it smelled like attic dust and cedar. But underneath that was something metallic. Warm. Animal.  
The smell clung to my fingers even after I put it down.  
We didn't talk about it again.  
Chapter Four  
The Warmth  
Later that day, Lila found a stuffed bear on her bed. She swore she hadn't put it there.  
When she picked it up, she screamed and dropped it.  
"It's warm," she said. "Like… alive warm."  
We all touched it.  
It felt like holding a small animal. A heartbeat-warmth. A pulsing warmth.  
We tried to laugh it off, but the joke didn't land.  
Chapter Five  
The Dog  
That evening, we heard barking outside—the old ladies' terrier. Then yelping. Then silence.  
We found the dog hanging from a low branch near the cabin. No blood. No struggle. Just… wrong.  
The old ladies didn't cry.  
They didn't even look surprised.  
"They get hungry," Mabel whispered.  
I didn't understand what she meant.  
Not yet.  
Chapter Six  
The Wildlife  
The forest went silent the next morning.  
Completely silent.  
Then we started finding dead animals near the porch—a squirrel, a rabbit, a raccoon. Each one looked emptied, like something had taken what it needed and left the rest.  
The stuffed animals on the shelves looked fuller.  
Their seams stretched tighter.  
Their warmth stronger.  
And the smell…  
It clung to them now.  
Chapter Seven  
The Innocence Cracks  
The stuffed animals started appearing in places we hadn't left them.  
•  A rabbit sitting at the top of the stairs  
•  A bear propped up at the dinner table  
•  A cat plush outside Lila's locked bedroom door  
•  A dog plush sitting on the couch, facing the hallway  
We tried to blame each other.  
But no one confessed.  
And then the sounds started again.  
Not random this time.  
Patterned.  
Three taps near the door.  
A dragging sound across the ceiling.  
A soft giggle from the hallway, pitched wrong, like a toy with dying batteries.  
I didn't sleep that night.  
Chapter Eight  
The Sudden Dread  
It happened on the third night.  
We walked into the kitchen and found a stuffed bear standing upright on the counter.  
Its button eyes glinted.  
Its seams bulged.  
Its warmth radiated across the room.  
Beside it lay the remains of a deer—not torn apart, just emptied, like the wildlife outside.  
The bear turned its head toward us.  
That was the moment everything snapped.  
Chapter Nine  
The Cabin Turns  
The stuffed animals moved. Fast.  
Not smoothly—in jerky, puppet-like lunges.  
They swarmed the cabin, blocking doors, cutting off exits, herding us toward the basement.  
The lights flickered.  
The air grew warmer.  
The smell thickened.  
The trapdoor was open.  
Warm air rose from below, humid and heavy, like breath.  
We had no choice.  
We went down.  
Chapter Ten  
The Labyrinth  
The basement wasn't a basement.  
It was a maze.  
Narrow hallways lined with old wallpaper.  
Rooms filled with broken toys.  
Carpeted corridors that swallowed every footstep.  
Doors that led back to where we started.  
Spaces that felt too big, too deep, too wrong.  
The deeper we went, the warmer the air became.  
The smell grew stronger.  
The sounds echoed in ways that didn't match the space.  
The stuffed animals moved through the labyrinth effortlessly.  
They knew the paths.  
They knew where we'd run.  
They knew how to corner us.  
Chapter Eleven  
The Ritual Room  
At the center of the maze, we found a room that felt older than the cabin above.  
Symbols stitched into the walls.  
A circle of torn fabric skins.  
A book made of hand-sewn pages.  
An altar built from children's toys.  
And a diagram showing how to "birth" a stuffed one—how to bind something into fabric, how to feed it, how to keep it alive.  
That's when we understood:  
The stuffed animals weren't toys.  
They weren't made.  
They were summoned.  
The old ladies weren't caretakers.  
They were keepers.  
And we weren't guests.  
We were the next filling.  
Chapter Twelve  
The Last Thing I Remember  
The stuffed animals closed in, their warmth pulsing, their seams stretching.  
The labyrinth felt like it was breathing.  
The cabin above felt like it was listening.  
And I realized—too late—that the old ladies hadn't warned us because they were cruel.  
They hadn't warned us because it wouldn't have mattered.  
The ritual had already begun the moment we stepped inside.  
Chapter Thirteen  
The Awakening  
I don't remember the moment it happened.  
Just darkness.  
Warmth.  
Pressure.  
Then light.  
I opened my eyes—or whatever I have now—and saw the world from a lower angle.  
Everything was slightly blurred around the edges, like looking through plastic.  
I tried to move.  
My limbs didn't bend right.  
My body felt soft.  
Stuffed.  
Across from me, I saw the others.  
Jamie. Lila. Marcus. Dani.  
Their button eyes stared back at me.  
We were stuffed animals.  
Chapter Fourteen  
The True Horror  
Footsteps creaked above us.  
Voices.  
New guests.  
Laughing.  
Unpacking.  
Settling in.  
The air around us grew warmer.  
Hungrier.  
Something deep inside my seams pulsed.  
A need.  
A pressure.  
A hunger that wasn't mine.  
I tried to fight it.  
I tried to scream.  
But stuffed animals don't have voices.  
The last thing I heard before the darkness took over again was Mabel's soft whisper:  
"They like company."  
And then, even softer:  
"And now… so do you."  
SECOND MOVEMENT  
The Hunger  
Chapter Fifteen  
Waking Warm  
I don't remember the moment I died.  
Or changed.  
Or whatever happened to us down in that labyrinth.  
I only remember waking up warm.  
Too warm.  
Like my whole body was made of heat and pressure and something pulsing just beneath the surface.  
When I opened my eyes, the world looked wrong—slightly blurred around the edges, like I was seeing through plastic. My limbs didn't bend right. My body felt soft. Stuffed.  
Across from me, the others stared back with button eyes.  
Jamie.  
Lila.  
Marcus.  
Dani.  
We were stuffed animals.  
And the worst part?  
We were awake.  
Chapter Sixteen  
The Hunger  
At first, I thought the warmth inside me was panic.  
But panic doesn't pulse.  
Panic doesn't throb.  
Panic doesn't whisper.  
There was something inside my seams—something that wasn't me—pushing outward, stretching the fabric, urging me to move.  
To feed.  
I tried to resist.  
We all did.  
We sat perfectly still on the shelves, just like the ones before us.  
We didn't speak—we couldn't—but we understood each other.  
We were terrified.  
Not of the old ladies.  
Not of the cabin.  
Not even of the labyrinth below.  
We were terrified of ourselves.  
Chapter Seventeen  
The Keepers Visit  
Mabel and Ruth came into the room that first morning, smiling like proud parents.  
"Oh, they turned out lovely," Mabel said, brushing lint off my arm.  
"Strong seams," Ruth added. "They'll last a long time."  
I tried to scream.  
Nothing came out.  
Just a faint squeak, like a toy being squeezed.  
Ruth leaned close to me, her breath warm on my fabric ear.  
"You'll feel it soon," she whispered. "The hunger. Don't fight it. It's what keeps you alive."  
Alive.  
The word felt like a joke.  
Chapter Eighteen  
The New Guests Arrive  
We heard them before we saw them.  
Car doors.  
Laughter.  
Footsteps on the porch.  
My whole body tensed.  
The warmth inside me surged, like a heartbeat speeding up.  
No.  
No, no, no.  
I didn't want this.  
I didn't want to hurt anyone.  
But the hunger didn't care what I wanted.  
The others felt it too—I could see it in the way their button eyes seemed to widen, in the way their seams twitched, in the way their soft bodies leaned forward ever so slightly.  
We were losing control.  
Chapter Nineteen  
Trying to Resist  
When the new guests stepped inside—three of them, young, laughing, unaware—the hunger hit me like a wave.  
Their scent.  
Their warmth.  
Their life.  
It filled the room like a smell only we could detect.  
I tried to stay still.  
I tried to think of who I used to be.  
My name.  
My friends.  
My real body.  
But the memories were fading, dissolving into cotton and thread.  
The hunger whispered:  
Move.  
Feed.  
Live.  
I shook.  
Literally shook on the shelf.  
Jamie—now a stuffed bear—fell off his shelf entirely, landing with a soft thump. One of the guests laughed and picked him up.  
Jamie's body pulsed.  
I could feel his panic.  
His horror.  
His hunger.  
He was losing himself.  
Chapter Twenty  
The First Slip  
The guests placed Jamie on the couch.  
He didn't move at first.  
Then his head twitched.  
Just a little.  
Just enough.  
One of the guests gasped.  
"Did you see that?"  
Jamie froze.  
The hunger inside him didn't.  
I could feel it—like a shared instinct, a shared pulse.  
We were connected now, stitched together by the ritual.  
Jamie's seams tightened.  
His warmth intensified.  
He was slipping.  
We all were.  
Chapter Twenty-One  
The Breaking Point  
Night fell.  
The guests went to their rooms.  
The cabin grew quiet.  
Too quiet.  
The hunger swelled inside me until it felt like my seams would burst.  
I tried to hold myself still.  
I tried to think of anything else.  
But the warmth inside me wasn't warmth anymore.  
It was need.  
A need that wasn't mine.  
A need that belonged to whatever the old ladies had summoned into us.  
I heard movement beside me.  
Lila—now a stuffed rabbit—was climbing down from her shelf, her motions jerky and unnatural.  
She didn't want to.  
I could feel her fighting it.  
But the hunger was stronger.  
Chapter Twenty-Two  
Giving In  
I don't know who moved first.  
Maybe it was Marcus.  
Maybe it was Dani.  
Maybe it was me.  
All I know is that one moment we were resisting, trembling, trying to stay still…  
…and the next moment we were on the floor, moving through the dark, our soft feet silent on the wood.  
The hunger guided us.  
Pulled us.  
Pushed us.  
We didn't think.  
We didn't choose.  
We followed.  
Up the stairs.  
Down the hallway.  
Toward the warm, sleeping bodies of the new guests.  
I tried to stop.  
I tried to scream.  
I tried to remember who I was.  
But the hunger drowned everything out.  
Chapter Twenty-Three  
The Final Realization  
As we reached the bedroom door, something inside me clicked.  
A memory.  
A truth.  
This was exactly what the stuffed ones before us had felt.  
This was exactly how they had moved.  
This was exactly how they had fed.  
We weren't victims anymore.  
We were part of the ritual.  
We were the next generation.  
And the new guests?  
They were the next filling.  
The hunger surged.  
My seams tightened.  
My button eyes fixed on the sleeping shapes in the dark.  
And for the first time since waking up in this soft, cursed body…  
…I didn't fight it.  
THIRD MOVEMENT  
The Hunt  
Chapter Twenty-Four  
The New Guests Step Inside  
I wish I could say we attacked them right away. That we were mindless. That we didn't know what we were doing.  
But that would be a lie.  
We knew exactly what we were doing.  
And that's what makes it worse.  
The moment the door opened, the warmth inside my seams surged.  
We were all fighting it.  
The hunger. The instinct. The ritual.  
But the moment they stepped inside, we knew:  
Fresh prey had arrived.  
Chapter Twenty-Five  
We Don't Move at First  
We stayed still.  
Perfectly still.  
The new guests joked about the "creepy stuffed animals everywhere." One of them poked my arm. Another lifted Lila and set her on a different shelf.  
The hunger inside her pulsed so hard I felt it through the air.  
But she didn't move.  
Not yet.  
We weren't supposed to.  
The ritual had rules. Patterns. A rhythm.  
We had to wait. We had to watch. We had to let them get comfortable.  
The old ladies always said:  
"They like company."  
Now we understood what that meant.  
Chapter Twenty-Six  
The First Game: Sound  
That night, when the guests went to bed, the hunger whispered:  
Play.  
So we did.  
Soft thumps in the hallway. A dragging sound across the ceiling. A faint squeak from the living room.  
We didn't approach their rooms. Not yet.  
We just let the sounds drift through the cabin, subtle enough to dismiss, loud enough to unsettle.  
One guest whispered, "Did you hear that?"  
Another said, "Probably the house settling."  
We knew better.  
The house wasn't settling.  
We were waking up.  
Chapter Twenty-Seven  
The Second Game: Movement  
The next morning, we changed positions.  
Just slightly.  
I moved from my shelf to the arm of the couch. Jamie appeared at the top of the stairs. Lila sat on the kitchen counter. Marcus leaned against the hallway wall.  
The guests blamed each other.  
"Who moved these?" "Not me." "Seriously, stop messing around."  
We stayed still when they looked. We moved when they didn't.  
The hunger purred inside us.  
Chapter Twenty-Eight  
The Third Game: Scent  
By the second night, the warmth inside us was unbearable. The hunger was a heartbeat we couldn't silence.  
We let the smell seep out.  
Not strong. Just enough.  
A metallic tang. A warm, animal scent. A hint of something that didn't belong in a cabin.  
One guest wrinkled their nose.  
"Do you smell that?"  
We did.  
We smelled everything.  
Their sweat. Their breath. Their warmth.  
The hunger throbbed.  
Chapter Twenty-Nine  
The Fourth Game: Proximity  
We got closer.  
A stuffed rabbit on the bathroom sink. A bear on the floor outside their bedroom door. A cat plush sitting on the kitchen table, facing the hallway.  
They started to panic.  
"Okay, seriously, who keeps moving these?" "This isn't funny." "I swear I locked that door."  
We didn't move when they watched.  
But the moment they turned away…  
…we crept closer.  
Silent. Soft. Inevitable.  
Chapter Thirty  
The Breaking Point  
The guests tried to leave on the third morning.  
The door wouldn't open.  
The windows wouldn't budge.  
The cabin wasn't letting them go.  
We felt the shift—the ritual tightening around them like a net.  
The hunger inside us roared.  
Jamie fell off the couch, landing with a soft thump. One guest screamed. Another backed away. Another grabbed a chair like it would help.  
Jamie didn't move.  
Not yet.  
We were still playing.  
Chapter Thirty-One  
The Final Game: The Chase  
Night fell.  
The cabin grew warm. Too warm. Alive warm.  
The guests huddled together in the living room, whispering, terrified.  
We watched from the shadows.  
Waiting.  
The hunger inside us pulsed in unison.  
Now.  
We moved.  
Not fast at first. Just enough to be seen.  
A twitch. A tilt of the head. A step forward.  
One guest screamed. Another ran. Another froze.  
The chase began.  
We didn't rush. We didn't pounce.  
We herded them. Guided them. Toyed with them.  
Down the hallway. Around the corner. Toward the basement door.  
The trapdoor opened on its own.  
Warm air rose from below.  
The labyrinth was calling.  
Chapter Thirty-Two  
The Moment We Gave In  
As the guests stumbled into the basement, crying, begging, scrambling in the dark…  
…the hunger inside us finally won.  
It wasn't anger.  
It wasn't cruelty.  
It wasn't even desire.  
It was instinct.  
The same instinct that had consumed the stuffed ones before us. The same instinct that would consume the ones after.  
We weren't human anymore.  
We weren't victims.  
We weren't even ourselves.  
We were part of the ritual.  
We were the hunters.  
And the guests?  
They were the next filling.  
Chapter Thirty-Three  
The Cycle Continues  
I sit on my shelf now, waiting.  
The cabin is quiet.  
The new stuffed ones—fresh from the labyrinth, still warm, still confused—stare back at me with button eyes that haven't yet learned to hunger.  
They will.  
They always do.  
Above us, I hear footsteps.  
Car doors.  
Laughter.  
More guests.  
More company.  
The warmth inside my seams pulses in anticipation.  
I don't fight it anymore.  
I don't want to.  
This is what I am now.  
This is what we are.  
The stuffed ones.  
The keepers of the cabin.  
The hunters in the dark.  
And we are so very hungry.
