Wikilink back to [[02_Novels/04_The_Cycle_of_Us|The Cycle of Us]].

The concept's organizing device is a soul-line passed through nine lives in three triads (a drowned boy, his grandson, his great-grandniece, then a wholly new soul across three more lives, then a final soul across the last three), and the draft is explicit about its own trick: "The soul that lived Lives 1–3 dissolves, and a new one begins — but the story never announces it." That's a rare case in this vault of a writer naming their intended reader-experience (an unmarked handoff the audience should feel rather than be told) directly inside the draft notes, which makes this document as much an author's craft memo as a narrative. The early lives are written in dense, run-on first-person confession (comma-spliced, breath-length sentences that mimic oral storytelling and family myth-making — "I think it scared my grandfather but he moved back to the beach and learned to swim again"), while Lives 4–9 shift abruptly to clipped, present-tense-adjacent, stage-direction-like third person ("He settled into routine as if it were a punishment. / A desk. / A clock."), marking the tonal seam between "voice recorded live" and "outline being drafted." The recurring bench-by-the-sea image closes the loop, ending not on death but on a whispered "It's alright now" that reframes the whole cycle as inherited peace rather than inherited trauma.

[[04_Book_Concepts/07_Narrative_Voice|Narrative Voice]] runs a compressed, earlier version of this same premise (a god-observer consciousness threading itself across repeating lives toward eventual peace) — likely an early seed of this fuller draft rather than an unrelated concept.
