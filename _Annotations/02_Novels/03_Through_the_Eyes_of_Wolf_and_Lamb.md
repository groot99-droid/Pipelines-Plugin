Wikilink back to [[02_Novels/03_Through_the_Eyes_of_Wolf_and_Lamb|Through the Eyes of Wolf and Lamb]].

The foreword is the real engine of this piece: a narrator who is complicit in the machinery of institutional euphemism ("we should say their name" / "names complicate liability") states their own ethical rules up front and then, formally, breaks them — the promise of neutrality curdles the moment the narrator admits proximity to the harm they're satirizing. The prose alternates two distinct registers on a tight loop: cold procedural diction (memos, checklists, "Restorative Action Request" forms) versus tender, concrete domestic fragments (a blue mug, a voicemail about pottery classes, a doodled cat with a bent ear) — the friction between those two registers, rather than plot, is what carries the tension. The found-document device (file timestamps, a device labeled "COMM-LAPTOP" overwriting the narrator's own drafts) literalizes the essay's thesis that institutional language colonizes personal language. Most notably for the vault's craft guide: the narrator states an explicit authorial boundary in the foreword — "I will not make victims into props for cleverness; they will have interior lives, small habits, and the dignity of being more than plot points" — which is the clearest first-person articulation of an anti-exploitation ethic found anywhere in this archive, and the piece later dramatizes the narrator failing to fully honor it before reclaiming the line verbatim in the closing "reclaiming" scene.
