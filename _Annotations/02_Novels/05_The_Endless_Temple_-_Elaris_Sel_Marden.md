Wikilink back to [[02_Novels/05_The_Endless_Temple_-_Elaris_Sel_Marden|The Endless Temple - Elaris Sel Marden]].

The premise is a double-frame: Elaris, an elf scholar imprisoned for eternity in a temple-library where every book is a living universe, drinks a tea that lets him narrate a book from inside it — so Chapter One's Xandri story is technically Elaris's read-aloud experience, which explains the abrupt slide from third-person description of Elaris into first-person "My hand shot up almost by instinct" once the book-within-the-book begins. That nested-narrator switch is the chapter's central craft risk: it's unmarked by any scene break, relying entirely on the tea's setup two paragraphs earlier to justify the pronoun shift. The prose leans on long, comma-linked descriptive sentences typical of introduction-heavy fantasy world-building (goblin's eyes and Ogres' tongues "kept bobbing and floating neatly in sealed jars"), and the Xandri strand itself is a fairly conventional coming-of-age exile-quest (disbelieving heir of a legendary father, punished for questioning the tribe's gods, walks into the desert at night) — notable mainly for how the frame device recontextualizes an otherwise-familiar quest opening as a story Elaris is choosing to live inside rather than merely read.
