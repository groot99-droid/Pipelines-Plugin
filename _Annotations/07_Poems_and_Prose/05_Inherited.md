Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/05_Inherited|Inherited]].

The poem addresses both parents under a single "you," sliding from the father ("my father's headlights") to the mother ("You were no older than I am now when you became a mother") without ever separating the two into distinct sections — the effect is that the poem is less about either individual parent than about parenthood itself as an inherited condition. Roughly two-thirds through, the tense shifts from past-tense reckoning to future-tense prophecy — "I know will make the same choices... I will seem angry to gain obedience because sometimes cruelty is mercy" — and that shift is the poem's real subject: understanding a parent's failures only by pre-living your own future repetition of them. The recurring paired-contradiction structure ("You saved me. / You hurt me. / And I will do both to them.") refuses to resolve the tension between gratitude and grief, and the poem's final claim — that clarity is the cost, not the cure — is stated flatly rather than dramatized, which gives the ending a tired, accepting register rather than a redemptive one.
