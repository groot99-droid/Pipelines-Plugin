Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/07_In_Search_Of_Truth|In Search Of Truth]].

Unlike the other pieces in this batch, the poem speaks almost entirely in a collective "we" rather than a personal "I," which turns it into something closer to a philosophical aphorism sequence than a confession — it argues about human self-deception in general rather than narrating a specific wound. The prose-poem form is built from short declarative fragments stacked without much connective tissue ("Its symmetry cracks. / Its safety suffocates. / Its sweetness turns sour in the mouth."), a rhythm that mimics the piece's own argument about truth arriving in sudden, unannounced collapse rather than gradual revelation. The turn from "This is not clarity. / This is nakedness." isolates two one-line sentences to slow the poem down at its most important distinction — clarity as intellectual, nakedness as exposure — and the closing couplet ("You no longer chase truth. / You must live it.") shifts from the collective "we" into a direct "you," the poem's only address to a reader, which sharpens the final imperative into something personal after pages of abstraction. Notably the piece is signed "Elijah W. Skinner" in the body text itself, the only poem in this set to carry the author's full name inline rather than leaving it to the file metadata.
