Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/08_The_Unfamiliar|The Unfamiliar]].

This is the only poem in the batch addressed entirely in second person — "you must open your arms," "you will feel the ground tilt" — which turns it into direct instruction rather than personal confession, closer to a motivational incantation than a memory poem. Its central image system opposes two spaces: the familiar as "a warm cage" with "invisible" bars and a floor "worn smooth by the pacing of your own feet," against the unfamiliar's shape-shifting, unlearned "voice," and the poem tracks movement between them as a literal ascent — "The ground tilt," "the horizon pull at your ribs," "cross that trembling threshold." The line "This is how ascent begins— / not with triumph, / but with a single step taken / before you feel ready" is the poem's clearest thesis statement, deliberately undercutting the grandeur of transformation narratives by locating the pivotal moment in an unready, almost accidental first step rather than a decisive one. The final stanza's image of "the person you once were" watching "from a distance, / grateful you did not stay with them" externalizes the old self as a separate, released character, giving the abstract growth-poem a small, concrete parting image to close on.
