Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/12_Darkness|Darkness]].

Five short stanzas build a single argument against retaliation, opening with the image of retributive violence as "a serpent devouring its own tail" — the ouroboros standing in for the closed loop of "strike for strike, wound for wound." The second stanza personifies darkness as something almost sentient that feeds on being fought: "it swells, / it thickens, / it learns your name," which raises the stakes before the poem's volta in stanza three ("Only those who have walked its corridors... can choose to turn"). The closing definition — "Light is not the absence of shadow— / it is the defiance of it" — reframes the whole poem as an ethic rather than a mood, and the final line, "until the horizon remembers the sun," lands as a patient, non-triumphant image of change happening in increments rather than all at once.
