Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/11_For_My_Papa|For My Papa]].

Written in the mode of a spoken eulogy rather than a private confession, the poem opens by naming its own emotional register directly — "I would be dishonest if I said my heart wasn't heavy" — and builds outward from personal grief to a broader claim about his marriage as the foundation of the family: "Many of us here today would not be here / if not for the commitment they showed." The extended metaphor of grandparents as "the roots of a great oak, / digging deep into the mantle of the earth, / holding us steady in a world of shifting ground" is the poem's structural core, and it pays off in the closing image of him as "a golden thread running through the fabric of our lives" — two different textile/organic metaphors for the same idea of continuity past death. The line "though we have lost his presence, / we have not lost him" is the poem's central paradox and its argument against grief as pure loss, stated plainly rather than elaborated. Notably, this eulogy's grandfather — marked by baseball, gardening, and small domestic ritual in spirit if not by the exact same details — sits alongside the grandfather remembered in "Growing Up" (peanuts in Coke, raised garden beds, baseball on the radio); the two poems likely commemorate the same man from different distances in time and grief.
