Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/02_Growing_Up|Growing Up]].

The poem opens as a flat philosophical argument about mortality ("no one dies of old age... it is the tool by which we measure") and then, almost exactly at the midpoint, drops out of abstraction into a specific man remembered through texture rather than statement: "peanuts bobbing in a cold Coke, baseball on the weekends, the faint smell of dirt under his nails." That turn from argument to sense-memory is the poem's engine — it earns its thesis about measuring life by "textures" by actually demonstrating the technique rather than just asserting it. The archivist/hoarder line ("when I was small I called him an archivist, later I called it what it was: hoarding") is a small, exact piece of a child's vocabulary aging into an adult's blunter one, and it's doing real emotional work by refusing to romanticize him. The ending breaks form entirely, dropping the long discursive lines for a string of short imperatives that culminate in "make it a… CRESCENDO!!!" — a tonal left turn from elegy into something closer to a pep talk, which reads as the speaker refusing to let the poem end on grief.
