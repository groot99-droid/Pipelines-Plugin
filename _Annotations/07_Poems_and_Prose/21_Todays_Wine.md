Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/21_Todays_Wine|Today's Wine]].

The poem organizes itself around a simple tricolon — yesterday's wine, tomorrow's wine, today's wine — using the metaphor to walk through past, future, and present in turn: yesterday's "has slipped from our grasp," tomorrow's "has yet to delight," and only today's is available to actually taste. Unlike the other pieces in this set, it carries no first-person "I" at all; the address is plural and generalized ("ours," "we grow") and the closing lines shift into direct imperative — "Embrace the present, let your spirit flow, / For today is a gift; in this moment, we grow." That absence of a confessing "I," combined with the proverb-like, carpe-diem argument, marks this as a didactic wisdom poem rather than autobiographical confession, in the same family as "Taking All From the Rich" and "A Crown Without a Kingdom."
