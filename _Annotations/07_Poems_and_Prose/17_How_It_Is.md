Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/17_How_It_Is|How It Is]].

The poem opens with a concrete childhood memory — the first homeless man the speaker ever saw, and the parents' verdict that "he didn't work hard enough" — before widening into a stacked catalog of abstractions each given its own line and em-dash gloss ("Poverty—sprawled in quiet corners... Hunger—lean and hollow, curling around ribs like a slow, patient fire"). The three movements are held together by a refrain that mutates each time it recurs: "Because that's just the way it is" becomes "Because it's easier than change," then "It's how it was bought to be," each iteration marking a deepening of the speaker's political awareness from childhood acceptance to adult indictment. The closing movement's inventory of inherited wealth ("They lived on money they never earned, / slept in beds they never built") pointedly mirrors the poverty catalog from movement one, making the final line's claim — that abundance "rot[s] but never nourish[es]" — the poem's harshest turn.
