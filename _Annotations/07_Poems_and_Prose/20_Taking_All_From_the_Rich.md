Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/20_Taking_All_From_the_Rich|Taking All From the Rich]].

Two quatrains built on a single chiastic reversal: "Taking all from the rich, to give to the poor / Is the same as taking from the poor, to give the rich more." There is no personal "I" anywhere in the poem — it argues in the abstract, using King John (the Robin Hood legend's villain) as shorthand for corrupt power ("though King John wore the villain's crown, / Two wrongs can't make a right"). This is closer to political epigram than confession: the closing lines settle for weary fatalism rather than resolution — "In the end, it's a dirge we all must face, / A cycle of heartache in an endless race" — positioning the poem alongside "A Crown Without a Kingdom" as one of this set's non-confessional, aphoristic pieces rather than personal testimony.
