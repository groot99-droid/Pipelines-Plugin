Wikilink back to [[10_Characters/05_The_Rossettian_Oracle|The Rossettian Oracle]].

Modeled on Dante Gabriel Rossetti, this variant is built from an aestheticist, Pre-Raphaelite register that binds love, beauty, and death into a single transcendence rather than treating them as separate concerns — its most distinctive recurring move is reframing mortality as generative rather than terminal ("Death is the mother of beauty," "Death gives us wings where once we bore only the weight of flesh"). That inverts the other three variants' relationship to darkness: the Bottonian Philosopher treats suffering as a condition to be honestly named, the Kafkan Seer treats confinement as a paradox to sit inside, and the Darwishan Exile treats loss as an ache to be carried — only the Rossettian Oracle treats death itself as beautiful and desirable, which is the clearest fingerprint of its source poet's aestheticism. No tie to any specific existing novel, story, or world in this vault was found; it reads as a standalone archetype reference, the fourth entry in what functions as a character bank of thinker-inspired mystic-sage variants rather than a character tied to one of the archive's actual stories.
