Wikilink back to [[11_Essays/03_What_is_The_Power_of_Art|What is The Power of Art]].

This one swaps the claim-parable-practice rhythm for a single sustained rhetorical engine: the "There would be no X without Y, no Y without Z" chain repeated across seven artistic lineages (film, sculpture, painting, literature, animation, music, architecture), which does the essay's actual argumentative work — it never needs a practice/action-item section because the anaphora itself is the persuasion. The cost is that the thesis gets stated outright three separate times in only slightly different words ("Art is not decoration. It is direction," "the question is not whether we can afford to value artists... whether we can afford not to," "that is how beauty dies — not in flames, but in forgetfulness") — a clear case of moralizing past the point the reader needed it, telling rather than trusting the lineage list to land on its own. The abstract-noun vocabulary is inheritance-and-continuity focused rather than architectural like 02 and 04 — "lineage," "inheritance," "scaffolding," "torch passed hand to hand," "branch," though it does reach for "invisible architecture of a culture" once, tying it back into the vault's dominant metaphor family. One concrete detail worth keeping: the essay's only image of a living, unresolved stake — "a child is drawing on scrap paper... another child has already stopped" — is buried two-thirds down after the thesis has already been over-stated twice, so its emotional weight gets diluted by repetition rather than built toward.
