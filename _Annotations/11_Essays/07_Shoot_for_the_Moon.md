Wikilink back to [[11_Essays/07_Shoot_for_the_Moon|Shoot for the Moon]].

The essay opens with a genuine parable (the mother's "shoot for the moon" saying, and the narrator's own memory of "launching into the void" and finding opportunity in uncertainty) but then loosens into a string of aphorisms about fear, complacency, and social critique that never fully resolves into a practice or action-item — it drifts rather than argues, and per the file's own note it's "untitled in source," which tracks with how unfinished the middle section reads (fragments like "Anytime you allow yourself to build up a negative how daunting falling into" break down mid-sentence). That roughness is why it's tagged `status: draft` rather than `complete` — this is the one essay in the folder where the syntax itself, not just the ideas, is unfinished. Its vocabulary is the outlier of the set: instead of the architecture/lineage/doctrine abstractions running through 02–04, it reaches for concrete natural and civic imagery — "undertow," "abyss," "wildflowers," "the teeth of the tiller," "campfire," "foundation of existence" — which reads as closer to the vault's fiction-prose register than to the other essays' more abstract-noun-driven mode, worth noting as a possible bridge case between `essay-self-help` and the narrative modes elsewhere in the vault. The closing line ("your heart beats a little stronger each time you extend or receive a warm gesture") does function as a callback to the opening's risk-and-reward framing, so the aphoristic-closing habit holds even where the middle structure doesn't.
