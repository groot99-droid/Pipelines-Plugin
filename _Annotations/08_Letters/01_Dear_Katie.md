Wikilink back to [[08_Letters/01_Dear_Katie|Dear Katie]].

The letter opens by undercutting its own address — "I don't think I'm writing this for you" — which reframes the whole piece as processing rather than correspondence, a private confession borrowing the letter's second-person shape without needing a reader on the other end. The sentence rhythm is run-on and self-correcting ("it was just a hormonal cry for help because I was alone... navigating changes I couldn't comprehend"), each clause chasing down a truer, less flattering version of the one before it, which is the piece's core move: repeated self-indictment that refuses the easier, more sympathetic account ("I went from a force of love in your life to just another example of the shitty men who leave"). Tense does real work here — the body of the letter is almost entirely retrospective past tense cataloguing harm, and it pivots to present/future ("I can't predict the future... I'll focus on the present moment") only in the final lines, so the grammar itself performs the turn from confession to resolve. It's short — thirteen sentences of guilt work followed by four of stated intention — and that imbalance is the point: the amends are earned by the length of the reckoning, not asserted cheaply.
