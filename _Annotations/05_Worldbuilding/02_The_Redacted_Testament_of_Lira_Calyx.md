Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/02_The_Redacted_Testament_of_Lira_Calyx|The Redacted Testament of Lira Calyx]].

The cosmology is vertical rather than the more common horizontal map: a post-nuclear world folded upward into three ecological bands (Scorched Lowlands, Verdant Mid-Slopes, Aetheric High Peaks), with a single resource — Aetheric Compounds, born from fallout fused with deep-earth minerals — driving both the magic-adjacent "science" and the politics. Structurally the piece moves in four registers across its four books: careful field-journal observation (Book I), forensic archive-diving (Book II), ethnographic myth-as-data (Book III), and finally an epistolary found-document collapse (Book IV) where Lira's increasingly fevered entries are interleaved with an unnamed second hand's marginal corrections, redaction bars, and a final confessional voice — the archivist — who turns and addresses the reader directly. The recurring "coupled oscillator" equations and stability criterion are a nice touch of specificity that ground the Aetheric magic system in something that reads like real physics being sabotaged rather than generic hand-waved magic. Most notable as a craft choice: the ending refuses the genre's usual reset-button relief. Lira's plan produces "a fragile pause and a partial fracture," not victory — the Continuum is wounded but not destroyed, and the testament survives as "taught in some academies, banned in others," a contested and incomplete inheritance rather than a triumphant one.
