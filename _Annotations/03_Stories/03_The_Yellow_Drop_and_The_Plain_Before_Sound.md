Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/03_The_Yellow_Drop_and_The_Plain_Before_Sound|The Yellow Drop and The Plain Before Sound]].

A genesis myth told entirely through sound-as-matter: notes don't accompany the world being built, they build it ("High notes lifted the air, giving it height. / Low notes thickened the ground, giving it weight."), and the piece commits hard to that synesthetic logic rather than lapsing into ordinary description. The rhythm mimics its subject — short, dripping clauses at the start ("Then a yellow drop of sound fell.") that lengthen and accumulate clause-by-clause as the soundscape "thickens," a form-matches-content trick that's the strongest craft choice in the piece. The pov move at the end is the most interesting structural beat in this batch: third-person omniscient describing an internal/dream sensorium suddenly widens to first-person-plural ("We see now through the body's eyes"), collapsing the built world back into the mundane frame of an alarm clock. Unlike 06/07/08's cyclical or unresolved horror endings, this is a closed loop — dream-world rises, dream-world collapses, waking supersedes it — tidy rather than haunting, though the last line ("the echo of that sound-born world lingers") keeps a trace of ambiguity about which reality is more real.
