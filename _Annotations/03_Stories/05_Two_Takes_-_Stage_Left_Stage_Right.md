Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/05_Two_Takes_-_Stage_Left_Stage_Right|Two Takes: Stage Left, Stage Right]].

Five paired first-person monologues, each giving the same thirty-second event to two people who read it completely differently — the strongest is the hydrant scene, where the Dreamer aestheticizes his own public soaking ("Rain falls like a film of light... if life gives you a wet punchline, you might as well laugh") while the Drunk experiences the identical moment as genuine humiliation with no romantic frame available to him. The prose voice per-character is distinct and consistent: clipped, image-forward metaphor-stacking for the "performing" characters (Dreamer, Teen, Applicant) versus flatter, task-oriented interiority for the "processing" characters (Nurse, Cashier, Interviewer) who have learned to metabolize other people's small tragedies as routine. The Nurse and Cashier pairs are the emotional core of the set — both end on a quiet, specific physical detail that undercuts their professional competence (the Nurse eating her own stale donut alone in the supply closet; the Cashier "cataloguing lives by barcode") — which gives two of the five vignettes a wistful, un-jokey close that breaks from the piece's lighter comic register elsewhere. Each pair is self-contained and tidy rather than cyclical; the irony is horizontal (between the two speakers) rather than in unresolved plot.
