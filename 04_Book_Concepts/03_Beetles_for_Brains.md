Beetles for Brains: A Psychological Horror About Sleep, Control, and the Things That Crawl in the Dark

In this world, sleep isn’t rest — it’s exposure.

Beetles for Brains follows a group of recruits entering an elite, secretive boot camp known for producing the toughest soldiers on the planet. What no one tells them is the real reason for the camp’s brutal schedule: mandatory, unnaturally long sleep cycles.

At first, it seems like a strange training method.  
Then the symptoms begin.

Recruits wake up disoriented.  
Their thoughts feel foggy.  
Memories slip.  
Reflexes dull.  
Personalities shift in subtle, unsettling ways.

Rumors spread about “the bugs” — tiny organisms that supposedly enter the body during sleep and slowly interfere with neural pathways. No one knows if they’re real, a myth, or a psychological tactic used by the instructors.

But the longer the recruits sleep, the worse the effects become.

Some start hearing faint clicking sounds when they close their eyes.  
Some feel phantom sensations under their skin.  
Some swear they can sense something moving in the quiet spaces of their minds.

The camp insists everything is normal.  
The recruits know something is wrong.  
And the truth is far more disturbing than any of them imagined.

Beetles for Brains is a slow-burn psychological horror about control, paranoia, and the terrifying idea that the most vulnerable moment in your life — sleep — might be the moment something else takes hold.
