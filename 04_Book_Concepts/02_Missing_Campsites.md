Missing Campsites: A Slow-Burn Horror Mystery About Vanishing Reality

Imagine arriving at a remote campground only to discover something is… off.

Not dangerous.  
Not dramatic.  
Just wrong.

In Missing Campsites, a group of campers pulls into a national forest expecting a quiet weekend. But the first few campsites — the ones that should be right there at the entrance — simply aren’t. Not destroyed. Not taped off. Not overgrown.

They just don’t exist.

The map shows them.  
The signs point to them.  
But the land is empty.

The campers are unsettled, but everyone else — rangers, families, hikers — acts like nothing is missing.

The next morning, more campsites have vanished.

No one notices.  
No one cares.  
No one even remembers they were there.

Except the campers.

By the end of the second day, the entire campground has been erased from reality — all except the last five sites, the ones where the campers are staying. They’re now the only fixed points in a landscape that’s quietly unraveling.

And then comes the final morning.

They wake up to find the forest transformed.  
The sky is wrong.  
The air feels heavier.  
The trees are arranged in patterns that shouldn’t exist.  
Their phones show no signal, no time, no date.

They haven’t just lost the campsites.

They’ve slipped into a different dimension — one that has been pulling them in, piece by piece, since the moment they arrived.

Missing Campsites is a creeping, atmospheric horror story about unnoticed disappearances, the fragility of shared reality, and the terrifying moment when you realize the world has been quietly rewriting itself around you.
