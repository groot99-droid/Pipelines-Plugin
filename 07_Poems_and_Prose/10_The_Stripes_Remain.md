# The Stripes Remain

 The Stripes Remain

She said, “A tiger can’t change its stripes.”  
She spoke it like a sentence passed,  
as if my flaws were carved into the marrow,  
as if my shadow was born of nothing but my own rot.  
And in that moment, I believed her.  
That was my first defeat.

I fell.  
Not once, but again and again,  
each failure a stone dragging me deeper.  
I learned the taste of ash,  
the weight of silence,  
the slow suffocation of being seen only as the sum of my sins.

But the trial did not end in the pit.  
It began there.  
I walked through fires that stripped the flesh from my pride.  
I descended into depths where the air itself conspired to crush me.  
And in those depths, I did not die.  
I endured.  
I endured until endurance became my crown.

When I rose, it was not as the man she left behind.  
The stripes remained,  
but they were no longer her prophecy.  
They were my regalia —  
marks of survival,  
sigils of the battles I had bled through.

Now I stand above the ruins,  
not cleansed,  
but sovereign.  
Not forgiven,  
but unbroken.  
The throne I claim is carved from the bones of my trials,  
and the crown I wear was forged in the heat of my own damnation.

She will never see the man I became.  
And that is my final triumph —  
for the one who swore I could never change  
will never know how high I have risen.
