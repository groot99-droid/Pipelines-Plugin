 For My Papa

I would be dishonest if I said my heart wasn’t heavy.  
The pain of his passing is a burden—  
but it’s a burden I will gladly carry,  
because it is the weight of love,  
and the blessings of his memory.

My papa, more than anyone else,  
taught me that when you love someone,  
you accept that there will be pain.  
For better and for worse—  
those words should never be confined to wedding vows.  
They are a way of living,  
a testament to the choice to love,  
to care,  
to stand beside someone no matter the season.

No one embodied that more than my grandparents.  
Their love overflowed into the branches of the family tree they built.  
Many of us here today would not be here  
if not for the commitment they showed.  
They are the roots of a great oak,  
digging deep into the mantle of the earth,  
holding us steady in a world of shifting ground.  
Even now, with his passing,  
he holds us strong.  
His memory will live forever in all of us.

People say not to judge a book by its cover—  
and the same is true of life.  
My grandfather was born in 1949,  
and today, as we close his casket,  
the story of his life does not end.  
The chapters remain with us—  
in the way we speak,  
in the way we love,  
in the way we carry ourselves through this world.

Though we have lost his presence,  
we have not lost him.  
He is woven into us,  
a golden thread running through the fabric of our lives.  
And as long as we live,  
his story will never close.
