# Inherited

**Type:** Autobiographical poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 3724–3793  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
Inherited

I remember the nights I tried to stay awake to see my father’s headlights touch my window.  
The way you would sleep in on the weekends because you were tired.  
My heartaches when you weren’t there to play catch.  
But now I know the noose money became as it trickled out like water, pushing you further out to sea.

My mother yelled not out of anger but fear.  
I felt like we were at odds but the truth is I fought while you protected.  
I believed you were my greatest enemy only to find you were the only one defending me.  
I thought you meant to break me but you were the one who made me.

You cared for me longer than I have memory.  
You cried for me before I had fears of my own to cry about.  
Yet you still bore them. Even when I added to them.

I’m not sorry for being young.  
I’m sorry for doubting your love when you never showed us contempt.

Now I see you.  
You were no older than I am now when you became a mother—  
just 19 facing the world with such courage.  
A caretaker. A protector. A dream builder in the dark.

I thank you for all the times you weren’t there dad because I needed you to be somewhere else.  
I thank you for the nights you slept because exhaustion was all you could muster.

You felt the pain deeper than I ever could as just a child.  
You bore it with honor.  
And now I grieve that suffering—the kind you never let me see,  
the kind I can only know now that it’s too late to unknow  
and the suffering ill never know because you spared me.

But here is what keeps me awake now:  
I see exactly why you had to leave.  
I see exactly why you had to yell.  
I understand the aftermath of survival.

And I am standing at the threshold of becoming what you were,  
I know will make the same choices.  
I know will not be there when they need me.  
I will seem angry to gain obedience because sometimes cruelty is mercy.  
I will stretch myself thin in ways they cannot comprehend  
until they stand where I’m standing now,  
understanding everything,  
unable to change anything.

My children will feel what I felt.  
They will doubt me.  
They will grow into the terrible knowledge that I did the best 
but it was never enough.

And they will stand at their own threshold,  
terrified they’ll do the same.

The cycle doesn’t break.  
It just gets inherited with fuller sight.  
We see it coming and we cannot stop it.  
We only bear it with whatever honor we can find.

The bridge between childhood anger and adulthood understanding  
is learning that both things are true at once:  
You saved me.  
You hurt me.  
And I will do both to them.

This is what clarity costs.  
This is what it means to grow up and become your parents,  
not despite understanding their pain,  
but because of it.
