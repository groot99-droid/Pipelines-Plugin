# The Unfamiliar

**Type:** Poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 5425–5483  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
 The Unfamiliar

To rise from the dust of the ordinary  
into the rare air of the extraordinary,  
one must step beyond the borders of the known,  
To know what can not be known.

The familiar is a warm cage-  
its bars invisible,  
its floor worn smooth by the pacing of your own feet.  
It will not harm you,  
but it’s fate is a hollow death.

The unfamiliar waits outside,  
its shape shifting in the half-light,  
its voice a language you have not yet learned.  
It will not promise safety,  
but it will promise change.

To ascend,  
you must open your arms to what unsettles you,  
let it press its strange weight into your skin,  
let it teach you the names of things  
you once feared to touch.

For only in the company of the unknown  
does the self shed its husk,  
and in that agonizing act of shedding the old 
the beginning of something new unfolds.

And as you move into the heart of the unfamiliar,  
you will feel the ground tilt,  
the air sharpen,  
the horizon pull at your ribs.

This is how ascent begins—  
not with triumph,  
but with a single step taken  
before you feel ready.

Followed by the toil of beginning 
again and again 
with that experience forever more 
As it presses itself into you deeper

The extraordinary is built  
from moments like these.

And when you cross that trembling threshold,  
the world will not applaud.  
It will simply rearrange itself around you,  
as if it had been waiting  
for your arrival all along.

The sky will feel wider.  
Your breath will feel truer.  
And the person you once were  
will watch from a distance,  
grateful you did not stay with them.
