# Past, Present, and Future

**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 5809–5859  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
 Past, Present, and Future

I am but a vessel —  
neither shackled to the ruins of my past  
nor tethered to the mirage of my future.  
Behold the fragments of who I was,  
woven with the tender threads of what I am becoming.  
Like all living things,  
I am caught in the endless turning of metamorphosis —  
never still, never finished,  
forever shedding, forever forming.

The path beneath your feet  
is the loom upon which your destiny is woven.  
Stand tall, with the poise of one who knows their own weight,  
and let no misstep cloud the horizon of your sight.  
Sink your roots deep into the soil of your being,  
for without knowing yourself,  
you risk becoming a ghost  
wandering through the corridors of your own life.

A flower, though radiant, cannot still the hunger in our bellies.  
If all the world’s flora were only petals,  
our bodies would starve.  
Yet if every plant were only sustenance,  
stripped of beauty,  
our spirits would wither.  
So it is with humanity:  
if all were teachers,  
wisdom would smother joy;  
if all sought only comfort,  
our minds would dull,  
and our days would pass like smoke.

Every taking demands its recompense.  
Nothing in this world comes without a price.  
The natural order knows this well —  
even the plants, breathing in the air,  
return it as oxygen,  
a quiet covenant with all who live.

What some call flaws,  
others name beauty —  
like a weed in a garden  
that refuses to bow to the gardener’s design.  
So too with us:  
if our worth is unseen,  
perhaps it is not we who are lacking,  
but the garden itself  
that fails to recognize  
the rare bloom in its midst.
