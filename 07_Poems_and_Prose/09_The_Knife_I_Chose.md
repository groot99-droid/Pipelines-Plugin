# The Knife I Chose

**Type:** Narrative poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 5566–5647  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.

The Knife I Chose

The night swallows her footsteps,  
each one a drumbeat of fear against the hollow streets.  
Shadows stretch long and hungry,  
the evils of the world at her back,  
their breath cold on her neck.

She runs toward the only light she knows—  
a figure waiting in the dark,  
familiar, steady,  
a harbor she has clung to in storms before.  
Her chest heaves, her pulse a frantic hymn,  
and when she reaches him,  
she falls into his arms  
as though the world might finally loosen its grip.

But the embrace is not salvation.  
It is the moment the blade finds her—  
cold, precise,  
sliding between ribs with the intimacy of a whispered secret.  
Her heart clenches,  
not only from the steel,  
but from the truth that blooms with it:  
trust has teeth,  
and wolf’s don’t only hide beneath sheep’s skin

Warmth spreads across her skin,  
thick and clinging,  
the metallic scent of betrayal rising with the steam of her breath.  
The night does not pause.  
The shadows do not retreat.  
And in that final clarity,  
she understands—  
the knife was always there,  
waiting in the arms she thought would save her.

The blade is still there,  
buried deep,  
its cold edge pulsing with her heartbeat.  
Each breath is a ragged thread,  
but she clings to it—  
not because she cannot pull away,  
but because the thought of facing the night alone  
is a greater terror than the steel in her chest.

The world’s evils still prowl beyond these arms,  
and here, at least,  
she knows the shape of the monster holding her.

Her hands rise, trembling,  
fingers brushing the jaw of her betrayer  
as though memorizing the lines of a face  
she has always known would end her.  
She draws him closer,  
their foreheads almost touching,  
and with the last breath she owns,  
she presses her lips to his—  
a kiss not of love,  
but of surrender to the only warmth left to her.

Her body slackens,  
the weight of her falling into his grasp.  
Blood hums against her skin,  
sticky and slow,  
and yet a faint smile curves her lips—  
not joy,  
but the quiet relief  
of someone who has chosen  
the pain they understand  
over the darkness they do not.

And yet—  
she does not die.  
Not in body, at least.  
For this is not an ending,  
but an inheritance:  
a story older than memory,  
repeated like a sacred curse,  
etched into the laws of this world  
where love and ruin  
so often share the same embrace.
