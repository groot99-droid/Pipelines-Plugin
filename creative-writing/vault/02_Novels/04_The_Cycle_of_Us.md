The Cycle of Us

the story starts with 9 people standing in a room sharing the same face but all wearing it differently. The background a blurred haze begins to take shape like sand shifting with the sea the void empty white space giving shape to nine paths that loom distantly on the horizon. Their lives call them earnestly, beckoning with voices so sweet they caress the very vail of nostalgic moments gently pull each of them away as they walk towards their respective lives falling back in as if the end had never came. 

The first life  
He lived his life to the fullest. Running, dancing, filling the air with laughter far before he’d grown old enough to ponder the worries of tomorrow. When one day while him and his brother where at play by the sea his brother slip, fell, and got washed out further than the his eyes could see but he bravely jumped summoning strength like no other to save him. He gets to his brother fighting the current and helps him get close to shore but he can’t save himself as his brother drifts with another current back to the shore where he later wakes up he is washed further and further out. Where for a time he tries to fight but it is no use and as he slips from the surface he sees a school of fish surrounding him and as his body goes stiff and lifeless, his eyes clenching the finale lingers of light as his last breathe fails and his lungs fill with water. He could’ve sworn that right before he died it was peaceful the school of fish’s bright scales reflected the light make him feel like an angelic choir surrounded him in his ascent from this earth. 

In the rest of the 8 he learned never to fear the water for no other moment filled him with such peace as the first time his soul died, he felt that no other end would bring such stillness of the mind. His brother lived on and he carried his late brothers spirit with him where he was rebirthed in his grandson. 

(The Second Life)  
The boy had watched his father sit on the bench watching his children play his whole childhood. His grandfather had made that bench in memorial of his late brother who died saving his life. My grandfather had moved away with his parents shortly after, they moved to the big city with no large body of water around. I think it scared my grandfather but he moved back to the beach and learned to swim again. He taught my dad to be a strong swimmer and now me. Some days I sit on this bench to feel him close to me. My grandfather said I reminded him of his brother and it made me want to be like him (my late great uncle, former reincarnation).  After the passing of my grandfather I became very close to my father. I lived in his shadow: observing him, nurturing him, watching as he aged beyond reckoning and his mind faded, I watched the grey slowly bleach his hair, leach into the color of his eyes, and in the end paint the complexion of his face as we closed his casket on that faithful we all come to grow quite close to even if we are scared of death. For me this moment felt more sacred than a birthday because this was the final page not the beginning. I wish I could’ve said this was the end of my story in his shadow but it was not. It was only after his death my mother told me about him before he was kind and gentle. When they first met he had a crippling relationship with his father (my grandfather, and past life brother). My grandfather had struggled with control after the death of his brother and that control crushed the young spirit of my father. When my mother met him he was unpredictable snapping from anger to deep isolation. He drank heavily, gambled his life with bad people he owed debts to, and in the end almost lost my mother before I was born because he had let the relationship with his father grow so stale. They never talked even though they only had each other, grandma left grandpa after he drove off their son and this put grandpa into bad health, my father couldn’t fight his feelings anymore after his father fell ill. He checked himself into AA, he slept by his fathers beside for months as his father fought to live, he found a religion (forgiveness, love, and compassion towards those close to him) that would grow the foundation for our family tree (the linage of this souls 9 reincarnations). I saw my father differently now he wasn’t perfect and the idolization of him had made our time together feel stifled and one sided but now I saw him as more: he wasn’t just my father he shared my path and my flaws, he was me. I started to realize the pattern that was forming around me. I bought my parents house and moved my mother into the upstairs after my father’s passing. She took up knitting, storytelling, and so much more. She bloomed like it was spring everyday of her life but in these later moments after the passing of my father I realized all to later how beautiful of a soul she was. Somewhere in my mind I lamented because of how little I knew about her in my idolization of my father. I spent so many moments breathing in the same air as my father just trying to catch a glimpse of where he found his strength but now I see she was the one who gave life to every breathe he took. I thought back to my life: I built a house hold I had fallen in love with my wife and I had 2 beautiful daughters with one on way, they’re first girls to be born in our line. I knew my fore fathers would be proud just as I was the first time I saw their faces. The innocence of baby girl will make even the strongest men weak with love and protection, there is no sweeter or more innocent sound than their first cries each and every cry touched my heart with a even greater feeling of love till I thought it would stop beating. I looked at my mother differently after I had girls now. She was still young when my father died and so the love my grandfather gave me carried on into my children in a feminine form like that of an all loving mother. I was happy to see such beautiful family roots connecting us all even from the grave. I wish I had taken it in a little more but just as always we learn the truth only when a lie has taken shape. 

The third Life

I hadn’t been born yet when my father passed. It was a day like any other day my mom told me when I was old enough to handle it, he had taken both of my older sisters to go to a concert, they didn’t make it back a drunk driver hit them head on as they were leaving. My dad and my oldest sister Anna had died first because they were up front but Emily my other sister had made it out ok, or so we thought. The real troubles began in the hospital, Emily didn’t do well after the accident, she had a lot of internal damage but most of all she had been damaged mentally. She would stare out the windows for hours with moving an inch,she hadn’t talked to anyone after she found out that the others had died. I hadn’t come see her much, the doctors managed to keep her sustained but she would most likely never recover. As I got older I could remember reheating leftovers when grandma wasn’t there to make food for me, I was about 8 now and mom hadn’t slept at the house for more than a weekend in that time. She was always at the hospital trying to console my sister and motivate her, I didn’t resent her for this but i didn’t exactly have anyone other than grandma to talk to and she was gone to the hospital only a little less than mom did. When. Grandma was home she would tell me stories of how my great great uncle had saved my great grandpa from drowning, why we live by the sea to remember him and keep him close, and how my father had built our beautiful home with his hard work and how he had saved up enough for us to be safe even when he didn’t plan to be gone. I loved all the stories she would tell me of my family it made me feel close to them but my favorite stories were of my mother, she had told me about how she had taken on 2 day jobs to pay for the medical bills, and put food on the table, she would tell me of the many beautiful dates that her son had taken my mom on, I feel that I learned from both the love my mother had for me and my sister and how my mother loved my father. I thought that I wouldn’t be happy unless I had a love like they did, in my mind it was a love that was parallel to a prince saving a princess. My mother decided one day that I was old enough to come to the hospital and see my sister, I have to admit I was scared to see her after all our time apart and I was still young. When we got to the hospital and we entered the hall towards my sisters room I felt a sense of dread hit my stomach as I could tell something was wrong.  Monitors and alarms blasted over the silence, doctors ran past us hurriedly trying to get to the patient in need of help, I heard my mom gasp and the bottom of my stomach nearly fall out of my body as only a few feet in front of us they swung the door of my sisters room open. What I saw in that quick moment, before me and my mom were swiftly leave the area, scarred me for life. My sister lay in her bed note on the side table and wrists gushing. The following year flew by and it was like I hit a reset. My mom and I had finally adjusted after months of therapy and she worked less and less planning for her early retirement so she could make up the lost time together. I had a weirded epiphany one day after graduating from psychiatric school; my first client walked through the door and it was none other than my mother. She said in a flush of emotion that she had booked my first session anonymously so that I wouldn’t be prepared and we could have this session more naturally. I told her I understood and smiled at her telling her I was proud of her doing this for us. We talked for hours well beyond normal session time, I had to cancel on 3 other clients but this was worth every bit of lost money. I felt so close to her, I understood after this moment that hers and father’s love was far from perfect. She told me he was a great man, he loved with all his heart, gave even to the undeserving, and forgave anyone who ever crossed him but he was distant. She said that she was worried that just like our (mother and daughter)  relationship my father and his father had grown so close there was no room for them to find themselves, he had regretted spending all his life in his fathers shadow and never finding his own path. I don’t want you to do the same, I grasped her hand and said “mom with this life, I’d like to spend it with you. No one else will do, for no one loved us like you.” When I said us I knew I ment my sister and so did my mom, we cried, we spoke words with no movement of mouths other than the cries that welled up from tears, we spoke more vulnerably than we ever had yet not one word was spoken. Something in my soul seemed to be full filled in this life and moment and I knew that in another life I would follow a much different path one carved out something raw and rugged but for this one I would love ever soft warm hug and sob that I could get from the woman who gave me life and filled my already broken heart with more love than I could hold.

The Fourth Life — The Brother’s First Stage (Running From Oneself)

The soul that lived Lives 1–3 dissolves, and a new one begins — but the story never announces it.

He was born with a restlessness that didn’t belong to him, a kind of inherited ache that pulsed beneath his ribs like a memory he never lived. His mother said he kicked in the womb like he was trying to outrun something, and when he grew older, he did exactly that.

He ran from town to town, job to job, face to face, never staying long enough to let anyone see the soft, frightened boy beneath the bravado. He didn’t know why he felt like he was being chased by a shadow shaped like grief, or why the ocean made him uneasy even though he’d never lived near one.

He wasn’t dying at the end of this stage — he was shedding.  
The boy who ran could no longer carry the weight of the man he needed to become.

The Fifth Life — The Brother’s Second Stage (The 9–5 Life)

Same soul, new phase — but the reader only senses the shift.

He settled into routine as if it were a punishment.  
A desk.  
A clock.  
A life measured in keystrokes and coffee breaks.

He lived quietly, mechanically, as though the world had pressed him into a mold he didn’t choose. But sometimes, when he walked home at dusk, he felt a strange tug — a longing for someone he’d never met, a sense that he was supposed to be part of something larger, something older.

He didn’t know it, but he was feeling the echo of the soul who came before him — the one whose life ended just as his began.

This stage ended not with death, but with a soft cracking inside him, the shell of monotony breaking open.

---

The Sixth Life — The Brother’s Final Stage (Loving Someone Who Didn’t Love Back)

The brother’s last transformation before the soul-line passes on.

He loved someone who could not love him back, and in that unreturned devotion he confronted every wound he had inherited — the fear of abandonment, the ache of longing, the quiet belief that he was always one step behind the life he was meant to live.

He poured himself into someone who remained distant, and in that heartbreak he felt something ancient stir — a grief that wasn’t his, a tenderness that didn’t belong to this lifetime.

When this stage ended, he didn’t die.  
He simply reached the limit of who he could be.  
And the soul that had carried him through three transformations dissolved into something new — something softer, something waiting to be born.

---

The Seventh Life — The Sister’s First Stage (Chasing Dreams)

The brother’s soul-line ends, and the sister’s begins — but the story never says this outright.

She was born with fire in her chest, a spark that felt older than her bones. She dreamed in colors too bright for the world she lived in, chasing visions that felt like memories from a life she never lived.

She traveled far, not to escape but to seek — though she didn’t know what. Sometimes she felt as though she were running toward someone she had once loved deeply, someone she had never met in this life but missed all the same.

She didn’t know it, but she was carrying the brother’s unfinished longing, transformed into ambition.

This stage ended when she realized that dreams were not destinations but doorways.

---

The Eighth Life — The Sister’s Second Stage (A Life Spent Alone)

The sister’s middle transformation — quieter, deeper.

She withdrew from the world, choosing solitude not out of fear but instinct. She lived near the sea, drawn to it without understanding why, as if the waves whispered stories she had forgotten.

She spent her days in silence, tending to a garden that grew wild and unruly. She felt a presence sometimes — a warmth beside her, a shadow that felt protective rather than frightening.

She didn’t know it, but she was feeling the echo of the brother — not as a ghost, but as a memory carried in the bloodline, a tenderness passed down like a family heirloom.

This stage ended when she realized solitude was not loneliness — it was preparation.

---

The Ninth Life — The Sister’s Final Stage (Healing and Peace)

The final transformation — the soul-line completes itself.

She became gentle, luminous, whole.  
She forgave easily.  
She loved slowly.  
She moved through the world with the calm certainty of someone who had carried many versions of herself and finally set them down.

She often sat by the sea on a weathered bench, feeling a presence settle beside her — familiar, steady, protective. She didn’t know why she whispered, “It’s alright now,” but she did. And the wind seemed to answer.

She wasn’t speaking to a ghost.  
She was speaking to the lineage — the brother, the soul before him, the entire chain of selves that had led to her.
