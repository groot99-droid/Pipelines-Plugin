**Working title:** What I Kept From the Fire

**Premise:** A first-person poem processing what survived a house fire
that took most of what mattered materially — not the objects lost, but
the specific small things noticed only afterward (a smell, a habit of
checking a door twice) that turn out to have been the actual freight all
along. Moves through grief and anger toward a landing on what "keeping"
means once there's nothing left to hold.

**Candidate themes:** loss, memory as habit rather than object, grief,
what survives

**Candidate archetypes:** none — first-person confessional, not
archetype-driven
