**OUTLINE — "What I Kept From the Fire"**
*(mode: confessional-poetry; structural model: "Growing Up")*

---

**1. Anaphoric frame (opening movement)**

Repeated stem to open successive stanzas/lines — something like *"They say I lost..."* or *"I did not lose..."* — establishing the poem's argument before it's stated outright: the inventory of the fire is wrong, or incomplete. This stem recurs at least twice more later (start of the second catalog stanza, and once more just before the pivot) so the repetition reads as a controlled anaphora, not a tic.

**2. Triadic image-catalog — what the fire actually took**

Not objects. Small noticed habits/textures, built in triads (groups of three, the way "Growing Up" stacks its small sensory details):

- *A habit-triad*: checking the front door twice before bed / the specific creak of the third stair / leaving one lamp on for no one
- *A body/texture-triad*: the smell of a particular detergent on a pillowcase / the grit of sand still in a coat pocket / a burn-scarred spot on the kitchen table's edge, worn smooth by an elbow
- *A voice/sound-triad*: someone's laugh caught mid-word / a specific rhythm of footsteps on the stairs / a phone ringtone never changed out of habit

Each triad should read as *evidence* — small, concrete, mundane enough to hurt more than the named objects (photo albums, furniture) that the poem should mention only in passing, almost dismissively, to set up the reversal: those things were replaceable; these were not.

**3. Rhetorical-question pivot (single hinge, not repeated)**

One question, placed after the catalogs have accumulated weight — the "But why?" equivalent from "Growing Up." Something that turns the poem from cataloguing toward confronting the actual claim, e.g. *"What is a house, if not the sum of what nobody thought to save?"* or *"If none of it can be rebuilt, what was I even keeping?"* This should function exactly as one hinge: before it, description; after it, the poem's argument becomes explicit and the tone shifts from grief-as-listing to grief-as-reckoning (anger, then acceptance).

**4. Post-pivot turn — reckoning with "keeping" as a verb without an object**

Short movement working out what "keeping" means once there is nothing physical left to hold — the poem should locate "keeping" in action/repetition rather than possession: still checking a door that no longer exists, still reaching for a smell that's gone, the body keeps the habit even after the house that housed it is ash. This is where the poem's real claim lands, in image before it lands in statement.

**5. Aphoristic closing line/image**

Landed explicitly, not left ambiguous — per mode voice notes, states rather than implies. Working directions (pick one register, refine in draft):

- An imperative, echoing "Growing Up"'s closing instruction but arriving at its own claim: *"So keep the habit, not the house — the house was only ever where you kept it."*
- Or a flat declarative aphorism: *"Nothing burns that was never a thing to begin with."*

Either way: the last line should explicitly name that what's "kept" was never material, closing the gap the rhetorical-question pivot opened.

This outline treats "What I Kept From the Fire" as a standalone piece using "Growing Up" purely as a structural model. If the author wants to deliberately pair it with an existing poem (e.g., an explicit echo or address to *Growing Up*), that would need to be a conscious choice made in the draft stage — flagging here rather than building in an unstated tie.
