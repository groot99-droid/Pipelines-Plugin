**Working title:** The Wordless Room

**Premise:** We inherit a vocabulary for describing our own minds —
diagnostic labels, therapy-speak, self-help frameworks — that promises to
help us see ourselves clearly. But language given in advance of
experience doesn't just describe what we notice; it pre-decides what
we're allowed to notice, quietly closing off whatever it has no word for.
The essay argues that real self-knowledge sometimes requires a
deliberate, temporary wordlessness — refusing the ready-made label long
enough to feel what's actually there — before a better, more particular
language can be built from that unnamed ground up.

**Candidate themes:** self-knowledge, language and perception, the limits
of diagnostic/therapeutic vocabulary, silence as method, naming vs.
noticing
