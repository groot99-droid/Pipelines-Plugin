# Shanty for the Morning Ferry

*(no music; rope on the bollard)*
Four in the mornin', end of August, eighty-nine,
Fog on the bay, and the foghorn moanin' the one note it knows, same as mine,
PORT-AU-LEVAIN — BY DECREE OF THE SOVEREIGN SOURDOUGH COUNCIL, cut in the arch at the head of the quay,
Mornin' Ferry don't wait on a soul — what misses her stays. What stays is me.

——
*(Yann takes the answer, a breath high)*
HAUL AWAY —
— for the Mornin' Ferry,
Gold on the Gallery, white on the Guild,
Burlap on the Abbey and the red crust on the quay,
Every platter in the Hall has gotta be filled,
And it's never been the Gallery fillin' one for me,
HAUL AWAY —
— for the Mornin' Ferry,
She takes out the cod and she brings back the tax,
The loaf on your platter at dawn is who you are —
So haul, boys, haul, and don't look back

——
*(storytelling cadence, laid back, heavy)*
Now Marrec's on the winch with a wheeze like a bellows with a hole,
Says the fog gets in his chest and charges rent on his soul,
Old Maela guts on the planks and she sings the low line flat,
Forty years flat, and nobody's told her, and nobody's gonna do that,
Nolwenn, twelve, chalks the crates with a stub the size of her thumb,
Counts out loud so the Counting-House can't say she's dumb,
And Petit-Yann on the rope, takin' the answer a breath too high,
Hauls with us by day, and by night in the dish pit he kneels where the old seal's ground in dry,
Three weeks Unregistered, copper short, and the tariff don't bend,
Seal struck off his bread, so he presses his thumb in the crust instead —
Says that way he'll know it's his. Says a man should know his own bread.
Keeps a ferry token in his pocket he never once spent.

Four, the salt for the Counting-House, weighed on the Counting-House scale,
Five, the saffron, stamped for the Apothecary, sweeter than the sail,
Six, the gold leaf in paper for the Gallery's cloches, light as a lie,
Seven, the customs crate — Crown wax, Crown chain, and nobody's to pry,
I haul the salt. I haul the gold.
I haul whatever the Gallery's sold.

Then up off the Salt-Flats, boots white to the knee,
Comes a sea-dog in a coat with no colour I could see,
Not gold, not flour, not crust, not burlap — nobody's man,
Opens the coat just enough: a pocket-mortar, small as a hand,
Don't point it. Don't need to. Just lets me look.
Then he asks me the one thing, quiet as a hook:
"Which crate's the Crown's?"
And I told him.
Silver piece in my palm, and he closes my fingers on it slow —
"Sing it loud tonight, shantyman. Louder than you know."

——

HAUL AWAY —
— for the Mornin' Ferry,
Night shift under the lamps and the fog comin' in on the slates,
Sing it to the bottom of your lungs and a little past that, mates,
HAUL AWAY —
— for the Mornin' Ferry,
Silver in my boot and the crew askin' why I'm callin' so strong,
The loaf on your platter at dawn is who you are —
So haul, boys, haul, and don't count the crates

——

First night, a crack, down under the second refrain,
Like a cask stove in — so I sang the refrain again,
Mornin', the customs crate's stood open, Crown wax broke in two,
And a silver piece on the lid with PROOF-READER scratched through,
Nolwenn wouldn't touch it. Maela crossed herself twice.
Master Proof-Reader come down himself at seven and didn't look at it twice,
Put it in his apron the way a man puts away what's his,
And nobody on this wharf has seen it since, or asked what it is.

Second night, glass, off the west side, under the low line,
Maela sang it flatter, and the flat covered it fine,
Mornin', the Apothecary's short of its saffron, every thread,
And short of the vials the register counts. That's all the Inquisitor said.

Third night, the Counting-House, iron on iron, three streets up,
Ledger-Clerk shot every deadbolt home, and every deadbolt held like a sieve,
Mornin', the gold reserve's gone, and the brass seal off the Clerk's own desk,
And the Gallery's sendin' down for a name, and the Gallery don't much care whose name is next.

Three nights I sang, and three nights the island slept,
And three silver pieces in my boot is the only count I kept.

——
*(darker, slower)*
Monday, the seventh, dusk, and I go up to the Hall like you do,
Look at my own seal on my own loaf, see it's still true,
Gilded platters nearest the Hearth, silver gone yellow with the heat,
Flour-white in the middle, red crust at the back where the draughts come in off the street,
Aboyeur comes down the Hall with the dusk loaf, calls no name,
Sets it on the empty platter nearest the fire — *for a name under seal* — same
As every night since before my grandmother. Nobody asks. I don't.
And at the back, by the red crust, by my own platter — the coat.
At Yann's.
Cloche up in one hand. Cloche back down.
I went back down to the wharf. I sang the night shift, loud as he paid.

Wednesday the word comes down the quay off the dawn judgment:
Audit on platter nine. Loaf with no thumb in it. Bailiff cracked it. Counting-House seal.
Fine's four onces of copper, the weight of the seal, by the noon bell, or the Hearth.
Yann on the rope, sayin' "That ain't my bread. There's no thumb."
Saying it to the rope.
I had the sea-dog's silver in my boot. Three pieces.
Noon bell.
I was haulin'.
And the sea-dog on the wharf that afternoon in gloves —
Never wore gloves in his life.

——

*(beat drops out)*
WEDNESDAY. NINTH OF SEPTEMBER. NOON BELL.

BY ORDER OF THE MORNING JUDGMENT.

PLATTER NINE.

YANN LE GOFF. PLONGEUR. RED CRUST.

THE CRATE. THE SAFFRON. THE VAULT.

FINE UNPAID.

TRIAL BY FIRE.

SUPPER AT NINE.

*(beat rolls back in slow and heavy)*
Gallery up in the gold with the menu cards tucked in their sleeves,
Quartet tunin' a fifth, and the Abbot in his burlap wouldn't bless it — kept his hands in his sleeves,
And Yann on the stage, hummin' the answer, a breath too high,
Waitin' on the call. Found me in the crowd. Waited.
I never gave it.
They gave him to the Hearth.
Menu's up on the board by the Gallery stair by three: the fish, the wine, the saffron bread.
His platter's bare by morning.

What's a shantyman worth on a burnin' day?
Who calls the time, if not the one that's paid?
And who'd have heard him answer, if I'd called?

——
*(half time; nobody takes the answer)*
HAUL AWAY —
HAUL AWAY —
Gold on the Gallery, white on the Guild,
Burlap on the Abbey and the red crust on the quay,
One platter in the Hall that won't be filled,
And it was never gonna be the Gallery fillin' it for me,
HAUL AWAY —
She takes out the cod and she brings back the tax,
The loaf on his platter at dawn was who he was —
So haul, boys. Haul. Don't look back.

——

Twenty-third, mornin', and she comes in out of the white,
Deckhands shoutin' it down before the rope's even tight —
Paris. A fortress taken in July. The King's own walls.
The Third Estate sittin' as the nation, in the King's own halls.
Marrec let go of the winch. Maela put down the knife.
Nolwenn set the chalk on the crate, first time in her life,
And a Crown man in a coat comes down the plank behind the paper, grey from the crossing, hat in his hand,
And nobody bows. And nobody hauls. And nobody's paid. And nobody's sure where to stand.
And I open my mouth, and the shanty's what comes, because the shanty's all I've got —
HAUL AWAY —
— the fortress is down —
First new line anybody's put to it in a hundred years, and it went up the Wharf, and up the Flats, faster than his coach.

Then one dawn after — don't ask me which, the fog don't keep a calendar —
She casts off with the cod and the mail and a coat with no colour aboard,
Crack out over the water like the crust breakin' at six,
Strongbox gone. Sea-dog gone. Ferry gone under him.
Some say inland. Some say the reef.
She came back in a fortnight with a mainland crew and no box.
She brought the news in. She took him out.
One crate, one shelf of saffron, one vault, one ferry — four,
And a fifth he never touched with his hands: a song. He paid for it. It's mine.

——
*(drops to almost a whisper, deliberate)*
So here's the count the Counting-House never kept.
He asked which crate. I said.
I saw the coat at Yann's platter. I sang.
The fine was copper by the noon bell, and his silver was in my boot. I hauled.
On the stage he waited for the call, a breath high, the way he always did,
And I kept my mouth shut — me, the one man on this rock whose whole trade is openin' it.
Thumb in every loaf so he'd know his own,
And they gave him to the fire for a loaf with no thumb in it.
Ferry token in his pocket, never spent. I don't know where it went.

But he never paid me to keep quiet.
He paid me to sing.

——

Now it goes up the hill — wharf to the dish pit, dish pit to the Flats, Flats to the Square,
Apprentices, Plongeurs, the gutters, the Unregistered, every red crust that's there,
Under the Gallery windows at supper, crowdin' the lane,
And somebody put him in it. Somebody put in his name.

HAUL THEM DOWN —
— from the Gallery,
Gold on the Gallery, white on the Guild,
Burlap on the Abbey and the red crust in the Square,
Every platter in the Hall is gonna be filled,
And Petit-Yann's the answer, and the answer's everywhere —
HAUL THEM DOWN —
— from the Gallery,
Same stage, same bell, same cloches, same fire,
The loaf on your platter at dawn is who they'll say you are —
And I'm the one that calls it. Every night. Louder. Higher.

——
*(half spoken, fading slow like the beat's dying)*
Haul away…
Marrec, mind the winch…
Nolwenn, chalk the crates…
Somebody tell Maela she's flat… no… don't…
Platter nine's bare…
See you at the ferry…

Every dawn the ferry goes, and every dawn the bell,
And the song I sold for silver is the one they sing so well.

They sing him now like the Gallery lit him alone.
I sang loud enough to cover a crate — and I've been singin' over it since.
