# Tenfold

*(no organ; the Company bell, once; the Dusk whistle going out through the pines)*
Third Sunday of Advent, ninety-three, seventeenth of December, dusk,
St Brannock's, under the bell the Company paid for,
Fourteen in the pews. Twenty-two last Sunday. Thirty the Sunday before.
I count you the way the Clerk counts rent.
I light the pink one. The candle for rejoicing.

——
(spoken, plain)
Everyone in HollowMere confesses to me sooner or later.
Tonight it's later. And it's me.

You know me. I came off the afternoon car on the seventeenth of October,
and a platform hand carried my trunk up the hill and said nothing the whole way.
I asked a child at the pump where the man before me had gone.
She told me.
I did not ask twice.

——

You told me things.

Maud told me one thing, at the Lantern, with her glass untouched.
I walked it behind the back car on the twentieth of November,
I carried it out of this town unopened,
and I will not open it here.

Three men at Mrs Grieves's bar told me things,
each one wanting a Liquid Courage he couldn't run to.
I bought three. I drank none.
One owed. One had a brother. One had a lie he'd told so long it had a room in his house.

And the town told me the rest without meaning to:
a baker halving the mourners' loaves to feed the dead,
a lamplighter who saves the fourth lantern for last,
a stone that goes up at dawn with a name on it, and Agnes reading it to the Row.
A town that lost its master on the platform Thursday,
and had a writ from the next one before the boards were swept.

And who hears the one who hears?
Who does the confessor kneel to, at the end of the line?

Tonight, you do.

——
(slower)
Every night I learn where one soul stands.
That's the gift, or the work, or the curse. I have called it all three.
Every night I choose how much to say.

I have known where one of you stands since November.
I chose how much to say.
I said nothing.

You are in this room.
Somebody has been taking my candles, one at a time, from the third pew.
Somebody has measured my robes with their eyes, collar to hem.
And the one beside you will swear you were somewhere else.

Why didn't I say?
Because I have seen what this town does with a name.
It takes it to the Bell. It tries it. It drops it through the floor.
Because the man before me rang that bell till his gloves wore through, they say —
and they say a lot.
Because I would rather this town lose a priest than hang a guess.

Is that mercy?
Or is it pride, wearing my collar?

This morning the stone went up. You've all read the name.
I read it too.

——
*(a draught up the Undercroft stair; the pink candle; the bell rope moves with no hand on it)*

(in the dark, slow)
"In the end I sought nothing else but to serve God.
And serve him I did.
With this death's last breath I pray this prayer: may the Almighty grant me the strength of ten in the next life.
And in return, tenfold my own."

*(no bell)*

*(Monday, dusk: the robes come out of the vestry and climb the pulpit, and preach. Nobody reports anything.)*

——
(the pews sing; one voice under the rest)
Where he's gone there's a lavender field,
and an orange tree, just the one,
and a sky that's blue — they swear it's blue —
though none of us here has seen one.

I told him the worst thing I ever did.
He kept it. It's his now,
wherever he is. In the lavender.
Or under the floor.

Ten for one, he said, ten for one,
strength of ten when the night is done,
light the lamp and hold the pew —
come down, come down, come down.

——

Five doors on the square had ash by morning,
ash and something darker, drawn by a careful hand,
and men went door to door by lantern, asking
which of us he'd made believe.
The pews went thin. The pews went thinner.
And up from the Undercroft, through the boards,
a second hymn, to our tune, in our time,
and not one word of it ours.

How many does it take to make a town believe?
Six, they say.
We counted.
We stopped counting at —

——
(the tune drops out)
KEEP SINGING.

DON'T LOOK AT THE PULPIT.

THE VICAR PREACHED AT DUSK TONIGHT.

WHO PREACHED AT DUSK TONIGHT.

NOBODY GOES DOWN THE STAIR.

NOBODY REPORTS ANYTHING.

SING.

(the tune comes back, lower)
Ten for one, he said — ten of what?
Tenfold my own — his own what?
His blood? His silence? Mine?
Come down, come down, come down.

——

They say the Friday morning train came in with a man on it
nobody sold a ticket to.
They say an iron crosier rang on the platform boards.
They say on Saturday a pillar of light stood over St Brannock's,
and he was ten of himself in it. Tenfold.
I saw a light. I'll say that much. I saw a light.
Then the bell rang,
and nobody in HollowMere can tell you which way.

Ten for one, he said, ten for one,
and I'm in the pew where I told him,
holding what he held for me —
come down.

——
(half-spoken, the tune going out)
The stone goes up at dawn…
Somebody light the pink one…
Don't go down the stair…
Who preached at dusk tonight…

He prayed for the strength of ten, and HollowMere heard a debt.
This town has never once let a debt go uncollected.
