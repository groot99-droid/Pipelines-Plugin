# Six Things a Hand Can Make

*(no music; hammer on the anvil)*
Thursday, December, fifty-two, and the hearth's not out. It don't go out.
Men stand in my door to call it the warmest light in the camp, and they stand there long, and I let 'em.
It's warm 'cause it's working, boys. A light is only a fire with a job.
Left hand healed shiny. I keep it out of the trough.

——

Six things in the hills, and six things in the hand,
Bring 'em in where it's lit, where the whole yard can see whose hands,
Nothing gets made in the dark in this camp, nothing gets made unseen,
Every hand in this room is a hand somebody's counted clean —
Bring it to the forge, I'll make it.
Don't tell me who it's for.

——
*(hammer, steady, one for each)*
One for the timber, off the yard and the saw,
Saw's run quiet since Monday, and nobody says what it saw.
One for the stone, off the yard, cheapest thing in the camp,
Only thing they carry me that nobody died for. Stone don't need a lamp.
One for the tin ore, from the seam, and the seam wants a spade,
And the spade wants my basin, so every lantern's mine at both ends of the trade.

One for the resin, off the pines, sticky as a debt,
Reverend used to thumb it on us at the gate, resin on the brow. He's not back yet.
Gone since the fourth. The collar still blesses at the gate, the same words, the same height of hand.
I don't look whose neck is in it. I'm a smith. I don't have to understand.
One for the herbs, out of the woods, and everybody wants 'em,
The ones who hand out the jobs want 'em, and the snares want 'em, and I can't tell you which is worse to be asked for, so I don't.
One for the mushrooms, up out of the adits, cold and pale as a palm,
Only thing they bring to my door I can't make into anything.
Somebody wants 'em. Never me.
Thank God for one thing in this camp that isn't mine to make.

——

Six things in the hills, and six things in the hand,
Bring 'em in where it's lit, where the whole yard can see whose hands,
Nothing gets made in the dark in this camp, nothing gets made unseen,
Every hand in this room is a hand somebody's counted clean —
Bring it to the forge, I'll make it.
Don't tell me who it's for.

Six for the hills. And six for the hand.

——
*(quench hiss)*
Timber and resin, that's a torch, and a torch don't need my fire.
Any man can make his own torch, and that's the saddest thing I know.
It's for the ones caught out past the whistle, between the bunkhouse and the pines.
Wolves won't cross a flame. They won't. They wait at the edge of it, patient as clerks.
Sawmill Five went out Tuesday at the Roll with his hands in his pockets and no light.
Dawn, at the treeline: his boot, and his foot still in it, laced.
His jacket in strips down the slope, like somebody sorting rags.
A drag line in the needles into the pines, wide as a man's shoulders, then narrower.
In the one pocket left, a spoon he was whittling for nobody. Half a bowl. A good half.
Ridge has a saying: *the whistle you did not answer.*
I don't know which whistle Five didn't answer. I know he whittled.

*(hammer)*
Timber and herbs, that's a snare. Somebody orders one most weeks.
I don't ask who. The tag on it tells you all it does:
holds whoever steps in, and logs the name.
That's all a snare is, boys. Holds whoever steps in, and logs the name.
It don't care whose foot.
Neither did the list they read to somebody I ironed a shirt for.

*(hammer)*
Timber and stone, at my basin, a spade, for the Workings crew:
*dig tin ore out of a seam*, and the ore comes back to me.
Every spade I hand out comes back to me as ore.
Some come back without the man.
There's one on the rack now, the handle worn to one man's grip, his thumb's dip in the ash.
Nobody asks for it. Nobody's hand fits.
And some nights a spade goes out for what isn't ore,
And comes back clean, and nobody brings me anything.

*(hammer)*
Tin ore, at my basin: picks. For whoever wants in.
I make 'em. I don't ask what door, and I don't ask after.
The camp says it like a hymn: *locks keep people out, never in.*
Nothing on my hut is locked from the outside. Go and look.
I could make a pick for every door in this camp,
And not one of them's the thing that keeps me here.

——
*(hammer slower)*
Then the lantern. *Three tin ore from the seam. I will make you a lantern for it.*
I say it to every one of 'em. Same words, every time, like the Reverend's.
*Good ore. This burns bright.* It nearly always does.
It's for every man they send down. The detail man fills his before he goes in the water,
Every one of 'em fills it, don't let the bunkhouse tell you different,
'Cause down there they live below the last lamp, and a man wants to be the last lamp, not below it.
Little Hob came up still hooked on a hand that wouldn't let go of the bail.
I cleaned him. That's my work.
This one's Patience. I'm sealing her now, under my breath. You didn't hear it.
Patience, go down. Come back up.
Come back up with him.

*(hammer, heavy)*
Timber and tin ore: a crowbar. Heaviest thing I make, and the plainest.
Says it on the tag: *break a locked door; it stays broken.*
For whoever wants in and don't care who knows it.
The yard says the crowbar goes to whoever never holds the knife.
I let the yard say it. I hand it over. I don't ask.
It's the honest one of the six. What it opens stays open.

——

Six things in the hills, and six things in the hand,
Brought in where it's lit, where the whole yard could see whose hands,
Nothing got made in the dark in this camp, nothing got made unseen,
Every hand in this room was a hand somebody'd counted clean —
I brought it to the forge. I made it.
Don't tell me who it was for.

——
*(beat drops out; the whistle)*
FIFTEEN SECONDS.

BANK THE TROUGH. LEAVE THE FIRE.

WHO'S GOT A TORCH.

NOBODY'S OUT. NOBODY'S OUT.

THE BUNK BY THE DOOR IS EMPTY.

DON'T ANSWER THE WHISTLE.

DON'T ANSWER IT.

*(beat rolls back in, slow)*
A bunk without a man gets the light, and the light found the bunk by the door.
The rack'll write him *absent* by morning, and my hearth's still going behind me, lit for nobody.

——
*(at the bunk; almost a whisper)*
Carl.
Six things a hand can make in this camp, and I've made every one of 'em for somebody else.
Yours made a seventh.
A name. Off a list. In a pressed shirt I ironed.
There's no tool for that in my forge. I've looked. I'm the best smith in this camp, and I've looked.
A crowbar's door stays broken. A said name stays said.

Who's the torch for? Who's the snare for?
Who was I for?
Did you need to? Did they ask? Did it cost you anything at all?

I could make a pick for the gate. I never have.
Wrong tool. There's no lock on what keeps me.
The men need lanterns, and I'm the one who makes 'em, so I stay, and I keep it lit,
and I call that a choice, 'cause it's the one thing in here I get to call one.
Our old lantern off the dump's still under my bunk. I could fix it by supper. I haven't.

I know who it's for.

——

Crowbar and lantern, picks and spade, snare and torch, back down the line,
Mushrooms and herbs and resin, and ore, and stone, and the pine,
Bring it in where it's lit, where the whole yard can see whose hands,
Every hand in this room is a hand somebody's counted clean —
Bring it to the forge, I'll make it.
Somebody tell me who I was for.

——
*(half-spoken, fading with the hammer)*
Three tin ore…
Good ore…
Bank the trough, not the fire…
See you at the whistle…

A hand can make six things in this camp, and every one of them goes out the door.
The seventh thing a hand can make is a name, and nothing in this forge can unmake it.
