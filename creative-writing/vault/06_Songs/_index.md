Song lyrics, spanning narrative rap and confessional verse.

- [[06_Songs/04_Cable_Construction_Circus|Cable Construction Circus]] — a narrative rap portraying a chaotic work site crew as a circus troupe → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/04_Cable_Construction_Circus|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/05_How_i_feel|How i feel]] — a confessional song lyric on isolation, creative burnout, and disillusionment with time and fame → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/05_How_i_feel|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/06_Ballad_of_the_Iron_Beast|Ballad of the Iron Beast]] — a HollowMere platform hand's work ballad for the Daily Train — the crew, the freight, the black wax, the back car — turning on the brother who stayed for him → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/06_Ballad_of_the_Iron_Beast|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/07_Tenfold|Tenfold]] — the new Vicar's last sermon at dusk on the third Sunday of Advent, 17 December 1893, a confession to his own congregation that ends on his last words verbatim, followed by the converts' hymn for what comes back, broken by a shouted capitals passage, WHO PREACHED AT DUSK TONIGHT → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/07_Tenfold|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/08_I_Want_to_Hang|I Want to Hang]] — the Drifter's slow waltz: a nameless stranger who came to the end of the line to be chosen practises looking guilty at the well and the doorpost, reads the stone every morning hoping for his own name, and pleads with the bench for the rope, knowing his hanging buys time for the real knife → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/08_I_Want_to_Hang|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/09_The_Blade_Sings_at_Dawn|The Blade Sings at Dawn]] — the head sawyer's work song for the dawn of Monday 8 December 1952, when Mirek, Sawmill No. 6, is sentenced to the Blade on a short count the singer's own silence put on him, and the singer works the lever: "The Blade didn't sing for him. / I did." → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/09_The_Blade_Sings_at_Dawn|notes]]
- [[06_Songs/10_Shanty_for_the_Morning_Ferry|Shanty for the Morning Ferry]] — the dockhands' work song as the shantyman's confession: paid by the Smuggler-Blackguard to sing louder over the crate, the saffron, the vault and the ferry strongbox, he keeps singing while Petit-Yann is framed by a seal in his loaf, fined by the noon bell and given to the Hearth at the nine o'clock supper on 9 September 1789; the ferry brings the news and takes the smuggler away, and the shanty becomes the Commons' song with Yann's name in it → annotation: [[_Annotations/06_Songs/10_Shanty_for_the_Morning_Ferry|notes]]
