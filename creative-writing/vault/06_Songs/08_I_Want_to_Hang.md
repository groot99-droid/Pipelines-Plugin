# I Want to Hang

*(waltz time, slow; no drum, just a chair creaking in three)*

Nobody comes to the end of the line
unless they're running, or hunting, they say.
I wasn't running. I wasn't hunting.
I paid one way to be picked.

——

One bag, one coat, one fare, no name.
In the bag, a knife I whittled out of pine
and blacked with stove polish till it shone like a sin.
A rag gone red with something that isn't blood.
A coat that came with somebody's trade:
you'd nod at it. You do.

I have never cut a thing that bled.
I have never held a thing that mattered.
I have never once been chosen.

So what does guilty look like, in this fog?
Somebody show me. I'll wear it.

——

Bell, and bench, and beam.
Acquittal, a fine, or the drop.
Don't acquit me.
Don't fine me.
I want to hang.
Choose me.

——

I learned to wash my hands at the well
at the wrong hour, where the shutters can see,
clean hands, and I scrub them like they're not.
I learned to leave one red thumb on a doorpost
where somebody careful will find it.
I learned to pass a lit door twice
and look back once, and not the third time.

Not too much. Too much and they laugh,
and a laugh is a town looking away,
and I have had a whole life of away.

Every morning I wet the rag again.
Every morning I read the stone with tomorrow on it,
slow, every letter, like a letter from home.
It's never me.
It's somebody who wanted the morning.
It's never me.

How guilty is guilty enough?
How long does a slip have to lie
before somebody writes my name on one?

——

Bell, and bench, and beam.
Acquittal, a fine, or the drop.
Don't acquit me.
Don't fine me.
I want to hang.
Look at me.

——

But I'm not the only one in a borrowed coat.
Somebody here washes at the well and means it.
Somebody here keeps the real one, warm from a pocket,
and tonight they'll be the loudest voice in the square
asking who did it,
and their finger will find me,
and I'll stand up straighter.

I know what my rope buys.
I know who it buys it for.
I don't know their name.

Every day you spend on me, the knife walks free:
another plate at the wake table,
another name on the stone that isn't mine.
And God help me, I'm grateful.
Whoever you are,
you're the only one in this town who ever needed me.

——

*(music stops; whispered)*
Dusk. They take the rope in.
They bolt the trap.
The crows walk the beam to keep me in sight.
I stand under it anyway,
like the last one at a dance, waiting to be asked.

They say the last man through the trap
went into the Gazette with no name,
because nobody could swear to it.
They say the rope comes off the Company mill
and gets billed to whoever drops.
Fine. Open me an account.
Put a name on it. Any name.

I never told anybody mine.
Not the conductor. Not the bench. Not you.
So if you choose me, what do you choose?
And who was all of this for?

——

*(music back, slower)*
Bell, and bench, and beam.
Acquittal, a fine, or the drop.
Hands go up at the bell like crows off a beam.
The town has spoken.
Say it's me.

——

*(half-sung, fading)*
I never wanted to die.
I wanted to be chosen,
and this town only chooses one way.
All my life the world looked straight through me.
A gallows is just a town that finally looks.
