# The Blade Sings at Dawn

*(no music; the wheel in the dark)*
Five to six, Monday mornin', December, fifty-two,
Ice on the race and the waterwheel turnin' like it's got someplace to get to,
Old rail bed by the trestle runs south, and none of us ever came up it,
PRODUCTION IS OBLIGATION on the gate, new paint on the old — somebody else's slogan, we just inherited it,
They tell you at the gate you're feedin' the furnace or you're fuel,
And the furnace never once ran cold, and the wheel never once ran cool.
Saw's been quiet all night. Saw's gonna sing before the bell.
Mondays like this it sings empty first — so the whole bunkhouse can tell.

——

PRODUCTION —
— IS OBLIGATION.
Wheel on the race and the carriage on the rail,
Quota on the board and your number on the nail,
PRODUCTION —
— IS OBLIGATION.
Feet by the bell and feet by the night,
Chalk don't lie and the Tallyman's always right,
Ten on the sawmill rack, and the rack don't care what you are —
The log on your rack at dawn is who you are.

——
(storytelling cadence, low, under the saw)
Now Two's on the tail with his hands in his sleeves,
Taps the rail twice before every cut, like a man sayin' please,
Came in off a boat from a war that ain't over and won't be,
Says *thank you* to nobody, soft, like the last thing he owns free.
Seven sets the dogs and he talks to the logs like they're mules —
*Easy now, girl, easy* — Seven never learned any other rules.
And Six takes the board off the saw and he walks it out to the stack,
Counts every plank twice 'cause he ran a lathe once and he trusts a number to count him back,
Names every log as it comes off the carriage, low, in a language I don't have,
Folds his scrip every Friday into a paper bird before he spends half,
Hums somethin' under the saw all day that the saw won't let you hear,
Asks me every night what time it is someplace, and I tell him, and he nods like I'm near.
And me, I'm Five, on the lever, I'm the one sets the bite,
Cough since the thaw — crew calls it a cold, and the crew's bein' polite,
Used to stand on a crate in the rain and say UNION like a prayer,
Now I stand on a lever and say nothin', and nobody's countin' what's there.
Mess-hall men hum with no words when the whistle blows; we sing with ours, so somebody knows.

——
(the count; flat, like the board)
Four hundred feet by the first bell — Tallyman chalks it on Six,
Eight hundred by noon, and the wheel takes its buckets and ticks,
Twelve hundred's the quota, eleven-sixty's a Monday,
Forty feet short is a Monday, and Monday comes round every someday.
I pull the lever. Six takes the board.
The Tallyman chalks what Six can hold.
Offbearer's count is the count goes up on the slate,
So whatever the lever don't cut, it's Six who comes up late.

——

PRODUCTION —
— IS OBLIGATION.
Man comin' in on Friday's train, so the quota goes up for his eyes,
Fifteen hundred feet, and the wheel don't turn faster for a man in a tie,
PRODUCTION?
— is obligation.
Short on a Monday, your week goes down the race,
No store, no bread, no paper bird, and a hungrier face,
Ten on the sawmill rack, and the rack's a ledger, not a star —
Who owes who, when the log on your rack at dawn is who you are?

——
(beat drops out; the platform at dawn)
WEIGH THE TEN.

WEIGH THE TEN.

CIRCUIT JUDGE, FLOWN IN THIS MORNING.

NUMBER SIX. SHORT FORTY.

ACQUITTAL. SCRIP FORFEITURE. THE BLADE.

MONDAY THE SEVENTEENTH. SCRIP FORFEITURE.

CIRCUIT JUDGE, FLOWN IN THIS MORNING.

NUMBER SIX. SHORT FORTY.

ACQUITTAL. SCRIP FORFEITURE. THE BLADE.

MONDAY THE EIGHTH —

(beat rolls back in, slow and heavy)
Billet goes on the scale. Billet comes off the rack.
Judge goes back to the airstrip. They'll fly him in again when they want him back.

——
(darker, slower)
Acquittal's back to the carriage — that's the mercy, that's the prize,
More feet for the same bread, the same wheel, the same skies,
Forfeiture's your week in the race, every cent of scrip gone,
And the Blade's the carriage with a man on it, where the log belongs.
Same rail. Same bite. Same lever. Same hand.
What the saw lets go of, the chute takes down to the race, and the wheel don't understand.
Race runs red for a minute. Then it runs clean.
Nobody looks. Nobody counts. Nobody says what they've seen.
Then they plane your billet blank, white wood where the number burned,
Brand it fresh for Friday's train, and a new man wears what you earned,
Wears your wood and your slot and your quota, and never knows who's gone —
That's the whole of the Blade. That's all. And the shift goes on.

What's a number worth on a fifty-two quota?
Forty feet of pine?
And who chalked it — the Tallyman,
or the man on the lever who kept still?

——
(drops to almost a whisper, deliberate)
Monday the seventeenth, the forty was mine.
Cough on the lever, I held the bite back, I was slow on the line,
The cough took the forty and Six took the chalk,
Tallyman turned to the crew and said *Whose?* — and nobody'd talk.
And I didn't talk.
Judge said forfeiture. Six said *okay*. That's all Six said.
No paper bird that Friday. No store. Camp ration and a heel of bread.
Three weeks, and his belt ran out of holes, and Seven punched him a new one with a nail,
Ribs come up through his shirt like the carriage rail,
Knuckles gone white as the planed wood, hands shakin' on the board,
Still countin' every plank twice. Still trustin' what the chalk would record.
Then the man off the train, and the quota went up for his week,
And a hungry man carries less board than a fed one, however you speak,
Monday the eighth the forty was Six's, honest and true —
Was it? I built it. I built it out of keepin' still, start to through.

Did he know?
He looked at the lever once, on the seventeenth, the way you look at weather.
Then he went back to countin'. He never said. We never talked about it, ever.
Whether that look was him forgivin' the man at the lever, or him knowin' all along who built his forty, I'll never hear it from a mouth the Blade sang shut.

His name was Mirek.
He named the logs for places he'd left, so somebody'd say 'em out loud.
He folded the birds for a child he couldn't send 'em to, and kept 'em in a cigar box, and never once was proud.
He hummed the whole shift long under the saw, so the saw would let it pass,
And Saturday night in the bunk I finally caught the tune —
It was a lullaby. For somebody small. He never told me whose.

I pulled the lever. I watched the chalk. I kept my wood.

——
(dawn; the lever)
PRODUCTION —
— is obligation.
Lever's mine on a Blade mornin' — that's the order, that's the trade,
Saw runs empty, one long note, and the whole camp hears the Blade,
PRODUCTION —
— is obligation.
Mirek on the carriage where the log should be, lookin' at the wheel, not me,
Hummin' — and this time I can hear it, 'cause the saw ain't bitin' yet, and the race is runnin' free.
Two taps the rail twice. Seven says *easy* — and don't say *girl*.
One voice short on the answer, and it's the quietest sound in the world,
Ten on the sawmill rack, and nine of us standin' where we are —
The log on my rack at dawn is who I am. That's the charge.

——
(half-spoken, fading as the wheel takes up)
First bell…
Somebody roll the first log…
Friday's train's bringin' a new Six…
Keep your wood…
Keep your wood…

The Blade didn't sing for him.
I did.
