# Don't Miss the Train

*(no music; boots on frozen planks; wind off the north)*
Quarter to four in the evenin', December the twenty-third, ninety-three,
Coach road went Tuesday under the white, and the white's still comin' for me,
HOLLOWMERE — EST. BY THE RAILROAD EXPANSE, sign iced stiff on its chain,
Whole town's out on the boards for once — and nobody standin' in threes for the train

——

Step on up, snow on the boards, keep your hat in your hand,
Wasn't saints laid this line, boys, and it wasn't the pioneers' hand,
Barons own the mill and the bank and the dirt where you stand,
You want bread, you want news, you ever hope to go —
Seven days' walk to a soul, and tonight it's all snow,
Here she sits on the spur, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
HollowMere, end of the line, wash the mud off your name,
And whatever you do, boys — don't miss the train

——
(storytelling cadence, laid back, heavy)
Hoods were out last night, flour sacks and torches, the lot,
So we come down one at a time and we stand apart like we're not,
There's a drummer in checks come up for Harold's sale,
Sold nothin', bought nothin', sits on his tonic trunk, pale,
There's a Mill Row woman with the beddin' roped tight,
Three kids and two fares, and she's done the sum twice,
Smallest wears her boots round her neck by the laces,
Savin' the soles for the other end, wherever that place is,
There's a man with a Bible, a flower pressed flat in the Psalms,
Won't say where he's goin', holds it shut in both palms.

Car 2's empty tonight, first time since I come,
Nothin' to sign for, nothin' to unload, nothin' to bring home,
I lift their trunks. I lift their beds.
I lift whatever they couldn't sell.
And what do you carry out of a town
when the year's in the Company's name as well?

——

Step on up, snow on the boards, keep your fare in your hand,
Ticket out costs a year, and the Clerk's had the year in his hand,
Five days, boys, and nothin' comes up, and nothin' goes down the line,
Five days of the white and the pines and whatever's behind,
Here she sits on the spur, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
She's the last for a while, boys, and they're tellin' you plain —
Whatever you do, don't miss the last train

——

Now Len's on the trunks singin' spike-time to the lift,
Clem reads the manifest out loud, the parts nobody asked for, every line of the shift,
Jack Rudd grins at the drummer and the drummer looks at the floor,
And Ernie, twelve, with the bucket, wipin' snow off the step by the door,
Askin' Pruett where she goes after us —
and Pruett ain't told him yet.

Mrs Grieves has the Lantern lit, one lamp in the glass,
Long coats on the bank front, watchin' the bank, watchin' us pass,
They come up for their Baron. Their Baron fell on these boards. They stayed.
Nobody's told 'em to go, so they stand where they're paid.
Down on the dock there's a lantern, Nora's, bought dear at noon,
So Cyril can see where she is, and Cyril's three weeks drowned,
Agnes'll have the stone's name by mornin', before anyone asks,
And the Vicar didn't come down to bless the cars.
He's not been himself since Sunday, they say.

And somewhere on these boards, wavin' her off like the rest,
Stands whoever did for the Baron on the fourteenth, hand to the chest,
And the bench at the end where they found the railyard man on the twelfth —
Snow on it, smooth as a sheet. Nobody's sat there since.

Then I slip in my one, outbound, Ma's name in my hand,
*Merry Christmas, Ma. The West agrees with me, Ma* — in Arthur's hand,
I sign it Arthur. I tie off the bag. I heave it aboard.
Last bag of the year. Last lie of the year, Lord.

——
(beat drops out; Pruett on the step, punch in his hand)
LAST TRAIN OUT.

LAST TRAIN OUT.

LINE'S SHUT TILL SHE CLEARS.

CAR THREE IS FULL.

NOBODY RIDES IN CAR THREE.

FARES ON THE STEP. FARES ON THE STEP.

A PASSENGER BOOKED IN IS A PASSENGER COUNTED OUT.

ERNIE. GET THAT BUCKET OFF MY STEP.

(beat rolls back in slow and heavy)
Nobody asked how many in the back car, and nobody said,
Car Three don't get asked. Car Three just gets fed.

——
(darker, slower)
Last of 'em up is a passenger with one case, carried low,
Hands it up to me, and I take it, and the weight goes right through,
Heavy as the mail on a Monday, heavy as a man,
I set it in the car with both arms and I take back my hand,
Didn't look at the face. Didn't look at the boots in the snow.
Freight is freight, and the Company don't ask, so don't I.
Somethin' pressed in my palm, cold and round, and I shut my fist on it slow —
Ain't opened it yet. Ain't goin' to. Some things a hand don't need to know.

And out past the end of the platform, where the lamp gives out,
Somethin' stands at the tree line. Don't board. Don't wave. Don't shout.

Who counts what goes out of here, if not the hand that lifts it?
Who asks what's in a case, in a town that sells its keys at noon?
And who'd hold the last train for a question, with the white comin' down?

——

Step on up, light's goin' down, take your hat off your head,
She brought the bread and the whiskey, the priest and the lead,
She brought the Baron and the dogs and the letter sealed black,
She took the ones we couldn't keep, in a box, in the back,
Now she takes the ones that paid, and she leaves us the rest,
Five days of nothin' at all comin' up from the east,
Here she goes, whistle and steam, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
Last train don't wait, boys — and she won't come back for you, all the same

——
(drops to almost a whisper, deliberate)
But I had a ticket.

Arthur's. In the hatband since the back car. Never punched past Tuesday.
I took off his hat. I took out his ticket. I held it up in the snow.
Thought: now. Get on. Go where he didn't go.
Pruett on the step with the crescent die up in his hand —
and I didn't get on.
I put it in Ernie's hand.

Go on, I said. Go see where she goes after us.
Go on. You always ask.
And he climbed to the step, twelve, with the bucket still in his fist,
and Pruett brought the punch down to the paper —

He looked at me once. He stepped back down.
He stayed for me. He stayed in this town.

Gave it me back, warm from his hand.
"Who'd fill the bucket," he says, "if I'm gone?"
And I took it back. I put it in the band. I was glad.
God help me. I was glad.

Never punched past Tuesday. Still.

So which of us taught him that?
The one who stepped down for me —
or the one who stood here and let him watch?

——

Step on up, snow on the boards, keep your hat in your hand,
Barons own the mill and the bank and the dirt where you stand,
You want bread, you want news, you ever hope to go —
Seven days' walk to a soul, and tonight it's all snow,
There she goes round the black pines, red lamp into the white,
Iron Beast — that was Arthur's word — she don't wait, don't explain,
HollowMere, end of the line, wash the mud off your name,
Nobody missed the last train, boys — we just stood where it left us. Again.

——
(half-spoken, fading slow like the beat's dying)
Dusk whistle…
Somebody's runnin'… a woman, a boy… down the boards after her…
Red lamp's in the snow…
Somebody get Ernie off the tracks…
Nothin' till it clears…
See you at five, boys. There's no five.

Don't miss the train, they tell you, and nobody here ever has —
we just hand the ticket to the next boy, and watch him give it back.
