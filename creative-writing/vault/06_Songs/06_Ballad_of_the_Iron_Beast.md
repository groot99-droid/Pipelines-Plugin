# Ballad of the Iron Beast

*(no music; boots on frozen planks)*
Ten past five in the mornin', November, ninety-three,
Frost on the spur and the platform lantern still burnin' for me,
HOLLOWMERE — EST. BY THE RAILROAD EXPANSE, sign swingin' on its chain,
Town don't wake till the Beast clears its throat — then the whole town's waitin' on the train

——

Step on up, frost on the boards, keep your hat in your hand,
Barons own the mill and the bank and the dirt where you stand,
You want bread, you want news, you want somewhere to go —
Seven days' walk to a soul and the walk's mostly snow,
Here she comes round the black pines, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
HollowMere, end of the line, wash the mud off your name,
Whatever you do, boys — don't miss the train

——
(storytelling cadence, laid back, heavy)
Now Len's on the spikes singin' time to the maul,
Swears the rails go walkin' when nobody's watchin' at all,
Stan Pike on the switch with a cough like a saw,
Calls it a cold — been a cold since the thaw,
Jack Rudd liftin' whiskey, one bottle a load,
Calls it a tariff, says the Company owes,
Clem reads the manifests out loud, every word,
Only man on the platform who reads, so the loudest one heard,
And Ernie, twelve, with the bucket, askin' every train
Where she goes after us — and nobody's ever explained

Six o'clock, flour for Crumb's, sacks white as the frost,
Seven, the whiskey for the Lantern, I count what got lost,
Eight, tonics for the morgue — morgue orders ahead,
Nine, the gunpowder, the railyard, sign here for the lead,
I unload the flour. I unload the lead.
I unload whatever the Barons said.

——

Step on up, frost on the boards, keep your scrip in your hand,
Clerk comes at noon for the rent on the dirt where you stand,
Banks fallin' back east like the pines in a blow,
Seven days' walk to a soul and the walk's mostly snow,
Here she sits on the spur, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
Ticket out costs a year, and the year's in the Company's name —
Can't afford the train, boys — so don't miss the train

——
(sacks hit the boards)
Ten o'clock, mailbags, heavy as a man,
Gazette on the top, tossed down like contraband,
Prints yesterday's secrets, every sin that got proved,
Names nobody it can't — so the rest of us move,
Clem reads the headline, nobody looks at each other,
Every man on the platform real interested in his brother,
And down in the bag there's a letter sealed black,
Somebody's name on it, and nobody's lookin' back,
I sort around it slow like a snake in the flour,
Don't touch it, don't turn it, don't ask it the hour.

Then I slip in my three, outbound, Ma's name in my hand,
Jack Rudd grins, "Arthur too rich now to write her? Too grand?"
"Arthur's busy gettin' rich," I say, and the whole crew roars,
And I laugh along, tie off the bag, and I heave it aboard.

What's a platform hand worth on a Panic year?
A dollar in scrip and a back that don't quit?
And who's countin' — the Clerk, or the man in the mirror?

——

(beat drops out)
WHO SIGNED FOR THE LEAD.

WHO SIGNED FOR THE LEAD.

CLEM. CLEM, PUT THE GAZETTE DOWN.

THAT CRATE IS BREATHING.

NOBODY OPENS THAT CRATE.

NOBODY TOUCHES THE BLACK WAX. NOBODY.

COFFINS TO THE BACK CAR.

THE BACK CAR.

EVERY DAY.

EVERY GODDAMN DAY.

(beat rolls back in slow and heavy)
Crate went quiet by one, and nobody asked why,
Freight is freight, and the Company don't ask, so don't I.

——
(darker, slower)
Afternoon car, and the new Vicar steps down,
Father Bray died or ran, depends who's drinkin' in town,
Collar so white it hurts in the fog,
Shakes every hand like he's countin' a flock,
And they say every soul here confesses to him, sooner or later —
I carry his trunk up the hill, and I keep mine for later.

Town rioted Tuesday. Wednesday, the Baron,
Cornelius Thorne in a coat worth more than I'm earnin',
Pinkertons, dogs, and a rifle apiece,
Steps on the boards like the boards owe him peace,
Took off my hat. Took his bags. Took his scrip.
Hated myself, and I bowed at the hip.

And Stan Pike's switch is quiet — cough stopped Monday night,
Stan caught the Dusk train, back car, lid on tight,
Len sang him spike-time, Ernie held his cap,
I signed for Stan Pike like a sack, and I set him in back.

And who loads the back car, if not the hand that's here?
Who's left to sign for a man?
Who'd carry them out, if I went?

——

Step on up, light's goin' down, take your hat off your head,
She brings the bread and the whiskey, the priest and the lead,
She brings the Baron and the dogs and the letter sealed black,
She takes the ones we can't keep, in a box, in the back,
Here she sits on the spur, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast on the Thorne and Expanse, she don't wait, don't explain,
Owns the clock, owns the town, owns the mud and the rain —
Don't miss the train, boys — she don't miss you, all the same

——
(drops to almost a whisper, deliberate)
But Arthur didn't miss the train.

Two springs back — two tickets, one for him, one for me,
Punched for a Tuesday, Dusk, out past the trees,
Arthur on the step with his hat in his hand,
Me on the boards like I'm bolted to the land,
Scrip debt for an excuse. Fear for a reason.
Clerk's rent for a story I could tell every season.
He looked at me once. He stepped back down.
He stayed for me. He stayed in this town.

Called her the Iron Beast, like he did when we were boys,
To make me laugh — and it did — just a word, just a noise,
Whistled the Dusk whistle a half-step flat,
Every night on the platform, and I'd tell him quit that,
Then winter. The mere-rot. The same cough as Stan.
Then the back car.
I loaded the flour. I loaded the lead.
I loaded my brother.

And every week since, Ma, I write you in his hand —
*Doin' fine, Ma. Good wages. The West agrees with me, Ma* —
I sign it Arthur. I post it outbound.
I load the bag myself.
His ticket's in the hatband, never punched past Tuesday.
I wear the hat. I whistle flat.

So which of us missed it?
The one who never boarded —
or the one who boarded in a box?

——

Step on up, frost on the boards, keep your hat in your hand,
Barons own the mill and the bank and the dirt where you stand,
You want bread, you want news, you want somewhere to go —
Seven days' walk to a soul and the walk's mostly snow,
There she goes round the black pines, smoke hangin' like chain,
Iron Beast — that was Arthur's word — she don't wait, don't explain,
HollowMere, end of the line, wash the mud off your name,
Town says don't miss the train, boys — I never missed the train

——
(half-spoken, fading slow like the beat's dying)
Dusk whistle…
Somebody tell Ernie to get off the tracks…
Back car's full…
See you at five…

Don't miss the train, they tell you. I never missed it once —
I just let it carry everyone I couldn't follow.
