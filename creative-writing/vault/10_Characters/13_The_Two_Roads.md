# The Two Roads

*Fifteen cards. Three hands. A card shows the face. It falls one of two ways. The falling is a fortune, and the face is only where you stand tonight.*

---

## I. Cloches

*Port-au-Levain. The eve of Saint John, Tuesday the 23rd of June, 1789. Read at the Smuggler's Wharf on a salt-cod crate turned over. In the reader's hand.*

On the mainland tonight they light a fire on every hill for Saint John. The Council allows the island one fire, and it is not mine. So the Hearth burns as it burns every night, the Hall sends its music down to the water, and up there the Gallery lifts its cloches to a bell. That is our Saint John.

My cards are painted the Paris way. A ferry brought the fashion two summers ago in a lady's trunk, and the lady's maid sold it to me when the lady tired of it. The backs are painted as silver domes, so I call the suit Cloches. To lift a card is to uncover a dish.

One Guild Copper a card. I read the masters for their apprentices. Only an apprentice wants to know which way a master will go, because the apprentice goes the same way a step behind, carrying.

I do not read the Gallery. The Gallery does not ask what will become of it. It decrees, and the Square was built to hear it. Their footmen come down in gold-edged livery and pay double to ask anyway, for their lords' amusement. I take the coin and lay nothing. They go back up laughing, and the coin stays on my side of the crate.

The beam from the reef crosses the crate four times between the proofing bell and the six. I read between passes.

A card has a face, and it falls one of two ways. The face is where a man stands tonight. The way it falls is a fortune, and a fortune is no more a fate than weather is.

**The Master Proof-Reader**

*Face.* He weighs the Wake Loaves at night and lifts every cloche on the island himself, except the gilded ones. The Gallery's bread is entered at whatever weight the house declares. He writes that number down as if he had weighed it, and his hand does not shake. That is the hard part of the office.

*One way: the Black-Market Baker.* If silver comes to him by the crate, a loaf may leave his scale heavier than its flour. Something in the crumb that is not crumb goes out past the Wardens on a platter they will not lift, because he already has. The card does not show what it is. Cards never do.

*The other way: the Archivist.* He keeps the density book, and every swapped platter on the island is in it to the ounce. The book could be read aloud to a swapper, privately, the night before the Dawn Tribunal, at a price. A scale is a confession nobody has asked for yet.

*Laid for.* His apprentice, a boy with flour in his eyebrows. At the end of each night he sweeps the scale pan into his own palm and eats what he sweeps. He asked me whether that was stealing. I told him it was weighing.

*How it lies.* Upright, pointing at the crate. "Silver comes by the crate," I told him. He looked down at the crate I was reading on. It was salt cod. He laughed and paid.

**The Spice-Master**

*Face.* The Apothecary, the kettle in the window, a register line for every bottle. He coats the cloches with Bitter-Citron Oil. A hand that lifts a cloche not its own goes yellow to the wrist and stays yellow a week. The Wardens look at hands before faces.

*One way: the Toxicologist.* A jar with no label on a shelf where every other jar has one. When a house buys the Black-Crust contract, somebody has to glaze the crust. The Guild calls it Nightshade Glaze. That name is all the card says, and all I will.

*The other way: the Alchemical Purifier.* Draughts against a rot that is not yet in the Vault, for a crowd that is not yet a crowd. The card is painted with a cup held out to many hands. I do not know those hands yet.

*Laid for.* His apprentice. His palms are yellow from the cloches he oils, and sand, lye and prayer will not scrub them clean. The Wharf takes him for a thief. He showed me his hands before I asked for the copper, the way honest ones always do, and it cost him something.

*How it lies.* Either way up, the same hand pours.

**The Tide-Watcher**

*Face.* The lamp on the reef. Four times a night the office turns it landward and sweeps the town. It keeps the customs ledger of what comes in and the ferry tally of who goes out. It is the only office on the island that sees all of us and never sees the Hearth.

*One way: the Wreck-Looter.* Fog, and a manifest that could say anything, because in fog nobody can see the gangplank to argue. A barrel entered as salt that is not salt. A crate entered as lost that is not lost.

*The other way: the Harbor Master.* The gangplank held and dockhands deputised with a ribbon round the arm, on the day a Crown's man who is not yet on any ferry steps off one. The card shows a man in a coat too good for the crossing. I have never seen him. The card has.

*Laid for.* A dockhand who counts the beam's passes aloud, one to four, so he knows when to be indoors. On four he goes home whatever the cards say. He paid on three.

*How it lies.* Face down, and it would not stay down. "The light doesn't choose which," I told him. "The weather does." He said the weather belonged to the Council too. Then the fourth pass came across the crate, and he went.

**The Hearth-Warden**

*Face.* The Port Tavern. He carries the iron poker across his shoulders like a yoke and keeps stone pillar shackles. He keeps the tavern's order, and its rum cellar, and the rum cellar is a cellar.

He is the only master who came himself. He paid one copper, not double. He is Guild, and he wanted me to know he knew the price.

*One way: the Mutiny Boss.* Someone the Wardens want, kept under the floor among the casks, and the apprentices rising with the poker on their side for once. I did not read this road in the card. I read it in the faces queued behind him on the wharf, every one of them an apprentice, every one waiting to hear it.

*The other way: the Ironside.* The mounted harpoon gun over his bar unlocked and turned on the smugglers of this wharf. My wharf. The card shows a man on a stair, looking down into water.

*Laid for.* Himself. When I had read it, he took his copper back off the crate and put it in his apron. On the next pass of the beam he laid it down again in the same place and said nothing. I do not know what he decided in between.

*How it lies.* Crosswise, with neither road up. I told him so. He said a poker lies crosswise too, and that is the only way it gets heavy.

**The Ledger-Clerk**

*Face.* The tariff desk in the Counting-House. He takes the nightly yeast-tax in Guild Copper and Yeast Tokens and in no other coin, and he shoots the iron deadbolts.

*One way: the Customs Embezzler.* The Counting-House gold, taken in the noise of somebody else's robbery, and out on the Dawn Ferry before the noise stops. The card shows a door standing open, with a ferry small in one corner.

*The other way: the Council Regent.* Every bolt on the island shot at once, on his word, if a man who has not yet come survives a night that has not yet come. The card shows the same door, shut.

*Laid for.* His apprentice, a thin one who never stops counting. Without being asked, he sorted my coppers by their mint, counted them right, and pushed the pile back to my side. He will make a Ledger-Clerk.

*How it lies.* Upright, whichever way you turn it. Both roads go through a door he holds the bolt of.

After the fourth pass, someone in the queue asked me to lay a card for the empty platter nearest the Hearth. It is the one the Aboyeur sets at dusk, for a name under seal. I said a card is laid for a name. Under seal, there is no name to lay it for. She said that was why she had asked.

I put the Cloches back in the crate. The Hall's music stopped as I shut the lid, as if it had been waiting for me.

A ferry will come with news. Every year one does.

## II. The Lantern Pack

*HollowMere. Sunday, 24 December 1893. Christmas Eve, the first night with no train. Read at the back table of the Drowned Lantern. Pencil.*

I was at the end of the boards at Dusk. There was nothing to watch go. The last train went yesterday, full of people with cases. The storm has the line now, and nothing comes up it until the storm lets go.

I have no chalk. It has been behind Harold's step since the fourth, on a ticket, a penny lent against my reach. Without chalk there is no ring, and without the ring the dead keep their own counsel. They like it that way. I do not tell what they know in any case.

So tonight it is the living. Mrs Grieves keeps a pack behind the bar for the drinkers, dealt for rounds and fought over. One card is creased down the middle where a thumb went through it the night of the fight that got into the Gazette. She lent me the pack without asking what for, and entered nothing.

Cards are for the living. The dead have had their turn.

I lay five, for the five who keep things in this town: the bread, the post, the dead, the bar and the rent. A card has a face, and it falls one way or the other. Which way is a fortune. Nobody in this town needs telling that a fortune is not a promise. They are paid in scrip.

**The Baker**

*Face.* Crumb's Ovens. Up before anyone, flour to the elbows. One loaf to each mourner and three to the dead, each with a crumb token sealed in wax. Tomorrow's wake table is already set out front, ten plates in a storm.

*One way: the Fence.* If gold comes as a tip, her oven may give back bread heavier than the flour she put in, and it will go past the Constable on a mourner's plate. Gold has come. On the fourth of this month a coin was laid on her counter as a tip, by somebody who did not wait for change.

*The other way: the Ration Tyrant.* If the mill burns, or the last mile is fired, the last flour on the Grant is in one kitchen, and the town eats when the hand on the scraper says it may. The mill loft burned in November. Four sticks lie unfired under the last mile, and the storm has the rest of the line.

*Laid for.* Herself, though she would not sit. Tamsin Crumb came in at nine with loaves for Mrs Grieves's Christmas board and stood in her shawl while I laid it. She had already fed the crock tonight, on the island's hour. She said so the way another woman says she has wound the clock.

"Which way?"

"Both doors are open, Mrs Crumb."

She said that was true of every oven, and went home.

*How it lies.* It would not lie still. I laid it three times. The pack is old and the table is not level, and I do not think either of those is the reason.

**The Postmaster**

*Face.* The Post Office and Telegraph Room. A servant of the mails, under oath, with a kettle the Company charges to the mails.

*One way: the Extortionist.* A second book beside the Company's, kept where the steam cannot reach: who owes, who lies, who is leaving. Read to the right man the night before a vote.

*The other way: the Handler.* The black wax comes in the bag like the rest of the mail, and it comes for whoever is here. This road is a hand from the shadows for whoever opens it and accepts. Or it is the hand that keeps for itself what was meant for them.

*Laid for.* Himself. He sat across from me with his hands flat on the table and asked me to read the card to him aloud. He has read every letter in this town before the one it was meant for. I read it. He listened the way a child listens, looking up. Once he said "Hester", only that, and did not explain her.

*How it lies.* Pointing at him. He did not touch it. On his way out he said nobody had read anything to him since he was small. I did not ask him what it had been like.

**The Undertaker**

*Face.* He bags the dead and inventories their pockets for scrip. He enters the number before the name. That is the trade.

*One way: the Grave Robber.* A box lined so that what is laid in it stays laid, and a favour in return from whoever wants it kept that way. Robes go on walking that ought to have stopped.

*The other way: the Chief Coroner.* The cause in a fair hand with the hour beside it, for the Constable, so that somebody's alibi breaks on his table.

*Laid for.* Himself. He measured me by eye while I dealt, crown to boot, as he measures everyone, and I let him. He said I would take a narrow box. I said I had been told so. He said Maud's line in his book is still blank. He has not written a cause. He said it as if it were the card's business.

*How it lies.* Pointing away from both of us. "The first train through will decide nothing for you," I said. He said nothing ever decided anything for him. He only entered it.

**The Bartender**

*Face.* Bar, Mrs Grieves's hired man. He pours stout that changes a man's mood, puts the shutters up for curfew, and listens.

*One way: the Union Boss.* The cellar opened under the boards for someone this town spared and never named, and a brawl staged at the right hour so that the law cannot get in the door.

*The other way: the Vigilante.* The sawed-off from under the counter, if the robberies turn to blood, and justice taken by the hand that pours.

*Laid for.* Himself. I laid the card twice, because he asked me to lay it again. Mrs Grieves watched from the tap. He keeps a tooth in his waistcoat, a man's tooth, from October. He turned it over in his fingers while I dealt and put it away when I looked. The drowned barman's Liquid Courage is still on the slate above him, unpaid.

*How it lies.* Once each way. He said that was about right.

**The Company Clerk**

*Face.* The rent ledger, the key in the Telegraph Room, every bed slept in and every bed that should have been. Nobody likes him, and he does not ask them to.

I laid this card unasked. At ten he stood in the Lantern door with his hat on and snow on the hat, and did not come in. Rent falls on a Monday, Christmas or not.

*One way: the Embezzler.* Now that the vault has been opened, a careful man's books could be made to say what he needs, and a case could go down on the morning train. The next morning train is Friday.

*The other way: the Heir.* A man to be guarded, guards, and the whole town locked down by one hand. The card is a king. But the man to be guarded is not on it. The guards are. Seven of them.

*Laid for.* The hat in the door. Before he turned to go he said one thing, not to me: "Miss Cotter paid the wick." I do not know her. I wrote it down because he said it as if somebody ought to.

*How it lies.* Pointing at the door, where he was standing. "Either road wants your signature," I said. He touched his hat and went out into the snow.

Mrs Grieves asked whether I would lay one for Jory. I squared the pack and laid none. He would only ask me whether the train has come, and tonight I could not tell him it has.

The pack goes back behind the bar, to be dealt for rounds again.

## III. Fifty-One

`GROUNDS 99 — YARD`
`FORKS — SUPPLEMENTARY`
`NOT WITH THIS REGISTER. NOT FOR THE TRIBUNAL.`
*Wed. 17 Dec. 1952. Tally office, at the stove, 23:05 to roll. Entered by: Slot 0. Not counter-initialled.*

The Tribunal sits Saturday, first whistle. The register is complete except for five lines. Under each trusty's billet the form prints *Forks*, an A and a B, and *Marked: ______*. The form does not say who marks it, or by what. I have kept the lines blank since October, waiting to be told. Nobody has told me.

The Powder-Monkey ends his count of caps on a word that is not a number. I sent to the Workings for their No. 7, the interpreter at intake, to put the word into the register. He came with a deck of cards in his shirt pocket. It was Fitch's. Fitch was the Workings' No. 2, went down on 5 Dec, and is on no board now. Fifty-one cards. No queen of hearts. Fitch played solitaire with it every night and said he would win without her.

No. 7 said he could not give me the word, but he could give me the forks. Entered as offered.

His rule. Red for A, black for B. The colour says which road is nearer tonight, not which road is walked. A card is not a finding.

Entered as said.

**Slot 1 — Powder-Monkey**
*G99-2290. Oak.*

*Face.* Issues caps and powder from the niche in the Workings. Each night he sets the dye-cap on this rack's latch, and the next hand to lift the latch goes red.

*One way (A): Demolitionist.* A cart on the coal track that should not be pushed, for whoever is breaking this camp from inside. The niche count has been short since Tue. 11 Nov. The card shows no more than that, and neither does this sheet.

*The other way (B): Ordnance Master.* Charges found and made dead. Flash-caps for the Warden that blind and do not kill.

*Laid for.* Him, in his absence; he was at the niche. While he shuffled, No. 7 said the word under his breath, once, and did not say it again. I asked what it meant. He said it is what you say at the end of a count so that the count is finished and has not just stopped. He said English has no column for it either.

*Drawn.* Nine of clubs. Black. B nearer.

**Slot 2 — Scaler**
*G99-0384. Pine.*

*Face.* Measures standing timber on Widow-Maker Ridge. Watches the camp through the surveyor's scope and reports any long-gun, any carried load, anything dragged across open ground.

*One way (A): Poacher.* The ridge has places the scope does not see, because the Scaler knows where he does not point it. Goods off camp, south, along a bed that is not on the map.

*The other way (B): Ridge Tracker.* Blazes in the pines marking where the pack runs, so that the Warden can hunt it.

*Laid for.* Him. He was in the office with his tally and sat for it. He draws each tree in the margin before it is cut, and he was drawing one while No. 7 shuffled. On 1 Dec he was kept indoors by order. He did not say what he heard that night, and I did not ask. It is not a column.

*Drawn.* Three of diamonds. Red. A nearer. He finished the tree.

**Slot 3 — Boilerman**
*G99-1702. Fir.*

*Face.* Keeps the steam for the saws, the lift and the heat. From the boiler house he can vent any building, or close it.

*One way (A): Saboteur's Stoker.* A main opened at the right minute, a corridor gone white so that nobody can see who walks through it, a dark that lets someone pass.

*The other way (B): Iron Master.* Steam cut to the lift and the Foundry doors sealed on the alert. Nobody passes.

*Laid for.* Him, not present; he was on the main. No. 7 asked me what he does with his hands. I said he lays two fingers on a valve before he opens it, the way you would on a wrist, and taps twice. No. 7 laid two fingers on the card before he turned it. The Boilerman says one line comes back to him rimed with frost on the inside, a line he did not lay. I enter it again here because nobody has answered it.

*Drawn.* Jack of diamonds. Red. A nearer. No. 7: "Nearer is two nights or two years. The card has no calendar."

**Slot 4 — Company Doctor**
*G99-0051. Fir.*

*Face.* Lung-Rot, and the oxygen tonic, issued against the program's measurement and not against the cough. Weekly: weight, chest, how long a breath holds. On an infirmary card I have seen, in his hand: ribs standing through a shirt like the slats of this rack. Knuckles gone tight and grey, then the face. A breath counted and counted until the count was all there was of it, and the man breathing a little after the count stopped, as if no one had told him.

*One way (A): Back-Alley Sawbones.* Lime, and a collar that walks at the gate after the man inside it has gone. No. 7 says a card can show a road already walked, and then it is not a fortune but a footprint. The infirmary requisition for the first week of November is one sack of lime more than the infirmary uses. Not explained. Not asked.

*The other way (B): Chief Surgeon.* Autopsies for Slot 5, cause and time in a fair hand, and men brought back off the table who were going.

*Laid for.* Him. He came in at 23:30 for the stove and stood at it. He keeps one autopsy in his own coat. He knows each patient by the cough before he sees the number. One of them hummed four notes until the breath went, and he asked whether the cards had those four notes in them. Dr Ambry's case is sealed and is not with this sheet either.

*Drawn.* Six of spades. Black. B nearer. He said, "Nearer than what," and went back to the infirmary.

**Slot 0 — Head Tallyman**
*G99-0117. Pine.*

*Face.* Keeps the ten logs and this register, and holds the only key to the scale's weights. Runs the grain-density checks. Says whether a billet was carved, counterfeited or swapped.

*One way (A): Counterfeiter.* An agency man's money left where it can be found, and a brand on a blank billet that the Forge never burned. Tue. 9 Dec.: forty scrip found on the store counter, unclaimed, and entered as lost property. Not since claimed.

*The other way (B): Quota Boss.* The yard has been eleven cord short since November, and the quota is mine. Read out at the Sawmill Tribunal, a shortfall can be laid on whichever billet I choose.

*Laid for.* Myself, last. I weigh my own billet last, with my eyes shut, and I shut them for this. No. 7 turned the deck over one card at a time, all fifty-one, face up on the table. Then he squared it. He said the colour that would have told me belongs to the card that is not there. I asked what that meant. He said Fitch would have told me I could win without her.

*Drawn.* None.

---

Forks, slots 0 to 4. *Marked:* ______. Not marked. Returned to the register as they were.

23:59:45. Whistle. No. 7 put the deck in his shirt pocket and went for his bunk with fifteen seconds. His rack is not mine.

The card on your table at dawn is not who you are. It is only which way you were facing.
