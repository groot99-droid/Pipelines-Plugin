# The Tally Rack

`▇▇▇ / ▇▇▇ / ▇▇▇▇▇▇▇▇ — ANOMALY UNKNOWN`
`GROUNDS 99 — YARD`
`DECLASSIFIED IN PART`

**TALLY RACK ROLL AND GRAIN-DENSITY REGISTER**
*For the Dawn Tribunal, Sawmill platform, Saturday 20 December 1952, first whistle.*
*Entered by: Slot 0. Counter-initialled: Slot 5.*

**PRODUCTION IS OBLIGATION.**

> *Order of the Circuit Judge, Fri. 19 Dec., 23:40, steam restored. Following the interruption of steam this night, and one person of grade not accounted for, all ten billets of the yard rack to be weighed before the Tribunal sits.*

---

## Standing Order on the Rack

1. Every person on Grounds 99 has a billet. Grade does not exempt. The program took the slogan with the site, and the rack with the slogan.
2. This is the yard rack. It holds the camp's ten offices and trades. Each work crew keeps its own rack, numbered 0 to 9: the Workings, the sawmill, the bunkhouse. A number means nothing without its rack.
3. The billet carries the number, the trade, the debts and the sins. You are told once, at the intake office: *you're either feeding the furnace or you're fuel.* The billet says which.
4. Woods by work. Pine for light work: the tally, the ridge, the office. Fir for fire work: the boilers, the infirmary, the kilns. Oak for heavy work, and for the law that goes under ground.
5. Nominal weights: pine 4 lb 2 oz, fir 5 lb 10 oz, oak 7 lb 8 oz. Tolerance two ounces either way. A billet out of tolerance is a billet in the wrong slot.
6. Brands are burned at the Forge, with irons from Ada Kerrow and charcoal from the Collier's kilns. Neither is on this rack. Neither is asked.
7. At the Midnight Roll Call you have fifteen seconds to stand by your bunk. Then come the searchlights, the masks and the dogs. A billet without a body is entered *absent*. A body without a billet is entered *surplus*.
8. The log on your rack at dawn is who you are.

## The Ten Billets

**Slot 0 — Head Tallyman**
*G99-0117. Pine.*

*Classification.* Enemy of the state. Trusty.
*Brand.* TALLY · 0117 · G99.
*Trade.* Keeps the rack and this register, and holds the only key to the scale's weights. Weighs any billet at night and says whether it was carved, counterfeited or swapped.
*Debts.* The yard's quota, which is his. Eleven cord short in November. Carried.
*Sins.* Kept the count.
*Remarks.* I weigh my own billet last, with my eyes shut. I know what it weighs. Tue. 9 Dec.: forty scrip found on the store counter, unclaimed, and entered as lost property. Not since claimed.
*Weighed.* 4 lb 2 oz.

**Forks.** A — Counterfeiter / B — Quota Boss. Marked: ______

**Slot 1 — Powder-Monkey**
*G99-2290. Oak.*

*Classification.* Prisoner of war.
*Brand.* POWDER · 2290.
*Trade.* Issues caps and powder from the niche in the Workings. Each night he sets the dye-cap on this rack's latch. The next hand to lift the latch goes red and stays red.
*Debts.* The niche count is short ▇▇ sticks and ▇▇ caps, carried since Tue. 11 Nov.
*Sins.* Answers for the count.
*Remarks.* He counts the caps aloud before he signs, in a language this register has no column for. He ends the count the same way every time, on a word that is not a number. Latch cap fired on the night of 19 Dec. Hands at roll were not inspected, because there was no light.
*Weighed.* 7 lb 8 oz.

**Forks.** A — Demolitionist / B — Ordnance Master. Marked: ______

**Slot 2 — Scaler**
*G99-0384. Pine.*

*Classification.* Enemy of the state.
*Brand.* SCALE · 0384.
*Trade.* Measures standing timber on Widow-Maker Ridge. Watches the camp through the surveyor's scope, and reports any long-gun, any carried load, anything dragged across open ground.
*Debts.* One scope lens, cracked. Charged.
*Sins.* Reports what he sees. For 19 Dec.: saw nothing. For 1 Dec.: kept indoors by order.
*Remarks.* He draws each tree in the margin of his tally before it is cut. He has asked about the old bed by the broken trestle: the sleepers rotted out, the ballast still level, running south off the camp map. He was told it is not on the map. Entered as asked and answered.
*Weighed.* 4 lb 2 oz.

**Forks.** A — Poacher / B — Ridge Tracker. Marked: ______

**Slot 3 — Boilerman**
*G99-1702. Fir.*

*Classification.* Prisoner of war.
*Brand.* BOILER · 1702.
*Trade.* Keeps the steam that runs the saws, the lift and the heat. From the boiler house he can vent any building or close it.
*Debts.* The boiler main, Tue. 11 Nov. Repairs charged to his billet, not his crew's.
*Sins.* The pressure log for 19 Dec. is not with this register.
*Remarks.* He lays two fingers on a valve before he opens it, the way you would on a wrist. Then he taps twice. He says the camp loses heat somewhere under it, more every week, and he cannot find where. He says one line comes back to him rimed with frost on the inside. He did not lay that line. Entered as said.
*Weighed.* 5 lb 10 oz.

**Forks.** A — Saboteur's Stoker / B — Iron Master. Marked: ______

**Slot 4 — Company Doctor**
*G99-0051. Fir.*

*Classification.* Other.
*Brand.* INFIRMARY · 0051.
*Trade.* Treats Lung-Rot with the oxygen tonic and does the autopsies: cause, and time. The program also measures the Lung-Rot. Tonic is issued against the measurement, not against the cough.
*Measured.* Weekly, for the program: weight, chest, how long a breath holds. Entered beside what the measuring leaves:
- ribs standing out like the slats of this rack;
- skin gone tight and grey across the knuckles, then across the face;
- fingers black from a cold the infirmary stove does not reach;
- three of those fingers on the infirmary table, weighed like billets and entered;
- a lung on the same table, black right through, entered by the ounce;
- a man who coughed until there was nothing left to cough, and went on.
*Debts.* Lime, infirmary requisition: one sack more than the infirmary uses, first week of November. Not explained. Not asked.
*Sins.* Fourteen certificates since October signed *exposure*. Exposure covers what the camp needs it to.
*Remarks.* He keeps one autopsy out of the file, in his own coat. He knows each patient by the cough before he sees the number. One of them hummed the same four notes until the measuring took the breath for it. Since Fri. 12 Dec. no findings are referred upward. Dr Ambry's case is sealed and is not with this register. He was not told why, and he says so, to the rack. Two clean autopsies and the officer may deputise him. He has had two. He has not been deputised.
*Weighed.* 5 lb 10 oz.

**Forks.** A — Back-Alley Sawbones / B — Chief Surgeon. Marked: ______

*Trade.* Soot-dust kit and ledger magnifier. Lifts coal-dust prints, wood chip, and three traits from a scene.
*Case File.* ▇▇▇▇▇▇ — ~~verified~~ / ~~not verified~~.

**Slot 6 — Mine Warden**
*G99-0006. Oak.*

*Classification.* Staff.
*Brand.* WARDEN · 0006.
*Trade.* Restraints, the coal-slug riot gun and the brass horn. Locks down the adits and the mill.
*Debts.* None in scrip.
*Sins.* Carries the fifteen seconds.

**Slot 7 — Circuit Judge**
*G99-0007. Pine.*

*Classification.* Staff, flown in.
*Brand.* JUDGE · 0007, three times: Mon. 17 Nov., Mon. 8 Dec., Thu. 18 Dec. The first two are struck through with the edge of the iron. He is flown in for each tribunal and re-branded on each arrival. The rack does not remember him between sittings. It is made to.
*Trade.* Sits at dawn on the Sawmill platform. Lays out the Case File, orders the ten weighed, and gives one of three verdicts: Acquittal, Scrip Forfeiture, the Blade. *The Blade* is the camp's word. He uses it too.
*Record.* 17 Nov.: Scrip Forfeiture. 8 Dec.: the Blade (sawmill rack, No. 6).
*Remarks.* He will not touch the rack. He points. He arrived Thu. 18 Dec. with his own weights for the scale. They were checked against mine and agree.
*Weighed.* 4 lb 2 oz.

**Slot 8 — Felling Hand**
*G99-3318. Oak.*

*Classification.* Prisoner of war. War: not specified.
*Brand.* FELL · 3318.
*Trade.* Fells and bucks on the skid road, and hauls what he fells.
*Debts.* Quota short four days in five. Tonic owed.
*Sins.* A slip, folded small, found in his lunchbox at roll. It is an enlistment. Department: ▇▇▇▇▇▇▇▇▇. Addressee: illegible. Terms: *success to the agency of this camp.* Retained. He says it was not his tin. Tins are not numbered.
*Remarks.* He saves the crust of his ration. It is not found on him at roll and not found in his bunk. His billet is old oak from stock, re-planed. On the underside, where the plane did not reach, an older brand shows: **A.K.** The yard reads it as the smith's, Kerrow's, from when she proved her irons. She has not been asked. Nobody has asked the wood.
*Weighed.* 7 lb 8 oz.

**Forks.** None listed for his trade.

**Slot 9 — Kiln Hand**
*G99-4471. Fir.*

*Classification.* Enemy of the state.
*Brand.* KILN · 4471.
*Trade.* Tends the clamps under the Collier and carries charcoal to the Forge.
*Debts.* Arrived owing.
*Sins.* His classification.
*Remarks.* He hums at the shift whistle. He is not alone in it: he keeps time with others who do not bunk near him. Entered: under observation; to the mess hall after roll.
*Weighed.* 5 lb 10 oz.

**Forks.** None listed for his trade. *Fuel.*

The difference is oak less pine. It is not fir less pine, which is 1 lb 8 oz. It is not oak less fir, which is 1 lb 14 oz.

So an oak billet stands in a pine slot. The pine slots are 0, 2, 5 and 7.

Every figure above is in this hand. Every figure above is exact.
