# The Density Audit

`HALL OF PLATTERS — ARCHIVE OF THE MASTER PROOF-READER`

**REGISTER OF THE DENSITY AUDIT**
*Night of Tuesday the 8th of September 1789, from the dusk loaf to the sixth bell of Wednesday the 9th.*
*Kept by the Master Proof-Reader under the standing order of the High Chancellor. Countersigned by the Chancellor's clerk, by seal.*

**BY DECREE OF THE SOVEREIGN SOURDOUGH COUNCIL.**

> *Standing order of the High Chancellor, given at the command desk, for the quarter of Saint-Michel. The Hall of Platters to be weighed entire, as at every quarter, and this register laid on the trial table before the Dawn Tribunal sits.*

---

## The Standing Rule of the Hall

1. Every soul on the rock has a platter. The long racks behind the Hearth hold the town's, and the journeymen weigh them. This rack holds ten, the platters the Council audits by office. An Unregistered platter is moved to the tenth place of this rack, where the Council can see it.
2. Loaves go by rank, and each rank has its weight. The Gallery's loaf is gold leaf, 4 livres. The Guild's is flour-white, 2 livres. The Commons' is red crust, 1 livre. An Unregistered platter gets the dish pit's issue loaf, 10 onces, with the seal struck. The tolerance is one gros either way, and a loaf outside it has been touched.
3. The bread of the Gallery is not handled by trade. Its weight is entered as the house declares it. The Proof-Reader lifts no gilded cloche.
4. The Abbey's bread is not on this rack. It sits at the Vault altar under burlap and is weighed by God.
5. At night the cloches are lifted by the Proof-Reader alone. At dawn each owner lifts his own. An Unregistered loaf is cracked by the Bailiff, because an Unregistered hand may not touch silver.
6. The platter nearest the Hearth carries no number and does not stand on the rack. It is not weighed and it is not entered.
7. As the Council painted it over the door: *the loaf sitting on your silver plate at dawn is who you are.*

---

## Dusk

The Morning Ferry had cast off at dawn. Nothing was signalled from the mainland. The fog took the harbour lamps before six.

At six the Aboyeur carried a loaf down the Hall. He called no name. He set it on the platter nearest the Hearth, for a name under seal, and the night was reckoned from it.

The Ledger-Clerk closed the tariff desk at the half hour. Nine platters of this rack paid or remitted. Platter 9 was Unregistered, carried three weeks.

A complaint came down from the gallery at seven, and I wrote it as received. Crusts had gone from the gilded platters. *The Unregistered steal.* On that complaint the Spice-Master coated cloche 9 with Bitter-Citron Oil. It goes on clear and shows nothing until a hand lifts the cloche. Then the oil is off the silver and on the hand, yellow, until morning.

At the midnight proofing bell the Iron-Wardens locked the Hall from outside.

---

## The Ten Platters

**Platter 0 — Le Levain, of the house of Vane, the eldest line**

*Colour.* Gold leaf.
*Seal.* A hand above a crock.
*Tariff.* Remitted, by courtesy.
*Loaf.* 4 livres, nominal.
*Declared.* 4 livres.
*Note.* The feeding hand. It is the only hand the Mother knows, and in the Hall it is always gloved, by right. I have kept this register eleven years and have never seen it bare. The left glove is worn thinner than the right. It is the left that feeds.

**Platter 1 — High Chancellor**

*Colour.* Gold leaf.
*Seal.* The Mother in her glass.
*Tariff.* Remitted, by courtesy.
*Loaf.* 4 livres, nominal.
*Declared.* 4 livres.
*Note.* He has never sat a Tribunal hungry. He keeps the card of what he ate in his sleeve until the verdict is entered, and then he gives it to his clerk to file, so that the archive holds his suppers beside his sentences.

**Ladder.** The last rung. He presides at the Dawn Tribunal, orders the cloches lifted, and gives one of three verdicts: Acquittal, a Guild Fine, or Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.

**Platter 2 — Master Proof-Reader**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A balance.
*Tariff.* Paid, in copper.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres, exact.
*Note.* This hand. I weigh the issue loaf of platter 9 on the same pan the Chancellor's loaf would go on, if it were weighed. The ordinance does not say I may not. Tampered loaves audited this season, before tonight: two.

At the end of August a silver piece was left on the lid of the customs crate at the East Dock, on the night the crate was opened. My office was scratched into it with a knife point. I took it to the Ledger-Clerk. She has no column for silver left to an office by no one. It is in my apron.

**Evolutions.** A — The Black-Market Baker / B — The Archivist. Neither entered.

**Platter 3 — Spice-Master**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A citron.
*Tariff.* Paid in yeast tokens at eleven at night, after the desk had closed. Entered by courtesy. The Commons do not get the courtesy.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* She tastes nothing she sells, and she says that is the whole of her art. She oiled cloche 9 on the gallery's word and asked to have the word written down. It is written above, at seven.

**Evolutions.** A — The Toxicologist / B — The Alchemical Purifier. Neither entered.

**Platter 4 — Tide-Watcher**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A lamp.
*Tariff.* Paid.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* Nothing signalled. The sweep went out at nine and at eleven and showed him fog, he says, the way a blind man is shown a wall. In the mornings he counts the Wharf lamps back aloud, one by one, as the fog gives them up. He has never once said a lamp was missing, and he counts as if one might be.

**Evolutions.** A — The Wreck-Looter / B — The Harbor Master. Neither entered.

**Platter 5 — Hearth-Warden**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A poker crossed with a key.
*Tariff.* Paid.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* Behind the Tavern bar he keeps a boy's shoe. An apprentice left it there and never came back for it, and the Warden does not say where the boy went. Tonight two apprentices were shackled to the stone pillar until the morning bell, for singing. He did not write down what they sang. I asked him, and he said it was the Wharf's song and that it had a new verse.

**Evolutions.** A — The Mutiny Boss / B — The Ironside. Neither entered.

**Platter 6 — Ledger-Clerk**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* An open book.
*Tariff.* Paid, and the receipt made out to herself.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* She blows on every line she writes, even a line already dry. Every fifth bell she opens the seal drawer of the Counting-House and counts what is in it. At the fifth bell tonight she came to the Hall and reported **the Counting-House seal missing from its drawer.** One seal, brass, 4 onces. She reported one.

**Evolutions.** A — The Customs Embezzler / B — The Council Regent. Neither entered.

**Platter 7 — Inquisitor-in-Training**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A lens.
*Tariff.* Paid.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* He is nineteen and holds his breath when he dusts, so as not to blow the evidence away. With the magnesium powder and the Ledger Lens he lifted one print of flour dust from the floor beside the rack. **The print has a heel.** The trades do not wear heels. He drew no conclusion and I enter none.

**Ladder.** His dossier is unverified, so it does not go up to the Bailiff.

**Platter 8 — Guild Bailiff**

*Colour.* Flour-white.
*Seal.* A shackle.
*Tariff.* Paid.
*Loaf.* 2 livres, nominal.
*Weighed.* 2 livres.
*Note.* He carries the iron staff and the brass shackles. He has never closed a shackle on a gloved wrist, and he says his warrant does not reach that far. I have read his warrant. It does not say.

**Ladder.** When he cuffs the prime suspect and brings him to Gallows Square, he goes up to the Chancellor's rung.

**Platter 9 — Plongeur, Commons**

*Colour.* Red crust.
*Seal.* Struck. **UNREGISTERED**, carried three weeks.
*Tariff.* Unpaid.
*Loaf.* The issue loaf, 10 onces, nominal.
*Weighed.* **14 onces.** *Tampered. Foreign object, about 4 onces.*
*Note.* He hauls on the Wharf by day. At night he works the dish pit, kneeling where the old seal of Le Champignon is ground into the floor. Since his own seal was struck he has pressed his thumb into every loaf he is issued, where the seal used to be. The loaf on platter 9 at the second bell bore no thumb.

Each quarter he asks me what his loaf weighed. I tell him, and he says it is more than he thought it would be. He keeps a ferry token he has never spent.

His name, which the struck seal does not carry: Yann Le Goff, called Petit-Yann.

Cloche 9 was dry of oil when I lifted it at the second bell. A hand had lifted it before mine. My hands were clean, and the Inquisitor-in-Training has entered that he saw them.

**Evolutions.** None listed for his trade.

## The Balance

At the fourth bell the whole rack went onto the Great Balance of the Hall against the Council's weights, tared.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sum of entries, platters 0–9, declared and weighed | 22 livres 14 onces |
| The rack on the Great Balance, fourth bell | 23 livres 2 onces |
| Difference | 4 onces |

Platter 9 is 4 onces over its nominal, and those 4 onces are already in the entries. The Balance finds 4 onces more.

The Ledger-Clerk reported one seal missing, at the fifth bell. A seal weighs 4 onces. The rack carries the weight of two.

The eight loaves I weighed are within their gros, all but platter 9, which is entered. The two I may not lift are entered as declared.

After the fifth bell I entered a request that the declared bread be weighed. It went up to the gallery and came back down before the sixth, under seal:

> *Ruling of the High Chancellor, from the gallery. The bread of a house is the word of that house, and its weight is its word. The Council does not weigh a word. Whoever asks to lift a gilded cloche asks to weigh the house beneath it, and his asking is entered so that it may be remembered. Refused.*

It is entered.

---

## The Cracking of the Crust

The Great Proofing Bell struck six, and the Hall came in to the Hearth.

Each owner lifted his own cloche. The Gallery's were lifted with gloved hands, and the loaves were cracked *by the house, and found sound by its own report.* The Guild's were cracked at the trial table, before the Chancellor's clerk. All seals were their own.

Platter 9 was cracked by the Bailiff. In the crumb lay the seal of the Counting-House. The Ledger-Clerk claimed it, wrote the receipt, and blew on it.

Hands were shown palms up at the Hearth. The Guild's were clean. The Commons' were clean, platter 9's among them. The Gallery was gloved, as is its right, and was not shown.

The Inquisitor-in-Training laid the print with the heel on the trial table. The Chancellor looked at it and asked whose heel. No one answered, and it was not asked a second time.

The verdict of the High Chancellor, as his clerk took it down:

> *The oil proves a lifting. It does not prove a lifter. A seal was found in a loaf, and a loaf is who you are. A Guild Fine of four onces of Guild Copper, being the weight of the seal, payable at the Counting-House by the noon bell, so that the Hall may be swept before the Gallery sups at nine. Unpaid at noon: Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.*

The Bailiff cuffed platter 9 and took him out through the south doors to the Square. He went up a rung for it.

The Aboyeur took the dusk loaf from the platter nearest the Hearth, as at every dawn. It is not eaten.

Fine: unpaid at the time of this entry, eleven in the morning.

Platter 9, weighed again empty of the seal: 10 onces. Nominal.

The entries again, with platter 9 at its 10 onces: 22 livres 10 onces. The rack on the Great Balance, weighed again with the seal taken out: 22 livres 14 onces. It is 4 onces over. No one has reported anything missing that weighs 4 onces.
