# Four Notices

*The board in the square, the Constable's corner. Four notices, as found, December 1893.*

---

## The Board

A notice posted on the board in the square is lawful notice to every person within the Grant. The Articles say so. They also say it holds whether the notice is read or not, and whether the person can read or not.

The board stands by the pump. Most of it belongs to the Gazette and to the ledger slips nobody admits to writing. The Constable keeps the left-hand corner. He pastes his notices there at the Station Rush, face out, with four tacks to a sheet and flour paste from Crumb's Ovens.

By the next Rush a notice has often been turned. Someone has taken it down, written on the back and pasted it up again face-in. What the square reads over its morning water is then a hand, not the Company's print. Nobody is seen doing it. Paper is short in a Panic year, and the back of a notice is a page. The Constable turns them out again, and by the next Rush they are turned.

Four hung in December. Each is for a killing, or for what a killing was for. The Company names the deed and prices it in scrip, at three to five to the dollar and falling. Then it leaves the face blank, as the Gazette does, for the Town to fill in.

The Company prints the deed. The town writes the rest. Nobody writes the name.

## I. THE ZEALOT — Conversion

### Front

**WANTED**

**THE ZEALOT**

*Date: torn away below the fold. The tacks are still in it.*

For marking five buildings within the Grant in ash and in blood: the door of St Brannock's, the grille of the Company Bank, Crumb's Ovens, the Mill, and the Station.

For desecrating St Brannock's, contrary to Article VIII.

The Company does not allege that the Vicar is dead. The Vicar preaches at dusk.

The Company's three particulars:

- **Height:** as the Vicar's.
- **Scarf:** none. A stole.
- **Hat:** none recovered.

**Trace:** candlewax, on the sill of every building marked, and on the Vicar's cuff.

**Persons sought in association, known as the Dark Positions:**

- **The Minion.** Keeps a body from being found. The Company is advised that a power is kept for as long as a body goes unfound. Bone chimes have been reported after curfew in Pump Row and at the Fenwood edge.
- **The Mob Boss.** Raises mobs against the Vicar and against every soul the Vicar has brought to faith. Shakedowns have been reported. No complainant will give a name.
- **The Cultist.** Sets the altars and recruits. Those recruited carry the faith from door to door, without the Company's licence.
- **The High Priest.** NOT SOUGHT. The Company is advised that if the Zealot is taken, the High Priest finishes it.

If six gather at an unlicensed altar, in the Flooded Gallery or in the desecrated chapel, the Town will be told that the sky has turned and the Bell tolls backward. The Company does not recognise a crimson sky. Under Article VIII, the Bell is rung in the ordinary order only.

The Company takes no side between faiths. It will treat with whichever holds the town.

**Reward:** 200 scrip.

**Name:** ____________. Unverified. The Town may supply it.

**[ CONSTABLE — HOLLOWMERE ]**

### Back

*Pencil, in an upright hand that presses hard. There are wax spots on the paper.*

I keep the candles at St Brannock's. I kept them for Father Bray and I keep them for the new one. I trim the wicks, I scrape the drips off the brass, and I save the ends in a box by the font. On Saturdays I melt the ends down at my own table, and the Row has light it did not pay the Company for. That is the only theft I have ever done, and I confessed it.

I went to him in October, the week he came. He asked my name and used it. Nobody had used it in a year. I told him three things. I will not write them here, because they were his.

Now I count.

One. The wax on the sills is ours. I know a chapel drip from a tallow dip with my eyes shut. My box is lighter than I left it.

Two. At dusk he said my name the way the Company says it, as if he were reading it off a sheet.

Three. At the door I spoke to him about the second of my three things, and he did not know it.

Four. Men came to my door on Thursday at curfew. They did not come to hurt me. They came to tell me which way the town was going, and that a woman who lives alone with a box of candles should go the same way. I have opened the door to nobody since, except to him.

They say he said this, at the end:

"In the end I sought nothing else but to serve God. And serve him I did. With this death's last breath I pray this prayer: may the Almighty grant me the strength of ten in the next life. And in return, tenfold my own."

I did not hear it. I was not there, and nobody I know was there. The words go round the Row like a loaf passed at a wake, and every hand that passes them on is sure.

So I have worked it out at my table, the way the Detective works things. If he preaches, he is not dead. If he is dead, the one who preaches is not him. And if he is dead, the town says he will come back ten times himself: a field of lavender, one orange tree, a blue sky over them, an iron crosier, a light standing up out of the ground like a pillar.

I have seen none of it. I keep the candles.

I have not gone the way the town is going. I am still his. I no longer know which of them that makes me.

*Tag: a body unfound is a sermon kept.*

## II. THE ACTIVIST — Sabotage

### Front

**WANTED**

**THE ACTIVIST**

Saturday, 16 December 1893. *Reissued. The notice of 16 November is withdrawn.*

**For the assassination of the President of the Company, Mr C. Thorne, on the platform, on Thursday 14 December.**

For Fire in the Mill: the loft, on Tuesday 14 November. The Indebted were turned out. The Company does not enter the Indebted by name.

For Dynamite on the Tracks: four sticks, unfired, under the last mile below Gallows Wood Siding.

For conspiracy against the Company Bank and its vault.

For Public Unrest under Article X: the soapbox at the Bell, held beyond the Shift.

The Company's three particulars:

- **Height:** middling.
- **Scarf:** red, as half the railyard wore on 14 November.
- **Hat:** a man's, felt, found at the soapbox. She did not come back for it.

**Trace:** black powder. Powder is delivered daily to the railyard, by the Train.

**NOTICE OF REDEMPTION.** If she is taken, or gives herself up, the Town will vote at the Bell on her Redemption. If she survives the vote, she will be restored to the rolls as Townsfolk under Article X. Her speech will be struck from the record. Her account will not.

**Reward:** 500 scrip. Payable at the Company Bank, at the Midday Rent, by order of the Heir to the Baron.

**Name:** ____________. Unverified. The Town may supply it.

**[ CONSTABLE — HOLLOWMERE ]** *Countersigned below the stamp, in a narrow hand.*

### Back

*A railyard pencil stub. The hand is unsteady on the figures.*

I sleep in the railyard bunks at ninety cents a bed, and I am two weeks behind. Every other week I send a postal order east to a girl who thinks I cook for a hotel. I read the reward three times before I understood it. Then I worked out how many of my weeks it was, which is the only way I know how to read a number. It is a lot of weeks.

I was in the square on the fourteenth of November. Everybody was. I stood near enough to the soapbox to have seen her face, and I didn't look at it. I looked at her hands, because they were black at the nails with powder, the same as mine and the same as everyone's from the yard. Powder comes up on the Train every morning, and we load it with our hands. If black under the nails hangs a woman, it will hang the whole railyard.

I was on the platform on the fourteenth of December. I heard it and I did not see it, because I was looking at the train, the way everybody looks at the train. Then there was a hat on the boards, and the Pinkertons had their dogs out, and nobody in the town was looking at anybody.

Now there is to be a vote.

They say it is about her. It isn't. The Bell will be asking whether we forgive the hand that loosened the grip, and so whether we forgive ourselves for cheering when it did. That is what the vote is for.

Only the grip did not loosen. Look at the foot of the front. The countersign is not the Constable's, and it is not Thorne's, which I never saw. It is a hand I have seen before, and I cannot think where. I don't like that I can't. The reward is paid at the Midday Rent.

Thorne came by train and left by train. Whoever signs now doesn't need the train. He is already here.

They say Thorne had no sons. They say it the way people say a thing they have been told to say.

I know how I will vote. I am not writing it down.

*Tag: the grip loosened, and another hand closed.*

## III. THE BANDIT — Heist

### Front

**WANTED**

**THE BANDIT**

Monday, 11 December 1893. *Reissued from the Gazette of Thursday 7 December, with additions.*

**Scores:**

1. **Crumb's Ovens.**
   - *First.* The week of 16 October, Wednesday or Thursday night; reported 5 December. The till was emptied. A gold coin was left on the hearth-lip. A bootprint in the flour dust, long in the stride, the toe toward the back door.
   - *Second.* Monday night, 4 December. The till was emptied. One gold coin was left on the counter, as a tip.
2. **The plate of St Brannock's.** Sunday 10 December, after the dusk sermon. A brass casing, .45, spent, was found standing upright where the collection had been. No shot was heard.
3. **The Company Bank, the vault.** Not yet.
4. **The Daily Train, the express safe.** Not yet.

The Company's three particulars:

- **Height:** short, by the bootprint in the spilt flour.
- **Scarf:** grey wool. Thread found caught on the till.
- **Hat:** none recovered.

By report: a bandana, and a six-shooter.

**Traces:** flour dust; a brass casing (.45).

Gold tendered within the Grant is scrip until proven otherwise.

**Reward:** 300 scrip.

**Name:** ____________. Unverified. The Town may supply it.

**[ CONSTABLE — HOLLOWMERE ]**

### Back

*Pencil. Flour in the creases of the sheet. Tamsin Crumb's hand.*

I am the robbed, so they say I may write.

There are three gold coins behind the crock in my cold room, in the place a hand can go.

The first is October's. The till was emptied on the Wednesday or the Thursday night (the week ran together). The coin was on the hearth-lip, the way you tip for good bread, and there was a boot in the flour dust, long in the stride, the toe toward the back door.

The second came on the twenty-sixth of October, and I did not put it there. Nobody had been at my till that night, but somebody had been behind my crock. Somebody knows the place a hand can go. I fed the crock that morning at the island's hour as I always do, with my back to the door, and I have fed it that way every morning since, because there is no other way to feed it.

The third is the fourth of December's. It was on the counter this time, and the print was short.

The two prints do not agree. One is a long stride and one is a short one. I have stood in both, in my own flour, to see. I fit neither, and I fit both, depending on how I walk. So it was two men, or one man walking two ways, or a man and a woman. Half the women of Mill Row are short. So am I. Grey wool is what a mill town wears. And I sweep. I always sweep. That is the trouble with having a baker for a witness.

On the Tuesday after the fourth, a woman paid me in full, in scrip, for ten wake loaves for her Row, and asked that the Row not be charged. I will not lay the one beside the other. Scrip is scrip. Everyone in this town has some, and none of it is theirs.

On Sunday they took the plate at St Brannock's and left a spent shell where the coins had been. The bakery gets gold and God gets brass. I do not know what that says about the bread.

I cannot spend the coins. Gold asks where it came from, and the Company says gold is scrip until proven otherwise. I have three proofs, and I will not show them.

I have been robbed by someone who tips. I would rather it had been someone who didn't. A thief who takes is only a thief. A thief who pays is keeping an account.

*Tag: everything is owned, so everything can be taken, and someone is keeping count.*

## IV. THE HITMAN — Contract

### Front

**WANTED**

**THE HITMAN**

Tuesday, 12 December 1893.

For the death of ____________, a man of the railyard bunks who came up the Line in October. He was found at the Station Rush on the bench by the platform lantern. The Gazette prints no unverified name, and neither does the Company.

By a needle, or a small-bore pistol. The Doctor's autopsy places the death before curfew ended.

The Company's three particulars:

- **Height:** unknown. No print on the boards.
- **Scarf:** none.
- **Hat:** felt, brown, common.

**Trace:** a seal of black wax, broken, on the boards beside him. No letter was found.

**NOTICE REGARDING BLACK WAX.** Correspondence sealed in black wax is correspondence of the Company and is exempt from the right to read (Article VI). The Company acknowledges no contract and offers no reward.

He will be on the Dusk train. So will others.

**Name:** ____________. Unverified. The Town may supply it.

**[ CONSTABLE — HOLLOWMERE ]**

### Back

*Ink, from a bottle nearly dry. Small writing, to fit.*

It came in the mailbag in November and was put into my hand at the window. The Postmaster did not look at it, and he did not write down that he had given it to me. Black wax, no sender. Inside, one printed line:

Burn the Letter & Keep Your Mundane Life, OR Accept the Baron's Contract.

Under the line, a name. I knew the name.

I burned it in the stove before I had finished being afraid. I kept my mundane life. Here it is: the rent at the Shift; the kettle on at five; my neighbour's lamp, which I trim for him because his hands shake. That is the size of it. I chose it, and I would choose it again. Write that down too.

I knew him. He was always early for the Train. He sat on the bench by the platform lantern an hour before the Rush every morning, in case it came early, which it never has. He kept his scrip in a sock and a one-way ticket in his hatband, and he was saving toward the date on it. January, he told me. He said it the way people here say "back east", as if it were a place you could already see from the platform. I will not write his name on the back of the Company's paper.

But I burned it, and he died anyway, on his bench, with black wax on the boards beside him.

So there was another letter, in another bag, to another hand, and that hand did not burn it. Nobody starts as the Hitman. I know that now from the other side. Somebody finishes as one.

Burning it kept my life the size it was. It did not keep his any size at all.

They say on the platform that the Baron is dead now, and that the contract was his. They say the wax still comes.

The Train comes at the Rush, and the bag comes with it. Check yours.

*Tag: nobody starts as the Hitman; somebody always finishes as one.*

## The Board, at Dawn

The Constable pastes the four face-out at the Station Rush. He uses fresh tacks and fresh paste. He stands back to see that they hang straight.

By dawn they are turned again: four backs to the square and four hands where the print was. Under every front there is the same line, set by the Company in the same type. Nobody has filled it in. Everybody has read it.

*The blank on the board at dawn is who you are.*
