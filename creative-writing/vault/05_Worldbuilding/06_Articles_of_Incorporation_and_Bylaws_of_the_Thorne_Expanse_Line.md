# Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws of the Thorne & Expanse Line

**[ THORNE SEAL ]**
*a locomotive lamp within a ring of thorns*

ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION AND BYLAWS OF THE THORNE & EXPANSE LINE,
a Company of the Railroad Expanse, at the Town of Hollowmere, Coldwater Territory.
Filed in the Year of Our Lord 1893.

*Certified a true copy, by the Company Clerk.*

---

## Recitals

WHEREAS the Territory of Coldwater is not a State, has no State to keep it, and is kept, so far as it is kept at all, by those who have laid down the means of keeping it; and

WHEREAS the Town of Hollowmere was not built by pioneers nor blessed by saints, but laid down tie by wooden tie and spike by iron spike by the subscribers hereto, at their own cost, which cost remains outstanding; and

WHEREAS every soul now living within the Grant came to the end of the line of their own free will, and by the same will consents to the terms upon which they remain;

NOW THEREFORE the subscribers do incorporate the Company under the name below, and do ordain these Articles and Bylaws, which shall bind every person, premises, beast and parcel within the Grant from the hour of filing, and shall bind them after.

For the purposes of these Articles, *"back east"* means any place not within the Grant. The Company is not answerable for anything that happens there, and is not answerable for much that happens here.

> *Nobody comes this far for nothing.*

---

## Article I: Name and Seat

**§1.** The name of the Company is the Thorne & Expanse Line. The name of the Town is the Company's to give, and the Company gives it as Hollowmere. The sign at the platform shall read HOLLOWMERE — EST. BY THE RAILROAD EXPANSE, and no sign, board, stone, nor painted wall within the Grant shall say otherwise.

**§2.** The seat of the Company is the Company Office at the platform, on the east spur, at the end of the line. A notice posted on the board in the square is lawful notice to every person within the Grant, whether read or not, whether they can read or not.

**§3.** No trade name given by the Town to premises of the Company has standing in law. The house the Town calls the Drowned Lantern is, and shall remain, the Rusty Lantern Saloon. It is let as such, licensed as such, and assessed as such.

> *Tell that to the man who carried the sign.*

---

## Article II: Purpose

**§1.** The Company exists to carry, to lend, to let, and to keep order. Trade, freight, lodging, faith, and law are incidental to carriage, and shall be construed as incidental to it.

**§2.** The Company may do any lawful thing that a person may do, together with such things as no person may do. It is not limited by any purpose it has not stated, and has stated few.

**§3.** Where these Articles are silent, the silence shall be construed in favour of the Company.

---

## Article III: Grant of Lands

**§1. The Mill.** To the Company: the Waterwheel Lumber Mill, its wheel and race, its loft, its saws and their teeth, and all timber, cut or uncut, standing or hanged.

**§2. The Bank.** To the Company: the Company Bank and its vault, and every deposit made therein. A deposit is the Company's until it is withdrawn, and the depositor's only upon withdrawal.

**§3. The Dirt.** To the Company: all ground within the Grant to any depth, the galleries of tin beneath it, flooded or dry, together with all that walks on it after curfew.

> *They own the dirt under your boots. They wanted that in writing.*

**§4. The Right of Way.** To the Company: a strip of forty feet on either side of the rails, and the platform entire. Any person standing within the Right of Way is, for so long as they stand there, freight, and may be moved as freight.

**§5. The Bed of the Mere.**
- To the Company: the bed of the mere, as and when drained into the lower workings, together with whatever lies therein: lanterns, boats, effects, and remains. Remains shall be delivered to the Officer of the Dead under Article IV §5. All else is salvage of the Company.
- The tides that remain to the mere by way of the drained-lake channel are the Company's tides, and shall keep the hours of the Company.
- The exhalation of the fen, called by the Company *miasma*, is a natural condition of the ground. The Company does not warrant against it and is not liable for any cough, false footstep, or boldness of wolves arising from it.

> *Rot. Call it what it is.*

**§6. The Vane Holdings.**
- Saving to the Company the precedence of these Articles, the mill rights, manor, and tin works known as the Vane holdings, whether situate in this Territory or beyond the reef, are held by **Mr. Aldous Vane, of Vane Tin Co,** who consents hereto.
- His consent is recited as given, and continuing.
- The inventory of the manor is annexed hereto, and includes one clock, standing, and one portrait, hung.
- Subject in all things to Article XI §4.

## Article IV: Officers

**§1. The President.** Cornelius Thorne, Railroad Baron, is President of the Company for life, and after. He resides back east. He shall be deemed present wherever the train is. His powers are general. His duties are none.

**§2. The Company Clerk.**
- The Clerk keeps the accounts, collects the rent, works the telegraph key, and keeps this copy.
- The Clerk may be raised to **Heir to the Baron** by a writ of the President, delivered by the train. Upon being raised, the Clerk holds the powers set out in Article X §5 and keeps the books besides.

> *Watch who's raised.*

**§3. The Constable.**
- The Constable keeps the peace, the Constable's Office, and the Morgue, together with the cuffs, the whistle, and the key.
- The Constable may deputise a Detective and a Police Officer under Article IX, and may undeputise them without cause.

**§4. The Postmaster, Officer of Mails.**
- The Postmaster keeps the mailbags, the sorting table, and the Telegraph Room, whose key the Clerk alone shall work.
- The Postmaster may open, inspect, and reseal any article of mail by any means, steam included, and shall keep a kettle for the purpose at the Company's expense, which expense is charged to the mails.

**§5. The Undertaker, Officer of the Dead.**
- The Undertaker receives every body within the Grant, by whatever cause and wherever found, including the bed of the mere.
- The Undertaker inventories each body's effects to the last button, and enters the inventory against the account of the deceased.
- The Undertaker conveys the dead by the hearse cart, at the Company's pace.
- The Company recognises no custom of mourning, but tolerates the wake table kept at Crumb's Ovens, one loaf to each mourner and three set for the dead. No rent shall be assessed on the three loaves set for the dead, nor on the wax-sealed crumb token pressed into each.

> *The one kind thing in here, and it's about bread.*

**§6.** Officers serve at the pleasure of the Company, and leave office by the pleasure of the Company or by death, whichever first occurs. Death does not discharge an officer's account.

---

## Article V: Scrip and Rent

**§1. Company Scrip.**
- Company Scrip is the only lawful money within the Grant. It is redeemable at the Company Store and the Company Bank at such rate as the Company shall post on the board each morning.
- Coin found within the Grant is scrip until proven otherwise. The burden of proof is on the coin.

**§2. The Clerk's Midday Rent.**
- Rent is collected in person by the Clerk at the midday Shift, at the rates in Schedule A.
- Rent is due on every bed slept in, and on every bed that should have been.

**§3. Indebted Status.**
- A person who does not pay at midday shall be entered on the rolls as Indebted.
- The Indebted may not purchase a ticket under Article VII §4, may not bear witness before the Tribunal, and shall keep the curfew twice over: from Dusk, and from the hour of their entry.

**[ INDEBTED ]** *(specimen impression, as entered against the rolls)*

COTTER, IVY. One room. In arrears by the price of a lamp-wick.

> *She paid the wick. She sat in the dark to pay it.*

**§4. Curfew.**
- Curfew runs from the departure of the Daily Train at Dusk until the Station Rush at dawn, at the hours in Schedule B.
- The Company keeps the lanterns that keep the wolves off the square. The Company does not warrant the dark beyond the lantern's reach.

**§5. Debtors' Labour.**
- The Indebted shall work off their debt at the Mill, at the day-rate posted in Schedule A, until it is discharged.
- The Clerk may, at the Clerk's discretion, substitute debtors' prison for labour at the Mill, or labour for prison, or neither for both.

> *Day-rate's lower than the rent on the bunk. Do the sum.*

The debt follows the debtor. Article XI says where.

---

## Article VI: Mails and Gazette

**§1.** All mail entering or leaving the Grant comes by the Daily Train, and is the property of the Company until delivered into the hand it names.

**§2. The Right to Read.**
- The Company may read any letter, telegram, or parcel within the Grant, by the Officer of Mails.
- Mail that has been read shall be resealed and stamped. The stamp is not evidence of reading.

**[ RECEIVED — OFFICE OF MAILS ]**

**§3. The Privilege of Black Wax.**
- Correspondence sealed in black wax is correspondence of the Company. It is exempt from the right to read.
- The Officer of Mails shall deliver it unopened and unsteamed into the hand it names, and shall not record its delivery.

> *Burn it.*

**§4. The Hollowmere Gazette.**
- The Gazette is printed back east and arrives with the mailbags. It shall carry the secrets of the previous day, as are found out.
- PROVIDED ALWAYS that the Gazette shall print **no name that has not been verified**. A secret found out without a verified name shall be printed without the name, and the Town may supply it.

> *The Town always does.*

---

## Article VII: The Train

**§1. The Schedule Is Law.**
- The Daily Train arrives at the start of each Day and departs at Dusk.
- Every clock within the Grant shall be set by the Train. A clock found to disagree shall be corrected or confiscated, save any clock that has stopped, which is exempt.

**§2. Cargo.**
- The Train carries flour for the ovens, whiskey for the saloon, tonics for the Morgue, lead and gunpowder for the railyard, and the mailbags.
- The manifest is the Company's, and may not be read.
- A person found in freight is cargo, and shall be rated and charged by the hundredweight.

**§3. The Right of Carriage.**
- The Company may carry into the Grant any person it names, without notice or ticket.
- This includes clergy, auditors, and the President with such men and dogs as he brings.

**§4. Extraction Only by Ticket.**
- The only lawful way out of the Grant is aboard the Train, by a ticket purchased in scrip at the platform window.
- No ticket shall be sold to the Indebted.
- A person who leaves the Grant by any other means remains, in law, within it. Their rent continues to fall due.

> *Some go out with no ticket and no debt. Ask who sold them the seat.*

---

## Article VIII: Faith

**§1. The Parish.**
- The Parish of St Brannock's is licensed by the Company. The licence is revocable at will and renewable yearly in scrip.
- The licence is made out to the incumbent for the time being, namely: ______________________.
- An incumbent is carried in under Article VII §3.

> *Bray. Gone.*

**§2. Indulgences.**
- Indulgences are priced in scrip by a table the Parish keeps and the Company audits.
- One part in ten of every Indulgence sold is remitted to the Company, as tithe upon the tithe.

**§3. Parish Seals.** Parish Seals are a recognised instrument of the Parish. They are no tender for rent.

**§4. Unlicensed Assembly.** No assembly for worship shall be held at any altar not licensed by the Company, whether above ground or below it.

**§5. The Bell.** The Bell of the square shall be rung in the ordinary order only.

---

## Article IX: Law and Tribunal

**§1. The Order of Law.**
- A Detective, deputised by the Constable, shall collect, inspect, and cross-reference, with magnifier, case file, and stamp.
- A Police Officer shall arrest, with baton, iron cuffs, tin star, and riot whistle.
- A Judge shall sit at the Tribunal at the Bell.
- No rung shall be skipped, except by the Company.

**§2. Ledger Slips.**
- Any person may file with the Clerk a Dirty Deed Ledger Slip naming another.
- A slip that has lain one day becomes a Tribunal Indictment without further act.
- A slip may not be withdrawn, the Company having already read it.

**§3. The Three Verdicts.** The Tribunal shall return one of three verdicts and no other:
- **Acquittal,** which is entered on the account of the accused and gives no remedy against the accuser.
- **A Heavy Fine,** payable in scrip at the Bell. Inability to pay shall be entered as Indebted status under Article V §3.
- **Death,** by the trapdoor of the gallows at the Bell, at an hour set by the Judge, which shall not fall during the Shift.

**§4. Gallows Lumber.**
- The scaffold, trap, and rope shall be supplied by the Company Mill at the Schedule rate, and charged to the account of the condemned.
- The charge shall survive the condemned, under Article XI §1.

> *Pine. They charge you for pine.*

**§5.** Upon the return of any verdict, the Judge shall record it in the following words and no others: *The Town has spoken.*

---

## Article X: Emergencies

**§1. The Bell.** The Emergency Town Bell may be rung by any person, at the charge listed in Schedule A. A person ringing the Bell without cause shall be entered as Indebted, for the charge and for the cause.

**§2. Public Unrest.**
- Public Unrest exists where the Constable declares it, or where any person holds the square from the soapbox at the Bell beyond the Shift.
- The Clerk shall record the temper of the Town each day under one of four heads: Calm, Panicked, Entranced, Rioting.
- A day entered as Rioting brings §3 into force.

**§3. The Pinkerton Provision.**
- In a state of Public Unrest, the President may come by the Train with such agents and guard dogs as he sees fit.
- Their wages, rations, lodging, and ammunition shall be apportioned against every account within the Grant, the peaceable included.

**§4. Restoration.**
- Any person who spoke against the Company during Public Unrest, and who remains within the Grant upon the President's departure, by whatever means, shall be restored to the rolls as Townsfolk.
- Their speech shall be struck from the record, and their account shall not.

> *By whatever means.*

**§5. Martial Law.**
- Where the President cannot attend, the Clerk raised as Heir to the Baron may declare martial law. The Heir then holds every power of the President, and such others as the hour requires.
- Martial law ends when the Heir declares it ended.

---

## Article XI: Succession

**§1. The Account Survives.** Upon the death of any person within the Grant, their account passes:
- first to their household;
- then to their lodgers;
- then to the Company as residuary creditor and heir.

The account comprises rent, fines, lumber, labour owed, and the Undertaker's charges.

**§2. An Officer's Holdings.** Upon the death of an officer, the Company holdings attached to the office revert to the Company. The President shall name a successor by the next train.

**§3. Effects of the Deceased.** Holdings of a deceased person that are not Company property shall be inventoried by the Officer of the Dead and held against the account for thirty days, then sold, and the proceeds applied to the account.

## Schedule C: Prohibited Acts

*Every act below is an offence against the Company, triable at the Bell under Article IX.*

**I. Sabotage.**
1. The setting of fire in the Mill or its loft.
2. The placing of explosives on, under, or near the rails.
3. The fouling of the wheel.

**II. Heist.**
1. Entering the vault of the Company Bank.
2. Entering the express safe of the Train while in transit.
3. Taking the plate of the Parish.
4. Taking goods from the ovens, the leaving of payment notwithstanding.

**III. Contract.**
1. Accepting any commission for a life, save as the Company may lawfully let.

**IV. Conversion.**
1. Preaching without the licence of the Company.
2. Drawing marks or signs, in ash or in any other matter, upon five or more premises.
3. Assembly of six or more persons at any altar not licensed.
4. Ringing the Bell in any but the ordinary order.

> *They wrote the list before anyone did any of it.*
