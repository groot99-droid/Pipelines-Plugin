## I. October: the roll

**Telephone log. Monday 6 October.**

0000. Roll. Workings 10. Sawmill 10. Bunkhouse 10. Yard 10. All present.

0310. Bell rang once. Line open. No voice. Logged.

0600. Line tried for the Washington switch. Dead.

This book is kept beside the telephone because the telephone is the one thing in this office that answers to Washington. It does not answer. Entries by the duty hand. — H.

## II. November: the unrest

**Telephone log. Monday 3 November.**

1400. Reverend Moss asked to place a call to his bishop. Line dead. He waited by it anyway, with his hat on his knee, for about the length of a hymn. He said a dead line is still a line. Then he went out to the gate to bless the evening shift: resin on the brow, the same words as every night. I could hear them from here.

**Telephone log. Friday 14 November.**

0610. Payroll on time.

0700. Sawmill No. 3 asked about mail. Told no. Then he stood at the counter a while and did not seem to know why he had come. Ribs through his shirt, plain as the slats of a crate. Knuckles white. His breath showed in here, and the stove was lit.

0715. Sawmill No. 6 asked to telephone his mother, because today is her birthday. He knew the date without a calendar. Told him the line is dead.

It was.

## III. 1–12 December: the Inspector

**Telephone log. Monday 1 December.**

Full moon. At first light there were marks on five doors, four long scores on each. They were painted out by noon. Not logged as damage. Logged.

**Telephone log. Friday 5 December.**

0610. Payroll on time. The Inspector-General came down from the coach with one bag and asked to see the telephone. I showed him. He lifted the receiver and listened for a long while, longer than anyone has.

He said, "Dead?"

I said, "Mostly."

He wrote that down.

**Telephone log. Saturday 6 December.**

0000. Roll. Workings No. 2 not at his slot. He went down on yesterday's first shift. Dogs out. Nothing. Workings 9.

**Telephone log. Monday 8 December.**

Tribunal at dawn on the mill platform. The judge was flown in. Verdict: the Blade. Sawmill No. 6.

His name was Mirek. I am writing it here because the rack will not. His mother's birthday is the fourteenth of November.

**Telephone log. Thursday 11 December.**

0310. Bell rang. Line open. No voice.

I held it. The line stayed open for about as long as the rack takes to weigh, and nothing came down it. I did not hang up. It did not frighten me, and that is what I am logging. It felt like being asked for. The earpiece was cold enough to hurt, and when I put it down there was frost on it, in this room, with the stove lit.

**Telephone log. Saturday 13 December.**

The search was made before the order came. I went with them, because somebody had to carry the lamp.

Under ████████████████ the floor was sound. There was no stair and no room. There was no one there, and nothing of the ones who were struck off. Not a button. But it was colder under those boards than in the yard. Our breath showed. There was frost on the underside of the planks, and nothing down there to make frost.

It's almost like they had help.
