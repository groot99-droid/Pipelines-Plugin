# The Canon and Trope Ledger of Who Did It

*Kept at HollowMere, Coldwater Territory, for the Thorne & Expanse Line. Anno 1893.*

---

## The Clerk's Oath

I swear to the Line that I will enter what is written. I swear I will enter what is said. I swear I will enter what is ruled.

I will enter nothing I have not seen written or heard said by more than one mouth. Where the paint says one thing and the town says another, I will set the two side by side and rule between them, as a clerk rules between two columns that ought to agree. I will not strike out a Company name. I will draw a line through it, so the next man can still read what it was.

My name is not needed for the ledger. The Line knows it, and that is enough.

*Margin: An oath to a railroad is an oath to a timetable. I have sworn it anyway.*

Entered.

---

## Entry I. The Year, the Territory, the Line, the Baron

**Record.** The sign at the platform reads "HOLLOWMERE — EST. BY THE RAILROAD EXPANSE". It hangs above the rail spur on the east edge, beside the platform lantern. The paint is newer than the post.

**Canon.** The town says it was not built by pioneers and not blessed by saints. It was laid down by the Railroad Barons, tie by wooden tie and spike by iron spike. The Barons own the mill. They own the bank. They own the dirt under a man's boots.

**Ruling.** The year is 1893, the year of the Panic. Banks are failing back east, railroads are failing back east, and strikes are spreading. Everywhere that is not here is "back east".

The country is Coldwater Territory. It is not yet a state, and the law is thin, so the Company is the law.

The line is the Thorne & Expanse. Its Baron is Cornelius Thorne. He is alive. He does not live here. He comes by train when the town riots, and he brings Pinkertons. "The Railroad Barons" is the town's word for Thorne and his partners, and the clerk enters it as a plural because the town says it as a plural.

*Margin: Scrip is still honoured at the Company window. For now.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry II. The Mere

**Record.** The town is named HollowMere. A mere is a lake.

**Canon.** There is no lake. There was one. It drained into the flooded tin mine and left a fen behind it: black water, reed, mud that freezes and thaws and freezes again. The town calls it a drowned marsh.

**Ruling.** The name is kept, and the lake is entered as lost.

The marsh gives off a sickness. The townsfolk call it mere-rot. The Company calls it miasma. The clerk enters them as one thing under two names, and lists what it does:

- It makes the wolves bold.
- It makes footsteps where nobody is walking.
- It makes the town cough.

*Margin: I have written "miasma" in the Company's books and "mere-rot" in my own. I cough in both.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry III. Aldous Vane, of Port-au-Levain

**Record.** The name is everywhere and the man is nowhere:

- VANE TIN CO on the crates in the Flooded Gallery and in the Deep Level at BlackPine;
- Vane Manor, with its hall clock stopped at 11:47;
- Vane Sawmill at BlackPine;
- the will, which names "whoever remains when the last lantern goes out".

**Canon.** Lord Aldous Vane came from Port-au-Levain, the fog-bound island of the Sovereign Sourdough Council, reached by sea. He came inland and made his money in tin before the Line came. This year he went home to the island, coughing, and he died there. On the island, every telling opens with Vane already dead.

The news reached HollowMere by wire or by rumour before any ferry could have carried it. In HollowMere he is "three nights" dead, and his will is read here.

**Ruling.** He died on the island, and he is entered as dead in HollowMere. Both are entered. On the island, he is a death the place remembers. Inland, he is a name and a will.

His widow, Widow Vane, keeps the parlour in HollowMere. His portrait hangs there turned to the wall. The clerk enters the portrait as hung and as turned, and does not enter why.

*Margin: Wire or rumour. The telegraph room has no slip for it. "Three nights" dead has been three nights dead for as long as I have kept this book.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry IV. The Drowned Lantern

**Record.** The Company's books list the "Rusty Lantern Saloon". The licence is in that name, and so is the whiskey order.

**Canon.** A barman drowned in the mere carrying the saloon's sign lantern. The town has called the place the Drowned Lantern ever since, and the Company name has not been spoken there since.

**Ruling.** The house is the Drowned Lantern. The Company name is struck through, not erased. Mrs Grieves keeps it.

It holds the dartboard, the shutters, the flour bin, and the photographs of drowned sailors behind the bar. In the back room there is an oil barrel.

The barman's name is not in the record. The clerk does not supply one. The town remembers one thing about him: at dusk, when he trimmed the wick of the sign lantern, he would hum the same hymn every time, never all the way through. The clerk enters the habit because it is what the town kept.

*Margin: There are photographs of drowned sailors in a town with no sea. I do not know which of them were sailors.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry V. Black Pine, Gallows Wood, the Fenwood

**Record.** The maps give three names: Black Pine, Gallows Wood, the Fenwood.

**Canon.** It is all one forest.

**Ruling.** Black Pine is the whole forest. It has two parts:

- **Gallows Wood** is the near edge, where the planks are laid and where the town hangs people.
- **The Fenwood** is the drowned heart. In it are the hanging oak, the charcoal clamp, the drowned lantern still glowing in the marsh fog, and the den.

Mother Moll keeps her charcoal hut in the Fenwood. Her terms are "Four sprigs of herb, dear. The fog is in everyone's chest." The clerk enters the terms as fair.

*Margin: The drowned lantern is still lit. Some say it is the old lake-light, fallen from the oak. Some say the barman went under with it. I do not know who lights it now.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry VI. Father Bray, and the New Vicar

**Record.** The chapel is St Brannock's. There is a rope in it, with a torn glove caught in the rope.

**Canon.** Father Bray, the old priest, is gone. The town says he died. The town also says he fled. A new Vicar came on the train.

**Ruling.** Bray's going is why the Vicar came. The clerk rules on that much. Whether Bray is dead or fled, the clerk does not rule.

The Vicar's own words are entered: "Everyone in Hollowmere confesses to you sooner or later." He brings the town to his faith. A Zealot, if one rises, would kill him and walk in his robes for as long as the body goes unreported. The clerk enters that as a thing that can happen in this town, not a thing that has happened.

His last words, should he come to them, are already known to the town and are entered here as the town has them:

> "In the end I sought nothing else but to serve God. And serve him I did. With this death's last breath I pray this prayer: may the Almighty grant me the strength of ten in the next life. And in return, tenfold my own."

His memorial is a lavender field, a lone orange tree, and a blue sky.

*Margin: Whose glove?*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry VII. Tamsin Crumb, of the Island

**Record.** Crumb's Ovens. In the shop are the wake table, ten plates and the dead loaves. Behind it are the cold room, the mixing room, the oven room and the proofing room.

**Canon.** Tamsin Crumb came from Port-au-Levain. She crossed with Vane. She brought the wake-loaf custom with her: one loaf for each mourner and three for the dead, each loaf carrying a crumb token sealed in wax.

**Ruling.** HollowMere's mourning is an island custom. Through it the island has a part in every funeral here. Crumb crossed with Vane, and the clerk enters that and nothing more. A shared crossing is not a shared purpose.

*Margin: Ten plates were laid. I counted the town afterwards. I will not write the number here.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry VIII. BlackPine, from Vane to Harrow; from Tin to Coal

**Record.** At BlackPine:

- the mill is called Vane Sawmill;
- the Deep Level holds a tin crate and a refuge table;
- the motto over the camp reads "PRODUCTION IS OBLIGATION.";
- the tally board in the Blackpine Workings has one name chalked out.

**Canon.** BlackPine was Vane's timber-and-tin camp. The tin ran out. The Harrow Timber & Coal Syndicate bought it and found coal under the old workings.

The men there are told when they sign at the intake office that they are either feeding the furnace or they are fuel. Their tally log stands between them and a shallow grave.

**Ruling.** The deed is Harrow's, and the name on the mill is Vane's. Both stay. The tin is entered as spent and the coal as found. The clerk enters the Tally Rack as BlackPine enters it: ten numbered pine billets and ten Identity Logs, branded with the man's job, his debts and his sins. In BlackPine, the log on a man's rack at dawn is who he is.

*Margin: One name is chalked out on the board in the Workings. The board does not say whose, and nobody at the Company Office would read it to me.*

*Margin, typed, in another hand, on a slip pasted to the page: SITE REDESIGNATED GROUNDS 99, 1952. PROGRAM: ANOMALY UNKNOWN. MOTTO RETAINED WITH SITE. NAME ON BOARD: DO NOT CARRY. SEE ENTRY XVII.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry IX. The Roles and the Five Trades

**Record.** The town's cards name these places a person may hold:

- Killer, Accomplice, Heir;
- Detective, Vicar, Watchman;
- Herbalist, Gamekeeper, Butler;
- Child, Drifter, Lamplighter;
- Baker, Fisher, Tinner.

Later arrivals are the Ranger, with a crossbow and the right to arrest; the Thief, who takes heirlooms and leaves by sea; and the Medium, with seance chalk.

**Canon.** The town counts five public trades: Baker, Postmaster, Undertaker, Bartender and Company Clerk.

**Ruling.** They all stand side by side. The clerk enters them as a census of the town, not of its people.

- **The Baker** is up before anyone, flour to the elbows. She bakes Wake Loaves and Rumor Loaves, and may become a Fence or a Ration Tyrant.
- **The Postmaster** steams envelopes over a kettle, and may become a Handler, a Postmaster Assassin, or an Extortionist.
- **The Undertaker** pushes a hearse cart, and may become a Grave Robber, who seals the Vicar in a lead coffin, or a Chief Coroner.
- **The Bartender** keeps a sawed-off behind the bar and serves Rotgut, Liquid Courage and Loose Lips Lager. He may become a Union Boss or a Vigilante.
- **The Company Clerk** collects rent and works the telegraph, and may become an Embezzler or the Baron's Heir.
- **The Watchman** walks the lanes after dark and stands by one door each night.
- **The Herbalist** holds two bottles. One saves, one ends.
- **The Gamekeeper** was paid by Vane to keep the wood. If they come for him, he will not go alone.
- **The Butler** knows every door in Hollowmere.
- **The Child** is small and quiet, and hides, watches, remembers.
- **The Drifter** wants to hang.
- **The Lamplighter** lights the lanterns that keep the wolves off the square.
- **The Fisher** does not take the boats out, because the fog keeps them in. He fishes the mere channels instead.
- **The Tinner**'s mine is shut. His hands are not.
- **The Doctor** is entered without remark.
- **The Killer** walks in a borrowed character, strikes after dark when the room is empty, cleans one clue, washes at the well, and is then the loudest voice asking who did it.
- **The Accomplice** never holds the knife, only the alibi.
- **The Heir** is the Accomplice who inherits the Knife.
- **The Detective** collects, inspects and cross-references.

Beside these the clerk enters positions a person may come to hold. They are not people:

- **The Zealot.** Under him stand the Dark Positions: Minion, Mob Boss, Cultist, High Priest.
- **The Activist.** She works the soapbox at the bell. If she survives the Redemption vote, she is plain Townsfolk again.
- **The Bandit.** Bandana, six-shooter, four scores.
- **The Hitman.** Nobody starts as one. He becomes one when the black-wax envelope comes.
- **The Law.** Detective, then Police Officer, then Judge.
- **The changed.** The Apostate, prayed for by both sides until he snapped. The Stowaway, in a freight crate with the Baron's manifest. The Starving.

*Margin: My own trade is on the list. I do not say which of its two evolutions I am closer to.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry X. The Telephone at BlackPine

**Record.** The Company Office at BlackPine holds a ledger desk, a safe, a map table, and a telephone. The telephone is dead.

**Canon.** The town tells no story about it. A telephone at the edge of Coldwater Territory in 1893 is a thing out of its time.

**Ruling.** It is Harrow's new line. It was strung lately, it rarely works, and it is entered as a rumour of modern times reaching a camp that has not yet had the old ones. A line that does not carry a voice is still a line on the Syndicate's books.

*Margin: It was heard to ring once. It was dead when it rang.*

*Margin, typed, on the same kind of slip: LINE IN SERVICE 1952, TO WASHINGTON. RINGS 0310. NO VOICE. LOGGED.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XI. The Tide Chart

**Record.** A tide chart, with the next low tide circled in ink.

**Canon.** HollowMere has no sea and the mere is not one. A tide chart has no business here.

**Ruling.** Both readings are entered, and neither is struck:

- The chart is an old one that Vane brought from the island, and it charts the island's tides.
- The mere still rises and falls through the channel where the lake drained, and the chart is used for that.

*Margin: The circled low tide came. The mere went down at that hour. I have not ruled which of the two readings explains it.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XII. Old Fennick's Light

**Record.** "Old Fennick at the light."

**Canon.** There is no lighthouse at HollowMere. There was a lake-light at the edge of the water, put up when there was a lake, and young Fennick kept it. He is old Fennick now, and he keeps it still.

**Ruling.** It was a lake-light and is now a marsh light. It burns at the marsh edge and guides nobody anywhere in particular. Fennick keeps it lit all the same. The island's lighthouse is entered separately, as the island's.

*Margin: Bog-wisps show lights too, and they lure. I have stood at the marsh edge and could not tell Fennick's light from theirs.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XIII. Harbourmaster Pell's Harbour

**Record.** The title is "Harbourmaster".

**Canon.** HollowMere has no harbour. It has a marsh dock, a leaking skiff, and a ferry that Tuck, the ferry boy, poles across the channels.

**Ruling.** "Harbour" means the marsh dock. The title is kept out of courtesy, or out of habit; the clerk cannot say which. Pell's terms of trade are entered as he states them: "The skiff leaks. Bring me resin and a plank of timber and I will lend you a lantern."

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XIV. The Mirroring

**Record.** Three towns keep three sets of books, and they come to the same column totals.

**Canon.** Nobody can say why.

**Ruling.** The parallels are entered in short. No cause is given.

- **Clocks.** HollowMere, the Daily Train. Port-au-Levain, the Morning Ferry. BlackPine, the Payroll Train and the Shift-Whistle.
- **Money and tax.** Scrip paid as the Clerk's rent; Copper paid as the platter tariff; Syndicate scrip held against the Tallyman's quota.
- **Identity.** The role card; the Wake Loaf platter in the Great Hearth; the branded tally log on the Rack.
- **First vendetta.** Zealot against Vicar; Leaven-Rot against the Abbot of the Crust; Timber-Wolf against the Forest Chaplain.
- **Second.** Activist against Baron; Pyre-Master against the Grand Inspector-General; Slate-Breaker against the Syndicate Inspector.
- **Third.** Bandit; Smuggler-Blackguard; Company Enforcer.
- **Fourth.** Hitman; Black-Crust Contract; Pit-Poisoner.
- **Law.** Detective, Police Officer, Judge; Inquisitor, Guild Bailiff, High Chancellor; Camp Investigator, Mine Warden, Circuit Judge.
- **Verdicts.** Acquittal, a Heavy Fine, or Death by the gallows trapdoor; Acquittal, a Guild Fine, or Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth; Acquittal, Scrip Forfeiture, or The Great Sawmill Blade.
- **Predators.** Wolves; Tide-Ghouls; Timber-Wolves and Tunnel Crawlers.
- **Enforcers.** Pinkertons; Iron-Wardens; OEAs.

On the island they say the loaf sitting on your silver plate at dawn is who you are. At BlackPine they say the log on your rack at dawn is who you are. The clerk has heard HollowMere say something like it about the card. He has not seen it written, so he does not enter the words.

*Margin: Accounts that balance this well were cooked by someone.*

*Margin, later, same hand: It has since been written down. "The card in your hand at dawn is who you are." I enter it now. I did not write it.*

*Margin, typed, pasted over the BlackPine column: FOURTH, BLACKPINE: CARRIED AS ENLISTMENT. DEPARTMENT ████. SECOND, BLACKPINE: SYNDICATE INSPECTOR CARRIED AS INSPECTOR-GENERAL (PROGRAM). PREDATORS: SEE SUBJECT FILES. ENFORCERS: MASKED.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XV. The Rumours

**Record.** None. Nothing about this is painted, chalked or stamped.

**Canon.** Something runs between HollowMere, BlackPine and Port-au-Levain. It travels faster than the train and faster than the ferry. Each town's crimes echo the others'.

**Ruling.** The clerk has ruled three columns and filled none of them.

*True?* The echoes are real. Entry XIV has them. A widow's portrait is turned to the wall inland, and a death opens every telling on the island. A name is chalked out at BlackPine.

*Alive?* At BlackPine they say something out there in the black pines isn't cutting trees. In HollowMere they say the pines were not empty. The marsh makes footsteps where nobody walks.

*Carried?* The train brings a black-wax envelope in the mailbag. News of a death on the island reached the telegraph room before any boat could have. The ferry casts off in fog. The Stowaway reads the manifest. Tamsin Crumb came across the water. Vane came across the water before her, and went back.

Against all three columns, the clerk enters three objects and does not rule on any of them:

- a gravestone with tomorrow's date;
- chalk hopscotch under a lantern, with no child seen playing it;
- the drowned lantern in the Fenwood, still glowing.

*Margin: I have tried to rule on this three times. Each time the page came out the same.*

Entered. Not settled.

---

## Entry XVI. The First Body

*Entered out of order. The book was closed when I found him.*

**Record.** A grave in the yard of St Brannock's, under the Company bell. The Company's entry for the man in it reads "lost in the workings".

**Canon.** His name was Jory Kitto, and he was a tinner. When the lake was drained into Vane's gallery to lay the Line's last mile, he struck the last plug. He was found afterwards at the end of the line. He was the first body it had.

**Ruling.** The Company's entry stands, struck through, so the next man can read what it was. Beneath it the clerk enters his name and his trade. On him was a tobacco tin, and in it were his wages, saved toward his sister's fare. The clerk enters the tin. He does not enter whether she came.

*Margin: The town says the plug went at 11:47. The town says it.*

Entered. Ruled. Not believed.

---

## Entry XVII. Grounds 99

*Not in the clerk's hand. Typed on four slips and pasted in behind Entry XVI. The paste has gone brown. The slips are later than the book.*

**Record.** A camp in the black pines with a mill called Vane Sawmill, a refuge lamp in the Deep Level, a dead telephone in the Company Office, and over the gate PRODUCTION IS OBLIGATION, in new paint on old. Its crews are numbered 0 to 9, a rack of ten to each crew. It is called Grounds 99, and the year on the slips is 1952.

**Canon.** The camp holds prisoners of war, men entered as enemies of the state, and men entered as other. The program that runs it is called ANOMALY UNKNOWN. Four services send it traffic, and every one of them disowns it. Its Midnight Roll Call allows fifteen seconds. Its tribunal sits at dawn and has three verdicts, and it calls the last of them the Blade. Under one of its houses there is a facility that no camp form names. In December one man was taken off the payroll platform with a sealed case. After that the facility was not found, nor were the people carried into it, and the search was stopped by an order that nobody would say they had sent.

**Ruling.** The slips do not say that Grounds 99 is the clerk's BlackPine. They say an old grade runs south of the broken trestle and that nobody owns it. They say the motto came with the site. Sixty years apart, two books keep the same rack, the same whistle and the same name chalked on a board. The clerk's entries for BlackPine stand as written, and so do the four places HollowMere speaks of it: the name chalked out, the drummer, the chaplain not at work, and the forge and the kilns. They are entered as the echo running forward in time as well as across the map. No cause is given.

*Margin, typed: THE NAME ON THE BOARD IS NOT TO BE CARRIED. IT CAN BE READ.*

*Margin, pencil, under the slips: almost like they had help.*

Entered. Not ruled. Not the clerk's.
