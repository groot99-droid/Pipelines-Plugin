# A Lamplighter's Round of HollowMere

*Kept at dusk, Coldwater Territory, 1893.*

> "You light the lanterns that keep the wolves off the square."
> — the Lamplighter's card

> "Only the Lamplighter's lanterns may be lit."
> — Articles of the Line, Schedule B: Curfew Hours

The Saloon bell went at dusk the way it always went, flat and quick, a bell that wanted to be done. The train stood at the platform with steam up and its doors still open. She took the can from its hook, the wick-pole from behind the door and the short ladder from the wall, and went out by the back way, because the round began where the oil was.

Curfew in HollowMere does not fall at an hour. It falls when the train leaves. After that, by the Company's own paper, the only lawful light in the town is hers.

She had kept the round long enough to know it was a list, and every list in HollowMere is a list of what the Company owns. She kept this one anyway, lantern by lantern, in her head and under her breath. A number, said low. A hand on the post to light it. A hand on the post again to leave it. Two touches, every post, every night. She could not have said where she learned that. She had never once skipped it.

The Company paid her in scrip, by the lantern lit. A lantern left dark was docked. That was the whole of her contract, and she understood it better than most people in the town understood theirs.

## Ring One: The Town Core

*These doors are never boarded.*

**The Drowned Lantern, back room — tavern_back**

**Light:** None yet. Hers begins here.

**What stands:** An oil barrel on a chock [tk.], a tin funnel hung from its tap on a string. Whiskey cases off the morning train. One door to the bar, one to the yard.

**What the town says:** That the barrel fed the sign lantern before the barman walked into the mere with the sign lantern in his arms. That it has been refilled since and is not the same oil. That he hummed the same hymn every dusk when he trimmed the wick, and never got to the end of it. That nobody remembers his name, and everybody remembers the hymn stopping.

She filled the can to the seam and no further. A full can slopped on the ladder, and slopped oil was docked like any other. Through the wall the saloon was getting louder. She did not hum. She had never hummed at this barrel, and she had never asked herself why not.

**The Drowned Lantern — tavern_bar**

**Light:** The saloon lamps. Lawful until the train goes. After that, the shutters come across, and what burns behind them is Mrs Grieves's business.

**What stands:** A dartboard. The shutters. A flour bin by the bar, where flour has no business [tb.]. Photographs of drowned sailors behind the bar, in black frames. Over the door, the Company's name on the old board, struck through and not erased, and spoken by nobody. Pinned by the door, an old harbour chart from the island, with the next low tide circled in ink.

**What the town says:** That the sailors in the photographs were never on any water near here, and that nobody can say which of them were sailors. That the flour bin is not for flour. That the mere keeps to the island's chart twice a day, a hand's breadth, with no sea for three hundred miles; or seems to. That Mrs Grieves keeps the house, and knows who has come through her back room, and when, without looking round.

She read the circle on the chart the way she read it every dusk, with her thumb under it. Low water early tonight. The Fenwood path held at low water and did not hold otherwise, and that one circle of ink decided how far her round went. A man at the bar said the wolves were bad this week. She said they were at the fence already, which was true, and went out the front to see the train go.

**The platform — overworld.platform**

**Light:** `ov.lt_platform`, the first of hers. Lit as the last car clears.

**What stands:** A rail spur along the east edge of the town. Drowned ties. One iron post and its lantern. The station sign: HOLLOWMERE — EST. BY THE RAILROAD EXPANSE.

**What the town says:** That the first body the line ever had lay at the end of this platform, face-up in the mud. That he was Jory Kitto, a tinner who was afraid of the dark. That the ledger has him lost in the workings, and that he was found here instead, by men who knew it. That nobody comes to the end of the line unless they are running from something, or hunting for it. That you should not miss the train.

The train went at dusk and did not wait; it had never waited for anyone. She stood with the pole up and watched the back car pass, the car the coffins ride out in, the conductor's lamp swinging at its rail. Then there was only the sound of it going into the pines toward the Gallows Wood siding, and then not that. *One.* Touch. Light. Touch. Behind the train the town closed, without anyone closing it.

**The railyard and its workbench — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None. A Company padlock on the gate, and a second Company padlock on the first one's chain.

**What stands:** A workbench under a tin roof, a vise, a rack of files. Lead and gunpowder off the morning train, stacked by weight. Freight crates stencilled for return to Thorne Junction.

**What the town says:** That some crates come in heavier than they go out. That some go out with a person in them. That a person in a crate can read the Baron's manifest through the slats, if the platform lantern is lit.

She had heard that one. It was not why she lit the platform first. She lit it first because it was first. But she had heard it.

Four lanterns stand on the square, one at each corner of what the town calls a square and what is really the place where four roads give up. She lit them in her own order, counting on from the platform. *Two.* *Three.* *Four.* The fourth lantern of the square, the one over the chalk by the well, she left. She always left it. She always came back to it last.

**Hollowmere, the square — overworld**

**Light:** Lanterns 0 to 3 in the Company's numbering [ov.]. Three lit. The fourth not yet.

**What stands:** The Emergency Town Bell on its frame. The pump. The notice board, papered with yesterday's Gazette and the ledger slips nobody admits to writing. The kit chest. The fence along the pines side. Black crepe on the rail, for Vane. A scarecrow in a trench coat, staked where no crop has ever grown. Under the fourth lantern, chalk hopscotch, fresh, with no child seen playing it. Beside it, the well.

**What the town says:** That the scarecrow faces a different way each dusk. That the wolves come to the fence and no further, because of the lanterns, or because of what is on this side of the fence. That if six are ever gathered below, this bell will toll backward and the sky go crimson; that nobody living has heard it, and nobody living would say so if they had. That the chalk is redrawn every morning, by a child, by anyone.

The wolves were at the fence. She did not look at them, and they did not come closer than the light, which was the arrangement. Across the square, under the bell, the Tribunal's bench stood out in the open with a tarpaulin over it against the fog.

**The Tribunal and the gallows at the Bell — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None. Nothing here is lit at night.

**What stands:** The Tribunal's bench under the bell. Three verdicts painted on a board for those who cannot read: Acquittal. A Heavy Fine. Death. Behind the bench, the gallows, its trapdoor bolted, its rope taken in at dusk. Crows along the beam.

**What the town says:** That the last man through the trap went unnamed in the Gazette, because his name could not be verified. That some evenings a stranger off the train comes and stands under the beam as if waiting to be chosen. That the crows know a day before anyone.

She had known the last man only by his whistle. He had whistled at her ladder most evenings that autumn, two notes, up and down, the way you whistle at a dog you are fond of. He had the hands of a man who mended boots, or who once had. He had never said his name and she had never asked it, and now nobody would print it. She went past the beam without looking up. The crows walked along it to keep her in sight.

**The Well Ladder — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** The square's fourth lantern stands over it, unlit.

**What stands:** The well: a stone lip, a windlass, a bucket. Below where the bucket stops, iron rungs set into the shaft, going down to the Undercroft under St Brannock's.

**What the town says:** That whoever does it washes at the well afterwards. That the rungs were the Company's idea, or Father Bray's, or older than both.

She did not look down the well. That was another habit. Across the lane the Post Office window was lit.

**The Post Office & Telegraph Room — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** A window, past curfew. Not hers. Against Schedule B.

**What stands:** Mailbags, emptied and hung on pegs. The sorting frame. The telegraph key on a table of its own, behind a rail. A kettle on the stove. The Gazette is opened here every morning, and read here first.

**What the town says:** That envelopes are steamed over that kettle before they are sorted. That some mornings there is black wax in a bag, and that the man it is meant for has a choice between burning it and keeping his life the size it was. That word of Vane's death came over that key before any ferry could have carried it; or that it came by rumour, which keeps its own timetable.

She did not report the window. She did not report lights. Nobody had ever asked her to in so many words, and she had decided long ago not to hear it asked in fewer.

**The Constable's Office & Morgue — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A desk. Iron cuffs and a riot whistle on nails. Through the back, a slab, empty and scrubbed, and a trestle beside it waiting for whatever rides out next in the back car.

**What the town says:** That an empty slab in HollowMere is a forecast and not a mercy. That the one who opens the dead here can tell the hour a man died, and that it is the hour, more than the knife, that hangs people.

The lane narrowed toward the apothecary. Doc Mallory's shutters were across. Above them, the loft window was green.

**Mallory Apothecary — apoth_shop**

**Light:** Shuttered.

**What stands:** A basin. A bench. A shelf marked NOT FOR SALE [as.], with one slot empty and a clean ring in the dust where the bottle stood.

**What the town says:** That Doc Mallory knows what stood in the slot, and when it went. That one bottle saves and one bottle ends, and that the labels are not always kind enough to say which. That the fog is in everyone's chest.

**The apothecary loft — apoth_loft**

**Light:** None. Not on the round. And yet the green lamp is burning [al.]. Not hers.

**What stands:** The green lamp.

**What the town says:** That the loft is dark in the Company's books and green in every window that faces it. That whoever reads up there reads by a colour that makes a face look drowned.

She noted the green the way she noted all of them, and walked on to the Widow's house, where the crepe on the knocker had gone grey with three nights of fog.

**Widow Vane's parlour — widow_parlour**

**Light:** None in the parlour.

**What stands:** A brass letter box in the door, its flap held up by a folded paper nobody has taken in. The portrait of Aldous Vane, turned to the wall [wp.]. Black crepe.

**What the town says:** That Vane is three nights dead, and has been three nights dead for longer than three nights. That his will was read in this room and left everything to whoever remains when the last lantern goes out. That nobody knows who turned the portrait. That the Widow does not know either, or will not say.

**Widow Vane's bedroom — widow_bedroom**

**Light:** A candle [wb.], upstairs, at the glass. Not hers. Unlawful.

**What stands:** The candle.

**What the town says:** That the Widow does not sleep. That she sleeps with it lit, so that she will see who comes. That she lights it to be seen from outside, by someone in particular.

She stood under the window a moment. She had passed it every round since the will was read, and the town said that was three nights, and she had passed it more often than three times. The candle did not move. She did not report it.

**Crumb's Ovens — bakery**

**Light:** The oven, through the back. Fire is lawful after curfew and light is not, and the Company has never ruled which an oven is.

**What stands:** The wake table. Ten plates on it [bk., plates 0 to 9], one for each chair. One loaf for each mourner and three for the dead, each loaf with a crumb token sealed in wax. On the end plate, one loaf gone hard and grey: the dead loaf.

**What the town says:** That Tamsin Crumb came inland from the island with Vane, before the Line, and is up before anyone, flour to the elbows. That on the island every soul has a silver platter and every platter a loaf, and that here there are ten plates. That nobody can say why the towns match, or who matched them.

Down the bakery yard the four back rooms each had a window on her side, and the round had never found a reason to skip a window.

**The cold room — no id frozen (round 7 room)**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A door to the bakery. The station: butter and lard on blocks of ice cut from the mere, under sacking. A hiding spot behind the sacking, deep enough for a child or a thin man. A clue spot at the sill, where flour lies thin enough to take a handprint.

**What the town says:** That the mere's ice keeps too long. That things put down on it come up looking exactly as they went down.

**The mixing room — no id frozen (round 7 room)**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A door to the bakery. The station: the dough trough, scraped clean. A hiding spot under the trough board. A clue spot at the flour sacks, where the dust takes a print of whatever leans on them.

**What the town says:** That Rumour Loaves are mixed here, with a slip inside, and that a slip baked into bread is harder to call a lie than one pinned to a board. That whatever is written on it hardens in a day.

**The oven room — no id frozen (round 7 room)**

**Light:** The oven mouth, banked.

**What stands:** A door to the bakery. The station: the clay oven Vane's men built behind the tents before there was a town, which the bakery was raised around afterwards. A hiding spot in the wood bay. A clue spot at the ash pit, raked each dawn.

**What the town says:** That a gold coin was once left on the peel as a tip, and nothing taken but bread. That the oven is older than the town and will be warm after it.

**The proofing room — no id frozen (round 7 room)**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A door to the bakery. The station: slatted shelves under linen, and on the top shelf Tamsin's crock, fed one spoon at a time on the island's hour. A hiding spot behind the linen racks. A clue spot at the crock's lid, where a seal of wax would show who had lifted it.

**What the town says:** That the crock is the island's. That it is hers. That the island would say otherwise, and that nobody in HollowMere has ever asked the island.

Past the bakery the lane gave out into planks, and the planks gave out into pines. She lit the post at the wood's edge. *Five.* Touch. Light. Touch.

**Gallows Wood — forest**

**Light:** One lantern on the edge post, hers. Beyond it, none.

**What stands:** Planks laid over the mud at the near edge [fo.], tarred and warped. The first trees of Black Pine. A beam between two of them that was not put there for building.

**What the town says:** "The pines were not empty." That this is where the town hangs people when the Tribunal is too slow, or too fair. That a gamekeeper was paid by a dead man to keep this wood and keeps it still, and that if they come for him he will not go alone.

From the end of the planks she could see the marsh. Fennick's light stood at the marsh edge, low and steady. Further out were other lights, which were not steady. Harbourmaster Pell's skiff was pulled up at the dock with a stone in it, and Tuck's ferry pole lay along the boards. Some nights she had stood here and not known which light was Fennick's. Tonight she knew, because one of the others was coming nearer.

**The Tin Gallery mouth — cave_entry**

**Light:** None. Not on the round.

**What stands:** A mouth in the hill, timbered, black. Cold air out of it, with the smell of stone that has had water on its far side for a long time.

**What the town says:** That Jory Kitto went down here afraid of the dark and kept his lamp turned all the way up, though it cost him oil. That the plug went at 11:47; the town says that, the town says it still. That he was saving for a coat, for someone who was coming on the train.

She did not light the dark places. That was not a habit; that was the contract. But she stood at the mouth for the space of a breath, because Jory Kitto had paid for his own oil, and someone should.

On the way back up the lane she met the Watchman at the Constable's corner.

"Quiet," he said.

"So far."

He had chosen his door for the night. She could see it in how he stood, already leaning toward it. She did not ask which. He did not say. It was not a thing people said in HollowMere after the train had gone.

## Ring Two: Mill & Bank, and Manor Rise

*On nights when there are fewer of us, these doors are boarded. Tonight they are open.*

The Rise went up from the square past the bank, and the channel ran down from it past the mill. The town did not know why the boards went up some nights and not others. It knew only that when there were fewer people at the wake table, there were more boards, and it had stopped asking which came first.

**The Company Bank & Vault — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None. A Company padlock. A guard's stool at the door, empty, with a cushion on it.

**What stands:** A counter with a brass grille. The rent book, chained to it. At the back, the vault door, the Company's mark on its wheel.

**What the town says:** That the Clerk's Midday Rent goes in at noon and does not come out. That a man in a bandana has been seen looking at the vault door, and so has a woman with a soapbox's worth of anger. That in a year when banks are failing everywhere back east, this is the one bank that cannot fail, because the town cannot leave it.

**The Baron's study — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None. Kept dark for a man who comes only by train.

**What stands:** Above the bank, up a stair with its own lock. A desk nobody sits at. A chair turned to the window that faces the line.

**What the town says:** That Cornelius Thorne comes when the town riots, with Pinkertons and their dogs. That the room is aired the day before he comes, so that somebody always knows first; and that whoever airs it is not saying.

**The Waterwheel Lumber Mill & loft — no id (not a scene)**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** The waterwheel on the drained-lake channel. The saw floor. The plank stack. Above, the loft, with straw pallets in rows.

**What the town says:** That debt walks to the mill. That the Indebted sleep in the loft and are counted in the morning. That the wheel turns a little when the mere's tide runs, with no hand on it. That a fire has been promised here, by someone, for a long time.

She heard the wheel go over once as she passed, a slow wet turn, and stop. The circle on the chart had said low water. She went faster up the Rise.

**Vane Manor hall — manor_hall**

**Light:** Her lantern on the gatepost. *Six.* Inside, none.

**What stands:** Black crepe on the door. A hall clock stopped at 11:47 [mh.]. A safe set into the panelling. Muddy bootprints on the carpet, going in.

**What the town says:** That the clock stopped at 11:47; it is said by people who were not there. That nobody has said who stopped it, and the hands stand where they stand. That the bootprints lead nowhere, and everyone in HollowMere has followed them once.

**Vane Manor, upstairs — manor_upper**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A landing. The study door [mu.], locked.

**What the town says:** That there is a key in the barn and a key under the chapel, and that the town stops counting at two. That whoever cut the keys meant more than one hand to hold them. That the study has not been opened since the will.

**Hale Barn — barn**

**Light:** None.

**What stands:** A goat in the end stall. A study key on a nail [ba.]. A wolf pelt nailed to the door, head down.

**What the town says:** That the goat did not bleat that night, whichever night is meant. That the pelt is a warning to the wolves. That it is a warning from them.

Behind the barn the Rise fell away to the marsh, and the marsh path went into the Fenwood on the mud the low water had left. She took it as far as the circle allowed and not a step further. She had no post to light there. She went because the round was the round, and because a list kept is a list complete.

**The Fenwood — forest_deep**

**Light:** The drowned lantern, under the black water beneath the hanging oak, green-gold and steady [fd.]. Not hers. She has never lit it and has never been asked to.

**What stands:** The hanging oak, its roots in mud that was a shore. A charcoal clamp, smoking low, and Mother Moll's hut beside it with the door shut. The den, under a fallen trunk, which nobody stands in front of.

**What the town says:** That it is the old lake-light, which fell from the oak the first winter and did not go out. That it is the barman's sign lantern, which went into the mere in his arms. That nobody says who keeps it lit. That nobody says that anyone does.

She stood at the end of the dry mud and looked at it the way she looked at the others, as a light she had not lit. She thought, as she thought here every night, that a lantern that wanted no oil and no hand would never be docked. She did not envy it. Then the water began to come back over her boot soles, and she turned.

**The Flooded Gallery — cave_gallery**

**Light:** None. Not on the round.

**What stands:** Under the Fenwood, a pump beam rusted at its pivot. An ore cart on a rail that runs into water. A crate stencilled VANE TIN CO, half-pried open [cg.], and no crowbar near it.

**What the town says:** That there is an altar down there, at the deep end, and that six would be enough. That something sleeps in the water, and the mere-rot wakes it. That footsteps come up out of the gallery that no one is making, and that the cough comes up with them.

## Ring Three: The Chapel and the Undercroft

*Tonight these doors are boarded.*

She came home by St Brannock's, because St Brannock's was on the way home. Company men had nailed the boards up in the afternoon; the nail heads were still bright. There was nothing for her to light here. She came anyway, for the list.

**St Brannock's — chapel**

**Light:** Behind boards.

**What stands:** The boarded door. The Company bell in its frame, the Company's mark stamped into its shoulder. Inside, unseen tonight, the bell rope, with a torn glove caught in its lay [ch.]. In the yard, under the bell, Jory Kitto's grave. By the path, a gravestone with tomorrow's date on it, and a name cut new this morning.

**What the town says:** That Father Bray died. That Father Bray fled. That the new Vicar came on the train after, and that everyone in HollowMere confesses to him sooner or later. That the name on the stone is the next to die. That the stone kills, or that the stone only knows. That nobody has ever seen the name being cut.

She lifted the pole until its flame was level with the stone. Then she looked at the bell instead, and lowered it. There are two kinds of knowing in HollowMere, and only one of them can be put off by not looking. She did not know which kind this was. She did not find out.

**The Undercroft — cellar**

**Light:** Behind boards, from above. Below, none.

**What stands:** Under St Brannock's, a vault of brick. A study key on a hook by the stair [cl.]. Gas alcoves, north and south, their pipes capped. Rats.

**What the town says:** That the boards cover the chapel stair and that nothing covers the well. That marks in ash and something darker have been seen on lintels in the town and washed off before noon, or not washed off. That the cough comes up from here as well as from the fen.

She crossed back to the square. At the well she heard feet on the rungs below: one, and then another, slow, the way someone climbs who does not want to be heard climbing. Mere-rot makes false footsteps. Everyone knows that. She did not go to the lip, and she did not call down. The steps stopped. They did not start again.

## The Fourth Lantern

The wolves were at the fence. Three lanterns burned on the square, and the platform behind her, and the post at the wood's edge, and the gatepost on the Rise. Six. She shook the can and heard the last of the oil move in it. If this post stayed dark, the scrip for it would be docked. The will was in her head, as it was in everyone's. The chalk was under her boots. She put her hand on the post: the first touch.

**The square's fourth lantern — overworld**

**Light:**

**What stands:** The post. The chalk. The well head.

**What the town says:** That everything goes to whoever remains when the last lantern goes out. That the light on your post at dawn is who you are.

She raised the pole.
