# The HollowMere Gazette, Morning Edition

*Proof, front page, forme one. The pencil is the compositor's.*

---

**THE HOLLOWMERE GAZETTE**

**MORNING EDITION.** Thursday, 7 December 1893.

Printed at the Eastern Office for the Thorne & Expanse Line. Carried by the Daily Train. Delivered sealed. The property of the Company until read.

**THE GAZETTE PRINTS THE SECRETS OF THE PREVIOUS DAY, AS ARE FOUND OUT. IT PRINTS NO NAME THAT HAS NOT BEEN VERIFIED. WHERE A NAME IS NOT VERIFIED, A BLANK IS LEFT, AND THE TOWN MAY SUPPLY IT.**

---

## I. THE STONE

The stone with tomorrow's date reads, as wired:

**~~ADA BROOK~~**

The Gazette reports the stone. It does not account for it.

> *9.40. Stet, wire copy. Two words and a disclaimer. Easiest inch on the page.*

---

## II. LOST & FOUND

FOUND, on the platform, at the Station Rush: a child's tin whistle, dented. Apply at the Post Office.

FOUND, by the workbench in the railyard: a ticket-punch die, star pattern. It is not the Conductor's. Apply at the Post Office.

~~LOST, between the pump and the platform, Monday, at the Station Rush: a scarf, grey wool, darned in red at one end where the moth had it. It was her mother's. The finder may have a loaf a week from her until Christmas. Ada Brook, Mill Row, third door. She will pay in bread.~~

> *10.05. Brook. Same name as col. I.*
> *A common name. There are two of everything in a mill town.*

---

## III. COMPANY RENT RATES

Schedule A, as posted. The Clerk collects at the Midday Shift.

| Row | Per bed, per week |
|---|---|
| Mill Row | 1.10 scrip |
| Pump Row | 1.25 scrip |
| Railyard bunks | 0.90 scrip |

**Entered as Indebted, as of Wednesday's Midday:**

- BROOK, A. Mill Row, third door. Two weeks. To the Mill at Midday.
- COTTER, I. Settled. Departed by the Line.
- HADDON, B. Pump Row. One week. Warned.
- PIKE, S. (deceased). The account passes to the household.

> *10.20. Mill Row, third door. Col. II says third door.*
> *One woman.*
> *Wired the night desk at H.M.: IS ADA BROOK LIVING. Thirty cents, out of my own pocket. My own rent is Friday.*

---

## IV. NOTICES

**The Company.** Curfew runs from the departure of the Daily Train at Dusk until the Station Rush. The Midday Rent is collected at the Shift, in person, and in scrip. Coin is scrip until proven otherwise.

**The Apothecary.** Doc Mallory gives notice that the fen-cough is in the Rows again. It is not catching. Keep the windows shut against the mere. The Company asks that the word be *miasma*.

**St Brannock's.** The Vicar preaches at dusk. Enquiries regarding Father Bray: none answered.

**The Lamplighter.** The light in the Fenwood was seen again on Tuesday. The Company has not lit it. Do not follow it.

**The Vane Holdings.** The Company gives notice that no claim under the will of the late Lord Aldous Vane will be heard from any person who believes himself to be among those who remain, until the question of the last lantern is settled. The lanterns within the Grant are the Company's.

**STOP-PRESS.** *Received by wire, H.M. night desk:* ADA BROOK LIVING STOP AT MILL STOP STONE IS RUMOUR STOP GAZETTE PRINTS NOTHING UNVERIFIED

> *12.15. Living. Then the stone isn't verified, and the Rule is the Rule.*
> *Struck her from col. I. Set a blank in her place. — 7.*
> *There. Now she's only a woman who lost a scarf.*

---

## V. THE DAILY TRAIN

Conductor of record: A. Pruett, No. 14.

| Station | Down | Up |
|---|---|---|
| Thorne Junction | Day | Dusk |
| Widow's Cut | Day | Dusk |
| Gallows Wood Siding | on signal | on signal |
| HollowMere | Day | Dusk |

**Inbound, Car 2.** Flour, Crumb's Ovens. Whiskey, the Drowned Lantern. Tonics, the Morgue. Lead and powder, the railyard. Mail, one bag. Gazette, one bundle, sealed.

**Outbound at Dusk, Car 3.** Coffin, one. Consigned from Mill Row. Name not entered.

> *12.40. Not entered.*
> *A blank. Not mine, though. Theirs.*
> *They book ahead in winter. A Company booking. Set as wired.*

---

## VI. CLASSIFIED

**CRUMB'S OVENS.** Wake loaves for Mill Row, after the island custom: one for each mourner and three for the dead, each with its crumb token under wax. Ten ordered. Paid in full, in scrip, on Tuesday by A. Brook, who asks that the Row not be charged.

**WANTED,** at Hale Barn: a girl to mind a goat. Must not be afraid of the pelt on the door.

> *1.05. Paid Tuesday. That was before the stone.*
> *She has the cough, then. See col. IV. A woman with the cough looks at Christmas and pays ahead. People do.*
> *Box it in mourning rule, house style for a death already notified.*

---

## VII. WANTED

**THE BANDIT**

For the score at Crumb's Ovens, Monday night. The till was emptied. One gold coin was left on the counter, as a tip.

The Company's three particulars:

- **Height:** short, by the bootprint in the spilt flour.
- **Scarf:** grey wool. Thread found caught on the till.
- **Hat:** none recovered.

**Name:** ____________. Unverified. The Town may supply it.

> *1.30. Grey wool. → col. II.*
> *Not the cough. Not settled at all. The Bell, then the trap.*

> *1.32. Pull col. II. Refund her the scrip.*
> *Kill the inch with a rule. If her scarf isn't on the page they can't lay it beside this.*

---

## VIII. YESTERDAY'S SECRETS

As found out, Wednesday:

- ______ of Pump Row struck ______ at the Drowned Lantern over a hand of cards, and paid for neither the glass nor the eye.
- ______ owes the Company Store eleven scrip and has stopped buying salt.
- ______ has been seen at the fence after curfew, feeding something through it.
- A woman of Mill Row, name withheld, asked at the Post Office after a grey scarf darned in red. She was told nothing had been handed in. She asked twice. ______.

> *1.50. Verified copy. Can't pull it. It's what we're for.*
> *The town was there. They heard her ask.*
