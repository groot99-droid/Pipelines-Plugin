# Standing Orders

*[Binding note: three books of different sizes, trimmed to the smallest and sewn on three cords into plain grey boards. The spine is stamped blind, without ink: STANDING ORDERS. No imprint, no binder's ticket, no date. Three silk ribbons, one sewn into the gathering of each book. The endpapers are blank.]*

---

## Book I

**GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS TO OPERATIVES ON DETAIL**
*to the Thorne & Expanse Line, Coldwater Territory*

*Issued by the Agency, back east. To be carried on the person. Not to be shown.*

Operative No. *6*
Detail: *Mr C. Thorne*
Strength: Operatives *9*. Dogs *4*.

*[Binding note: a card-book in an oilcloth cover, the corners worn round. The blanks are filled in one ink. The margins are in pencil and in two other inks, in several hands, none signed. On the back board, the ring of a glass, twice.]*

---

### Order 1. Of the Principal

1. The detail travels in the private car with the Principal. It is not entered on the passenger count of the Line.
2. At every platform the dogs go down first, the operatives after them, and the Principal last.
3. The detail does not carry the Principal's bags. Bags are for the platform crew.
4. The detail does not speak to the town. The town is spoken to by the Constable.

> *I took off my hat. I took his bags. He gave me scrip for the carrying and I bowed at the hip, and the dog beside him watched me do it. I pay a glass every week on my brother's line here, that's closed since the cough. I paid it that night with the Baron's scrip. It didn't feel like paying.*

---

### Order 2. Of the Dogs

Dogs on strength: *Major. Bess. Turk. Jip.*

1. The dogs do not maul unless provoked.
2. A dog gives voice at a man who runs, a man at a lock, and a man with a weapon out.
3. A dog that gives voice is answered by its operative. It is not hushed.
4. The dogs are not taken past the last lantern.

> *The wolf didn't bark. That's how I know the one from the other. I have the difference under the binding on my arm. I go up to the stone every dawn and read it to the Row, and these four watch me up and watch me down, and not one of them has made a sound at me. I'm not running. I've no lock and no knife. I've an arm I can't close.*

> *They whine at the fen edge. Every dog of the four. They stand where the lanterns stop and won't go a step past, and nobody had to read them this page.*

---

### Order 3. Of Public Unrest

The Company's Clerk enters the temper of the town each day under one of four heads. The detail is governed by the head, as follows.

| Temper, as entered | The detail |
|---|---|
| Calm | Not deployed. |
| Panicked | Not deployed. The Principal is wired. |
| Rioting | Deployed by the first train. The square is cleared. The soapbox is removed and is not returned. |
| Entranced | Not provided for. |

> *Fri 20 Oct. Wed 15 Nov, the day after. Sat 18 Nov. Thu 14 Dec, and that time they stayed.*

---

### Order 4. Of Answering the Armed

An unholstered weapon within the hearing of the dogs is answered. The baton answers first. The rifle answers after. The order of the answer is the Agency's.

The manner is not set down here.

*[Binding note: the margins of this leaf are clean on both sides.]*

---

### Order 5. Of the Telegraph Curfew

1. Curfew is declared by the detail on receipt of a wire from the Company's key. The Company's key is worked by the Clerk alone.
2. From the declaration, only the Lamplighter's lanterns may be lit.
3. The detail keeps to the lit ground. The dark beyond the lantern is not warranted by the Company, and is not warranted by the Agency.

> *They wait at each post while I light it, and walk on when it's lit, two of them and a dog, as if I were lighting for them. I'm paid by the lantern. I don't light for anybody. I leave the one over the chalk and the well till last, the same as always. I don't tell them that.*

---

### Order 6. Of Wages and Rations

The wages, rations, lodging and ammunition of the detail are charged by the Company against the accounts of the Grant. The peaceable are not excused.

> *Twine, ordered on account, for a man who drowned in water to his ankles. I mend the nets he'd have used and sell them to nobody. Now the same line carries a share of nine men's bread and four dogs' meat. I mend at the window here for the light. They drink standing. They don't look at my hands.*

---

### Order 7. Of the Death of the Principal

1. Should the Principal die within the Grant, the detail wires east, holds its post, and awaits instruction.
2. Until instruction comes, the detail answers to the Principal, or to such person as the Principal's writ names: ________________________.

*[Pasted in below, two telegraph slips, copied out in the ink of the blanks:]*

> **Wired east.** ESCORT REMAINS IN TOWN STOP AWAIT INSTRUCTION STOP
>
> **Reply.** COMPANY TAKES NO POSITION ON MATTERS OF FAITH STOP ESCORT IS YOURS TO INSTRUCT SIR STOP

*[Binding note: the blank at 2 is not filled.]*

---

### Order 8. Of Return

The detail returns by the first train after the Principal's departure, and not before.

> *The coach road shut Tuesday the 19th. The last train went down at dusk on Saturday the 23rd, and they weren't on it. Nothing has come up since. Mornings they stand at the Company counter. Afternoons they keep the square in their long coats, facing the bank, the way you'd wait on a door that might open. The dogs lie down when they stand still.*
>
> *Nine came. Seven stand.*
>
> *Two were at the Gate.*
>
> *Thurs. 28th.*

*[Binding note: Ribbon I lies at this leaf.]*

## Book II

**RULE OF THE ROUNDS OF THE IRON-WARDENS OF THE GUILD**
*Of Port-au-Levain. Printed at the press of the Counting-House at Candlemas, in the year 1789, by order of the Council. To be read to every Warden at his taking of the staff, and kept.*

[PROPERTY OF THE COUNTING-HOUSE]

*[Binding note: a quarto gathering, stained along its lower edge by damp from a cold wall. Four nail holes at the head of the first leaf, each ringed with rust. The margins are scratched with a point that is not a pen, in several hands, except one pressed in flour and one in ink. The print is not altered on any leaf.]*

---

### Title I. Of the Rounds

**Art. 1.** The watches of the night are counted from the dusk loaf, set by L'Aboyeur on the empty platter nearest the Hearth, for a name under seal. The rounds of the Wardens begin from it. A Warden does not ask whose.

**Art. 2.** There are four rounds, at the four watches. At each round a Warden passes the Hall, the Counting-House, the Wharf steps and Gallows Square, in that order, and back in the other.

---

### Title II. Of the Habit

**Art. 3.** A Warden wears brass-plated leather, buckled to the Guild's pattern and kept bright at his own charge. He carries the shock-staff over the shoulder, and carries it so in the Hall as in the street.

**Art. 4.** The staff is laid on the Commons. It is shown to the Guild. In the presence of the Gallery it is neither raised nor lowered, but held still.

**Art. 5.** A holder of the Gallery is not hauled. He is accompanied, a pace behind, at his own pace. A holder of the Commons is hauled, at the Warden's.

The manner of either is not set down in this book.

---

### Title III. Of the Log from the Tower

**Art. 6.** After every proofing bell two Wardens go down the reef road for the Tide-Watcher's log, which is lowered to them through the hatch on its cord and drawn up again when read.

**Art. 7.** A line struck through in the log is read. Wardens are taught to read through ink. A struck line is a line.

---

### Title IV. Of the Hall at Midnight

**Art. 8.** At the midnight proofing bell the Hall is locked from outside, and the key goes on the round.

---

### Title V. Of the Cold Pantry and the Stool

**Art. 9.** The accused waits in the cold pantry, which is the only room of the Sacred Kitchen with its lock on the outside. This Rule shall be fixed inside its door, so that the accused may know what holds him.

**Art. 10.** At dawn the Wardens bring the accused up and set him on the stool. They turn out his coat onto the trial table, and touch nothing on it.

*[Beside Art. 10, a thumb pressed in flour. No letters.]*

---

### Title VI. Of the Alarm

**Art. 11.** The alarm of the Wardens is three strokes, and three, and three. Lanterns come down the hill on it, and every door that hears it is shut.

*[Scratched, in capitals, the letters uneven:]*

> *I TOOK IT OF THE SCONSE TO SEE MAMAN HOME ALONG THE WARF. IT WAS DARK. IT WAS ONLY THE END OF IT*

*[Beneath, three short strokes and one long, then three short again, as a tune might be written by someone who could not write one.]*

---

### Title VII. Of Tariffs Unpaid

**Art. 12.** Where the yeast-tax goes unpaid in more than one quarter of the island on one night, the Wardens shut the gates of the Hall and of the Counting-House and cry the curfew from the Square.

**Art. 13.** A holder Unregistered three nights together is brought to the cold pantry on the third.

> *Her ring is on its cord at my neck. They may value it when I have done wearing it.*

---

### Title VIII. Of the Crown's Man

**Art. 14.** When the Crown sends a Grand Inspector-General, the Council shall go down to the Wharf to receive him, and the Wardens shall stand at the Council's back, two paces behind, with staves shouldered.

> *Nobody went down to him but the Wardens.*

---

### Title IX. Of the Order of Rank in the Pantry

**Art. 15.** No holder of the Gallery shall be held in the cold pantry. A Gallery holder waits in his own house, at his own table, and is accompanied from it.

*[In ink, a fine hand:]*

> *The young man with the staff addresses me as "the accused". I have corrected him. Madame. He has not been taught to say it, and there is no time now to teach him.*
>
> *I have asked for the heel of the loaf. I have always had the heel. It has not come.*
>
> *I am held in a room the article says I am not held in. I shall tell the Chancellor that his article is wrong. I shall see him at six.*
>
> *the 24th*

---

### Title X. Of Whose Hand

**Art. 16.** The Wardens serve the Council and the Hearth, and no other hand.

**Art. 17.** This Rule shall not be amended save by the Council.

*[Scratched, small, in an old woman's hand. Under it, the date 1793:]*

> *Who will carry the cage to the window? The bird must see the harbour every morning or it will not sing. I have asked the Warden. It is the same Warden who stood behind my chair at the ball, with the same staff, held still. He has chosen, he says, as they all chose. He says the committee has not provided for birds.*

*[Binding note: Ribbon II lies at this leaf. The print is the print of 1789 throughout.]*

## Book III

**RELEASED IN PART**
EXEMPTIONS (b)(1) (b)(3) · RELEASE DATE ████████████

**GROUNDS 99 · ORDER ENFORCEMENT AGENTS**
**STANDING ORDERS · ROLL CALL DETAIL**
ISSUED MON 6 OCT 52 · REISSUED SAT 13 DEC 52 · CORRECTED FRI 19 DEC 52
SPONSORING OFFICE: ██████████████
POST IN GUARDROOM. NOT FOR DETAINEE CIRCULATION.

*[Binding note: two mimeographed sheets folded to a gathering of eight leaves, with staple holes and a drawing-pin hole at each corner. Pencil throughout, in the margins and between the typed lines. The hand is very small, and grows smaller toward the end. Several hands. Each is signed with a number, or not at all.]*

---

**1. THE ROLL.**

1.1 AT THE WHISTLE THE DETAINEE HAS FIFTEEN SECONDS TO STAND BESIDE HIS ASSIGNED BUNK. A BUNK WITHOUT A DETAINEE AT THE END OF FIFTEEN SECONDS IS LIT.
1.2 AGENTS DO NOT SPEAK THE NUMBER. AGENTS LOOK AT IT.
1.3 DETAINEES NOT IN PLACE ARE RECOVERED. SEE 4.

> *Brown button, off the coat they took at the gate. In the cheek, so the teeth don't go. A man whose teeth go is saying something. — 4*

---

**2. EQUIPMENT.**

2.1 MASK, PROTECTIVE. WORN ON DETAIL AT ALL TIMES.
2.2 MASKS FOR LABOUR NOT AUTHORIZED.
2.3 ARC-LIGHT, CARBON, 60 IN. TWO (2), FOUNDRY ROOF AND YARD.
2.4 DOGS. REPLACEMENTS AS REQUISITIONED.
2.5 GAS, AND RIOT SLUG-GUNS, ARE ISSUED ON THE WARDEN'S HORN AND RETURNED ON IT.
2.6 THE MANNER OF THEIR USE IS NOT SET DOWN HERE.

> *Masks for them, so they can breathe it. None for us, so they can measure us breathing it.*

---

**3. ABSENT AND SURPLUS.**

3.1 A BILLET WITHOUT A BODY IS ENTERED ABSENT.
3.2 A BODY WITHOUT A BILLET IS ENTERED SURPLUS.
3.3 NEITHER IS ENTERED BY NAME.

> *Fri 10 Oct. Sawmill 3 asked at the counter whether the train had brought mail. Told no. He thanked the clerk for looking.*

> *Fri 21 Nov. Sawmill 3 not at his slot when the whistle stopped. Dogs out. Lights over the pond and the trestle. Nothing found. His billet still on the rack in the morning. Absent.*

---

**4. RECOVERY.**

4.1 RECOVERED DETAINEES ARE DELIVERED TO ████████████ AND NOT TO THE INFIRMARY.
4.2 THE ROUTE IS NOT LOGGED.
4.3 AGENTS DO NOT DESCRIBE THE ROUTE, THE ROOM OR THE DETAINEE.

> *Tue 25 Nov, about two. Sawmill 8, across the yard in a sack between two masks, under the arc-light. Not heavy. You could see that from the way they carried him. His ribs through the burlap, every one, like a washboard. One hand out of the mouth of the sack. Fingers black to the second knuckle. No nails. The cold came off him in the light like steam off a horse.*
>
> *He sang bass at the bread line. He saved his sugar for the man in the next bunk, who has the cough. He told me once he had a boy in Duluth who'd be eight at Christmas.*
>
> *The hand moved. It opened and shut, slow, like it was counting.*
>
> *I am not describing the route.*

---

**5. THE MESS HALL AFTER ROLL.**

5.1 AFTER ROLL CALL THE MESS HALL IS CLEARED OF STAFF.
5.2 A HUM IN THE MESS HALL IS NOT AN ASSEMBLY. AGENTS DO NOT ENTER.
5.3 GAS IS NOT TO BE USED IN THE MESS HALL.

> *They use it in the yard and never in the mess. Ask yourself why. I did, and I stopped humming, and then I heard I'd started again.*

---

**6. STEAM MAINS.**

6.1 ON THE RUPTURE OF A STEAM MAIN THE DETAIL DEPLOYS AT ONCE, MASKED, WITH LIGHTS AND DOGS.
6.2 DETAINEES ARE HELD STANDING AT THEIR SLOTS UNTIL THE MAIN IS CLOSED.

> *Tue 11 Nov. The boiler main. Four hours at the slots. I counted to keep from sitting down. Eins, zwei. My mother's counting. I caught myself at it and went on. — 7*

> *Tue 18 Nov. The seam. The same four hours.*

---

**7. THE DEEP LEVEL.**

7.1 OIL, LAMP, 1 QT. REFUGE, DEEP LEVEL. WEEKLY. STANDING.
7.2 THE DETAIL ESCORTS THE MAN WHO CARRIES IT TO THE WINZE, AND NO FARTHER.
7.3 WHO FILLS THE LAMP IS NOT LOGGED.

---

**8. REISSUE, SAT 13 DEC 52.**

8.1 SEARCH OF CAMP PREMISES FOR ████████████ IS SUSPENDED BY ██████████████.
8.2 DO NOT ENTER IN LOG.
8.3 ORDERS 4.1 TO 4.3 STAND.
8.4 A DETAINEE PRESENTING ████ ████████ ████ ███ IS PASSED THROUGH THE GATE WITHOUT ENTRY.

> *Friday they took a man in a good coat off the payroll platform. Saturday the masks went through every hut, under every floor. By night they'd stopped, all at once, the way they stop when the whistle stops. Nothing found. Nobody's been down since. You would think somebody helped.*

---

**9. CORRECTION, FRI 19 DEC 52.** *(in ink, on the stencil)*

STEAM OUT, ALL MAINS. ARC-LIGHTS OUT. ROLL BY HAND LAMP.
1.1 CANNOT BE COMPLIED WITH.

*(in another ink, below)*
SAT 20 DEC, 0600. INSPECTOR-GENERAL NOT ACCOUNTED FOR. ROLL STANDS AS TAKEN.

> *Fri 19 Dec. No light. The whistle went, and nobody could be lit, so nobody was missing. We stood by the bunks in the dark. I put my hand out to the right, and there was a shoulder there. I don't know whose bunk it was. I don't know whose shoulder. I kept my hand on it till the dogs came back.*

*[Binding note: Ribbon III lies at this leaf.]*
