# The Testament of the Mother of Mothers

*Being the Archive of the Sovereign Sourdough Council, as kept in the Counting-House of Port-au-Levain, its last entry dated in the year 1893.*

[PROPERTY OF THE COUNTING-HOUSE]

> "Out here beyond the reef, bread isn't just food. It's law, it's lineage, and it's life."
> — from the proclamation-board of the Council, painted over three times and never changed

---

## Of the Rock

**The Reef.** It is the one way in. Black stone lies under green water, and the channel through it is known to pilots and to the drowned. The Morning Ferry is the island's clock. It casts off at dawn, and what misses it stays until the next dawn, or for good. The distance to the mainland is a distance the Council has never published.

**The Great Hearth & Hall of Platters.** The Hearth is the oldest fire on the rock and has never been let go out. The Hall around it holds a silver platter for every soul on the island. On each platter sits a Wake Loaf stamped with its owner's guild seal. A soul without a platter has no name in law. A platter without a loaf is a death, or a sentence, or both.

**The Windmill Cliffs.** Here the Council's flour is ground. In fog the fishermen read the turning sails the way other people read a clock face. When the sails stop, the wind has stopped, and when the wind stops the island waits.

**The Subterranean Yeast Vault.** The Mother of Mothers lives here, in a vat of pressurized glass, fed by steam that rises out of the mountain's throat. The Vault has two keys and one feeding hand. Below the vat there is only heat.

**The Salt-Flats & Smuggler's Wharf.** This is where things come ashore that the Counting-House did not count. The Salt-Flat Drowners walk here at low tide, and the Tide-Ghouls when the tide is wrong. Neither is entered in the census. Both are entered in the burial rolls.

**The Alchemist-Herbalist's Apothecary.** It sells tinctures and sour-salts, and keeps a register of poisons that the Inquisitor reads every quarter.

**Gallows Square.** It is the second-oldest structure on the rock, after the Hearth. The rope is replaced each spring whether it has been used or not.

**The Lighthouse.** It lights the reef and not the town. When the fog comes in off the water it takes the harbour lamps one by one, like a hand pinching wicks.

**The Counting-House.** It holds the archive, the tariffs, the Guild Copper and the yeast tokens. It is this book's home and, some will say, this book's author.

**The Port Tavern.** Here rumour is weighed before the Council weighs it. Its weights are kinder.

**The Sacred Kitchen.** Ten rooms, entered here as the liturgy enters them:
- The **cellar**, for the cool rest.
- The **cold pantry**, for what is kept.
- The **command desk**, where the day's bake is ordered.
- The **dish pit**, for penance.
- The **expediter**, where nothing passes unseen.
- **Front of house**, where the people receive.
- **Hygiene**, where purity is inspected.
- **Lamination**, for patience in folds.
- The **shaping bench**, where a loaf becomes a person's.
- The **sub-basement**, the door above the Vault, whose key is one of the two.

**The Council & the Guilds.** The Sovereign Sourdough Council sits in the gallery above the Hall. Below it sit the guild offices: Master Proof-Reader, Spice-Master, Tide-Watcher, Hearth-Warden, Ledger-Clerk. The Iron-Wardens enforce what the Council decides, and do not ask why.

**Copper & Yeast-Tax.** Every platter owes a tariff, paid in Guild Copper and in yeast tokens. *An unpaid tariff makes a loaf Unregistered. An Unregistered loaf is a soul the Council no longer feeds.*

**The Law.** The Inquisitor asks. The Guild Bailiff takes. The High Chancellor decides. There are three verdicts:
- Acquittal.
- A Guild Fine.
- Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth. *The Hearth receives the loaf, and then the one the loaf named.*

> the loaf sitting on your silver plate at dawn is who you are.

## Book One: Of the Mother

**The Bringing.** Four hundred years before this entry, the Master Bakers came over the reef with a crock wrapped in wool. They carried it the way one carries a sleeping child, as though it might wake and cry. The Mother of Mothers was older than their names. They set her down where the mountain breathed, and the mountain fed her.

**The Hymn.** The founding hymn is sung at every first loaf. It has four verses and a refrain, and children learn it before they can count. They are counting when they sing it, though they do not know it. The first verse is the feeding hour. The second is the steam, given in the old measure. The third is the flour-to-water, and the fourth is the rest. Whoever forgets the song forgets the Mother. The Council has never written the song down, because a song cannot be confiscated.

*[— in another hand: the second verse is wrong in every copy after the plague year. Sing it a breath slower.]*

**The Vat.** The first vat was clay and the second was copper. The third, which stands now, is glass, so that the Mother can be watched. Glass also means that anyone who comes down the stair can see her: a pale tide inside a lamp, breathing.

**The Platters.** The first census on the rock was baked, not written. Each family shaped one loaf from the Mother's leaven and set it on a plate. The Council counted plates. Where there was a plate, there was a person. Ink came later, to keep the count honest. It has not.

## Book Two: Of Bread Made Law

**Lineage.** A child's first loaf is shaped from a parent's leaven. So the line of a house is not written but fermented: what fed the mother feeds the daughter, and what soured in the father sours in the son. A disinherited child is given flour and no leaven. On the rock, that is the whole of the punishment, and it is enough.

**The Tariff.** The yeast-tax was first levied in the eleventh year of the Council. The names of the first loaves left Unregistered are kept in this ledger:

████████, a net-mender.
████, who kept goats on the cliff path.
████████████, and her two.
███, no trade given.

*[— in another hand: the goatkeeper's name was Ysolde. She sang to the goats. She was not behind in her tariff; she was behind in her prayers.]*

**The Four Sour-Practices.** The Council records four heresies against the Mother. Each is given here as it was charged and judged, and no further.

*Leaven-Rot, against the Abbot of the Crust.* This was the belief that the Mother could be soured on purpose, and that a sour Mother could be prayed through. Its heretics were named by office, not by name: the Spore-Caller, the Groat-Master, the Cellar-Ghoul and the Arch-Fermenter. Verdict: Trial by Fire, all four. The Arch-Fermenter asked to keep his apron, which his wife had mended twice at the same corner. It was not permitted.

*The Pyre-Master, against the Grand Inspector-General.* This was the belief that the Hearth belonged to the island and not to the Council. Verdict: Trial by Fire. *[STRUCK BY ORDER OF THE HIGH CHANCELLOR]*

*The Smuggler-Blackguard.* This was a pocket-mortar at the Wharf, and loaves swapped on the platters, so that one man's name sat on another's bread. Verdict: a Guild Fine, then Gallows Square when the fine went unpaid.

*The Black-Crust Contract.* This was a message rolled into a pigeon's cylinder and a glaze the Apothecary's register could not account for. The bird was never found. Verdict: ████████.

*[— in another hand: the Black-Crust woman kept a child's loaf back from her own platter every week for a year. The Council called it hoarding. It was for the boy she lost in the plague year.]*

**Trial by Fire.** The Hearth has received ████ souls since the Council first sat.

## Book Three: Of the Offices

**The Old Brigade.** Before the Council had titles it had a kitchen, and the kitchen had ranks. They survive as the island's oldest offices, older than the gallery and the seal. Each is entered with its duty and its disgrace.

- **Le Boulanger.** The bake. Disgrace: a loaf that does not rise on the day of a wedding.
- **Le Levain.** The feeding hand, the only hand that touches the Mother. Hereditary. Disgrace: there is no recorded disgrace of this office, which is itself the record.
- **Le Commis.** The carrying hands. They carry flour, water and ash. They carry everything to the Mother and nothing from her. A Commis may carry, but may never feed the Mother.
- **L'Apprenti.** The one who watches. Disgrace: speaking.
- **Le Rat de Cave.** Keeper of the cellar door. Disgrace: the door.
- **Le Champignon.** The office of the first heresy, abolished. Its seal was ground into the floor of the dish pit, where the Plongeur kneels on it.
- **Le Chef de Cuisine** and **Le Sous-Chef.** Command and its shadow. These became the Council and its clerks.
- **L'Aboyeur.** The caller at the expediter, who calls each loaf by its owner's name as it passes. The island's first herald.
- **Le Tourrier.** The pastry, the folds, lamination. Disgrace: haste.
- **Le Plongeur.** The dish pit. Penance, by sentence or by birth.
- **Le Garde Manger.** The cold pantry and what is kept there.
- **L'Inspecteur Sanitaire.** The hygiene room, and the forerunner of the Grand Inspector-General.
- **Le Critique Gastronomique.** The outsider whose verdict the Council could not overrule. The office has been vacant for two hundred years. The Council keeps a chair for it, so that no one may sit in it.
- **Le Croissant Rassis.** "The stale one." This is not an office but a title, given to a disgraced holder of any other.

**The House of Vane.** The Vanes are a Council family. Their platters sit nearest the Hearth, where the silver goes faintly gold from four centuries of heat. For as long as the archive runs, the office of Le Levain has passed down their eldest line. Theirs is the feeding hand. The Mother has known the touch of no other house.

## Book Four: Of the Second Son

**The Stamping.** In the year ████, a younger son of the house of Vane came to his shaping. His name was Aldous. The Council looked at his loaf, at his line and at his elder brother. It stamped his platter **Commis**, not Levain. The feeding hand went to the elder line, as it always had. Aldous bowed to the gallery, as the custom is. From that day his platter said what he was: a Vane who may carry, but may never feed the Mother. He carried flour down the Vault stair every morning for six years. Every morning he set it at the foot of the vat and climbed back up without touching the glass.

*[— in another hand: he counted the steps. Ninety-one. He told me once that the Mother breathed slower when his brother fed her, and faster when no one did.]*

**The Cup.** He was a Vane who would never feed the Mother, and on a night in the seventh year he went down to her anyway. The archive is plain about what happened and silent about how. The sub-basement door was opened with its key. The Vault was opened. By whose hand the record does not say; it names only a keyholder the record bars.

The keyholder on the night in question was ████████████.

He took a cup. It was a single cup, the kind a Commis uses to carry water down. For the first time in four hundred years, part of the Mother of Mothers was outside the vat. The steam went on rising into glass that held a cup less of her than it ever had. The keyholder locked the door behind him, and the record locked the keyholder behind a bar.

*[— in another hand: ]*

**The Short Count.** At the dawn feeding the elder brother found the Mother lower in her glass than the hymn allowed. The Hearth-Warden was called, and the Inquisitor after him. A thing outside the vat is a thing outside the law, and the Inquisitor opened a file on it before the fog had lifted from the Hall.

By then the Morning Ferry had cast off. On it, according to the ferryman's tally, was a young man with a carrying-cup wrapped in wool, held the way one holds a sleeping child. Aldous Vane was gone inland with the cup. The Inquisitor's file was open, and nothing on the rock could close it except the one who had left.

**Verdict in Absence.** The Inquisitor's file was open and the accused was across the water, so the Council did what the law allows. It tried an empty chair. The charge was theft of the Mother. The verdict was Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.

The accused could not be given to the fire, so his loaf was given in his stead. His Wake Loaf was lifted from the Vane platter and carried down the Hall by the Aboyeur, who called his name as it passed. Then it was set in the Hearth. The platter was left where it stood, empty, nearest the fire, and stamped:

[UNREGISTERED]

## Book Five: Of Tin

**The Years.** The record of the next years is short.
- Year one: the platter stands empty and Unregistered in the Hall. The house of Vane pays its tariff on the other platters and not on that one.
- Year three: the same.
- Year six: the house of Vane falls behind. Two Vane cousins are moved to the dish pit.
- Year nine: ████████.

**Copper from Inland.** In the eleventh year copper came ashore at Smuggler's Wharf. It did not come through the Counting-House. It came in crates stamped **VANE TIN CO**, set on the salt at low tide by men who did not stay for supper. The copper was good, mainland coin melted down and recast in the Guild's weight. It paid the house of Vane's arrears, every year of them, to the token.

The empty platter was meant to be the end of him. A son the Council had unwritten was meant to stay unwritten. Instead he fed his house from across the water. The island learned what everyone inland already knew: there was tin in the ground back there, before the Barons came for it, and a man named Vane had found it. The Vane house was fed by copper from the stolen bread. Every loaf on its platters, the elder brother's among them, was risen on it.

*[— in another hand: the elder brother fed the Mother every dawn with hands his brother paid for. He never once said so.]*

**The Sealing.** The house was fed by copper, and soon the Counting-House was too. The tariffs of the Vane house had become the largest single sum in the ledger. In a lean year the Council does not burn its largest sum. So the Vane seat in the gallery was let stand. The chair was empty, but it was not struck. And wherever the name of Aldous Vane appeared in the archive, in the Inquisitor's file, in the verdict, in the ferryman's tally, a clerk set the Council's seal over it.

**Rumours Cross.** *The following is entered as an aside and is not part of the record of the house of Vane.*

In these years rumours began to reach the island from a place inland called HollowMere. One was of a town at the end of a railroad, where the Barons own the dirt under your boots. One was of a baker there who set a wake table with one loaf for each mourner and three for the dead, each sealed with a crumb in wax. That is the island's custom, said in another accent. One was of a sickness in a drowned marsh that made the wolves bold.

Rumours of the island seemed to reach HollowMere too. A clerk of the Counting-House noted that the crossings fell on days when the vat ran sour. He was not asked to note it again.

## Book Six: Of the Return

**The Ferry Home.** In the year 1893, when the banks were failing inland and the railroads after them, the Morning Ferry brought back a man who was coughing.

His name was under a Council seal, and the seal had been the bargain: the copper came, the name stayed buried, and the man stayed away. He broke it by stepping onto the wharf. He was the younger son, and he looked older than his brother. He coughed with the wet, low cough of the inland fens, which the island had no name for. In his coat, wrapped in wool, he carried a cup.

He went up through the town in the fog and asked for the Ledger-Clerk by office. He asked one thing: that his platter be registered before he died.

*"It is empty,"* the Clerk said.
*"I know,"* he said. *"I paid for the others."*

The archive records that the cup came back lighter than it left. It records nothing further on the point.

So the cup was back on the rock, and Aldous Vane, who had been out of reach for thirty years, was in the Council's reach.

**The Verdict Called Due.** The Inquisitor had him in reach, and so the file was reopened. It had never been closed, only sealed. The case went up to the High Chancellor. The High Chancellor ruled from the gallery, and the Council's stenographer took it down:

*A verdict passed in absence is not discharged by a loaf. The loaf was the accused's proxy, not his sentence. The accused being present, the verdict stands, and is due.*

The Hearth was set for him. The Iron-Wardens took him to the cold pantry to wait. It was the coolest room in the Sacred Kitchen, and the only one with a lock on the outside. The old verdict was called due for the third dawn.

*[— in another hand: he asked for flour. They did not give him any. He shaped nothing with his hands for two nights and I think that was worse to him than the fire.]*

**The Night Before the Hearth.** The verdict was called due, and the Hearth was banked high, and the Hall was swept. On the night before, the Rat de Cave brought the prisoner's water, and the entry for that night reads in full:

*The prisoner was found at the second bell.*
*He was not breathing.*
████████████████████████████████████████

Aldous Vane died with his sentence unserved. The archive does not say whether it was the cough he brought from the fens, the fog that came under the pantry door, or a hand. On the rock people ask what the island has always asked, in the Tavern and in the Hall and under their breath at the platters: *who did it.* The Council has not answered.

In his coat they found the cup, washed and empty, and a folded tide chart of the mainland coast with the next low tide circled in pencil. The tide had come and gone two days before. He had never used it.

The news reached the mainland before any ferry could have carried it, by wire or by rumour. The archive cannot say which. Inland, in HollowMere, he was Lord Aldous Vane. There his widow keeps the parlour, his manor stands with its clock stopped, and his will was read. There he is three nights dead, and has been for as long as anyone on the rock can reckon.

*[— in another hand: three nights. It is always three nights, there. I have stopped asking how.]*

**The Ordinance of Dusk.** A sentence unserved is a wound in the law, and the Council does not leave wounds open. It cannot burn a dead man, and it cannot leave a verdict standing over nothing. So the Council ordained a third thing.

From that night on, every night on Port-au-Levain begins with his death. At dusk, when the Morning Ferry is long gone and the fog has taken the harbour lamps, the Aboyeur carries a Wake Loaf down the Hall. He calls no name. He sets it on the empty Vane platter nearest the Hearth, and the night is reckoned from that loaf. The night watches are counted from it. The Iron-Wardens begin their rounds from it. Whatever happens on the rock after dark, and things do, happens in the hours after Aldous Vane is dead.

The loaf is taken away at dawn and never eaten.

### I. The Record Reconsidered

From this point the archive is kept in one hand only, and it is not the Counting-House's.

*[— in another hand: I have corrected this book for thirty years. I have restored the goatkeeper's name and the Black-Crust woman's boy. I have sung the second verse a breath slower. I have unsealed every name in it but one.]*

### III. The Cup

The cup was taken from the dead man's coat and carried down the ninety-one steps by the elder brother, who held the Levain office. What it held was poured back into the vat. The Mother rose in her glass, a little, and has fed the island since.

The cup came back lighter than it left.

*[— in another hand: I will not write why, because I do not know. I will write what I do know. The rumours from inland have not stopped. They have not slowed.
