# Ordinances of the Sovereign Sourdough Council

*Of Port-au-Levain, with the Letters Patent of the Crown. Printed at the press of the Counting-House in the year 1787, by order, and to be read aloud in the Hall of Platters once in every year, at the first bell after Candlemas.*

[PROPERTY OF THE COUNTING-HOUSE]

*[red, 1793: Read aloud this year at the first bell after Candlemas, as directed, by the committee. We have kept the day.]*

---

## The Letters Patent

His Majesty, by the grace of God King of France and of Navarre, to all who shall see these present letters, greeting.

Our well-beloved the Master Bakers of Our island of Port-au-Levain, beyond the reef and in the fog of Our seas of New France, have shown Us that from time out of mind they have kept upon that rock a Hearth that has not gone out, a Hall of Platters in which every soul of the island has its place, and a living leaven they name the Mother of Mothers. They have shown Us that by these three they are governed, fed and numbered, and that the order of it is kept by a Council of their oldest houses.

Wishing that so old an order not fall into dispute for want of Our hand, We confirm to the Guild of Port-au-Levain and to its Council their privileges as anciently used: the keeping of the Hearth and of the Hall; the guard of the Mother of Mothers in her Vault; the nightly tariff in Guild Copper and in Yeast Tokens; the three verdicts of their Dawn Judgment; and the ordinances that follow, which We have caused to be read to Us and find to be in order.

We permit the said Council to style itself ~~Sovereign, by Our grace,~~ Sovereign, it being understood that the word signifies the bread and not the rock.

*[red, 1793: The grace is withdrawn. The word stands.]*

Given at Versailles, in the month of March, in the year of grace one thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven, and of Our reign the thirteenth.

By the King. ________________

*[red, 1793: The hand that signed this has been dead since January. The patent is not struck. It is not needed.]*

Registered in the Council, read in the Hall and entered at the Counting-House, the fourteenth of May, 1787.

---

## Title I: Of Rank and the Platters

**Art. 1. Of the order of the Hall.** Every soul upon the rock has a silver platter in the Hall of Platters, and no soul has two. The platters stand in order of their distance from the Great Hearth. The gilded platters of the Gallery stand nearest. The platters of the Guild stand after them, the platters of the Commons after the Guild, and the platters of the Abbey of the Crust along the north wall, where the heat does not reach and is not asked for.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Order reversed. The gilded platters are moved to the cold end of the Hall, by the great south doors and the Square. They stand there in the order of their former nearness, the nearest now the farthest, measured by the finger-breadth of Art. 4. Excepted: the empty platter nearest the Hearth, which is not moved. See Title VIII.]*

**Art. 2. Of gold leaf.** The rim of a Gallery platter carries gold leaf according to rank: for a house of the first rank, four finger-breadths of rim; of the second, three; for a cadet line, two; for a widow, what her house allows. The leaf is renewed on each quarter-day by the Council's gilder, and never by a hand that has been in the dish pit since the bell before.

*[red, 1793: The leaf is to be scraped, by the dish pit, and entered at the Counting-House by weight. Entered: eleven ounces and some grains. The gilder asked to keep his office. Granted. He scrapes.]*

**Art. 3. Of the colours of rank.** A Gallery loaf is gilt at the seal. A Guild loaf is dusted flour-white before the calling. A Commons loaf is baked at the Hearth's mouth, the cheaper heat, and comes out red in the crust. An Abbey loaf is wrapped in burlap and is not looked at. No loaf shall wear the colour of a rank not its holder's. A Commons loaf found dusted shall be read as false.

*[red, 1793: Retained. All loaves red. The Abbey's burlap retained: it is no rank, it is a cloth.]*

**Art. 4. Of platters moved.** No platter shall be moved nearer the Hearth than its rank allows. For every finger-breadth a platter is found moved, a Guild holder shall pay one Guild Copper, and a Commons holder one Guild Copper and one night's tokens. A Gallery platter is not found moved. The finger-breadth is the High Chancellor's, and a measure of it in brass is kept at the Counting-House.

*[red, 1793: Applied to the Gallery from the printing of this book, for every finger-breadth the gilded platters stood nearer the fire than the platters of the Commons. Computed by the brass measure, which we have kept.]*

**Art. 5. Of polishing.** A Commons platter shall be polished by its holder and by no other, before the proofing bell, with ash and not with flour. A Gallery platter shall be polished by the Hall's polishers, who are ~~of the Commons~~ of the Gallery, and who shall not breathe upon the gilt.

*[red, 1793: And who may breathe as they please.]*

---

## Title II: Of the Wake Loaf and its Seal

**Art. 6. Of the shaping.** Each Wake Loaf is shaped at the shaping bench from the leaven of its holder's house, and sealed by Le Boulanger with its holder's guild seal before it is proved. A holder without a house is given the leaven of the Hall, which is the cheapest, and the seal of the Commons, which is a plain ring.

*[red, 1793: Retained. All seals a plain ring. The Gallery's seals are at the Counting-House, in a box, counted.]*

**Art. 7. Of the calling.** At the expediter, L'Aboyeur calls each loaf by its holder's name as it passes. He calls the Gallery first, by precedence; the Guild next, by office; the Commons last, in a single breath where the hour is late.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Call the Gallery last, and in one breath.]*

**Art. 8. Of the reading of the platter.** The loaf upon a platter at dawn is read as that platter's holder. A loaf found at dawn upon a platter not its own is read as the holder of the platter on which it sits. The Council presumes the platter and not the person, for a platter is entered in the census and a person is only entered on it.

*[red, 1793: Retained. It has served us.]*

**Art. 9. Of the seal smudged.** A seal found smudged at dawn shall be read against its holder, no hand but the holder's being presumed to have touched it. ~~A Gallery seal found smudged shall be re-impressed by Le Boulanger at the Council's charge, and the smudge not entered.~~

*[red, 1793: Struck. Under this article, in the spring of 1788: Ondine Leroux, of the dish pit, whose right thumb was not once dry in eleven years at the basins. She pressed her own seal at the shaping bench every morning to see that it sat straight. It was her thumb that smudged it. Entered here, so that it is entered.]*

---

## Title III: Of the Yeast-Tax and of Registration

**Art. 10. Of the tariff.** Every platter owes a tariff each night. It is paid at the proofing bell, at the Ledger-Clerk's tariff desk in the Counting-House, in Guild Copper and in Yeast Tokens, and in no other coin.

*[red, 1793: Retained.]*

**Art. 11. Of the tariff by rank.**
- A Commons platter: two Guild Copper and one Yeast Token, nightly.
- A Guild platter: one Yeast Token, nightly.
- A Gallery platter: one Yeast Token on the first night of each quarter, or as the house shall see fit. It is excused on feast nights, on the nights of a Trial, and on any night the house dines.

*[red, 1793: The Gallery's excused nights are computed, every house, to the token, from the printing of this book to the night of this mark. They are entered at folio 212 of the Counting-House ledger and underlined twice. A house that dines every night has been excused every night. They dined every night.]*

**Art. 12. Of the stamp.** A platter whose tariff is unpaid at the bell is stamped [UNREGISTERED]. L'Aboyeur shall not call it. Its holder shall not be heard at the Dawn Judgment, neither in accusation nor in answer. Its loaf shall be covered with the Iron Cloche, which is of iron and not of silver, so that no officer need look upon it.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Apply to the gilded platters, beginning with the arrears of Art. 11. The Iron Cloches have been cleaned. There were more of them in the Counting-House than the Commons ever had platters. We have counted them.]*

**Art. 13. Of arrears.** A holder in arrears may not inherit Yeast Tokens, which pass to the Counting-House. Copper rings, buckles and the like may be given at the tariff desk in place of coin, at the Counting-House's valuation, which is not disputed.

*[red, 1793: Under Arts. 12 and 13, in June of 1789: Hervé Morel, dockhand of the Wharf, a widower. He would not give in his wife's copper ring at the Counting-House's valuation, and wore it on a cord at his neck. His platter went Unregistered three nights. On the third it was a crime (Art. 15). The ring was valued after.]*

---

## Title IV: Of Crimes Against the Bread

*The following are crimes against the Mother and the Hall. They are set down with their definitions, so that no one may say he did not know what a crumb was.*

**Art. 14. A crumb ~~taken~~ withheld.** A crumb is a portion of crust or of crumb not exceeding, in its greatest length, the nail of the High Chancellor's smallest finger, the said nail being measured at Michaelmas and entered. A portion larger is a loaf, and its taking is theft of a loaf. The crumbs that fall from the gallery to the floor of the Hall ~~belong to the floor~~ were withheld from the floor. They shall not be taken up by any hand, but swept at dawn into the Hearth by the Plongeur.

*[red, 1793: The definition is retained to the word, and the nail with it; the High Chancellor's is in our keeping. For "taken" read "withheld".]*

*[red, 1793: Under this article as printed, in the autumn of 1787: Jehanne Coquart, a net-mender's daughter of fourteen. She counted the gulls off the Wharf every morning and told her mother the number. She took up one crumb from under the gallery rail. It was within the nail.]*

*[red, 1793: Under this article as amended, in April of 1793: Thibault Lormeau, of the house of Lormeau. In all his life he did not eat the crust of his own Wake Loaf. He kept the crusts in a drawer of his writing table and fed them to the gulls from the gallery window. Withheld from the floor. Within the nail.]*

**Art. 15. A platter unpaid.** A platter Unregistered three nights together (Art. 12) is a crime on the third night.

*[red, 1793: Retained. The Gallery's quarter has come.]*

**Art. 16. A seal smudged.** As at Art. 9.

*[red, 1793: See Art. 9. Entered there.]*

**Art. 17. A loaf eaten before dawn.** This includes the holder's own, for the Wake Loaf is not the holder's to eat but the Hall's to read.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Hunger is no plea. It was none before.]*

**Art. 18. Speaking below the gallery during the calling.** This is the old disgrace of L'Apprenti, extended to all of the Commons, the calling being the Council's hour.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Extended to the gallery, which shall be silent while it is called.]*

**Art. 19. Kneeling off the seal.** Le Plongeur kneels at the basins upon the seal of the abolished office of Le Champignon, which is ground into the floor of the dish pit, and upon no other part of that floor.

*[red, 1793: Retained. The Gallery's widows kneel there now. They have asked for cushions. The article does not provide for cushions.]*

**Art. 20. Carrying from the Mother.** Le Commis carries flour, water and ash down to the Mother of Mothers, and carries nothing up from her.

The verdicts for this Title are in the Schedule, save that of Art. 20.

---

## Title V: Of the Dawn Judgment and of the Hearth

**Art. 21. Of the law.** The Inquisitor asks. The Guild Bailiff takes. The High Chancellor decides, from the gallery, and does not come down. There are three verdicts: Acquittal; a Guild Fine; Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.

*[red, 1793: Retained. The President of the committee decides, from the floor, and does not go up.]*

**Art. 22. Of the ceremony of a Trial by Fire.**
- The Great Proofing Bell strikes six, and not before.
- The great south doors of the Hall are opened onto Gallows Square.
- The cloches are lifted in the order of rank, the Gallery first.
- The Council's consort plays from the gallery stair: hautboys and a viol, and nothing in a minor key before the sentence.
- The sentence is read twice: once by the High Chancellor, and once by a member of the Gallery chosen by lot at supper the evening before, who shall be thanked.
- The places in the Square: the first three rows to the Gallery, seated, with cushions; the Guild behind them, standing; the Commons behind the Guild, standing. The attendance of the Commons is required, and counted.

*[red, 1793: Retained in every particular. The lot is drawn at the committee's supper. The Gallery shall attend, standing, and be counted. The cushions are entered at the Counting-House.]*

**Art. 23. Of the hour.** No sentence of Fire shall be carried out within the hour before the Council's supper, nor in any way that delays it. Where the hour of a sentence and the hour of a supper fall together, the sentence shall be moved. The Council sups in the gallery afterward, the doors to the Square being closed.

*[red, 1793: Retained. The committee also eats.]*

**Art. 24. Of the manner.** The manner of the Hearth is not set down in this book.

*[red, 1793: Beside the Hearth, since the ferry of the spring: the National Razor, which came in crates and was put together there by Guild carpenters. Its manner is not set down either. The consort played while it was put together. No one had told them to stop.]*

*[red, 1793: Under Arts. 21 to 24: the dowager of the house of Belcourt. Every morning of her life she carried her linnet's cage to the gallery window, so that the bird could see the harbour. She heard her sentence read twice, and thanked the second reader, as the article requires.]*

**Art. 25. Of judgment in absence.** Where the accused is beyond the reef or under the water, the Council judges the platter. The accused's Wake Loaf is given to the Hearth in the accused's place, and L'Aboyeur calls the name as it passes.

*[red, 1793: Retained. Applied since the autumn of 1789 to the gilded platters of those who took the ferry. The ferry is watched now.]*

---

## Title VI: Of the Offices

**Art. 26. Of the Old Brigade.** These are the kitchen's ranks, which are older than the Council:
- Le Boulanger, the bake.
- Le Levain, the feeding hand (Art. 27).
- Le Commis, the carrying hands (Art. 20).
- L'Apprenti, who watches, and does not speak.
- Le Rat de Cave, the cellar door.
- Le Champignon, abolished; its seal is in the floor of the dish pit.
- Le Chef de Cuisine and Le Sous-Chef, ~~now the Council and its clerks~~ now the committee and its clerks.
- L'Aboyeur, the calling.
- Le Tourrier, the folds.
- Le Plongeur, the dish pit, by sentence or by birth.
- Le Garde Manger, the cold pantry and its lock.
- L'Inspecteur Sanitaire, the hygiene room.

*[red, 1793: The clerks are the same clerks.]*

**Art. 27. Of Le Levain.** The office of the feeding hand is held by the eldest line of the house of Vane, as it has always been held. No hand of any other house shall touch the Mother of Mothers, nor be in the Vault at the feeding hour.

*[red, 1793: This article is suspended, not struck, the feeding hand being before the tribunal.]*

**Art. 28. Of Le Critique Gastronomique.** The office is vacant. Its chair at the front of house is dusted daily, and sat in by no one, so that it stays empty.

*[red, 1793: Retained.]*

**Art. 29. Of Le Croissant Rassis.** This is not an office but a style. A holder of the Gallery who is disgraced is styled Le Croissant Rassis, and keeps his platter, his gilt and his seat. A holder of the Commons who is disgraced goes to the dish pit and is styled nothing.

*[red, 1793: Every gilded platter is styled Croissant Rassis, and keeps nothing.]*

**Art. 30. Of the Guild offices and of the Crown's officer.** The Master Proof-Reader weighs. The Spice-Master keeps the Apothecary and its register. The Tide-Watcher keeps the lighthouse and the Wharf's tally. The Hearth-Warden keeps the peace of the Tavern. The Ledger-Clerk keeps the tariff desk and the deadbolts. The Iron-Wardens enforce. When the Crown sends a Grand Inspector-General, successor to L'Inspecteur Sanitaire, the Council shall receive him at the Wharf, seat him at the gallery's table, and show him what it shows.

*[red, 1793: The Guild offices are retained, their holders having chosen. The Crown's man came, in the autumn of 1789. This article is answered.]*

---

## Title VII: Of the Abbey and of the Heresies

**Art. 31. Of the blessing.** The Abbot of the Crust walks the island and blesses the flour with the Apostolic Starter. The Council attends the blessing on the first day of each month, in burlap over its gilt.

*[red, 1793: Retained as to the burlap.]*

**Art. 32. Of the Four Sour-Practices.** These are charged against the Mother, and named here by practice and not by any person.
- *The Leaven-Rot*, which would sour the Mother on purpose; against the Abbot. ~~Heresy. Trial by Fire.~~
- *The Pyre-Master's practice*, which holds that the Hearth belongs to the island and not to the Council. Trial by Fire.
- *The Smuggler-Blackguard's practice*: the pocket-mortar at the Wharf, and loaves lifted from one platter to another under the cloche. A Guild Fine, then the Square.
- *The Black-Crust Contract*: a commission rolled in a cylinder sealed in black wax and brought by pigeon. Trial by Fire for the one who accepts it, and for the one who bought it, if a buyer is found.

*[red, 1793: The Leaven-Rot: heresy no longer. See the Rite. The Pyre-Master's: the island holds it. The Smuggler-Blackguard's: retained; it is no one's. The Black-Crust Contract: retained. No buyer has been found.]*

**Art. 33. Of black wax.** Every cylinder sealed in black wax found on the rock shall be delivered unopened to the Counting-House. No pigeon shall be kept but in the Council's loft.

*[red, 1793: Retained. The committee keeps the loft.]*

**Art. 34. Of the glaze.** No apothecary shall keep the Nightshade Glaze, in the register or out of it, and the word shall not be written on any label.

*[red, 1793: Retained.]*

**Art. 35. Of rumour.** Rumour off the Morning Ferry, whether it runs inland or comes from there, shall be posted on the board in Gallows Square only by order of the Council, and in the Council's hand.

*[red, 1793: Retained. It was by the ferry that we heard, in the September of '89. We post it in our hand.]*

---

## Title VIII: Of Dusk

**Art. 36.** At dusk, when the Morning Ferry is gone and the harbour lamps are lit, L'Aboyeur shall carry a Wake Loaf down the Hall of Platters, calling no name, and set it upon the empty platter nearest the Hearth, for a name under seal. The watches of the night are counted from the setting down of that loaf, and the rounds of the Iron-Wardens begin from it. It is taken up at dawn and not eaten. Its tariff is not entered.
