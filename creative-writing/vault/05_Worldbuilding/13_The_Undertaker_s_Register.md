# The Undertaker's Register

*Register of the Grant. Kept under Article IV §5 by the Officer of the Dead. Company paper, forty lines, ruled in red.*

Every soul on the Grant has a line in this book. The dead have a date. The living have not yet.

I write only what I have carried.

---

## Sheet I. Tuesday, 21 November 1893. Carried forward.

The Clerk's order came up with the Articles. Under Article XI an account survives the man, so the Company wants every account on the Grant in one book, kept by the officer who sees the end of them. Forty souls, one line each: name, trade, habit, burial line. The living are to be marked *not yet*, and the book is to be shown at the Midday Rent on Saturdays.

I took Maud to the back car yesterday at Dusk, so I begin the day after Maud.

Forty lines ruled. Nine of them I can fill from my own two hands. These are the nine I carried.

**No. 2. WILF.** Mouth-organ, at the Drowned Lantern. Played for his pours in the key of G and never once asked the house to sing along. The mouth-organ went in with him.
**Burial:** Saturday, 21 October. Back car.

> Three of mine that day. Car 3 went out with four. I brought three to the platform. The fourth was not one of my forty.

**No. 3. DORA FINCH.** Read sums. Read Mrs Grieves's sums upside down across the bar, through spectacles mended with thread, and made her strike a quarter off Len's line. The spectacles went in with her.
**Burial:** Saturday, 21 October.

**No. 5. EDITH.** Washing, for the manor and the mill office. One room at the far end of Pump Row, two weeks behind. She said "two weeks" as if, said carefully enough, it might come to one. A sister back east. On the table a letter, folded once and not finished, with the stamp lying loose on top.
**Burial:** November, after the riot. The day: ______.

> I have not filled in the day. The bolt was shot from inside and the blanket was straight. Mallory wrote one word and crossed it out. I have not written one.

**No. 6. BERT HADDON.** Pump Row. A pipe. Went to string before the end, and he was light to lift. The pipe went with him.
**Burial:** Monday, 16 October.

**No. 7. NELL.** A girl. First at the pump, first to drink. Blue hair ribbons, and they went with her.
**Burial:** Monday, 16 October.

**No. 8. PERCY.** A child. Three marbles and a bootlace. Came into the Lantern on Tuesdays for Ernie, and would not play for keeps.
**Burial:** Saturday, 21 October.

**No. 14. STAN PIKE.** The switch. He had a cough like a saw from the thaw on. On his last night he paid his whole tab, the three-quarters from October with it, and stood Len a round. The cough stopped on Monday night, the 13th.
**Burial:** Tuesday, 14 November, Dusk. Len sang him spike-time. Ernie held his cap.

> The stone had his name that Monday morning. I write it down because it is so. I draw no line from it.

**No. 19. MAUD.** Darning. Took her Rotgut as a wage, never as a gift. Darned both of Bar's cuffs so they matched. No household, lodged with nobody, square with everybody.
**Burial:** Monday, 20 November, Dusk. The Vicar walked behind. Bar carried one end and I carried the other.

**No. 36. ARTHUR.** Platform crew. Called the train the Iron Beast. Whistled the Dusk whistle a half-step flat every night until his brother told him to quit. He had a ticket out once and stepped back down off the car. It is in his brother's hatband now, never punched past Tuesday.
**Burial:** last winter. I did not enter the day. There was no book.

> His brother puts a Rotgut on Arthur's line every week and drinks it himself. The Company keeps Arthur's line open too. Only one of them is kind.

Two lines will not obey the rule.

**No. 9. IVY COTTER.** Seamstress, one of the mill's cutting hands. Sat in the dark of an evening to save the wick, and saved her fare that way, coin by coin.

I cannot write her.

On 16 October I brought two boxes to the platform: Bert Haddon and the girl Nell. Two. Car 3 went out with three. The crew say that the next day she stood on that platform alive, in a grey coat, with her fare twisted up in a paper, and boarded. They say a box went down the Line on the 23rd with a thimble on a string in it. I did not lay out a thimble.

I never carried her, so she is not mine to date. I did not see her after the 17th either, so she is not mine to mark *not yet*. Mrs Grieves wrote *Gone east* against her in the tab book, and I have written what Mrs Grieves wrote.
**Burial:** Gone east.

**No. 32. FRANK.** Hauls for the railyard. Barred from the Lantern on 18 October for a thing he said about the girl Nell's fever. Mrs Grieves will not write it down, and neither will I. Not in since.

No body has come to me, and nobody has seen him to tell me.
**Burial:**

One line in another woman's words. One line in nobody's. My rule did not last the first page.

---

## Sheet II. Wednesday, 22 November, to Friday, 24 November. The living, walked.

Frank's line sits empty on the page. Ivy Cotter's sits in Mrs Grieves's hand. That is enough hearsay for one book. Every living line, I will take from life, with my own eyes.

And I have ruled a fifth column down every page: **Cause.** The Company asks for an inventory. A coroner asks how. I went back up the nine and filled it in: Bert Haddon, lung. Nell, fever. Stan Pike, the cough. Arthur, the cough, the same as Stan. For Wilf, Dora Finch and Percy, a dash: three in one day in a riot week, and I did not ask. Edith, a dash. Maud, a dash. She was square, and I have not made her owe a cause.

I walked.

**The platform, at Station Rush.**

**No. 18. LEN.** Platform crew. Sings while the others haul, and stood at the Lantern window when Dora Finch went out and did not drink after. He drinks again now.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 26. ERNIE.** Platform crew. Pump water at the Lantern, no charge. Held Stan's cap. Percy used to come in for him on Tuesdays. He looked at my book a long time and asked whose hand it was.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 28. CLEM.** Platform crew. Reads the Gazette aloud at the Lantern, and the Articles too, the parts nobody asked for.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 30. JACK RUDD.** Platform crew. Grins. Pays in kind.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The manor.**

**No. 20. HORACE.** Butler, Vane Manor, twenty-two years. Keeps a household day-book, and writes the round down before he walks it. He showed me tonight's page: written, not yet walked.
**Burial:** not yet.

> A man who writes ahead and then goes to make it true. I wrote it down as a habit. I did not think it was catching.

**Pump Row.**

**No. 17. GERT.** Laundress, fourth door. A widow, with a boy of eight who carries the baskets for her. Scrubs her step every morning, though the mud is back by noon.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 21. AGNES.** Edith's neighbour. Goes up to the churchyard at first light and comes back down the Row telling everyone the stone's name before they ask. My boy gets it from her for Crumb's orders.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 23. ELSIE.** Keeps hens behind the Row and sells the eggs for scrip. Names every hen after a Company officer. The Clerk lays nothing. Counts the eggs twice into her apron.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 29. ROSE.** Young. A geranium in a tin on her sill, the only flower on Pump Row. She carries it in at curfew, the way you would carry in a child.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The mill, and Mill Row.**

**No. 1. MABEL.** Takes in mending. Lines her boots against the mud with hymn sheets she copied out in her own hand, the alto line marked. Keeps spare buttons in a cocoa tin and would give you one off the top.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 24. WALT.** Sawyer. Short two fingers from before the Panic, and counts on the hand that has five.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 25. ADA BROOK.** Mill hand. Mill Row, third door. A grey scarf darned in red at one end, which was her mother's.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 27. VERA.** Seamstress at the cutting table, in Ivy Cotter's place. Keeps Ivy's chair empty beside her and won't let anyone sit in it.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 38. TED.** A child. At the mill-loft window, working off the rent with his hands. He saw me write him down. He doesn't wave.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The railyard.**

**No. 16. ALBERT.** Smith, at the workbench. Rings every spike against the anvil before he passes it, and throws back the ones that sound wrong.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The Tin Gallery mouth.**

**No. 10. REG.** Tinner with no mine. Cracks his knuckles in the order of the old gallery levels, top to bottom.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 34. NORMAN.** Tinner, Reg's partner. Sits at the Gallery mouth on Sundays with his hat on his knee.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The dock.**

**No. 12. CYRIL.** Fisher of the mere channels. In fog he whistles two notes, high then low, so Nora knows where he is on the water.
**Burial:** not yet.

**No. 37. NORA.** Cyril's wife. Mends the nets on the dock, and answers his two notes with her own.
**Burial:** not yet.

**The foot of the Rise.**

**No. 11. HATTIE.** Washerwoman, at the wash-house. Has a son back east, and writes to him every week through the Postmaster. A jar on the shelf, lid on. She did not say what was in it, and I did not ask.
**Burial:** not yet.

**Crumb's Ovens.**

**No. 35. PEG.** A child. Takes the heel off a loaf whenever Tamsin turns her back. Tamsin lets her.
**Burial:** not yet.

**St Brannock's, at the evening hymn.**

**No. 31. LIL.** A child. Sings a beat ahead of the hymn, always, as if she wants to be there first.
**Burial:** not yet.

> Mabel sings in the same pew, alto, a half-beat late. Between the two of them the hymn comes out right.

**My own back room.**

**No. 33. DORIS.** Layer-out. Washes the dead with me, and tells them the news by their first names while she works: the rate, the weather, who has been barred.
**Burial:** not yet.

**Harold's step.**

**No. 40. HAROLD.** The oldest man on the Grant. Came up with the tin, before the Line. Says "before the drain" about everything, including bread.
**Burial:** not yet.

That made twenty-four with my own eyes on them. Five more lines are children, and those I could not find.

Alfie's mother says he is about. Winnie's says she is at the well. Flo's broom is propped by the Lantern door, and nobody has seen the hand that props it. Sid's cap is on the fence. May Potter's mother looked at me as if I had asked for money.

So I went to the square, to the chalk under the fourth lantern, where the children have it all written out, and I copied them from that.

**No. 4. ALFIE.** *Counted the gallows stairs.* **Burial:** not yet.
**No. 13. WINNIE.** *Dropped pebbles and counted the splash.* **Burial:** not yet.
**No. 15. FLO.** *Swept the floor for a penny a night.* **Burial:** not yet.
**No. 22. SID.** *Chased his shadow to where the light stops.* **Burial:** not yet.
**No. 39. MAY POTTER.** *Walked toward every light.* **Burial:** not yet.

Five lines from chalk. Children are about. Children are always about.

---

## Sheet III. Saturday, 25 November.

I opened the book at Station Rush to carry it to the Midday Rent.

**No. 23. ELSIE.** **Burial:** Saturday, 25 November.

Today's date, in my ink and my hand. I know my own downstroke.

I had seen her at first light. With my own eyes, the way I see them all now: in her yard, in her apron, counting. The rule held at dawn. She was alive and her line said otherwise.

I told no one. I took the book to the Clerk at Midday. He ran his finger down the forty and said it balanced.

At Dusk I was sent for. She was sitting in the hen run with her back to the fence and the eggs in her apron. I counted them. Eleven. She had counted them too, twice.

**Cause:** heart, by the look.

I did not open her. The Company does not pay for opening.

Then I thought of the boy. Every dawn the boy gets the stone's name from Agnes and runs it down to Crumb's, so the loaves are proving before the box is nailed. On 21 October the boy went to Crumb's with a paper that said four, and I had counted three. The boy has a paper habit. The boy copies the stone.

That is all a boy is for.

---

## Sheet IV. Sunday, 26 November, to Tuesday, 28 November.

Sunday. No train. I took the book from the boy. It lives in the box under the seat of the hearse cart now, and the box has a lock, and the key is on a string round my neck. The boy pushes the cart. He does not touch the pen. He cried a little, and then he pushed the cart.

The key is warm against me all day.

Sunday night, after eleven, Mabel. Monday at Station Rush they found her in the lane behind the Drowned Lantern, and the boy came running for the cart.

The town says a cold edge. That is all the town says, and it is all I will write.

Before I went, I took the key off my neck and unlocked the box and opened to her.

**No. 1. MABEL.** **Burial:** not yet.

Undated. The book had not known.

So when she was carried I entered her afterwards, the way a register should be kept.

**Burial:** Monday, 27 November. **Cause:** a cold edge.

Doris washed her. She did not tell her the news. Doris always tells them the news. I took off the boots and the hymn sheets came out of them folded, the alto line marked in pencil, and I entered them on the inventory one by one. The cocoa tin had nine buttons in it. I entered nine.

On Tuesday the Tribunal sat at the Bell, and I carried the man it hanged. His name is a blank in their book, and it is a blank in mine. He is not on my forty. The Company billed the gallows pine to an account, and the account was Gert's. Gert was entered Indebted at Midday.

> The Company charges the living for the wood.

The lock holds. The book was behind her. Good. A book should be behind.

---

## Sheet V. Wednesday, 29 November.

The key never left my neck. I slept in it.

**No. 12. CYRIL.** **Burial:** Wednesday, 29 November.

My hand. Today's date. Locked in a box, with the key on a string against my skin all night.

Low water was circled on the chart at the Lantern, and Cyril went out on the channel at the circle. Nora was on the dock with the nets. She heard his two notes, high then low. She whistled back. She heard them again, and then she did not.

They brought him to me at noon. The channel where they found him comes to the ankle at low water. I stood in it to see.

**Cause:** drowned.

I wrote it. I knew it was short. A man does not drown standing up in a channel that comes to his ankle. I wrote it anyway, because the line wanted a word and I had no other.

Nora is on the dock this evening, whistling the two notes. Nobody answers.

So it is not the boy. The boy cannot get at a box with my key in it. That leaves the hand that wears the key. Mere-rot does things to a man at night. It makes footsteps in empty lanes. It can make a hand get up and write while the man is sleeping.

My own sleeping hand. I know my own hand. I would know it anywhere.

---

## Sheet VI. Thursday, 30 November.

A length of rope off the hearse cart. Right wrist to the bedpost, a knot I can tie in the dark and could not undo in the dark with the other hand. The ink at the far side of the room, on the sill, where my sleeping hand would have to drag the bed to reach it.

Knot first. Then prayers. Then the dark.

---

## Sheet VII. Saturday, 2 December.

Morning. The knot was as I tied it, my wrist red where it had pulled. The ink was on the sill. I untied myself and unlocked the box.

**No. 11. HATTIE.** **Burial:** Saturday, 2 December.

Agnes was coming down the Row as I shut it. The stone, she said. Hattie.

I went down to the foot of the Rise at noon. I don't know what I meant to do. Hattie was at the Post Office, giving the Postmaster a letter for her son. She came back to the wash-house and found me waiting, and asked if I had a shirt for her. I said not yet.

She took the jar down off the shelf. She said I might as well know. One button off every dead man's shirt I had ever sent down to be washed for the box. She said a man should go into the ground with something of his kept back up here, and nobody misses one button.

I miss one button. Every inventory I have written since I came has come up one button short. I had put it down to the cart.

She said her son writes back when he can.

She was dead by the next light. At night. I do not know how, and I did not ask Doris to tell me. The Watchman was stood down the next day and his lantern taken from him. He is not on my forty.

Doris and I laid her out. I came to the Cause column with the pen.

**Cause:**

I left it.

The first I have left blank because I did not want to know. Not Edith's dash, which was Mallory's. Mine.

Then the inventory. The jar was on the shelf. I counted it against my inventories, back to the first. It was complete. Every button I ever lost, accounted for.

I did not enter the jar.

---

## Sheet VIII. Thursday, 7 December.

I left a Cause blank on Saturday and the pen did not mind. A man who has stopped asking how has no reason to open anything.

Agnes came down the Row at first light. Ada Brook.

At noon a box came to me already nailed. Company stencil, a Company booking, consigned from Mill Row, for Car 3 at Dusk. No name on it. Under Article IV every body on the Grant comes to me and is inventoried to the last button. A coroner would take a bar to the lid.

I did not lift it. I sent it.

The Gazette this morning still lists HADDON, B., Pump Row, one week, warned. I carried Bert Haddon in October. The Company has not caught up with him. The Company does not have to.

Mill Row says Ada lost the grey scarf on Monday between the pump and the platform, the one darned in red that was her mother's. On Tuesday she paid Crumb's in full for ten wake loaves for Mill Row. Paid ahead.

I opened to her line.

**No. 25. ADA BROOK.** **Burial:**

I did not write a date, because I did not open the box. I did not write *not yet*, because I sent the box. Neither.

A coroner would have opened it. I sent it.

---

## Sheet IX. Friday, 8 December, and Saturday, 9 December.

Friday, Station Rush. The boy came back from Crumb's with his cap in his hands. Mrs Crumb asks is it right. Wake loaves for Vera, one for each mourner and three for the dead. Ordered and paid. By whom she would not say, or did not know.

**No. 27. VERA.** Undated.

So the town is ahead of the book now. The stone, the bakery, the boy, whoever pays for loaves. I left Ada's line alone, and a box went out from Mill Row all the same. Leaving a line alone keeps nothing off it.

I unlocked the box. I took the pen in my waking hand, at the table, in the light, with the boy watching, and I wrote Vera's date to match the bakery.

**Burial:** Saturday, 9 December.

The same train brought a consignment for the Officer of the Dead. Lead, sheet, one: a coffin lining. No sender. I unrolled the end of it on the platform. It is longer than any of my forty.

I signed for it. *Officer of the Dead.*

Saturday. Vera did not come to the cutting table. They found her at home on Mill Row. Ivy Cotter's chair is still empty beside hers, and now there are two.

Did I write it because it was coming? Or did it come because I wrote it? I sat at the table with the book open and could not get the two to lie down in order. No telling which came first.

At Midday the Clerk ran his finger down the forty and said it balanced.

I wrote it. Or it was coming. The ink is dry either way.

---

## Sheet X. Saturday, 9 December, late.

The fifth column has nothing left in it for a coroner to find.

I took the rule and drew one line through the heading. **~~Cause.~~**

Then I went out to the shed and moved the trestles and the spare wheel, and made room against the back wall for the lead. It lies there rolled. I measured it with my arms, twice. Nobody on my forty is that long.

The lead is the length of a man who is not on my forty.

---

## Sheet XI. Monday, 11 December.

Station Rush. The cart, the box, the key.

There was no new date in the book this morning. I sat with the pen over the page a while.

Then I dated the next line myself. I am almost sure I wrote it before Agnes came down the Row. I think before. The ink was wet when she called the name up to my window, and the name was the name I had written.

**No. 29. ROSE.** **Burial:** Monday, 11 December.

Her line has no Cause. I struck the column through on Saturday.

I put the book in the box and locked it and put the key on my neck. I put the boy to the shafts, and we brought the hearse cart up Pump Row, and I stood it at Rose's door, where the Row could see.

The Row saw.

Her geranium is on the sill in its tin. It is the only flower on the Row. She has not carried it in. It is not curfew yet.

I am standing beside the cart. It is not dusk yet.

Not yet.

---

## The last page.

**Dated:** fifteen. Nine carried forward, and Elsie, Mabel, Cyril, Hattie, Vera, and one more not yet carried.
**Neither:** three. Ivy Cotter. Ada Brook. Frank.
**Not yet:** twenty-two.

Every soul on the Grant has a line in this book. The dead have a date. The living have not yet.

The word on your line at dawn is who you are.

*Officer of the Dead.*
