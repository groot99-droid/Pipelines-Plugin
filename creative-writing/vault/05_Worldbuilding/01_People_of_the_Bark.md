# People of the Bark

People of the Bark

A mythic micro‑civilization clinging to the skin of a giant world they can’t comprehend

They live on the outer rind of a towering tree — a world so vast that even a single inch of bark is an entire country to them. Their cities cling to ridges and grooves. Their roads run along cracks. Their temples are carved into knots. Their watchtowers rise from splinters.

To them, the tree is everything:  
the sky, the ground, the history, the future.

They call it The Endless Trunk.

But to the real world?  
It’s just a tree in someone’s backyard.

That’s the quiet moral humming beneath the story — their wars, their politics, their fears, their triumphs… all unfolding on a surface we barely notice.



🎬 Opening Scene — “The Tremor”

At dawn, the bark people wake to a strange vibration.

Not an earthquake.  
Not a storm.  
Not an enemy.

Just a squirrel landing on the trunk.

But to them, it’s a cataclysm.

Dust rains from the ridges.  
Homes carved into the bark shudder.  
The elders declare omens.  
The young warriors rush to the battlements.  
The scholars scramble to record the tremor’s pattern, believing it to be a message from the gods.

Meanwhile, the squirrel is just scratching an itch.

This is the tone:  
Epic stakes inside a world that is cosmically tiny.



🌿 The Culture of the Bark People

They are small, agile, and deeply spiritual. Their entire worldview is shaped by the tree’s rhythms:

• Windstorms are divine trials  
• Rain is a seasonal blessing  
• Birdsong is prophecy  
• Falling leaves are omens of death  
• Sap is sacred water  
• Insects are monsters or gods depending on their size


They believe the tree is alive and conscious — a slow, ancient being whose moods shape their fate.

They’re not wrong.  
But they’re not right either.

The tree is alive, yes.  
But it’s just… a tree.



⚔️ The Conflict — “The Coming of the Shadow”

One day, a shadow falls across the bark.

It is enormous, stretching across their entire world.  
The people panic.  
The priests chant.  
The warriors prepare for war.

But the shadow is just a human hand reaching toward the trunk.

To them, it is the arrival of a god.  
To us, it’s someone checking for termites.

This is where the tension comes from — the collision between their mythic worldview and the mundane reality outside it.



🌱 The Moral (woven, not stated)

Their fears are enormous.  
Their hopes are enormous.  
Their conflicts feel world‑ending.

But in the grand scheme?  
They are tiny.

And yet — their lives still matter.  
Their stories still matter.  
Their courage still matters.

The moral isn’t “your worries don’t matter.”  
It’s:

Even the smallest world is a universe to the ones who live in it.
