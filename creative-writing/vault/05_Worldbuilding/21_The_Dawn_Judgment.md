# The Dawn Judgment

*The Book of the Morning Judgment, Gallows Square. Kept by the Council's stenographer. Two records.*

[PROPERTY OF THE COUNTING-HOUSE]

---

## THE FIRST RECORD

**Tuesday, 11 March 1788. Before the High Chancellor.**

### The Eve

At dusk the Aboyeur carried a loaf down the Hall and set it on the empty platter nearest the Hearth, for a name under seal. He called no name. The night was reckoned from it.

Entered.

**Minute.** I enter that loaf every evening, the way the Tide-Watcher enters the wind. It is not mine to ask about and I have not asked.

At the second bell the Order of the Morning Judgment came up from the command desk to be copied fair. The supper card was pinned to it with a dressmaker's pin.

### The Order of the Morning Judgment

1. At the sixth bell the great south doors of the Hall are opened onto Gallows Square, so that the Square may see the Hearth.
2. The Hall is seated by rank, as the stenographer has it.
3. The consort plays from the gallery.
4. The accused bows to the gallery, once, and lower than to the Hearth.
5. The charge is read.
6. The verdicts are three. Acquittal. A Guild Fine. Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.
7. The cloches are lifted in order of precedence, the platters nearest the Hearth first.
8. The bell.
9. The Hearth receives the loaf, and then the one the loaf named.
10. No Fire within the hour before supper. The Judgment shall be concluded before the consommé is carried up.
11. The supper card to be entered in the record, as served.

### The Supper Card

**Le Souper de l'Aube.** At the eighth bell, in the gallery.

- A consommé of salt cod, clarified.
- Oysters of the reef, on ice from the cold pantry.
- A brioche, gilded.
- *After the Fire:* quince, in its syrup.

**Minute.** A course marked after the Fire. I have copied that line two hundred times. The printer is paid by the year, and it is cheaper to set the Fire on every card than to set two cards, so the cards are printed for any dawn. Some dawns there is no Fire and the quince comes up anyway. I copied it fair and pinned it back.

### The Seating

It is my office to seat the Hall. I seat it the evening before, on paper, and the ushers follow the paper.

- **Gold leaf.** The gilded chairs nearest the Hearth. The house of Vane first, as always, then the rest by precedence.
- **Flour-white.** The Guild benches. Master Proof-Reader, Spice-Master, Tide-Watcher, Hearth-Warden, Ledger-Clerk.
- **Burlap.** The Abbey's rail.
- **Red crust.** Standing, in the rope pen, beyond the south doors, in the Square.
- **The Critique's chair.** Empty.
- **The stenographer.** The desk below the gallery rail.

There was a slip under the pin I had not seen. It was in the command desk's hand and it was one line long:

*One stool, before the Hearth.*

**Minute.** So the card was not for any dawn. It was for this one. Somebody at the command desk had known since the second bell, and the printer had known since last year.

I wrote the stool onto the plan. A stool is not a sentence. There are three verdicts. I have seen a boy walk off that stool with a fine and go back to his bench by noon.

### The Sitting

Sixth bell. The south doors opened. Fog in the Square, and the Square full to the rope.

The Iron-Wardens brought the accused up from the cold pantry and set him on the stool. A boy of about fourteen. Flour to the elbows. The apron of the shaping bench. He bowed to the gallery as the Order says, too low, and a Warden had to lift him by the collar.

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: Read it.

The charge, entered as read: *That the accused, L'Apprenti of the shaping bench, red crust, did on the night of the ninth take up from the scraps owed to the Hearth the heel of a gilded loaf of the gallery's table, and did eat of it in the dish pit, which is to eat above his platter.*

**EXHIBIT 1.** What is left of the heel. Gilt on the crust. A bite out of the crumb. Brought up on a silver dish under a cloche.

THE MASTER PROOF-READER, sworn. He set it on the Density Scales.

THE MASTER PROOF-READER: The bite, by the difference from a heel of that bake. Eleven grains.

THE STENOGRAPHER: Again, if the bench permits.

THE MASTER PROOF-READER: Eleven grains.

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: And by the Ordinance?

The Ordinance of the crumb measures a crumb against the nail of the High Chancellor's smallest finger. The High Chancellor put his hand out over the gallery rail. The Proof-Reader held the dish up to it. The Hall watched a man compare a bite of bread to his own fingernail.

RULING: The portion exceeds the nail. It is a loaf. The theft is of a loaf of the Gallery.

**Minute.** I asked for the weight twice so that I would have it right, and I have it right. A Guild copper weighs more than fifty grains. I set the weight on its own line, so that no one copying after me can make it larger:

eleven grains

A crumb is entered in law as the whole loaf. Still, there are three verdicts, and a Guild Fine is one of the three verdicts. Boys are fined every quarter. I have the forms in the drawer.

### The Fine

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: The Guild Fine is the price of the loaf. Can the accused pay?

THE LEDGER-CLERK: [Summarised: the price of a gilded loaf of the gallery's table, in Guild Copper. The accused's own platter paid to the week. His mother's platter in arrears, and three nights from the stamp.]

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: Has the accused anything to say?

The question is asked by form. By form the Apprentice does not answer it, because speaking is his office's disgrace.

He answered.

RULING: What follows is entered as an offence and not as testimony.

[What the accused said. Summarised, in his order, which I have kept.] He took a square of sailcloth out of his apron, folded in four, and opened it on his knee. Gold leaf. Flakes of it, pressed flat. He had scraped them from the gallery's crusts in the dish pit, where the plates come down to be scraped for the Hearth, a flake or two a night since the autumn. He pressed them under the weights of the shaping bench overnight so that they would lie flat and could be counted. They were for his mother's tariff. Her platter was three nights from the stamp, and he had counted, and it was nearly enough. He said he could tell a loaf by its crack when it came out of the oven. A short crack for a tight crumb. A long one, like ice going on the mill pond, for an open one. The gallery's loaves crack long, and he liked to hear them. He had not meant to eat the heel. He had meant to scrape it. It was still warm.

The Ledger-Clerk weighed the leaf. It came to very nearly the fine.

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: Whose gilding?

Nobody answered. It was not a question.

RULING: The gilding is the Gallery's. It is entered as a second theft.

**Minute.** I took down every word he said. The ruling says they are not testimony. It does not say they are not to be written, so the record has them, the cracks and the pond, in the hand I keep for the Council's letters. The gold that would have paid the fine is entered as a second theft. I write what is said. What is done with it is not my office.

### The Sentence

RULING: Two thefts, and no fine. Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth.

The High Chancellor looked at the supper card. Then at the clock over the south doors.

THE HIGH CHANCELLOR: Supper at eight. No Fire within the hour before supper. Before the seventh bell, then. The consommé does not wait.

Entered: *Trial by Fire, before the seventh bell, the consommé not to wait.*

**Minute.** The hour of the Fire was set by the soup. I entered it in the same size of hand as the charge, because it is part of the sentence now.

### The Ceremony

In the order of the Order.

- The consort played a minuet from the gallery.
- The cloches were lifted, the house of Vane's first, its silver gone faintly gold from the heat.
- The bell.
- The Aboyeur went down to the boy's platter at the far end of the Hall, where the red crust's platters stand, lifted his Wake Loaf, and carried it up the Hall to the Hearth, calling its owner's name as it passed, as he calls every loaf:

**Toinot.**

- The Hearth received the loaf, and then the one the loaf named.
- At the seventh bell the south doors were closed on the Square.

**Minute.** I entered the name because the Aboyeur called it, and what is called at the Hearth is entered. It is the one place in this Book his name is written. With it the record is complete. The Square went home in the fog. The rope pen was taken down.

### The Supper

My fee for a complete record is a seat: the foot of the gallery's table, below the salt, by the door the dishes come through. I took it.

The consommé came up at the eighth bell. It was clear. The gallery talked about the fog and whether the ferry would run. I ate the quince after the Fire. The plates went down to the dish pit to be scraped for the Hearth, as the Ordinance requires, and the crusts went with them, with the gilt still on.

**Le Souper de l'Aube, as served.**

- A consommé of salt cod, clarified. *Served.*
- Oysters of the reef, on ice from the cold pantry. *Served.*
- A brioche, gilded. *Served.*
- *After the Fire:* quince, in its syrup. *Served.*

**Minute.** I have eaten at the foot of the gallery's table, and the menu is in the record, in the same ink as the pond.

---

## BETWEEN THE RECORDS

**Minute.** September 1789. The news came off the Morning Ferry. The night it came is not this Book's.

**Minute.** The Commons took the Square. The same stage, the same south doors, the same bell. The Hearth was used again, and I took those records too. Sentences, 1790 to the winter of 1792: ████.

**Minute.** No one asked me to leave my desk. Somebody had to know the order of the cloches.

## THE SECOND RECORD

**Thursday, 2 May 1793. Before the Committee of the Common Loaf.**

### The Eve

At dusk the Aboyeur carried a loaf down the Hall and set it on the empty platter nearest the Hearth, for a name under seal. He called no name. The Committee has struck the Council's ordinances one by one, all spring, in red. Not that one. The record does not say why.

Entered.

That evening the Committee sat in the gallery with the Gallery's books open on the rail. The seating plans. All of them, back to 1787. Every one in my hand.

At the foot of every supper there is the same line.

The President of the Committee read it out. She was born in the dish pit, and she reads slowly, with her finger. She read my name from the foot of the list. I do not enter it. The Book has never carried it.

THE PRESIDENT: At the foot. Every supper since 'eighty-seven.

THE STENOGRAPHER: Yes.

THE PRESIDENT: Who seats the Hall?

THE STENOGRAPHER: The stenographer.

She held out a pen. It was mine, from my own desk; someone had gone down for it.

THE PRESIDENT: Then seat it.

She gave me the Order of 1788 to re-ink. They had a press, and a Secretary, and an ordinance for everything by then. They did not have the order of the cloches. I am kept because I know the form.

### The Order of the Morning Judgment, re-inked

1. At the ~~sixth~~ *fifth* bell the great south doors of the Hall are opened onto Gallows Square, so that the Square may see the Hearth.
2. The Hall is seated by rank, as the stenographer has it.
3. The consort ~~plays from the gallery~~ *is abolished. The Square sings.*
4. The accused bows to the ~~gallery~~ *Square*, once, and lower than to the Hearth.
5. The charge is read.
6. The verdicts are three. Acquittal. A ~~Guild~~ *Common* Fine. ~~Trial by Fire in the Great Hearth~~ *The National Razor, beside the Hearth.*
7. The cloches are lifted in order of precedence, the platters ~~nearest the Hearth~~ *of the Common Loaf* first.
8. The bell.
9. ~~The Hearth receives the loaf, and then the one the loaf named.~~ *Struck.*
10. No ~~Fire~~ *sentence* within the hour before supper. The Judgment shall be concluded before the ~~consommé~~ *Citizen's Broth* is carried up.
11. The supper card to be entered in the record, as served.

### The Seating, re-inked

- **Red crust.** The gilded chairs nearest the Hearth. The President in the High Chancellor's.
- **Flour-white.** The Guild benches, as before.
- **Burlap.** The Abbey's rail. Empty.
- **Gold leaf.** Standing, in the rope pen, beyond the south doors, in the Square.
- **The Critique's chair.** Empty.
- **The stenographer.** The desk below the gallery rail.
- **The National Razor.** Beside the Hearth.

It came off the spring ferry in a crate stencilled for the Committee. I drew its place on the plan in the same ink I used for the stool, five years ago, and the ushers followed the paper. Out in the Square the rope on the gallows was replaced, as it is every spring, used or not.

### The Sitting

Fifth bell. The south doors opened. The Square sang. I have no sign for singing.

The Iron-Wardens brought the accused up from the cold pantry and set him on the stool. It is the same stool. A man past fifty, grey, in a good coat gone shiny at the cuffs. He bowed to the Square as the re-inked Order says, and not low.

THE PRESIDENT read the charge: *That Évariste Vane, of the house of Vane, holding Le Levain, the feeding hand, did feed the Mother of Mothers for the Gallery while the red crust went Unregistered; and did sit at the Gallery's suppers, after the Fire.*

**EXHIBIT 1.** This Book. The First Record, 11 March 1788: the seating, with the house of Vane first among the gilded chairs; the charge; the sentence; the supper card as served.

THE PRESIDENT: The stenographer will read it.

[Exhibit 1 read into the record in full, by the stenographer, standing.]

**Minute.** Eleven grains. The cracks, the pond. The quince. *Served*, four times. The name, in my own voice, to the Square. I wrote him down to keep him. The Committee kept him as the case against the Vane.

### The Feeding Hand

THE PRESIDENT: Has the accused anything to say?

THE ACCUSED: I sat at the supper. I did not speak for him.

He stopped there. It was not a defence, and he did not make it one.

THE ACCUSED: No hand but a Vane's has fed her since the Bringing. Stop mine. Who goes down at dawn?

Nobody answered. The Hall was quiet, and then the Square. The red crust had eaten the Mother's bread that morning, as the Gallery had eaten it every morning before.

The Iron-Wardens turned out his coat onto the trial table. A handkerchief. A pencil. A small tin, the lid stiff. In the tin a pinch of dried leaven, grey.

[Summarised, in his words.] His daughter's. The leaven her first loaf was shaped from, when she was six. He kept it because a house that is given flour and no leaven is ended, and he did not mean his to be.

They gave him water. He took it in his left hand. He has not touched bread with his right since he came to the office. The right is the Mother's.

The Hall had heard it, and could not unhear it: the Mother has no other hand.

### The Vote

THE PRESIDENT: The Committee will say.

Counting is the form. I counted the hands.

For the Razor: nineteen. Against: four. The four kept their hands up a long time, as if to be sure I had them.

RULING: The National Razor, beside the Hearth.

THE PRESIDENT: The Mother has no other hand. He will feed her at the sixth bell. After.

RULING: The sentence waits on the dawn feeding.

Entered, with the count.

The supper card was pinned to the Order with the same kind of pin. It was the 1788 card, re-inked.

**~~Le Souper de l'Aube~~ *The Supper of the Common Loaf.*** At the eighth bell, in the gallery.

- ~~A consommé of salt cod~~ *Citizen's Broth*, clarified.
- Oysters of the reef, on ice from the cold pantry, *for all.*
- ~~A brioche, gilded~~ *The red crust.*
- *After the* ~~*Fire*~~ *Razor:* quince, in its syrup.

**Minute.** The quince again.

### The Bell

Sixth bell.

The Iron-Wardens took him across the Hall to the sub-basement door. He went with his right hand held a little away from his coat, the way he must have carried it every dawn for thirty years. The door. The stair. Ninety-one steps down to the Vault. My office ends at the Hall. I did not go down, and the record does not.

**RETURN OF EXECUTION.** The printed form, from the Order.

> Sentence of the National Razor, beside the Hearth, carried out upon the accused of this record at the hour of ______.
> *Hour to be entered on his return.*

Then the fee. The President showed me to the foot of the table. The same seat, below the salt, by the door the dishes come through, in the gallery the Gallery used to sit in. The red crust was on the table, and it was warm, and I broke it and ate it. The broth did not come up. The supper waits on the Razor and the Razor waits on the Mother. I laid my pen beside the bowl with the hour still blank.

**Minute.** The bread is good.

**The Supper of the Common Loaf, as served.**

- Citizen's Broth, clarified.
- Oysters of the reef, on ice from the cold pantry, for all.
- The red crust.
- *After the Razor:* quince, in its syrup.
