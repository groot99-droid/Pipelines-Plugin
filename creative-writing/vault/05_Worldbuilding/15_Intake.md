## II. Rules of the Camp

**GROUNDS 99 · RULES OF THE CAMP**
**READ AND INITIAL EACH RULE. DETAINEES INITIAL WITH THE NUMBER.**

**RULE ONE. THE SHIFT-WHISTLE.**
The Shift-Whistle is the camp's clock. Shifts begin on it and end on it. Detainees do not carry time.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *The watch is in field 9. I count now. Eins, zwei. My mother's counting. I catch myself at it and go on anyway.*

**RULE TWO. THE RATION.**
Detainees eat the mess ration and nothing else. No food is to be taken outside the mess, and no food from outside the mess is to be eaten. The ration is set by the Program and is not to be traded, saved or refused.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *Not refused. That is the word they underlined.*
>
> *Grey meat in the evening, cut small. I could not name the animal in any of my three languages. I ate it all and was hungrier when the plate was clean than when it was full. They gave us bread after, to fill the space. The men who have been here since the first roll eat fast, with their elbows out, and their wrists are thinner than Hanne's.*

**RULE THREE. THE MIDNIGHT ROLL CALL.**
At the whistle, the detainee has fifteen seconds to stand beside his assigned bunk. A bunk without a detainee at the end of fifteen seconds is lit. Equipment for the Roll: searchlights; Order Enforcement Agents, masked; dogs. Detainees not in place are recovered.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *Recovered / brought back. Or found. I have not stood one yet. The man in the next bunk said, sleep in your boots. It is all he has said to me.*
>
> *After the Roll the mess hall hums through the wall, one note, all of them. One man told me not to join in. Later another told me to.*

**RULE FOUR. THE QUOTA.**
Production is entered against the number daily. A shortfall is debited in scrip. A repeated shortfall goes before the Tribunal for Scrip Forfeiture.

> **UNITED STATES SECRET ██████**
> Scrip is the property of the Office. Possession of altered or counterfeit scrip is an offence against ████████████ and will be referred.

READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *Quota / the price of tomorrow. The scrip carries a seal, as if it were money. It is only paper that tells you how far behind you are.*

**RULE FIVE. LUNG-ROT AND THE TONIC.**
A detainee who coughs on shift is to be reported by the detainees beside him. The oxygen tonic is issued at the Infirmary and debited against quota.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *So the cure is billed to the cough, and the cough is reported by the man next to you. I have started listening to my own breathing at night. So, I think, has he.*

**RULE SIX. THE BLADE.**
Sentences of the Tribunal are carried out at the head-saw platform at dawn. The camp refers to this as the Blade. Detainees will use the camp's term.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *The Blade / ——. I looked for the plain word. On the train I had it in three languages. Here I could not write it down. They have made it so that I won't.*

**RULE SEVEN. RELIGIOUS OBSERVANCE.**
The camp chapel hut. The Reverend E. Moss. Sundays, by permission of the Office.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *He was at the gate when we came through, thumbing resin onto every brow and saying the same words each time. Nobody stopped him. It is still on mine. It smells of the woods we are going to cut.*

**RULE EIGHT. THE RACK.**
Every detainee is a billet on his crew's rack. The rack is checked at dawn. The log on your rack at dawn is who you are.
READ AND UNDERSTOOD: *99-0417*

> *The only rule they wrote as if it were a hymn.*

## IV. Requisition for Language Services

*[Release note: a half-sheet, folded into the packet after Part III.]*

**COUNTER INTELLIGENCE ██████ ████**

REQUISITION. DETAINEE 99-0417 IS TO BE RELEASED FROM SHIFT TO PROVIDE LANGUAGE SERVICES.
DATE: SUN 19 OCT 52
SUBJECT TO BE INTERVIEWED: ███-████
LANGUAGE: ██████
QUESTIONS (ASK IN ORDER. RECORD ANSWERS ONLY.)
1. Do you know your number?
2. Are you cold?
3. Are you hungry?
THE DETAINEE IS NOT TO DESCRIBE THE ROUTE, THE ROOM OR THE SUBJECT. ANSWERS ARE THE PROPERTY OF THE PROGRAM.
ANSWERS RECORDED: *1. —  2. —  3. Yes.*

> *A sack over my head from the yard. Stairs, more than I counted, and I count everything now. Then cold. Not October. Cold that came out of the walls, so I saw my breath indoors and it hung there.*
>
> *It was the pier man. Two days.*
>
> *Skin drawn tight on him like wet paper dried on a frame. Ribs standing out, every one, as if something inside were pushing to get out between them. Knuckles white. Fingertips black. A tube taped into the crook of one arm.*
>
> *On the table by the wall, under a cloth that did not cover all of it, the rest of two fingers, and a foot, each with a tag. A man in a white coat was writing on the tags. He did not look up either.*
>
> *I asked the first question. Nothing. The second. Nothing. He was watching my mouth the way the dogs at the platform watched the strongbox. At the third his head came round, all at once, and he made a sound that was not yes in any language, and the man in the white coat wrote "yes".*
>
> *I said the word for pier. That is not one of the questions. Something went over his face and was gone, like a coin going down in green water. I do not know whether he heard it. I wanted him to have heard it.*
>
> *Going back up I counted the stairs again and got a different number. The cold stayed down there. I was in the yard before I stopped seeing my breath.*

> *They gave me the billet at dawn. Oak, as promised. It is worn smooth at the two places where a hand takes it down from the rack and puts it back. Someone did that every morning, with a wider grip than mine. My number is burned in fresh. Under it the old brand is planed nearly out. I could read only that it was not mine.*
>
> *They moved me to his bunk. There is a black band on the post, and a dent in the straw on the left side, so he slept facing the wall. On the slat by the dent he scratched a few words in the third language, the one I have only part of. I know enough of it to know they are a children's counting rhyme, and not enough to know how it ends. I sleep on my left now, so the dent does not change.*
>
> *I asked the man in the next bunk where No. 7 went. He said the word the Office uses, and I will not write it, because I know now what it means here.*
>
> *He has said a third thing, too. Down in the Workings there is a board where the crew is chalked on and off each shift, and there is a name on it that nobody chalks off. He said it the way you say weather. I go down on Monday.*
>
> *The log on my rack at dawn is who I am. It was someone first.*
>
> *I have written my name nowhere in this packet. Field 2 would not take it. So, small, here, so that it is somewhere: ████████ ██████████.*
>
> *If they hand you this form after me, with my number planed off the oak: the pencil is in the ash box of the stove, at the back, on the left. Eat what they give you; you will not be able to stop. Write small. They do not ████ ███ ███████.*
