# Exhibits A to R

REGISTER OF EXHIBITS, BY KIND
THE TRIBUNAL AT THE BELL
HOLLOWMERE, COLDWATER TERRITORY. 1893.

*A Company form, printed back east, with eighteen kinds lettered down the margin. It is kept in the press behind the rent rolls in the Clerk's Office, because the Tribunal sits under a tarpaulin and has no room of its own.*

A. Body. B. Footprints. C. Thread. D. Snagged felt. E. Blood. F. Wet Bucket. G. Fingerprint. H. Drag smear. I. Time of death. J. Silhouette. K. Ledger. L. Dropped items. M. Fur. N. Claw marks. O. Drip trail. P. Glowing footprints. Q. Landing footprints. R. Long-stride footprints.

*Printed at the foot of the form: Exhibits are entered by kind, not by case.*

*[The bench numbers its exhibits by case: Exhibit 1, Exhibit 2, in the order they come up on the day. When the train goes at Dusk, the numbers go with it. The press keeps them by kind. A case ends. A kind does not. So one woman's things lie in four drawers here, and one drawer holds people who never met.*

*I was lent to the Tribunal for a day. The press came with the day, and the day has not given me back. — R.]*

---

**A. BODY.**
*Shows:* the cause. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* not held; the tag only. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** CYRIL, fisher of the mere channels. Wednesday, 29 November. Brought to the Undertaker at noon, from the channel below the marsh dock.

**Cause.** The form prints four words for the Doctor to strike: *knife / wolf / poison / shot.* None of them is struck. In the margin, in the Undertaker's word: *drowned.*

**The life.** In fog he whistled two notes, high then low, so Nora would know where he was on the water. She answered with two of her own from the dock, where she mends the nets.

*[The channel where they found him comes to the ankle at low water. That morning the low tide was circled on the chart at the Lantern. I have entered both facts. I draw no line between them; the form has no column for a line.*

*The square says a body put out of sight calls out again when its place is opened. Cyril was not put out of sight. He lay in the open channel with the water at his ankle, and nobody heard him at all. Nora still whistles at evening. — R.]*

---

**B. FOOTPRINTS.**
*Shows:* height, by the spacing of the stride. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* yes. *May be laid by a hand:* yes. A laid trail is marked Forged when it is inspected. None in this drawer is so marked.

**Card 1. Tag.** MABEL. Monday, 27 November, at the Station Rush. The lane behind the Drowned Lantern. Entered at the bench as part of Exhibit 2, with the Detective's two other traits: "Company boot, middling."

**The life.** See D, and see I. She is in more than one drawer.

*[A hundred men in this town wear the Company boot. Every boot on Mill Row has the boot-mender's stitch in the welt, because there was only the one boot-mender. Mine has it, in the right heel. Eleven cents scrip, still owing. — R.]*

**Card 2. Tag.** HATTIE, washerwoman, at the foot of the Rise. The Undertaker dates her Saturday, 2 December; she died in the night and was found at first light. In the mud of the lane, from the fence line down to the wash-house door: boot prints, and behind each one a long gouge where a heel was dragged.

*[The gouge is not a trait. The form has a line for height and none for a heel. The square read it as a trait anyway, all together, and the Watchman was fined three months' scrip and stood down. He drags his left heel. Everybody knows it. That is what made it evidence. — R.]*

---

**C. THREAD.**
*Shows:* scarf, by the colour. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* yes, on a square of clean paper. *May be laid by a hand:* yes. None in this drawer is marked Forged.

**Tag.** HATTIE. Taken out of her fist. The form says *snagged on a door or furniture.* Beside it, in my hand: *in a hand.*

"One thread, the weave of every scarf on the square."

**The life.** She did the town's wash and counted her rows every night, the way you count what is owed. She had a son back east and wrote to him every week through the Postmaster.

**Held with it, not entered.** The Watchman's lantern. The Tribunal took it off him "there on the table beside the thread". The press has no kind for a lantern, so it stands on top of the press, unlit.

*[Half the square wears that weave. Before I shut the drawer I laid my own scarf beside the thread on the rent roll. I have written nothing here about that. — R.]*

---

**D. SNAGGED FELT.**
*Shows:* hat. *Read by:* the Detective's dust. *Held:* yes. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** MABEL. "Felt nap, brown, on the same fence, higher." The form says *on the exit door frame.* Beside it: *fence; the lane has no door.*

**The life.** She took in mending on Mill Row and charged by the button, not by the hour. She sang alto at St Brannock's. She "came in a half-beat behind the rest, every verse, as if checking that the others meant it."

*[Height, scarf and hat, each Verified, each stamped. The Detective counted nineteen men in HollowMere who answer to all three. I have written the number in letters again, nineteen, as I did at the bench, so that nobody east can make it into another figure with one stroke. — R.]*

---

**E. BLOOD.**
*Shows:* that a person is bloody. *Read by:* anyone near enough to see it on them. *Held:* never. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** None. The drawer is empty by its nature. Blood is on a person, not on a thing, and it goes to the well before it comes to the press.

**Cross-filed here.** One rag, cotton, folded in four, red in two places: Tribunal Exhibit 4, from the boot-mender's box, under the last. Asked whether it was blood, Doc Mallory said: "It is red, sir. I would not swear it is anyone's." It is entered under E with one word: *not.*

*[See F. — R.]*

---

**F. WET BUCKET.**
*Shows:* that someone washed here. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* yes. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** The well bucket on the square, unhooked from its chain the morning Hattie was found (see B and C). For three days the square drew its water in its own pails and complained about it at the pump.

*[A well bucket is always wet. It was entered because the form has the line, and because the Watchman's lantern had been taken out of his hand and something had to go into the press that morning. The bucket sits in the bottom of the press and drips on the Pump Row roll. I have moved the roll. — R.]*

---

**G. FINGERPRINT.**
*Shows:* the last hand to touch. *Read by:* the Detective's dust. Compare gives *Match* or *No match*, never a name. *Held:* the lifts, on card. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** EDITH. One room at the far end of Pump Row, in November, after the riot. The bolt of her door, shot from the inside all night. Stamped NO CASE at the Constable's.

**Compared.** The lift from the bolt, against the lift from the edge of the letter on her table (see L).

*Match.*

*[The form never gives a name, and here it did not need one. Two prints, one hand, and the bolt shot from her side of the wood. Nobody had come in. — R.]*

---

**H. DRAG SMEAR.**
*Shows:* a trail, from where it was done to where it was put. *Read by:* anyone. Verified when inspected. *Held:* the lifted card. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** VERA, seamstress at the mill's cutting table. Saturday, 9 December, Mill Row. A smear across the boards of her one room, from the door to the clothes press, where they found her at home. Verified.

**The life.** She sat at the cutting table in Ivy Cotter's place and kept Ivy's chair empty beside her, and would not let anyone sit in it. Now there are two empty chairs at that table, and nobody sits in either.

*[The Police Officer opened the press in her room. He would not tell me what he heard when he did. He went out and stood in the Row for a while, with his back to her door.*

*Nothing from past the mouth of the Tin Gallery has come to this press. Nothing more is written here. — R.]*

---

**I. TIME OF DEATH.**
*Shows:* the hour, within the error. *Read by:* the Doctor, by Autopsy. *Held:* not held. The Doctor's word is entered, Verified.

**Tag.** MABEL. Opened at the Morgue on Monday, 27 November. Doc Mallory: between eleven on Sunday night and one on Monday morning. "Not before eleven. Not after one."

**Cross-filed.** The account of the witness Gert, Pump Row, 4th door. It covered the hour from eleven to one: the defendant sat at her table by her one candle and resoled her boy's boot. It was struck at the Third Sitting under Article V §3, the witness having been entered Indebted at Midday. Struck, and legible, the way the Company likes a strike made.

*[The hour was the case. Not the knife, and not the voices. I have kept the strike here exactly as I made it at the bench, the line through it and every word under the line. I have not added a word to it. I would have liked to. — R.]*

---

**J. SILHOUETTE.**
*Shows:* height and scarf. *Read by:* the Child, as "The Child saw it." *Held:* not held. The Child's word would be entered, Verified, at the next sitting.

**Tag.** None this season. The card is blank.

*[The Constable asked the girl of about nine, the one who counts. Everybody knows she counts things: the planks on the platform, the hands that go up at the Bell. She told him she counts feet. She does not look up at faces.*

*The press has asked. Children are about. — R.]*

---

**K. LEDGER.**
*Shows:* the last house each living soul entered this cycle. House only, no hour. *Read by:* the Butler. *Held:* a leaf, lodged.

**Tag.** A leaf from the household day-book of Vane Manor, in the hand of Horace, Butler for twenty-two years. It is headed Sunday, 26 November, was lodged at the Clerk's Office on the Monday, and was not called at the Tribunal.

Against the defendant, the line reads in full: *the widow's, Pump Row, 4th door.*

*[House only, no hour. It would not have helped him. The hour was the case.*

*The leaf is in one ink from heading to foot, without a blot. The Undertaker says Horace writes the round down before he walks it. I have entered that as the Undertaker did, as a habit. — R.]*

---

**L. DROPPED ITEMS.**
*Shows:* what the dead carried, except what belonged to an office; an office keeps its own. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* not by the form. These came in by the Constable's hand.

**Tag.** EDITH. See G.
- A letter to a sister back east, folded once, not finished.
- A postage stamp, two cents, bought at the Post Office with money kept back from the scrip. It was lying loose on top, not stuck down.

**The life.** She did washing for the manor and the mill office and was two weeks behind. The Undertaker writes that she said "two weeks" "as if, said carefully enough, it might come to one." Her sister had written to her twice. Edith had not finished writing back.

*[The press has not stuck the stamp down. Once it is stuck down the letter has to go, and nobody here knows where to. — R.]*

---

**M. FUR.**
*Shows:* wolf. Wolf kills leave fur, never footprints. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* yes. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** A MAN NOT OF THE GRANT. Saturday, 21 October, the rail bed below Gallows Wood Siding. He got off the down train at the Siding and walked in along the bed at dusk with a carpet bag. The platform crew found him at the Station Rush. Entered by the Constable. Name not entered: he was the Line's, not the Company's town, and the Line did not send one.

**Found.**
- On his back on the ballast, the bag still in his left hand.
- The throat open. The coat off the shoulder in strips, and the shoulder under it.
- The right arm gone at the elbow. It was found two days later, forty yards up the bed, without the hand.
- The face left alone.
- Grey fur in the wounds. No prints round him but his own, coming in from the Siding.

**Autopsy.** On the printed line, *wolf* is struck. Verified.

**The life.** In the carpet bag, wrapped in a Gazette: a pair of child's boots, new, the laces still tied together through the eyes. Nobody in HollowMere has asked for them. They lie in the drawer under the fur.

*[The Undertaker carried three that day, and Car 3 went out with four. This is the fourth. He was not one of the forty, so he was not the Undertaker's, so he is the press's.*

*The form and the town agreed this once. The Autopsy said wolf, the square said wolf, and it was a wolf. That is why the town can say it of the others. — R.]*

---

**N. CLAW MARKS.**
*Shows:* wolf. Wolf kills leave claw marks. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* a tracing on Company paper. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** No body. The lantern post at the Gallows Wood edge, on the Lamplighter's round. The marks are below the lamp, at a child's height, and the wood above them is scored the way bark goes when something climbs it in a hurry.

The chalk under the fourth lantern says Sid "chased his shadow to where the light stops". At the Gallows Wood edge the light stops at this post. The Lamplighter lit it at her hour. The wolves hunt the edge of the light and never cross it. In the morning the tracing was taken, and there was a child's cap on the fence beside the post. The Row says it is Sid's.

*[Whether a child came down off that post, the tracing does not say. His mother says he is about. Children are about. I have entered the cap as seen, not held: it is still on the fence.*

*On the morning of his Tribunal the Watchman asked for this tracing to be read. There was a cry at the fence, he said, and wolves in the square, and he went, because that is what the lantern is for. It was not read. The post is a quarter mile from the wash-house, and the marks are older than his night. — R.]*

---

**O. DRIP TRAIL.**
*Shows:* wounded. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* never; it is gone by the next rain. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** WALT, sawyer at the mill. Thursday, 12 October, after Dusk. The trail ran from the wood's edge above the mill race to Mallory's step. He was gored in the thigh by a boar at the wood's edge. Boars wound and do not kill. Salts and a bandage at Mallory's. Living.

The Constable's man followed the drips by lantern from the edge of the wood, expecting a killer at the end of them. At the end of them was Walt, sitting on the step with his trouser leg cut away, counting.

**The life.** Short two fingers from before the Panic, he counts on the hand that has five. He had counted the drips back toward the wood as far as he could see them, and he got to forty, and said that was about right for a pig.

*[The form has a line for wounded and none for living. I wrote it in the margin: living. It is the only word of its kind in the press. — R.]*

---

**P. GLOWING FOOTPRINTS.**
*Shows:* hunger, and the walker's own height, on the ground the walker crosses until a stroke is made; then they stop. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* never; they are gone by the Station Rush. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** ROSE. Pump Row, the night of Monday, 11 December.

A line of prints, faint and green, lit from inside like a coal under ash, came up the Row from the square end. No lantern reaches that end, because the Lamplighter's round does not come that way. The prints stop at Rose's door.

**The life.** She kept a geranium in a tin on her sill, the only flower on Pump Row, and carried it in at curfew the way you would carry in a child. That night it stayed out.

**Witness.** GERT, Pump Row, 4th door. *Indebted; not a witness.* Entered by order, and struck:

> ~~They came up past my step. I saw them through the crack of the door. They were the size of anybody's. They went to Rose's and they did not go on.~~

*[Struck, and legible. She is Indebted and may not bear witness, so the Company keeps what she saw and has the line put through it, so that the east can see what was taken out.*

*Tuck, who poles the marsh, says the island has prints like these. He says it the way the town says the weather. Nobody has asked him more. I have not asked him either.*

*The form calls it a kind. Somebody back east set type for it and gave it a letter. So it has happened before, somewhere the form was printed for. — R.]*

---

**Q. LANDING FOOTPRINTS.**
*Shows:* height, at the foot of a drop that does not go back up. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* a tracing. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** Monday, 4 December, the platform. At the foot of the drop from the platform edge to the rail bed: a trail, short in the stride. It goes east along the bed a few yards and is lost in the ballast. Nothing climbs back up.

**The day.** Mill Row says Ada Brook lost her grey scarf that Monday, between the pump and the platform. It was her mother's, darned in red at one end. On the Tuesday she paid Crumb's ahead for ten wake loaves for Mill Row.

*[I have not laid her beside the tracing. I do not know her height. The Undertaker's line for her reads neither, and I have made the press's line read the same. A few yards, and then the ballast. — R.]*

---

**R. LONG-STRIDE FOOTPRINTS.**
*Shows:* height. *Read by:* anyone. *Held:* not from any case. *May be laid by a hand:* no.

**Tag.** Specimen. The print beside the well on the square, older than the books. The Constable's office keeps a card of it so new hands learn the reading before they are sent out. The card says, in print: *Footprints, long stride: Tall.*

**Cross-filed.** Crumb's Ovens. In October, on a Wednesday or a Thursday night, the till was emptied and there was "a boot in the flour dust, long in the stride". On the fourth of December the print was short. The baker stood in both in her own flour and wrote on her notice: "I fit neither, and I fit both, depending on how I walk."

*[I went out at the Midday Rent with the roll under my arm and stood in the specimen. I fit it. Then I walked the way I walk when I am carrying the roll, and I did not. — R.]*

---

*Last leaf, under the eighteenth line.*

*[Eighteen kinds, A to R. Height, scarf and hat. The hour. The house. Wolf, wounded, hunger. The Detective said it at the bench and I wrote it down: "Verified clues narrow the board. They don't name." The press has a drawer for every kind of thing a hand leaves. It has none for a name. The defendant's name is a blank in the bench's book, and it is a blank in mine.*

*Exhibit R and I share a letter. I have signed under it anyway, as the form wants, and blotted it. Nobody east will need to know the difference.*

*— R.]*

*Exhibits are entered by kind, not by case.*
