Wikilink back to [[11_Essays/05_How_to_Help_People|How to Help People]].

Of the six essays in this folder, this is the one that actually executes the full claim → parable → practice → closing-callback rhythm cleanly: the claim (a childhood conviction that self-sacrifice equals worth), the parable (the airline oxygen-mask safety demonstration), the practice (concretely: "I stopped treating self-care as indulgence... I learned to align helping others with my own goals"), and a closing callback that reframes the opening's "ledger" language into "the world doesn't need a martyr." Unlike 02 and 03, it mostly resists over-explaining the central image — the oxygen-mask line is allowed to land once, plainly, rather than being immediately re-narrated in abstract terms — though the final paragraph does drift into moralizing ("there is a fierce selfishness in loving your life, and it is the kind that saves others") that restates the thesis rather than trusting the mask parable to carry it alone. Its abstract-noun vocabulary is comparatively sparse and personal rather than architectural — "ledger," "mission," "boundaries" — a narrower, more narrative register than 02's "architecture/sovereignty/doctrine" family, likely because this piece stays inside a single autobiographical anecdote instead of building a system. Genuinely first-person and confessional throughout, with no POV drift into "we" or "you," which makes it the most stable POV of the set.
