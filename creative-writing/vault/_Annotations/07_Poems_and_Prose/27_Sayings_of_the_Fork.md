The game's forty-seven abilities, turned into what a town makes of the things that keep happening to it: proverbs. *Never wash at the well after dark*; *a bell rung for nothing is a bell rung for you*. Each section of the poem starts from a saying the Row says without thinking, and Agnes follows it back to the person it came from, a habit or a death worn smooth by repetition until no one hears whose it was. The ability rules (the Killer's well-wash, the Bell vote, hiding, counting, the borrowed face, the bottle) appear only as folk wisdom. None of them is a manual, and the reader who knows the game recognises the mechanics in the custom.

The address holds it together. Agnes speaks to Edith, her neighbour, at the stone, the morning after Rose, with the arm the wolf took at the pump bound, so the poem's own speaker carries *One Shot* in her body. Section VIII, *What we don't give sayings to*, is the turn. Some deaths are too near to be worn smooth yet, and there the sayings stop and the poem names nothing. The stone's place follows the Register and *Persons of Note* (the churchyard), a disagreement with *The Loudest Voice* the vault has carried since wave 3 and leaves standing. The Doctor stays without a pronoun, *the word the Doc writes down*. The last line states the method as a saying of its own: *A saying is a name worn smooth. Say it first, and no one has to hear whose it was.*
