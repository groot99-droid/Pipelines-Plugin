Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/30_Eight_Phrases|Eight Phrases]].

The Medium's card (Seance Chalk, three Candles, eight seconds, one of eight phrases, "Cabal ghosts may lie") rendered entirely in 1893 terms: a twist of paper with three sticks of chalk, three candles, eight counts "a held breath's length", and the liar rule as "the dead who were on the knife's side may lie." The eight phrases are an invention, since the game fixes only the number, and each heads a block in small capitals.

The mode's engine is the refrain *I do not tell*, which shifts as it returns (*I did not tell Nora*, *I do not tell him*) and turns over in the last line. Each block hinges on one rhetorical question ("What use is a phrase that is true of every death in HollowMere?"; "Which would you rather I brought up out of the ring, a wolf or a neighbour?"), and the dead each keep one habit taken from the Register: Mabel's hymn-sheet boots, Cyril's two notes, Hattie's capitals over the copper and her button jar, Maud's darned cuffs. No phrase is ever given to any of them, so no death is settled, and the hanged man's waxed thread leans toward the boot-mender reading without saying it.

The turn is block five's admission that there is "one liar I am sure of in every ring I draw", paid off in block eight: Jory picks *Has it come?* every night and she answers *It has.* The dead may lie to keep a side, and she lies to keep a man asking. The poem sits between *Confession Book* No. 18 (20 November) and *Pledged* No. 4097 (4 December), when she pawns the chalk, and it closes on the mode's explicit aphorism: *I do not tell the living what the dead know. I tell the dead what they need to hear.*
