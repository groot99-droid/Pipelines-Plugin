Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/01_Trips_Are_Like_Exes|Trips Are Like Exes]].

The piece runs on one extended conceit — trips as breakups, "Love the memories, but wouldn't ever go back" — and stretches it until it becomes a whole theory of living rather than a joke about vacations. The four unlineated blocks each escalate in register: the first sets the metaphor, the second turns crude and aphoristic ("we'd drink poison to feel more alive"), and the third and fourth pivot from cynicism into something closer to earnest metaphysics, describing the self "dissolved" and then reassembling "like a pool of identity in the shards of the after-party." That image of identity as shards collecting back into a pool is the poem's best moment — it makes ego death and ego recovery a single continuous gesture rather than two separate ideas. The closing quotation, set off and labeled "PART 1," functions as a thesis statement retrofitted onto the poem after the fact, and the "PART 1" tag signals the author was already thinking of this as the opening move in a longer, unwritten sequence about beauty and ugliness as inseparable.
