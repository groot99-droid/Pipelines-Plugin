Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/24_The_Apostate_the_Stowaway_the_Starving|The Apostate, the Stowaway, the Starving]].

The three world-state figures of the Bible (the Apostate, the Stowaway, the Starving) as one triptych built on a single sum: what each was owed against what each was sent. Each panel lands on its own aphorism, and the coda adds them up: "One was owed a god. One was owed a fare. We were owed bread. / Nobody came to pay. Everybody came to count." The Apostate's grief is the cause the game leaves offstage. His wife coughed in October, and the Light and the Ash both came after, never before. The rusted fork is a fact about neglect ("Rust is what iron grows when nobody asks it for anything") before it is a weapon, and the snap is one fair stroke against each faith. The Stowaway's panel turns the Baron's freight manifest into the poem's ledger (flour six, six, six, five, *Cancelled*; powder doubling; lead rising). The Starving are spoken in the first person plural, and the cruelty is a train that came back once and so taught them to keep waiting. Restraint by the collective voice: "Nobody here is a lesson. Everybody here is hungry in their own way". It refuses to make the hungry an allegory even as the form invites it. The Ascended Vicar is only talk at the Lantern ("ten more of him praying at me"), the soapbox woman stands unnamed, and the dawn line is turned to an empty plate. The child counting crumbs was kept at the owner's word. Plot logic: not applicable, poem form.
