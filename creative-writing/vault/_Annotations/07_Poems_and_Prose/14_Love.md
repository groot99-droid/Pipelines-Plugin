Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/14_Love|Love]].

The poem's central move is treating love itself as a weapon rather than a comfort: it "was sharpened, / where it glinted in the light like something beautiful / until you felt it slide between your ribs." Two parallel stanzas — "I was conditioned to love my mother," "I was conditioned to love my father" — indict each parent in a distinct register, the mother through the image of hugs as "snares" and a body that "remembered / what her hands had done before," the father through transactional imagery of a love that "came with a price tag." The poem's most exposed line is the admission that self-harm functioned as preemptive control — "if I lay the blade to my own skin / before they ever get the chance" — and the closing stanza deliberately loops back to the opening's "sharpened... glinted" language, resolving on love as "a shadow / I watch from across the room," an image of permanent, guarded distance rather than resolution.
