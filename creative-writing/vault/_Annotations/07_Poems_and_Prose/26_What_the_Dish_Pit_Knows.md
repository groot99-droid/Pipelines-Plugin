Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/26_What_the_Dish_Pit_Knows|What the Dish Pit Knows]].

The Old Brigade of the Testament (the island's oldest kitchen offices, once the quick mode's cards) given voice as the people who kneel below the Hall. Each of the eight sections is one first-person voice and one date, so the sequence walks through the wave's history from underneath: 1787 under the Gallery, the night of the Crown's man in September 1789, the Commons' committee at the command desk, May 1793 with the feeding hand upstairs. The kitchen brigade trope is settled as a caste. The office does not change, and only the colour of the crust above it does. The Critique's empty chair is kept in the front of house as a seat no one sits in and no one clears. The Commis carries plates to the pit and ash to the Mother, and asks once whose ash it is. By the owner's ruling it stays a question. The quick mode's folk figures come back as island custom. The Sin-Eater eats a crust over an empty platter for a copper, so that the sin goes into the eater and not into the ground, and the children of Soizic, the Debtor, pay in buttons. The victims keep their lives in single objects: Yannick's odd buttons sorted by colour on the Wharf steps, Soizic's blue-thread initials inside her children's caps, Armand's sea-pinks pressed in a psalter and his one spoken line, asking which way the kitchen stairs went. By the check-in he is no longer "the first". The dusk loaf is heard through the floor, set "for a name under seal". The refrain ("every plate comes down") closes on the wave's thesis: "They changed the colour of the crust. They never changed who kneels."
