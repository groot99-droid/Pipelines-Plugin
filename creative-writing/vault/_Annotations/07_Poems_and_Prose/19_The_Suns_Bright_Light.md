Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/19_The_Suns_Bright_Light|The Sun's Bright Light]].

A single ten-line stanza in rhymed couplets (hue/too, ledge/pledges, clear/dear, known/shone, breath/death), this is the gentlest piece in the set — a gratitude lyric rather than a trauma narrative. Its central move is a paradox held in one line, "Forever young, yet ancient too," which the poem never resolves so much as sits inside, using the sun as a stable, permanent presence against which the speaker measures their own memory ("I recall what I've always known— / Like a tree in December, its light has shone"). The closing couplet raises the stakes past simple description into something closer to reverence: the sun as "a radiant gift that conquers death," a claim that stands out for its unguarded warmth next to the wariness and reckoning that dominate the surrounding poems in this file set.
