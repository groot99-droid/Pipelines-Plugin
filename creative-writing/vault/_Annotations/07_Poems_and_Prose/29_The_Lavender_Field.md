Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/29_The_Lavender_Field|The Lavender Field]].

The memorial cutscene, told by the one person in the square who knows what an unreported death sounds like. Nora's grief is already in canon (Cyril, the channel that came to the ankle, the word *drowned* the Undertaker let stand), and the poem uses it as its measure: she counts the twenty-one seconds by the gap between Cyril's two notes and her answer, and she sees the 8-bit picture the way a net-mender would, in squares held up to a lamp. That keeps the game's pixel-art memorial inside 1893 without explaining it. The Vicar's last words are given verbatim, broken only at sentence ends.

The turn is section IV, when the picture goes out and the square looks round, the way you look for the widow at a wake, and finds the robes at the back. Three rhetorical questions (*Who wears a dead man's coat to his wake?*) do the mode's work, and the wearer is never named, faced or given a gender. A single corrected slip, *He's here … They're here*, is the only time the robes come near a pronoun. Section V is the confession, built on the anaphora *I said dream*. It reads *The Holy War*'s entry that "more than one roof dreamt the same thing" from inside: they were awake, and they chose the word. Nora also wrote the unsigned paper that crossed the Clerk's counter, so the poem makes her the town's one report, given anonymously. The last line states the point outright, as the mode asks: *A dream is what a town calls the thing it saw together and will not report.* It leans toward the Trope Ledger's "it was all a dream" without settling it, which is left to wave 8.
