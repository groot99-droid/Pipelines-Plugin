Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/23_Hopscotch|Hopscotch]].

A skipping rhyme in the folk-horror tradition, one stanza per chalk square, each tied to a landmark (the bell, the pump, the gallows stairs, the pines, the well, the chapel rope), with a refrain that is also the game's rule for the square: "Ten chalk squares and ten of us. / Hop the stone and don't look down." The children carry adult villager names from the canon list, so the rhyme is a roll-call of the town's futures. Nell's verse ("Nell rode the back car east") agrees with the wave-1 Train manifest, which sends her coffin out on 16 October. "Hop the stone" works as the children's word for confessing, and the rhyme never says to whom. Ambiguity locks: the glove in the rope is unassigned ("Nobody says whose"); the light in the fen may or may not be the drowned lantern; who redraws the chalk each morning is never shown. Alfie's thirteen gallows steps are flagged as a child's count, not town canon. Plot logic: not applicable, poem form.
