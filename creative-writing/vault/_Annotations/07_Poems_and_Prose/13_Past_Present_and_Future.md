The poem opens with a direct self-definition ("I am but a vessel... neither shackled to the ruins of my past / nor tethered to the mirage of my future") before shifting into a loom/weaving conceit — "The path beneath your feet / is the loom upon which your destiny is woven" — that carries the poem's middle section. The third stanza runs a repeated conditional structure ("If all the world's flora were only petals... Yet if every plant were only sustenance...") as a thought experiment about balance, applying it to people ("if all were teachers, / wisdom would smother joy"). The closing stanza pivots the whole poem's argument onto self-worth with a garden/weed image — "perhaps it is not we who are lacking, / but the garden itself / that fails to recognize / the rare bloom in its midst" — turning a poem that started as self-address into a reframing of how others' judgment should be read.
