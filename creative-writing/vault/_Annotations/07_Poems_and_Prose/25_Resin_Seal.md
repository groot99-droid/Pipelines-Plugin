Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/25_Resin_Seal|Resin-Seal]].

A confession that changes speakers when the confessor dies. Part I is Moss in the vault's confessional mode: plain lines, the Friday Pastoral Return as the sin, and a question chain that will not let him off ("Did my word walk him out there? Or was he already walking"). It ends on its aphorism: "I was a good shepherd to the ledger." The stores slip and the roster frame him in the camp's own forms, and the roster supplies his epitaph: "not at work for reasons of religion", the echo *Cables* also carries. Part III hands the poem to his collar, an object narrator who knows him only as a throat. That device lets the death come in three short lines at the owner's Man Eater level ("Then the jaw. / Then my starch went soft and red") without the man ever being seen to suffer, and lets the transformation be read through cloth: a throat that runs hot in frost, a jaw that clicks against the edge, healed marks "in rows" ("I do not know what was done to him. I know it was done in order."). The Timber-Wolf wearing the chaplain's collar and saying his blessing with numbers is the skin-walking trope turned liturgical: "It sounds the same, and it works the other way." Part IV gives the Pack their answers, each a refrain on "You said come down a man". The Den-Keeper, a woman, keeps Moss's body in the frost as sexton, and her role is the game's Minion-style keeping of the body. Part V is the Howl as a yard book and a night log: lines, 5, on five buildings; six figures, fifteen seconds timed on the roll-call watch; every rack ten of ten at dawn. The Pack is entirely inside the rules, which is the horror. The flute-like groan was cut here. The blessing's names, and Moss's own said once at the end, are the poem's argument about what a name is for.
