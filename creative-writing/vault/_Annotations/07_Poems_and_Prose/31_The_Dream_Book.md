Wikilink back to [[07_Poems_and_Prose/31_The_Dream_Book|The Dream Book]].

The Trope Ledger's last open row, *It was all a dream* (tired), settled by subversion: here the dream is not what erases events but what a mother says to keep her boy out of every column, and the boy turns the comfort down because it would erase the one thing he ever did. His test is in the head note and the last line: a dream doesn't leave you the spelling. Each Monday entry runs the same three moves (what he saw; Mam at the clock, "It was a dream"; a station name in large pencil capitals copied off something he could put his finger on in daylight), and the breaks in the pattern are his honesty: a blank margin under the light on the chapel step, and Percy's marbles written small "in the middle of the page, with the dreams".

The hinge is entry IV. He agrees that the Percy dream was a dream ("This one was a dream. I know the difference. That's how I know the others aren't."), which is what makes the refusal credible. Entry III holds the poem's one echo of *The Lavender Field*'s last line, paraphrased: a dream in their house is "a thing two of us saw at the same time and neither of us will say." The confession is entry V, the step up onto the last train with Arthur's ticket and back down, given as a triad of maybes, and ending on the sharpest question in the poem: "If it was a dream, I never gave it back. If I never gave it back, I'm gone. Which boy does she want at the table?"

The 29 and 30 December images keep strictly to what *Arrivals* and *The Holy War* already show, and settle nothing. The margins copy the Line's real stations (WIDOWS CUT, GALLOWS WOOD SIDING, THORNE JCT), leaving out the apostrophe as Clem let him, and the last is the first word he ever copied, HOLLOWMERE, the only name his mother will let be real. The aphoristic close is exact to the book: *Everything in my margins did.*
