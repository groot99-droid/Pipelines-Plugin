The vault's horror engine — escalation-by-catalogued-incident — run through a railroad form: the Company print is flat procedure (timetable, twelve Rules of Carriage, a manifest per day), and the plot lives entirely in Conductor Amos Pruett's pencil. The numbered rules work the way Project: Man Eater's rules do: four of them are loaded early (coffins unopened, black wax unread, "a passenger booked in is a passenger counted out", and Rule 12's "signed by whoever remains") and each goes off in turn. The causal ledger (12 rows, PASS, cyclical) alternates cleanly: every manifest's wrongness is a *but* that breaks the previous day's shelving — tin ballast does not carry a name; one off, one on does not explain a ticket punched where she never got off — and every shelving is the *therefore* the next incident must break. The decay of agency is typographic: the excuses shrink from three lines to one, then to bare rule numbers ("4."), then to nothing, and the PRUETT, A. coffin is entered in Company print rather than pencil. The ending repeats the opening pencil line in a second hand on a new Manifest I, Melting Away's cyclical hand-off rendered as paperwork. Restraint holds by objects: Bert Haddon's pipe, Nell's blue ribbons, Ivy Cotter's thimble on its string — the dead are present only as what was packed with them, and no coffin is ever opened.
