The vault's catalogue-of-incidents engine aimed at the game's own scenery. The fourteen standing props every player walks past become a case file that has to come back clean. The causal ledger (22 rows, PASS, complicit) is a ladder of shelving rules, each one broken by the next thing on the list. "Every hand has a receipt" is broken by the clock no hand can move. "Older than the books is history" is broken by the chart's wet ink. "The town keeps its old things current out of habit" is broken by the lost hour under the fourth lantern, which leads on to miasma, the second witness and coincidence. Then "Reserved: Det." breaks coincidence, the Gamekeeper breaks the Constable's "the office is new", and the crate shows that the careless predecessor was careful. The decay is typographic: the working lines (Collected / Inspected / Cross-referenced / Stamped) lose Inspected at the portrait he will not turn, and lose Cross-referenced at the stone that renews itself. The prose carries the decay so the device never announces itself. The one unnumbered entry is the piece's act of restraint. Edith gets her rent, her younger sister back east, the unfinished letter and the two-cent stamp she never stuck down. She dies behind a door bolted from inside, with the Detective's back against it, and the cause is never written. Owner decisions: the drowned lantern is 13th and the bootprints 14th, so nobody is ever shown lighting the lantern; the Detective stays unnamed; the ending is complicit. "The detective did it" is subverted: he files himself rather than killing anyone. The first line returns as the last over a new list.
