Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/24_Arrivals|Arrivals]].

The Daily Train as the master clock, made into the book a platform hand keeps against the Company's own form. Its method is exclusion. Clem's head note lists what the book does not count, quoting the Rules of Carriage verbatim: freight is the Conductor's, Car 3 the Undertaker's, persons of rank go uncounted under Rule 6, and men walking the ties never come off a train. The book's dread lives in those gaps. The Baron cannot be entered, so Clem counts his dogs (*Twelve dogs in this book, and no Baron*). Owen, killed on the ties, has no line, which keeps *A Field Guide*'s claim true. The railyard man who arrives on 11 October gives a name that Clem will "write when he leaves", and when he leaves in Car 3 on 12 December Clem leaves the space blank.

The book also reconciles the wave. The weeks agree line by line with the Timetable's manifests, with *Exhibits*, *The Holy War*, *Four Notices* and the wave-7 stories. The last train carries eleven, one of them with a case carried up the step alone. A woman and a boy come running after it has gone. Then there are five pages ruled through as *No train*, their margins shrinking day by day, and Wenna asks for the first time *has it come*. The only second hand is a child's: Ernie copies one station name into the margin each week as Clem teaches him to read, from HOLLOWMERE to THORNE JUNCTION, and asks where it goes after that. The close breaks the form once. Under *Off, at Day*, against Rule 6, Clem writes *1* for the man off the private car: *Not counted (Rule 6). Counted.* The Dusk cell is left as a dash.
