The Bible's "Last Shift's Tally Board" turned into a horror story about a bookkeeping rule. The causal ledger (12 rows, PASS, complicit) runs on what a clerk can and cannot write. He signs for the board, so the count is his to close; but the old name never comes up, and the crew eats short for his residual. Therefore he reads CLEAN SLATE EACH SHIFT as licence and washes it, and the bread comes back. But on payroll Friday Wrona's chalk is gone and the name is back in its place, and the office cannot log a missing man the board does not show. Therefore Hask writes *Transferred*, and Corwin lets it stand. But Wrona's lamp check comes up wet to the nail, with a curl of fingertip skin round it, and through the winze boards he sees the refuge light and the white thing that holds its breath for 212 counts. Therefore he works out the trade (wash the name, the board takes a man) and leaves it up, and the crew hates him. But the audit notice sets his own billet against a clean board. Therefore he washes it, the Inspector-General signs a closed count, and Danny does not come up. From there the turns are complicit: he writes *transferred* in his own hand ("The word was easy once it was his"), is rotated down by the rule he served, and chalks the name back in for the next keeper, gaps and all. The smudge is the wave's main chalked-name lock: guessable, never read aloud. Inner lives are kept in small objects: his daughter's long division with its remainder ("the remainder is not a mistake"), Wrona's pine whistle finished below by somebody else, Danny's deck with no queen. The scientist and the blackout pass as single lines that Corwin "did not follow", which is the story's indictment. The last line repeats the first, read aloud by the new keeper.
