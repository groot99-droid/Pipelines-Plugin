Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/01_People_of_the_Bark|People of the Bark]].

This is a pitch-format screen treatment, not a prose narrative — it uses scene-slug headers ("Opening Scene — 'The Tremor'"), emoji section markers, and a stated "tone" and "moral" the way a concept deck does, rather than showing the story through action alone. The core device is scale-inversion: the bark people's cosmology (windstorms as trials, birdsong as prophecy, sap as sacred water) is built entirely from real natural phenomena reinterpreted as myth, and each beat pairs an in-world cataclysm with its mundane real-world cause stated flatly right after ("Just a squirrel landing on the trunk," "someone checking for termites"). That punchline structure is the whole comic-cosmic engine of the piece — it never plays the juxtaposition as dramatic irony to be discovered, it states both readings side by side and trusts the gap between them to do the work. The closing line ("Even the smallest world is a universe to the ones who live in it") is delivered as authorial thesis rather than earned through scene, which marks this as a concept/pitch document rather than a finished narrative — useful as worldbuilding shorthand for a "micro-scale epic" premise rather than as executable prose.
