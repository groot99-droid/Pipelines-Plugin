The eighteen clue types of the game, entered as an evidence register by the one man who has handled all of them. Each drawer has three voices. The printed form gives the game's wording (*snagged on a door or furniture*), the Recorder corrects it in his own hand (*in a hand*; *fence; the lane has no door*), and then comes **The life**, where the victim is given back what the evidence cannot hold: Cyril's two whistled notes in the fog for Nora, Hattie counting her wash rows like a debt and writing to a son back east, Mabel charging by the button and coming in half a beat late at alto. The restraint rule is the register's own method: the form records what was done, and the Recorder insists on who it was done to.

The cross-filing is how the piece reasons. Mabel is in more than one drawer; Gert's account is struck because she is Indebted, which leaves the green prints at Rose's sill seen and unwitnessed at once; Exhibit E is empty by its nature, because blood goes to the well before it comes to the press. The drawers tie the wave's new cases (the traveller in Car 3's fourth box, Walt's October boar wound, Sid's fate left open after *Hopscotch*) to cases already in the vault, so the register works as an index of the town's dead. Fur and Claw marks are where the wolves stand in as the alibi: the form accepts them and the Doctor's book does not. The last kind, R, turns the method on its keeper. A long-stride print he stands in and fits, and the register closes on a rule of filing rather than a confession: *Exhibits are entered by kind, not by case.*
