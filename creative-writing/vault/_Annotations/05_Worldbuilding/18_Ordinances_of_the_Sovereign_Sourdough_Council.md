The island's founding law and its revolution in one copy. The black type is the Gallery's 1787 codex, written in the Bible's own institutions (the platters, the seal, the nightly yeast-tax, UNREGISTERED, the Inquisitor-to-Chancellor ladder and its three verdicts), and it reads as order: rank is a seating plan, crime is a weight, sentence is a supper. The red is the Commons' committee in 1793, and its method is the piece's argument. Almost nothing is struck. The committee writes *Retained*, with a substitution or an order reversed. The gilded platters move to the cold end by the south doors, gilt is scraped and entered by weight, and the reader watches the Terror arrive as an editor keeping a law it means to use. The restraint rule is held by bookkeeping. Under Art. 14 the red gives one case from each half, a net-mender's daughter of fourteen in 1787 and a Lormeau in 1793, and each is given a habit with the gulls (counting them; feeding them crusts from the gallery window). The margin never says the two mirror each other, and the reader has to. Title VIII, *Of Dusk*, is the only title with no red. The committee that rewrote rank, tax and judgment does not touch the loaf on the empty platter "for a name under seal". That silence carries the wave's backward echo of the Testament without a word of explanation. The closing red line, *We are the Council*, settles who won and how little changed.
