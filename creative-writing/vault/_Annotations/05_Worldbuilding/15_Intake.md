BlackPine re-founded as Grounds 99 begins at the gate, so the first BlackPine work is the form every arrival fills in. The printed fields and the eight Rules carry the camp's whole system (number for name, billet wood by trade, the rack, the Midnight Roll, scrip, Lung-Rot, the Blade, the motto), and the detainee's pencil carries the person, in italic blocks after each field. His signature device is the slash pair (*Available / empty. Empty / someone.*, *Quota / the price of tomorrow*, *The Blade / ——*), a translator's habit of holding the camp's word against the plain one, and it decays: at "Fuel / " he cannot fill the second half. The interpreter's three and a half languages are the piece's measure of what the camp takes. He has a word for everything until Rule Six, and the requisition in Part IV makes his trade the instrument: three questions asked in order, the third answered with "a sound that was not yes in any language", which the man in the white coat writes down as yes. That scene is the wave's first plain look at the trials, at the owner's Man Eater level (skin like wet paper on a frame, ribs, black fingertips, a taped tube, tagged parts under a cloth), and it is kept to what the interpreter saw: there is no procedure on the page. The pier man's dive for pennies, set against a breath-hold baseline of 41 seconds, is the one mercy the form permits: "I said the word for pier. That is not one of the questions." Diet as control (Rule Two, "not refused") echoes Rule One of *Project: Man Eater* without naming it. The rack slip closes the packet as a hand-me-down life: a billet worn smooth by a wider grip, a dent in the straw, a counting rhyme in the third language. His name is written once and redacted, which is the packet's last joke and its sharpest. No chalked-name fragment appears here, by the continuity lock; the "name nobody chalks off" is only weather, passed on by the man in the next bunk.
