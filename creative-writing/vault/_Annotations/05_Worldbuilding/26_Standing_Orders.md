Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/26_Standing_Orders|Standing Orders]].

The three faction enforcers of the Bible (Pinkertons, Iron-Wardens, OEAs) as three found documents that never speak to each other. The binding is the only thing that joins them, and the binder's bracketed notes are strictly physical: paper, nail holes, a glass ring, three ribbons. Each print stays in its own era's register (the Articles' commercial English, the Ordinances' Article-and-Title French, the 1952 mimeograph with redaction bars), and the margins belong to the people the orders were used on.

The rhyme the binding exposes is one clause in each book: *The manner is not set down here.* It is the vault's no-technique rule turned into the documents' own policy. Baton, staff, gas and slug-gun are issued and listed and never shown in use, and the blank Book I margin under Order 4 is the loudest entry in it. Each book carries a different restraint level by map: HollowMere's dogs whine at the fen edge (the shape at the edge of the light); the island's spectacle is classist and absurd (Montaury held in a pantry her own article says she cannot be held in, asking for the heel); Grounds 99's one Man Eater-level margin is a man carried in a sack, given back his inner life in three lines (bass at the bread line, sugar saved for the man with the cough, a boy in Duluth).

The ribbons mark failure, not authority: Book I at the Order of Return, when the detail cannot leave ("Nine came. Seven stand. / Two were at the Gate."); Book II at the last Title, where the unaltered 1789 rule served both halves of the Revolution; Book III at the 19 December blackout, when no bunk could be lit and so nobody was missing. The close is a physical fact, boards that will not open flat, and the reader does the sum.
