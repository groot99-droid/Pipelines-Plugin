Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/14_The_Holy_War|The Holy War]].

The owner's Holy War mode written as an epic-fantasy chronicle by the one narrator with no faith in it, a man of columns. The form is the Company's: carbons wired east, codes that fit in a wire (*sown*, *doubt*, *lapsed*), and a roof-by-roof tally (**L**, **A**, **—**, **?**) that turns the mode's win condition ("the first faith to hold every roof holds HollowMere") into something a reader can watch move. Five books follow the campaign. In the Sowing, the faithful walk in threes and see in the dark. In the Doubt, the stone names THE VICAR on 17 December and the robes go on preaching. In the Dark Positions, each of the owner's four offices gets a section title and a deed but never a face: the keeper (the body unfound, bone chimes, no drag in the mud), the collector (brass knuckles, mobs in flour-sack hoods hunting the threes), the herald (the five marks scrubbed and back by morning, tracts in the mailbags, the Entranced walking to the Gallery), and the shepherd ("the one who will carry on"). In the Return, the Ascended Vicar comes off the train and Mill Row goes from *1 L* to *10 L* in an afternoon, and the crosier opens the manor's locked wings. The Count closes the year. The refrain is a watch. The Vicar asks the Clerk the time at every meeting, and the false Vicar does not, which is how the Clerk knows before the town does. The Baron's succession is carried entirely by hints, as the owner ruled. The writ "uses a word for me that my mother never once said aloud". The tag on the key is for the study door. The year's end is written in that study. "The columns are mine now; I have not said so and will not." The last **—** in HollowMere is under his own roof, so neither faith can win while he stays neither, and the signed but undated curfew-lock form sits on his desk. The outcome stays open: five walkers, a sixth lost in fog, a margin numeral that could be 5 or 6, and the wire breaking off at "SKY". Locks: the cabal and the Dark Positions are never named, the borrowed face under the robes is "a face from this town" and no more, and whether the stone cuts the name or only knows it first is entered as not watched. Plot logic: not applicable, chronicle form.
