The Reign-of-Terror register the owner asked for, made by keeping the horror in the kitchen's own grammar. Each receipt is a law or a crime. The Wake Loaf is identity. The loaf for the empty platter is plain, unsealed and carried "for a name under seal". The Bitter-Citron varnish "marks a thief's hands yellow and does nothing else". The absurd cruelty is the execution supper: courses timed to the sentence, a seating plan, a costing that charges the condemned's tariff for the leaf on the brioche. The death itself is one subordinate clause between two dishes, which is the restraint rule kept at its strictest. Victims keep a life in the guest lists and kitchen notes: Jacquette Gaudet's drowned husband's thirty-one hooks counted into a tin, Corentin Hébert's driftwood cod on the pantry shelf. The Nightshade Glaze is three ruled blank lines and an ordinance in place of the receipt, so the sweet that hides poison is settled with no technique at all. The Inspector's supper of 23 September 1789 stops mid-menu, and the next page is in red. The Commons do not invent a cuisine. They rename one. The Consommé becomes Citizen's Broth and the Gilded Brioche becomes the Bread of Equality, the subverted *let them eat cake*. A Soup for the Tumbril Crowd is timed to the cart from the Hall's south doors. The Supper for the Razor is the 1787 menu again, a sugar Razor beside the pièce montée in shape only. Its *Served* column is empty because the desk did not record it, which leaves the Vane's hour open as *The Dawn Judgment* does.
