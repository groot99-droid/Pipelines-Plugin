This is the project's keystone and its bookkeeping in one: a codex disguised as a company ledger. Every entry runs the same three-beat form — **Record** (what is painted or stamped: the game's frozen names), **Canon** (what the town says: the owner's Bible), **Ruling** — so the reconciliation work of the whole project (the Rusty Lantern that became the Drowned Lantern, Gallows Wood and the Fenwood as one forest, Vane dying on the island but three nights dead inland) is done in-world rather than in a design note. The clerk's refrain, "Entered. Ruled. Not believed.", turns each ruling into its own small doubt, and the margin notes in the same hand let that doubt leak ("I have written 'miasma' in the Company's books and 'mere-rot' in my own. I cough in both."). The structure deliberately breaks at Entry XV, the rumours, which the clerk sets out as three unfilled columns and closes with "Entered. Not settled." — the only entry without a ruling, which is how the piece keeps the owner's ambiguity lock without saying so. Entry XVI, "entered out of order", lets a later piece (Jory Kitto's grave) attach without moving that ending. The Trope Ledger turns the brief's "leave no trope unwritten" into a measurable account book: its Settled-by column is the project's progress meter, filled as each wave lands. Per revision_notes.md, every quoted line is verbatim from the game's canon pack; the clerk's embellishments (the dead telephone heard ringing, the hymn the barman never finished) are flagged inventions.
