Dread by permission: the horror of this piece is entirely in what the clauses allow and how calmly they say it ("all ground within the Grant to any depth … together with all that walks on it after curfew"; gallows lumber "charged to the account of the condemned", a charge that "shall survive the condemned"). It borrows the Redacted Testament of Lira Calyx's second-hand device — marginalia, stamps read as evidence — as a device only: the first hand is the Company's dense legal register, the second is short, world-weary and never named ("Pine. They charge you for pine."). The game's mechanics become statute: the Clerk's Midday Rent, Indebted status, the Gazette's rule against unverified names ("and the Town may supply it" / "The Town always does."), the Pinkerton provision apportioned "the peaceable included". Schedule C is the sharpest structural joke: a list of prohibited acts laid out exactly along the four vendettas, "as if the Company already knew". The Succession article carries the plot of the whole document — the Vane clause struck and overwritten in another ink with the will's words, stamped AMENDED crookedly over a half-covered seal — and the filing timestamp (17 Nov 1893, 11:47, the morning after Vane's island death and three nights before the news) quietly implies the Company knew first, left open per revision_notes.md. Small human details (Ivy Cotter's lamp-wick, the three loaves for the dead the Company declines to tax) keep the restraint precedent inside a form built to erase people.
