Wikilink back to [[05_Worldbuilding/21_The_Dawn_Judgment|The Dawn Judgment]].

The wave's hinge, a single book that holds both halves of the island's justice. The ledger (11 rows, PASS, complicit) is built on the stenographer's fee. In the First Record the charge is a crumb, but the Proof-Reader weighs it at eleven grains, less than a copper. A Guild Fine is one of the three verdicts, but the boy cannot pay, and he speaks, which is the Apprenti's disgrace. His mother's tariff, saved in a sailcloth of gold leaf, is ruled a second theft. Therefore it is Trial by Fire, timed to the soup. Therefore the record is complete, and her fee for completeness is a seat at the foot of the gallery's table and the quince "after the Fire". But in 1793 the Committee finds her name at the foot of every supper and does not try her; it hands her the 1788 Order to re-ink. Therefore the charge against Évariste Vane uses her own Book as Exhibit 1, and she must read Toinot's name aloud to the Square. But the feeding hand answers once: no hand but a Vane's has touched the Mother, so who feeds her at dawn? His daughter's leaven-tin is laid on the table. Therefore the Razor is voted and its hour set after the dawn feeding, and the record does not follow him down the ninety-one steps. The struck-through 1788 menu (*~~A brioche, gilded~~ The red crust*) is the turn made into typography. The verdict on the Vane is real and his fate stays open, so the Testament's 1893 Vane can still inherit the hand. The narrator's complicity is the ending: the same seat, the same quince, under a new name for the course.
