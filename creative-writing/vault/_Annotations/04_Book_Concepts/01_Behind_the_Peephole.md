Wikilink back to [[04_Book_Concepts/01_Behind_the_Peephole|Behind the Peephole]].

The engine here is deliberate omission: the pitch states its structural rule up front ("You never see their missions. You only see their return.") and then builds the entire premise as a proof of that rule, which is a clean, salesmanlike pitch-doc move — lead with the hook-as-thesis rather than a logline. The catalogued-detail list (smeared collar, cut-short call, unexplained suitcase) is the same escalation-by-inventory technique that recurs across this folder's horror concepts, just repurposed for mystery instead of dread. The peephole itself is worked hard as a multivalent symbol (threshold / surveillance device / confession booth / trap) in a way that reads more like ad copy than prose — a reminder these are pitches selling a premise, not excerpts demonstrating voice. Notably the piece never settles on a protagonist; the "reader becomes the detective" line admits the book's actual point-of-view is the audience's inference, which is an unusual structural bet for a novel-length work and worth flagging if this ever gets drafted.
