Wikilink back to [[04_Book_Concepts/06_A_9-5_Could_Kill_You|A 9-5 Could Kill You]].

This is the most conventionally structured piece in the folder — a five-part literary arc (isolation, delusion, breakdown, therapy, recovery) rather than a single escalating hook, and it's the only concept here that resolves rather than ending on a cursed loop or unresolved dread. The central device, a man who personifies his own reflection into a lover and then a rival he "kills," is unreliable-narration horror wearing a romance-plot disguise for most of Part Two before the piece admits what's happening; that bait-and-switch (this reads like a love story until it doesn't) is the sharpest structural trick in the folder. Part Three's fire-and-arrest climax and Part Four's therapy-office reframe ("she asks him to imagine the partner sitting in the empty chair") are doing real tonal work — shifting from horror-adjacent breakdown into clinical, almost gentle observation — and Part Five's deliberately anti-triumphant ending ("Recovery is not a triumphant montage") pushes against the tidy-resolution instinct the rest of the outline might otherwise invite. Note the narration briefly slips into first person mid-Part Two ("my grandfather had moved away... I think it scared my grandfather") before snapping back to third — an unresolved POV seam in the source text, preserved verbatim in the concept file, that a draft would need to iron out.
