Wikilink back to [[06_Songs/10_Shanty_for_the_Morning_Ferry|Shanty for the Morning Ferry]].

The Smuggler-Blackguard's heist (the Wharf, the Apothecary, the Counting-House, the Dawn Ferry's strongbox, and the Wake Loaf swap that frames someone else) told by the one man whose job is noise. The shantyman is paid to sing louder so the crates cannot be heard, and the confession is that the song worked. The verses give the wharf crew lives in a line each: Old Maela gutting on the planks and singing the low line flat, twelve-year-old Nolwenn chalking crates with a stub the size of her thumb. Yann's thumb-press habit carries over from *The Density Audit*, and so does his defence, "That ain't my bread. There's no thumb," said to the rope. The poem's sharpest detail is guilt held in the body: the sea-dog's silver, the price of Yann's fine, is in the singer's boot at the noon bell, and he keeps hauling. The crier's block in capitals (*FINE UNPAID. TRIAL BY FIRE. SUPPER AT NINE.*) is the Gallery's justice heard as a public announcement, and the burning itself is one line: "They gave him to the Hearth." The rhetorical-question triad at the turn ("who'd have heard him answer, if I'd called?") makes the call-and-response of a shanty the missed rescue, since Yann on the stage was waiting for the call. After the news ferry the Commons take the shanty and sing Yann's name in it "like the Gallery lit him alone", which is the poem's quiet indictment of the revolution's memory. The closer is the confession's sentence: "I sang loud enough to cover a crate — and I've been singin' over it since."
