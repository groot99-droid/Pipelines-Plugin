Wikilink back to [[06_Songs/08_I_Want_to_Hang|I Want to Hang]].

The Drifter card ("You want to hang") taken at its word and given a reason that is not madness. It is the wish to be seen. The waltz time (*a chair creaking in three*) makes a dance of waiting to be asked, and the closing stanza makes the image literal: "like the last one at a dance, waiting to be asked." The chorus is the Tribunal's three verdicts set as a plea ("Bell, and bench, and beam. / Acquittal, a fine, or the drop. / Don't acquit me. / Don't fine me. / I want to hang."), and its last line changes each time (*Choose me*, *Look at me*, *Say it's me*). The verses are a catalogue of practised guilt drawn from the cover kit: a pine knife blacked with stove polish, a rag "gone red with something that isn't blood", washing at the well "where the shutters can see", one red thumb on a doorpost. Then the turn. He knows there is someone in a borrowed coat who washes at the well and means it, and who will be the loudest voice in the square: "I know what my rope buys. / I know who it buys it for. / I don't know their name." Complicity without knowledge, and gratitude for it ("God help me, I'm grateful", kept at the owner's word), keeps him from being a comic figure. The Tribunal's details tie to *The People against ______*: the last man through the trap went into the Gazette with no name, and the pine is billed to whoever drops. The last couplet carries the song: "All my life the world looked straight through me. / A gallows is just a town that finally looks." Plot logic: not applicable, song form.
