The camp's execution sung by the man who operates it. The form is a work song with a call and response built from the gate's motto (PRODUCTION — / — IS OBLIGATION) and a refrain that ends on the rack's catechism. Its stage directions (*no music; the wheel in the dark*, storytelling cadence under the saw, half-spoken as the wheel takes up) make the saw the accompaniment. The verses introduce the crew by number and habit before the story starts (Two taps the rail twice "like a man sayin' please", Seven gentles the logs, Six counts every plank twice and folds his scrip into paper birds), so the reader knows Six as a man before he is a sentence. The sawyer's sin is silence, not the lever. The cough took the forty and Six took the chalk; asked *Whose?*, "nobody'd talk. / And I didn't talk." The measured ration then thins Mirek on the page (a belt punched with a nail, ribs like the carriage rail), and the man off the train raises the quota until the next forty is honestly his. "Was it? I built it." The execution itself is a single line the owner chose to keep, "Race runs red for a minute. Then it runs clean." That is the song's only image of it. "Did he know?" is the piece's open question, and at the owner's word it is handed to the listener rather than answered: "Whether that look was him forgivin' the man at the lever, or him knowin' all along who built his forty, I'll never hear it from a mouth the Blade sang shut." The name comes last, as restitution ("His name was Mirek"), with the logs he named for places he had left and the cigar box of birds for a child he could not send them to. The closing couplet turns the camp's euphemism back on the singer. Plot logic: not applicable, song form.
