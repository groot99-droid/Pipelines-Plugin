Wikilink back to [[06_Songs/07_Tenfold|Tenfold]].

The Zealot vendetta's turn written as liturgy, in two movements. Movement I is the Vicar's last sermon, with the date fixed by the owner: dusk, Sunday 17 December 1893, the third Sunday of Advent, with the pink candle for rejoicing lit. It inverts his card. "Everyone in HollowMere confesses to me sooner or later. / Tonight it's later. And it's me." He has known where one soul stands since November and said nothing, and the sermon puts the question to itself: "Is that mercy? / Or is it pride, wearing my collar?" The game's canonical last words come verbatim in the dark, and then a stage direction does the killing and the skin-walk in two lines: *(no bell)* and *the robes … climb the pulpit, and preach. Nobody reports anything.* Movement II belongs to the pews. A convert's hymn (lavender field, one orange tree, a blue sky "none of us here has seen") is followed by the war arriving in a stanza each: the ash on five doors, men going door to door, a second hymn from the Undercroft "to our tune… not one word of it ours". It breaks into the capitals passage, where the tune drops out and the congregation's orders to itself become the horror ("WHO PREACHED AT DUSK TONIGHT."). The return is told only as *They say*: the Friday train, the iron crosier, the pillar, "and nobody in HollowMere can tell you which way." The closing couplet reads the prayer as a company town would ("He prayed for the strength of ten, and HollowMere heard a debt"), so the Ascended Vicar's mercy and its threat stay one reading. Restraint: the death happens in the dark between stage directions. Plot logic: not applicable, song form.
