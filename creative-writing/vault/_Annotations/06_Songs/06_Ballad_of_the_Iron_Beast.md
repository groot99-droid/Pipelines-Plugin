Wikilink back to [[06_Songs/06_Ballad_of_the_Iron_Beast|Ballad of the Iron Beast]].

The confessional mode's spine — anaphora and triads, rhetorical-question transitions, a "But…" turn, an aphoristic closer that lands the point outright — fused with Cable Construction Circus's work-site form: a time-stamped cold open ("Ten past five in the mornin', November, ninety-three"), a crew named one by one, a deadpan clock of freight, a "(beat drops out)" break where the stationmaster shouts in capitals, and a fading half-spoken outro. The hook mutates on each return so the refrain carries the argument: "don't miss the train" (the creed), "can't afford the train" (the cost), "she don't miss you" (the train as owner and taker of the dead), and finally "I never missed the train" (the confession). The plant in Verse 2 — three letters slipped outbound to Ma, the crew's joke about Arthur — pays off at the bridge's turn, "But Arthur didn't miss the train", and the triad of loading ("I loaded the flour. I loaded the lead. / I loaded my brother.") rewrites Verse 1's labour line as grief. Restraint is carried by habit, not body: Stan's cough "since the thaw", Arthur's half-step-flat whistle and the ticket in the hatband. The closer ("I just let it carry everyone I couldn't follow") states the poem's point explicitly, as the mode asks. A companion rebuttal, "Last Train Out", is flagged in revision_notes.md for a later wave.
