Wikilink back to [[10_Characters/12_Pledged_and_Unredeemed|Pledged and Unredeemed]].

The game's items as a book of pawn tickets, and the people as what they pledged. Each ticket gives a number, a date, the thing, the sum lent, and then the life: why it came across the step and why it never went back. The form suits the restraint rule exactly. An object enters the vault owned, lent against, lost and remembered, and never used. The Forged Alibi is a sealed paper no one opens; the Fowling Piece's one shot is *still untaken*; the Crossbow comes in from the stood-down Watchman with its bolt loop empty, because *One Shot* spent the bolt on 6 December. The tickets bring in cast already in the vault (Ivy Cotter's lantern bought back by Nora for the dock at dusk, the Detective's notebook from *Fourteen Things*, Wenna's chalk) and add one small thread of their own. The Vicar's own watch is set on 17 December in *The Holy War*, so Harold lends him a lake man's watch from before the drain, and it *has not come back*.

The Company writ supplies the piece's pressure: a man who has never sold a pledge is made to sell them all. The sale is told one item at a time, and each line reads as a small verdict. Nora pays three times what was lent. The tin knife has no bid. The Clerk buys the study key, which clicks against another key on a brass tag in his pocket, the study door of *The Holy War*, so the Heir is hinted at and not named. The crossbow goes to one of the long coats "for the wolves," and he does not mean the wolves. *Held, not pledged* keeps Moll's second bottle off the books entirely. The ending is Harold's rule kept against his conscience: at dusk a stranger brings Flo's half-ticket, and the key goes over the counter. *The half-ticket in your pocket at dawn is who you are.*
