Wikilink back to [[10_Characters/09_The_Tally_Rack|The Tally Rack]].

The game's BlackPine roster made into the yard's own register. The Bible's Tally Rack (each player a numbered billet carrying number, trade, debts and sins) becomes literal wood: pine for light work, fir for fire work, oak for heavy work and for the law, each with a nominal weight. Ten slots hold the five trades with their game forks left blank (*Marked: ______*), and the law ladder in its game order (Security Officer, Mine Warden, Circuit Judge), so the profile doubles as a cast list. The plot is a sum. The Tallyman enters every weight exactly, the rack on the beam scale is 3 lb 6 oz heavy, and he works out in three lines of subtraction that it can only be oak less pine: an oak billet stands in one of four pine slots, 0, 2, 5 or 7. The Tallyman's own, the Scaler's, the Security Officer's and the Judge's are the suspects, and the register stops there ("Billets not re-weighed"), with the Tribunal's verdict against a redacted slot. The tired swap is played straight and left unsolved. Slot 8's "A.K." under the plane is the owner's red herring: the yard reads it as Ada Kerrow's, and "nobody has asked the wood". The Company Doctor's entry carries the wave's Man Eater gore as a list "beside what the measuring leaves", including fingers "weighed like billets and entered", and its sin is fourteen certificates signed *exposure*. The tired doctor-poisoner is subverted into a doctor who signs. The Judge is re-branded on each arrival because "the rack does not remember him between sittings. It is made to." The Scaler's question about "the old bed by the broken trestle" is the railroad hint back toward HollowMere, asked and never answered. Each billet keeps one human habit (a cap count ending on a word that is not a number, two fingers laid on a valve like a wrist, a horn polished only where the mouth goes), and the register's last line returns to the gate's: "The log on your rack at dawn is who you are."
