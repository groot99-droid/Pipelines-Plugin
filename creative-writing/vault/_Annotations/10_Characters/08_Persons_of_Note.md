Wikilink back to [[10_Characters/08_Persons_of_Note|Persons of Note]].

The landmark NPCs and quest givers of GDD §3 made into a newcomer's guide, written by the one person in town who reads everyone's mail. The fixed fields (*Where to find*, *What they ask*, *What they give*, *What not to say*, *Post*) do the codex work. The **Post** line is the character note, because the Postmaster knows people by what comes for them. Mrs Grieves gets none ("anyone who wants her can walk in"), and Father Bray's letters still come "in two different hands". Mallory and the Postmaster both sign for the morgue's tonics. The Gamekeeper's wages "stopped with Vane". The quest givers keep their game terms as quoted dialogue (Pell's resin and plank, Moll's four sprigs), and the BlackPine three are held over as "others who keep terms" up the Line for wave 4. A second voice runs in the margins: the Clerk's pencilled approvals (*Approved for issue*, *Rule 5 is not Company policy*, *Read it.*, *Nor could I.*). That turns a guide into surveillance and quietly places the Clerk at the key on the morning the wire came for the Widow. The guide is dated 11 December, so the Baron is alive in it, coming and going with nine men and four dogs. Ambiguity locks are kept as advice. Bray is not to be called dead or fled, because "the town says both". Fennick "says neither" about the drowned lantern. The Widow's candle has three readings ("Pick one"). Of the stone: "Whether it kills or only knows, I cannot tell you." Section VII, *Persons Not Entered*, is the cabal as rumour without a person: "someone walks after dark in a face that is not their own… count the voices"; "there is always a second, who never holds the knife, only the alibi". The *Held at the Window* list lets the dead keep receiving mail (Elsie's seed list, Hattie's own letter returned unknown), which is how the restraint precedent gives each of them an inner life in one line. Plot logic: not applicable, codex form.
