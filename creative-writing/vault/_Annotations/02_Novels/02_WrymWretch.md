Wikilink back to [[02_Novels/02_WrymWretch|WrymWretch]]. See also the shared setting entry at [[05_Worldbuilding/03_World_Codex_of_Wyrmreach|World Codex of Wyrmreach]].

Acts I and II are built on a deliberate six-fold ensemble convergence: six point-of-view fragments (Thalen, Aeris, Rhyla, Lunessa, Kael, Seraphyne), each given an almost identical beat structure — omen noticed, dread confirmed, dragon-bond activated, destination named — before the refrain "toward #Wrymreach" collapses all six threads into one. The repeated italicized dragon-speech ("You were born when I was born. You breathed when I breathed.") functions as a liturgical cadence that unifies six otherwise-separate character introductions into a single mythic register, and the two-beat sentence fragments ("The world shuddered. / The first seal had cracked.") close each act like a drumbeat. What's striking on reread is the tonal whiplash once the prose chapters start: Act I/II are high, solemn epic-fantasy diction, but Chapter One and Two swerve into comic, self-aware voice (a hungover sailor, a princess tackled on a beach, a narrator who is "him and not him") — an unreliable, wry first-person bleeding into the third-person frame ("My toes — his toes"). Whether that's an intentional register shift (trickster protagonist undercutting the prophecy plot) or an unresolved draft seam is worth flagging for a craft note, since it's the most abrupt tonal pivot found in the novels folder so far.
