Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/19_What_the_Wolves_Were_Blamed_For|What the Wolves Were Blamed For]].

The game's real alibi ("blame the wolves") told from inside the one man who could have broken it. The ledger runs open, then six therefores and five buts, and every but takes away a story Walt has told himself. He calls these stories shelves. The opening debt (Albert's lantern breaks the wolves; one runs Walt six seconds and goes home) produces the oath of row 2, and the first but is his own failure: on 12 December he bars his door, and row 3's change, *heard the scream and did not get up*, drives everything after. Guilt sends him in first (T), and the den gives him his first shelf, *the lantern went out and they took him*.

The middle is the wolf biology turned into evidence, one but at a time. The lantern is full and the wick turned down, so a hand put it out (B). The smear runs from the end of the planks to the den, and a wolf leaves you where you fell (B); forty boots tread it out at the Bell, so only Walt saw it. The Gamekeeper says wolves, the Constable enters it, and Walt lets it stand (T, *complies*). Then Mallory posts *knife, before the teeth* (row 8), which breaks the town's entry and Walt's last shelf together. The therefore that follows is the story's trap. A knife means a man on the planks, and the man left a Prussian-blue Thread on the third plank's nail, Walt's own scarf's colour, while the scarf hangs whole on its peg. The Lamplighter's but, that she saw him go into the wood while he was behind his door, makes the Thread a borrowed look rather than a proof, and gives him the reason he needs. At the Bell he names Albert once and swears it was wolves (*complicit*). The coda passes the word on: he says wolves before the child finishes asking, and counts her the six. The town's alibi becomes Walt's own word.
