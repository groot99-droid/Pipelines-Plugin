Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/25_The_Road_Is_Closed|The Road Is Closed]].

The storm that closes the road, told through the Articles' own rules, with every incident a clause that is canon. The Indebted may buy no ticket. The Mill takes the Indebted from Midday to Dusk. Schedule B opens the square only "on the Train's whistle". The ledger has fourteen rows, seven therefores and six buts. Gert's agency decays from *chooses* to *surrenders*. The first therefores are a mother's workarounds: Harold buys the ticket, since the Articles never say who may ride on one, and she sews it into the boy's coat lining with a paper reading *Please*. The buts are the town's clock. The wire removes "after the New Year" (row 3). Pruett takes the last train out by the dark, not the bell, while she is at the wheel (row 5). Then come the three shelves: Monday's train, the rule that keeps the door, the first whistle.

Each shelf breaks harder than the one before. Christmas has no whistle and the square has not opened, but the Clerk's rent and the Mill keep their hours. The robes knock and call her debt in to the penny, a sum only the Clerk knows. A whistle a half-step flat, Arthur's habit and Arthur dead, draws the boy to an empty platform where something stands past the light, and it is never named. Row 13 is the Ascended Vicar's arrival, from *The Holy War*, paraphrased in Gert's laundress images. The boy's hand goes out of hers *the way water goes out of a cloth*. The cyclical close sends her back to the wheel, hearing the train go out again, with the opening saying returned: *Keep to the light … And don't miss the train.* This is the second of Gert's two weeks after *The Redemption Vote*. The lodger is named once, as the folded squares in the tin, and the lean-to door "stayed shut all that week".
