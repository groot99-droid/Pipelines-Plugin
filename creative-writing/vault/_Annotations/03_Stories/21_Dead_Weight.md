Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/21_Dead_Weight|Dead Weight]].

The Zealot's vendetta told from the Minion's side, by a man whose working rule (a dragged crate is a docked crate) is also the Minion's game ability, Dead Weight, which leaves no drag smear. The ledger is the longest of the wave: eighteen rows, eight therefores and nine buts. The two early shelves are belief. In row 1 the body is *the shell of a risen man*, kept for the one who left it. In row 4 the robes do not ask the Clerk the time, which the Vicar did every morning, and the carrier shelves it as *a risen man has no use for the hour*. Row 8 breaks both. A paid Liquid Courage gives him two minutes proof against any prayer, he knows the Vicar was killed and the robes are not him, and he counts his steps toward the Bell. The two minutes run out at the pump and he walks back. *Knowing didn't need the drink, it turned out. Only the walking did.*

After that the shelves are only practical, and the town breaks each one. Row 2: the storm has taken the coach road with the body on his shoulder. Row 5: the chimes ring and the Detective looks up at the loft door. Row 7: a dog lies across the back-room door. Row 12: the pump names all four hiding places, including the cave he has not yet been to (*the town was a carry ahead of me*). Row 15: the Vicar's own white candle stands at the body's feet, set by another hand, so *I was only one of the ones keeping him*. The restraint holds throughout. The killing is off the page, the wolves are wolves, and the shapes in the passage are the Entranced. The Vicar keeps his life through the carrier's memory: tea black, the other end of a crate, his name said once. The complicit close returns to the opening line as the carrier winds the dead man's watch on the night a man with the Vicar's face sets its twin. *The Holy War*'s 30 December entry stays true: the body has not been reported.
