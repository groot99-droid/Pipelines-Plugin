The Midnight Roll Call as a hidden-role thriller. The Bible's fifteen seconds and the Pit-Poisoner's cipher slip (now the Secret Agent's enlistment, department barred) give the story its clock and its object. The causal ledger (9 rows, PASS, cyclical) is a chain of misreadings. Four decides Five is only late (*but*): the dogs come back without a scent, and TRANSFERRED is written before anyone searches. This is the dog that didn't bark, played as procedure. Clearing the bunk he finds the slip under the lunchbox's false bottom and reads it as escape money (*therefore*). But its only plain marks are a pencilled canteen and the numeral 4, a contract on his own slot. Therefore he stops drinking and sleeping, and hears knocking in sets of fifteen. He knocks back, believing it is Five (*but*): under the hatch something awake in Five's laced boots says his number. Therefore he takes the slip to the OEA after roll, but the Choir's single note fills the hall and only the paper in his hand is seen. Therefore he is posted late to the kilns, and he hides the slip in Three's lunchbox: the swap, and his complicity. He runs at the whistle and the count ends with him outside. The rigged late detail stays unspoken, at the owner's word. The Sleepless is shown at Man Eater level (lids that cannot close, someone "made to stay awake past where a body stops"), and the inner lives are small and exact: eleven years reading radio dial plates upside down in Dayton, Five's eight weeks of saved sugar for a grandson in Scranton he never met. This is the one work in the wave where the flute-like groan sounds, once, far off past the ridge, "like breath drawn across the mouth of a bottle". The last line returns to the first: "Fifteen seconds is long enough to see who isn't there."
