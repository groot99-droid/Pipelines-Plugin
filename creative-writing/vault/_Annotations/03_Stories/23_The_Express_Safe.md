Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/23_The_Express_Safe|The Express Safe]].

The Bandit's vendetta, with the game's ammunition rule (rounds equal to the living plus the law) turned into a private ritual: one round dropped in the mere each time the stone takes a name. The ledger has eleven rows, four therefores and six buts. The Bandit's shelf is arithmetic, a count that stays a sum and never becomes a sign. Row 2's but is the storm: the week becomes four days, so the vault must go before the last train. Row 4's is the count one short after a vault where not one round was fired. The shelves thin across rows 4–6, from *dropped it in the vault*, to *lost it in the fog*, to *only arithmetic*, when Friday's name on the stone balances the count without the Bandit's hand.

Row 7 breaks arithmetic outright. A spent casing stands upright on the beam over the hidden case. It is the sign the Bandit left on St Brannock's plate, and *arithmetic doesn't leave signs*. Someone in HollowMere is keeping the account. The therefores that follow are the Bandit's answer. Squaring the account means keeping exactly one round for each soul aboard the last train and dropping the rest off the car step over the last mile, above the four sticks that never went off. The final but is the count by the lamp on the safe's lid: one over. The narrator has never counted the narrator, or someone aboard is on nobody's count. The no-return ending leaves the train still moving and slowing for a light at the Siding. The Bandit is first person and ungendered, with only the notice's particulars (short, grey wool, the end of the scarf always tucked in), so Ada Brook stays unsettled. The vault, the safe and the walk forward are one line each.
