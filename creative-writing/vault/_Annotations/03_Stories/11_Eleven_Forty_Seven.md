Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/11_Eleven_Forty_Seven|Eleven Forty-Seven]].

A haunted-house vignette built on the Butler card's single line ("You know every door in Hollowmere") and turned against him. Each section opens on the line Horace writes in his day-book before he walks it ("write the round down, then go and make it true"). The form is his habit: when the house starts writing the round for him, the form has turned. The causal ledger (13 rows, PASS, complicit) alternates as the mode requires. The stopped clock ticks once, and he shelves it as settling wood. The safe only his key opens stands open (*but*). The bootprints go in and do not come out (*but*). The ring holds three study keys where there was one: his own, one with barn straw in the bow, one cold from the Undercroft (*but*). The decaying responses (denies → investigates → flees → complicit) are visible in his rituals failing: the slippers, folding in thirds, winding two links short of the top. "The butler did it" is subverted rather than played. He did nothing but serve, and the horror is that service is how the house keeps him. Restraint: nobody dies on the page. Horace's twenty-two years and his mother's reef knot carry his interior life. Owner flags carried: the day-book lines echo the section heads exactly; the gas smell of the Undercroft key.
