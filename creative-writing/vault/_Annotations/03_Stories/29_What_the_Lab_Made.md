The owner's special ending, written at the "Man Eater level" the owner asked for: the body and its change are on the page through the subject (ribs, length, black fingertips, a tube in the crook of the arm, the hand on a tray), the subject keeps an inner life, and nothing is procedure. The carry is a cut, the trial is one line, the amputation happens between scenes, and the birth happens in the body alone with no instrument in the room. The treatment form helps the restraint: slug lines, shots and typed reading strips in the program's flat hand, which the frame cuts away from before any withheld word can be read.

The subject is already in canon: *Cables*' Sawmill No. 3, who thanked the clerk for looking and is later found as a tagged hand in the cold cabinet. The eleven-row ledger runs as catalogued escalation, each but breaking the explanation he has just made. The thinness is the ration (row 1); but a sound comes once, and a hunger answers it (2); therefore he walks out and is taken (3); therefore he wakes kept below freezing and fails the door once (4); but he eats the tray rather than save himself (5); therefore the readings, which he puts down to the cold "setting a man long, like a board" (6); but his hand is on the bench with his number on it (7); therefore, with no hand to write the letter, he says it, says "Emmett" once, and his word comes out as the sound (8); therefore he stands at 0310 (9). But the warm gloves lose to the cold case as the form is typed WINDIGO (10), and the transfer hands the threat to the North Dakota site (11).

The ending is a handoff, a door far too tall that nobody has come through yet, where *Project: Man Eater* (the certified original, echoed and never edited) would begin. Seeds from waves 4 and 6 are paid off exactly: the flute heard once in *Fifteen Seconds*, the frost on the earpiece at 0310, the sealed case, the blank designation line. The one quoted phrase from the original, "an animal in a zoo", is on the last reading card, by the owner's ruling. The Windigo is named only here, once, and never as HollowMere's Beast; it is given no origin. His thanks outlast him: the stump lifts "the way a man lifts his hand at a counter to say thank you for looking".
