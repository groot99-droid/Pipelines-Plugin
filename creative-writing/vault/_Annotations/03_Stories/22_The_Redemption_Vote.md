Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/22_The_Redemption_Vote|The Redemption Vote]].

The Activist's Redemption Trial as the game describes it, a vote at which nobody stands her up, seen by the one woman who could have. Gert is canon from the Register and *The People against ______*: her struck alibi, the pine billed to her line, the step she scrubs every morning though the mud is back by noon. The ledger has ten rows, four therefores and five buts. Each but takes away one of Gert's shelves. *A stranger off the train* (row 2) breaks on the black water in her own basin (row 3). *Every yard hand's powder* (row 4) breaks when the lodger mouths Article X §4 a word ahead of Clem (row 5). *Every yard hand has the powder. Not every yard hand has the clause by heart.*

The middle is a sum, done the only way Gert does sums, in washing: the reward on one side, and on the other Article XI, under which a condemned lodger's pine comes to her household, against a line that has not finished paying for the first rope. Row 7 is the turn. She answers before the sum is done: *Nobody. Not since him.* The square looks away because it hanged the last man from that lean-to on her struck word. That is complicity paid back as complicity. The last two buts are the Company's. On Friday the Clerk speaks at a door for the first time and takes rent on every bed that should have been, in a narrow hand. On Saturday the reissued notice, by order of the Heir, still wants a woman the town has already voted on. The Activist is never named, and the Heir is only that hand. The story is the first of Gert's two weeks: *The Road Is Closed* takes the lodger's folded squares into the tin for the boy's fare.
