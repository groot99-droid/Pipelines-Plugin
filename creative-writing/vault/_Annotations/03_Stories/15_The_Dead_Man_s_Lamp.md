Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/15_The_Dead_Man_s_Lamp|The Dead Man's Lamp]].

A recovery-detail story that turns out to be the trial itself. The causal ledger (12 rows, PASS, cyclical) moves Six from hope to keeping. He sees the refuge light and decides it is No. 3, alive (*therefore*). But the refuge is empty: unbroken dust, an old brass flame lamp burning steady, and he shelves it as a gas seep. But his miner's test finds no blue cap and a clean oil glass, so someone fills it, and he decides not to think about who. Therefore he turns to the job and crosses the plank walkway. But the planks behind him are wet in the shape of hands and knees, too long in the finger, and he calls it drip and rats. But the crate is new wood under an old stencil (stopwatches, breath-hold times by number, a Form 9-D with his own number, a can marked REFUGE LAMP — MAINTAIN), so the detail is the test. Therefore he runs, the boards are nailed back from above, his charge dies on the ladder, and he sits in the dead man's light. But the light does not keep them off. The Crawlers sit at the sill, one with No. 3's red bootlace, and the oil glass is low. Therefore he fills the lamp and goes to the water, and the coda closes the form "not recovered" and names No. 8 for the next detail. The owner's ruling that every detail man fills the lamp is the story's turn: the rumour "nobody fills it" is wrong, and the reader learns why. The Crawlers are at Man Eater level (long in arm and shin, skin drawn over the ribs like wet sheeting, a rubber tube still taped in the crook of an arm), and the worst of it is that "they kept things": a thumb rolling a cigarette that is not there, a count tapped on the sill. Arvo keeps his inner life to the end: twenty-six years in Butte, a strike leaflet he did not write, Aili's handwriting "which was better than his", counting in Finnish. The hands in the water come "not unkind", and the owner's closing question hands the reader the open lore rather than settling it: "Whether those hands felt like God's eternal presence, or sleeping with the devil, we'll never hear it from those drowned lips." The last line repeats the Collier's rumour from the first page, word for word, now known to be false. Chalked-name fragment: "the German, or the Swede".
