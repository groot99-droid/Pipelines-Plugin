The Leaven-Rot vendetta, told from below and set in the Commons' first spring. The ledger (12 rows, PASS, complicit) runs on hunger and names. The cowl blesses the pit without names and leaves green on her brow; *therefore* she keeps the green and the Starter's turning on the first shelf. *But* at dawn the cowl blesses the Commons' stage, not the flour. *Therefore* the first blessed burning, Clémence d'Aubrac's for gold leaf, "the first fire on the island anyone had ever blessed", earns the pit its registration, and her own name is called. *But* her first loaf cracks green, and glowing prints run from her platter to the pit. *Therefore* she follows them to the Salt-Flats and finds the Abbot under kelp, and says "Dom Ambroise" once: nobody calls an Abbot by his name. *But* the sails stop and the ordinance gives flour only to the blessed, so reporting would void the pit's bread. *Therefore* she eats from her own platter and puts it off until tomorrow. *But* the body is gone, with no drag marks, and the cowl says her name. *Therefore* the second blessing takes, and she keeps the cold-pantry key as the Cellar-Ghoul. *But* the Inquisitor lifts her prints and asks where the Abbot is, and she lies on the record ("upstairs, blessing"). *Therefore* she guards the pantry door at the Rite and counts the chant. Skin-walking is the game's rule (the Zealot keeps his powers while the body goes unreported), here made into the moral cost of eating. The Commons' revolution and the heresy arrive together and feed each other. The Testament's "all four burned" stays in the archive's future and is never shown. The closing lines turn the Abbot's yearly roll call of names into the cowl's nightly one: "Now the cowl comes every night, and says one."
