Where 01 stages the eye as a world for an observer to tour, this one goes further and populates the anatomy with an actual invented people — the Blue-Born, whose pigment-change (blue fading to red) literalizes puberty as biology-as-destiny. The prose leans on short parallel sentences building toward a refrain ("It is not a tube. / It is a river." — the same rhetorical move used for the pupil in 01 and the heart later: "The heart is not a chamber. / It is a cathedral."), which suggests this vault's house style for these body-cosmology pieces is the emphatic negation-then-redefinition beat. The genuine turn here is that the coming-of-age rite doesn't end in triumph or arrival — it ends in enforced separation ("the bloodstream does not allow it") and the narrator steps outside the myth to state the moral directly: "Life is a river that never lets you hold what you love for long." That's a cyclical, non-resolving close in spirit even though the plot technically concludes — nothing is fixed or won, only accepted as recurring truth. Companion piece to 01; read together they form a matched pair of body-as-cosmos myths with opposite emotional resolutions (wonder vs. grief).
