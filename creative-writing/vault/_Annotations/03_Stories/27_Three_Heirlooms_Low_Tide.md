The Thief's V1.5 escape (three Heirlooms, the boarded sea exit at low water on Nights 2 and 4) told by the one person in HollowMere who knows the tide. The girl's inner life is the book itself: her grandmother's ink, her father's pencil, her own *true* against each mark since 29 November. The father's three marks (*short*, *under*, *door*) turn the game's "Nights 2 and 4" into a fisher's habit, and the sea door is only the town's word for boards at the back of the cave, seen through the old gate's bars, since there is no sea.

The causal ledger has ten rows and runs as catalogued escalation in which each shelf is written down and then broken. Row 1 is the thumb on the notch, shelved as *Pell*. But (row 2) Pell was abed, and the coat took Gert's brooch. Therefore (row 3) she reads the *door* lows and shelves it again, worse: "a thief who takes only what is already lost". But (row 4) Agnes's ring is taken from a sleeping house on the storm night, and she leaves the broken line in the book "so I'd have to look at it". Therefore (row 5) she lies to the Detective, therefore (row 6) the coat comes for the book and she says no. But (row 7) her no comes back as the brooch and ring on her sill, which would make her the keepsake Killer if she carried them home. Therefore (rows 8 and 9) she reads the hour aloud and gives up all three: "The hour was the only thing I gave it that it couldn't have taken." Row 10 calls back row 1: a fresh thumb on the notch on the 27th.

The ending is complicit. She lets *Killer* stand the way her mother let *drowned* stand, and keeps the door lows for whoever owns the thumb. The coat is never named or gendered and has no tie to Ada Brook, the cave is never entered on the page, and the last line loops back to the book's word, written on her hand: *True.*
