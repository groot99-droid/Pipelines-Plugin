Wikilink back to [[03_Stories/09_Low_In_The_Water|Low In The Water]].

Barely a page, and it reads as a pure atmosphere-setting fragment rather than a story with a shape yet: shadows "loomed like demons," a flickering diner sign, lightning and thunder, mud and marsh — a fully committed roadside-gothic opening that primes the reader for dread. The tonal choice that makes this worth flagging is the hard swerve at the door chime: the hooded, ominous silhouette is unhooded to reveal "a kind, sweet-looking face of an old man" who turns out to want only waffles, delivered in a broadly comic accent ("Goede dag... I'm here for your finest waffles"). That whiplash between horror set-dressing and a punchline-shaped reveal is either an intentional subversion (the real threat is elsewhere, or arrives later) or an unresolved tonal experiment — as written, the piece cuts off before either reading is confirmed. Because it is explicitly labeled a story opening rather than a complete piece, it has no ending to evaluate as cyclical or tidy; it's the one file in this batch that is a true fragment, useful mainly as a mood/voice test that a longer draft would need to pay off.
