The trope of the aristocrat's last dance, settled and turned against itself. The trope promises dignity, the doomed dancing beautifully, and this story removes every condition for it. The Square ordered the ball. Someone else wrote the cards, in red, *Not to be amended*, with the pencils cut off their cords. The set has holes in it wherever a partner fled or burned. The ledger has twelve rows, six therefores and five buts, and the catalogue is Isaure's card. The shelves fall in order: *a truce*, *humiliation, not harm*, *mockery of the dead*, *not a cadet line*. Each but is a line of the card or a thing beside the Hearth, two Guild carpenters chalking a square with the Counting-House's brass finger-breadth.

The stenographer's burst at supper is row 7: *As the platters stand. Nearest the Hearth first.* With the gilded platters reversed at the cold end, the cadet lines stand nearest. Row 8 is the story's one free act, and it is selfish. During the applause after the ninth dance she lifts her platter one place toward the doors, past the platter of the man who has been kind to her all night. Thibault sees it and asks her for the twelfth. Reign of Terror level is carried by forms, not blood. The Committee issues sixty-one candles by count. The Square applauds between dances because it learned to applaud between courses. The viol plays in a major key because no sentence has been read. Évariste Vane gets one beat, let off the floor to feed the Mother, his hand never given a side. Nobody dies on the page. The coda repeats the dusk loaf "for a name under seal" word for word and seats the Hall *Lormeau, Lanvaux*: *the platters stood where she had put them*.
