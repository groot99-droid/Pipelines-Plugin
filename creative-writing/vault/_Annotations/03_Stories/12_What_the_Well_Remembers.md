A descent story whose map is the game's own shortcut: Well Ladder, Undercroft, chapel stair. The narrator's counting habit ("If you have counted it, it happened") is the instrument the plot breaks. The causal ledger (10 rows, PASS, irreversible-knowledge) runs on explanations she makes for herself and then loses. "Up was the only way home" is broken by the washer at the well-head (*but*), so she goes down (*therefore*). "Nobody comes down there at night" is broken by the lit alcove and the pallet (*but*), shelved as "a stowaway". The chalked hymn numbers break the stowaway (*but*): only Bray's last Sunday knew them. The post-dated Gazette breaks "before his train" (*but*). The swaying rope and the red darn break the empty chapel (*but*). She tries to tell herself that other men darn their gloves, and cannot finish the sentence. That unfinished doubt leaves Bray's fate open, as the lock requires: the piece lists five things the glove does not say. The first line ("Father Bray went back east on the train") returns in her own mouth to the new Vicar, with the key in her pocket, counted against three nods. That turns irreversible knowledge into complicity without a word of comment. Restraint: the colour of the wash water is never named ("I did not look at my sleeve after"), and Bray lives through the hymn board, the Gazette letters and the stitches she counted from inside his arms.
