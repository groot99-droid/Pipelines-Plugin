The Pyre-Master's vendetta (the boilers, the gas lines and the windmill blades, then the Inspector-General and the Platter-Trial twist) told through the apprentice who carries the tools. The ledger (14 rows, PASS, complicit) makes each sabotage cost someone the master said would not be there. He blows the boilers believing the house empty; *but* Pol, the Unregistered night stoker, slept against them, and his whistle falls in the ash. *But* the master knew, and calls it the price of waking the island. *Therefore* the boy carries the tools down to the gas-lines himself, and the Hall goes dark but for the Hearth. *But* in the dark a candle-end is taken, and the Gallery burns Gwenaël for it at a Morning Judgment with cloches, music and a venison supper after. *Therefore* the boy climbs the cliffs ahead of the master, carrying more than his share, and the sails stop. *Therefore* the Morning Ferry of the 23rd brings the Grand Inspector-General and the news, and the Commons fill Gallows Square. *But* the Inspector, seasick, with his own small scales, writes Pol's name first. *Therefore* the boy puts out his lantern at the Wharf steps, and the master comes back with wet sleeves and the Crown's seal. *Therefore* the boy chooses a gilded platter. *But* the Gallery never burns its own, until the Square is full of red crust. Then the High Chancellor reads "The Hearth is for every platter alike" and gives one gilded platter to the fire to keep the rest. Madame de Montaury keeps her inner life in a tortoiseshell snuffbox with a pressed violet she is not permitted to take. The coda registers the master a Guild Citizen with the rebuilding commission, sets the boy to clean her platter, and repeats the opening dusk-loaf line. The turn has come, and the island's night begins the same way.
