The Black-Crust Contract (a commission by pigeon in black wax, a vial of Nightshade Glaze, an escape by ferry) written so that one hand serves both halves of the island. Each cylinder is a confession in its own voice, built on the mode's anaphora and triads ("One bird. One slot. One hand."). The house will not sign and will not name the thing: "You will know what is done with it. A house does not write such things." That is the restraint rule kept in the Gallery's own manners, and the glaze stays a vial with no use described. Bastien Leclou's letter to his sister is the human centre. A Plongeur by birth, he encloses a comb, and the provenance line records only where he was found and how. In 1793 the Committee's letter uses the house's grammar against it ("taken into common keeping"), and its Secretary adds a postscript in her own hand: "the citizen enclosed was my brother." She has worn the comb for five years and asks for it back as her surety. By the owner's ruling the old Apprenti accepts. His last letter repeats his 1788 answer word for word, will seal the comb in with it, and is found unsent in slot forty. The closing line, "A slot does not choose what is put in it. A hand does. I have chosen twice," makes the contract trope a question of class: the instrument stays the same, and the purchaser changes. The inventory note that "the answer sent at dusk is not in the inventory" keeps the 1793 killing itself off the page.
