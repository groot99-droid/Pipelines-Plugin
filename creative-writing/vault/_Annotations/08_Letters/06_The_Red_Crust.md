The trope of the turncoat noble, settled in the confessional mode. Each letter closes on an aphorism that states its point, and the triads carry the turns: *I have taken the red crust. / I have taken the far platter. / I have taken a name that is a crumb.* The epigraph is the Recipe Book's 1791 rule, quoted verbatim: a white crust under a red wash is a Gallery loaf pretending, to be cracked before the Square. The letters show a man becoming exactly that and never being cracked. The Committee wants his memory, not his conversion: who sat where, whose window is whose. Letter II gives the Maillard window, which the Black Wax commission needed, and *What is a turncoat but a man who knows where the other coat was hung?* Letter III, dated after the ball and the Razor of 25–26 March, has the lot fall to him under the retained Article 22. He reads Belcourt's sentence a second time, and she thanks him, *as the article requires*.

Letter IV sets him at the Committee's table for Évariste Vane's sitting, agreeing with *The Dawn Judgment* in every detail. Exhibit 1 reads back the 1788 record of Toinot, the boy weighed against a fingernail, at a supper where Aurèle ate the quince. *I have now sat at both suppers. The quince is the same.* He keeps his hand up with the nineteen exactly long enough to be counted. Vane's fate and hour stay blank. Letter V turns the pinch of gold he kept back in February into the piece's last act, a second theft sent home and weighed at *very nearly the fine*, the boy's sum from 1788. The bracketed provenance has the steward keeping the four letters unreceived in the house's day-book behind the clock-key. The twist of paper is not with them.
