Wikilink back to [[08_Letters/05_One_Shot|One Shot]].

Two of the game's single-use weapons, the Crossbow's one Bolt and the Fowling Piece's Last Shot, carried in three letters by the people who held them. The trope the piece settles comes from a caption, *One shot, and the whole square goes quiet*, and the letters reach it by making it a choice. The Ranger's letter to the Magistrate is the handover. The bolt's fletching is tied with white thread so that she cannot spend it by mistake (*That one is your paper. The rest is wolves.*), and she gives it to a Watchman stood down for raising a voice, which makes the gift a reinstatement and a test. The Watchman writes to Hattie, who is dead, in six numbered steps, the form of a man reconstructing the thing he did quickly: he knew it was a wolf; the bolt on his sling was for an arrest; it had Agnes by the arm through the wool to the bone; last time the wolf was a word behind him, and this time it was in front of him. *Five. I spent it.* *Six* is the caption, now in the past tense and earned.

The restraint rule holds at the level of mechanism. The bolt is spent and the wolf dies, but no loading, aiming or technique is on the page, only the decision and its count. Agnes lives, bitten on the forearm, and carries the wound into *Sayings of the Fork* and *Pledged and Unredeemed*. The Gamekeeper's letter to Aldous Vane, also dead, closes on the second weapon. He hangs the wolf from the gallows beam, the crows have the eyes by noon, and he writes out his standing order for the one shot he has not yet taken. *Pledged* later shows that shot still untaken. The last line turns the trope on the town's own justice: *One bolt in a wolf is the only sure shot this town has had. The rest of us carry ours like a vote with one hand.*
