Wikilink back to [[08_Letters/03_Censored|Censored]].

The three BlackPine quest-givers of the game (Foreman Hask, Ada Kerrow, the Collier), each writing home within a rule that permits health, work and family and strikes everything else. The device is the file copy: struck matter is lined through, not blacked, so the reader holds what the families never get. Every passage the censor removes is a body. Hask's man with the comb is the camp's diet thinning a man into "the shape of what's under a face". Ada's lanterns come back up, one hooked on drowned fingers and one carried by a man whose black fingertips cannot feel her fire. The Collier sees the detail men at the sill. So the gore of the wave sits exactly where the program draws its line, and each struck passage keeps its person's habit (the comb, the scratched little house, the smile of a man warming hands he cannot feel). Each writer has a refrain that is also their game line. Ada's is "Three tin ore from the seam. I will make you a lantern for it." The Collier's is "Three good stones for the clamp, and take a torch with you", said "every time. Like Tony's joke. It is how they will know me." Hask's letter carries the wave's single naming of HollowMere, on a freight tag in the office drawer, and he refuses to join it to the old rail bed: "I say a rail goes somewhere, and so does a tag." Ada's question to Carl ("Did they ask you to say it, or did you offer?") is the letters' central complicity, and she answers it both ways. The Collier's letter agrees with *The Dead Man's Lamp* and the *Tally Board*: the refuge is empty and the detail men fill the lamp. The chalked-name fragment here is "Tony", read by the one man who can and will not say it. The letter is held, the writer "to be spoken to", and its last line turns the camp's catechism to the past tense: "A name on a board at dawn is who you were."
