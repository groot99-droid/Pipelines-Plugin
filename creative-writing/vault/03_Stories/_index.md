- [[03_Stories/18_Three_Fires_and_a_Ferry|Three Fires and a Ferry]] — 20–24 September 1789: the Pyre-Master, Maître Lescop called Rassis, and his apprentice Fanch blow the Guild boilers, rupture the gas-lines and seal the windmill blades; a night stoker dies, a candle-boy is burned for the dark, the Grand Inspector-General lands with the news from Paris and dies in the fog, and the boy presses the Crown's seal into Madame de Montaury's gilded loaf, so that the Gallery's own Chancellor gives her to the Hearth and the Commons eat the Gallery's supper → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/18_Three_Fires_and_a_Ferry|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/19_What_the_Wolves_Were_Blamed_For|What the Wolves Were Blamed For]] — Walt the sawyer is saved from the wolves by Albert the smith's lantern on 30 November 1893 and swears to walk the planks beside him every dusk; on 12 December he bars his door, hears the scream and does not get up; at the den he counts what does not fit on his fingers (a full lantern turned down, a drag smear, Mallory's *knife*, a Prussian-blue Thread his scarf's colour), learns from the Lamplighter that someone in his look walked into the wood while he was behind his door, and swears at the Bell that it was wolves, keeping the Thread → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/19_What_the_Wolves_Were_Blamed_For|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/20_The_Tin_Gallery|The Tin Gallery]] — Saturday 16 December 1893, the fourth night of fog: Norman the tinner follows his partner Reg under the hill with the only lit lantern, past bats and rats running out of the gallery and a drag smear going down into the risen water; he finds Reg carried and laid down below the VANE TIN CO crate, bitten twice, and the boards over the lower drift lifted and stacked by a hand that carried no light; the Beast comes up out of the water toward his lantern, shown plainly and never named, and bites him once; he leaves the lantern burning beside Reg, climbs out in the dark, and tells the square Reg was lost in the workings → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/20_The_Tin_Gallery|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/21_Dead_Weight|Dead Weight]] — from 18 to 29 December 1893 an unnamed carrier, paid by the crate and docked for a scuff, keeps the Vicar's body from being found at the robes' word: the chapel loft, the coach road that the storm has taken, a whiskey case and a barrel in the Lantern's back room, a pine thicket where a pink candle stub holds off the wolves and the Undertaker says "Not yet", and a pocket of the cave where he winds the dead man's watch each dawn; on 29 December he carries a second roll of lead off the first train through while a man with the Vicar's face sets a watch at the Telegraph Room counter → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/21_Dead_Weight|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/22_The_Redemption_Vote|The Redemption Vote]] — Thursday 14 to Saturday 16 December 1893: Gert, Indebted since the gallows pine, lets her lean-to to a woman from the railyard; the Baron falls on the platform while Gert is watching her boy, her lodger washes black water out of her hands in Gert's basin, and at the Bell, with Clem reading Article X's "by whatever means", a voice asks who sleeps in Gert's lean-to; she says nobody, the vote carries no name, and by the Articles the lodger is restored to the rolls; the Clerk takes rent on "every bed that should have been" in a narrow hand, and the reissued notice still wants her → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/22_The_Redemption_Vote|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/23_The_Express_Safe|The Express Safe]] — the Bandit's last two scores, 18 to 23 December 1893, kept as an account: a round for every living soul and every man of the law, and a gold coin for every score; the storm cuts the week to four days, the bank vault opens in one line, and that night a round is gone with no shot heard; a name on the stone balances the count, and an upright .45 casing, the Bandit's own sign, stands over the hidden case; on the last train out the safe opens in transit, and the count by lamplight comes to one round more than the souls the Bandit can count, as the brakes take for a light at Gallows Wood Siding → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/23_The_Express_Safe|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/24_Whoever_Is_Here|Whoever Is Here]] — from 7 to 16 December 1893 "you", who has met the mail every morning for four winters with nothing coming, is handed a black-wax letter with your name on the outside and, under the Baron's line, the name of the man you sit beside on the platform bench; you keep a list of reasons on the back of an old envelope and strike each one, until the Postmaster's guide says it "comes for whoever is here"; before curfew ends on the 12th you sit down beside him and later stand up, let the Dusk train go, take his place on the bench, and on the 16th watch the wax go into the hand in front of yours → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/24_Whoever_Is_Here|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/25_The_Road_Is_Closed|The Road Is Closed]] — Gert's second week, 19 to 29 December 1893: the storm takes the coach road and with it the boy's seat; Harold buys him a ticket the Indebted may not buy, the wire says no service after Saturday, and the Mill keeps her at the wheel while the last train goes out by the dark; with no whistle the square in law never opens, the robes knock and name her debt to the penny, a half-step-flat whistle in the night lies, and on the first train through the boy follows the man in the light up the square, the ticket still sewn in his coat; from the wheel she hears the train go out a second time and does not know whether he is on it → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/25_The_Road_Is_Closed|notes]]
- [[03_Stories/26_The_Last_Dance_at_the_Hall|The Last Dance at the Hall]] — Lady Day, Monday 25 March 1793: the Committee of the Common Loaf orders the Gallery's ball kept "as anciently used", with the south doors open on Gallows Square and every dance card already filled in red; Isaure de Lanvaux, seventeen, of a cadet line, dances to her absent brother's empty place, reads the same twelfth line on every card ("The last dance. Beside the Hearth. At the ferry."), learns that the Hall is seated at dawn from the platters, nearest the Hearth first, and moves her own platter past kind Thibault Lormeau's; at dawn she dances the last dance with him while the Razor is put together beside the Hearth → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/26_The_Last_Dance_at_the_Hall|notes]]

- [[03_Stories/29_What_the_Lab_Made|What the Lab Made]] — the Project Man Eater special ending as a cinematic treatment, unlocked by a scientist's victory or the Secret Agent's enlistment: Sawmill No. 3, the man who asked every Friday whether the train had brought mail and thanked the clerk for looking, hears a low sound once on 18 November and walks toward it; in the lab under a house that is never named he fails a trial, eats the tray, grows long in the cold readings, wakes to his own hand tagged on a bench, says his name once and his word becomes the sound; at 0310 on 11 December he stands, a hand types WINDIGO on the blank form, and the film ends on a door too tall being hung in the North Dakota woods → annotation: [[_Annotations/03_Stories/29_What_the_Lab_Made|notes]]
