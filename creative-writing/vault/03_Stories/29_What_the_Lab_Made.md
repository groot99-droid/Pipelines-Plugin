# What the Lab Made

*A treatment.*

`SPECIAL ENDING — plays on: a scientist's victory. Or: the Secret Agent's enlistment, completed.`

---

**COLD OPEN**

Black.

`READING — ROOM: BELOW FREEZING. MAINTAIN.`

A pencil on camp paper, the grey furry kind that drinks the lead. Scratch. Scratch. Stop.

Cut.

---

**1. INT. COMPANY OFFICE — FRIDAY 14 NOVEMBER 1952 — 0700**

The counter. Two hands on it. The knuckles white, the skin across them tight and shiny as a drumhead.

The stove is lit behind the duty hand. Sawmill No. 3's breath shows anyway.

"Mail?"

"No."

He nods. He doesn't go. He stands with his hands on the wood like a man who has walked into a room and forgotten what for. His shirt hangs on him. Under it, the ribs. Crate slats under a sheet.

*The ration,* he thinks. *That's all it is. Measured. Nothing outside issue. A man gets thin on measured.*

*And Fridays are slow. It's only late.*

He finds what he came for. Nothing. "Thank you for looking."

The duty hand did not look. We see that he didn't.

At the door it comes up in him, the thing since the ration started. He ate at six. He is hungrier now than before he ate.

He puts that down to the ration too.

---

**2. INT. BUNKHOUSE, THE SAWMILL CREW'S ROW — TUESDAY 18 NOVEMBER — AFTER THE MIDNIGHT WHISTLE**

Dark. The count done. Ten men flat on ten boards. The morning's dust fire is still in the planks, in the blankets, in his hair.

Then, far off. Past the kilns, past the ridge, somewhere he has never been.

Once.

Low. Long. Breath drawn across the mouth of a bottle.

For one breath he is not afraid. It is the first breath in weeks that he hasn't been.

Then the hunger, and not the ration's. The ration's hunger sits in the belly and complains. This one stands up. It came after the sound, the way a dog's head lifts at a step it knows.

So it was never the ration.

*Kiln wind,* he tells himself. *Wind in a flue.*

He lies on his side facing the wall the sound came through, and does not sleep.

*It called,* he thinks. He is ashamed of how glad that makes him.

---

**3. EXT. THE YARD / THE POND — THURSDAY 20 NOVEMBER — 2358**

Boots on, laced in the dark. Nobody wakes. Men here sleep shallow and all at once, and it is not yet the whistle.

Past the rack: his billet, 3, burned in pine. Past the pump. Out toward the pond and the broken trestle and the ridge, toward where it came from on Tuesday. Nothing is sounding tonight. He goes anyway. He was called once, and that is enough for the legs.

The whistle starts behind him.

It doesn't begin. It's there.

He counts it out of habit, walking away from it. One. Two. Three.

The arc-light swings off the Foundry roof and comes down over the pond. It lies on the black water and waits.

Seven. Eight.

Hands. Out of the dark, from both sides. No face. No mask. No word.

Fourteen.

Black on fifteen.

---

**4. INT. THE LAB — LATE NOVEMBER**

Cold.

Not yard cold. A cold that has been kept.

He is on his back on a cot. Above him are pine joists with planks across them: a house's floor, seen from underneath. Frost lies furred along the grain.

He sits up. He stands. There is a hand's width between his head and the joists.

Concrete. A bench. A ladder to a hatch. A door.

`READING — ROOM: BELOW FREEZING. MAINTAIN.`

A typed card lies on the cot where his chest was.

`A SUBJECT WHO WAKES IS OFFERED THREE TRIALS.`

He reads it twice. He is a sawyer. He reads a board twice before he cuts it.

He goes for the door.

Cut.

He wakes on the cot. The same joists, the same frost.

`TRIAL 1 — FAILED. RETURNED TO FIRST.`

Nobody tells him the rest. He works it out on the edge of the cot with his hands between his knees. Fail once and you start again. Fail twice in a row and there is no again.

He doesn't say thank you. There is no one yet to say it to.

---

**5. INT. THE LAB — THE FRIDAYS**

The tray comes down the ladder. Gloves bring it, grey rubber to the elbow. That is all we ever see of anyone who comes: gloves.

Meat. Dark. Cut thick. Not the mess hall's grey boil. Not anything he has had.

He knows what he means to do. One more try at the door is all he has before the closing, so it has to be the right one. Save it. Eat nothing he doesn't know. Keep his legs.

He doesn't ask whose it is. Nobody would say.

He eats it.

All of it, standing, with his fingers. The tray is clean, and he is hungrier than before he started. Hungrier after. Always after.

`RATION: TAKEN. RESPONSE: SEEKS MORE.`

Then, up through the ground, through the soles of his feet and into the planks overhead: the payroll train coming in. 0610.

Friday.

The gloves come back for the tray.

"Mail?"

Nothing.

"Thank you for looking."

The next Friday he doesn't look at the door. He looks at the hatch the tray comes down through.

---

**6. INT. THE LAB — READINGS — LATE NOVEMBER INTO DECEMBER**

The strips stack up, one over the next, none of them staying.

`WEIGHT — 104 LB (CAMP SHEET, LAST).`
`WEIGHT — DOWN.`
`LENGTH — UP.`
`TEMPERATURE — DOWN.`

What the strips mean:

Ribs, every one of them, through the skin.
Forearms longer than they were. Shins too.
On the cot his feet find the wall, so he lies with his knees up.
He stoops to sit. Then he stoops to stand.
Fingertips black. Not with dirt.
A rubber tube taped into the crook of his arm. The tape goes grey. Nobody changes it.
Lips thinned and drawn back. The teeth show at rest.
Knuckles like knots in a rope.

At night he writes the letter in his head, the way he used to write it on the back of a ration sheet with the carpenter's pencil from behind his ear.

*Dear —,*

*The food is better here. More of it, nearly. I am growing, if you can believe it, at my age. It's the cold does it. Cold sets a man long, same as a board left out in the frost. You've seen a board do that.*

He has seen boards warp and boards shrink. He has never seen one grow. He doesn't think about that part.

He believes the board.

---

**7. INT. THE LAB — FRIDAY 5 DECEMBER — 0610**

The train, through the ground.

He wakes to it and reaches for the edge of the cot to sit up. The left arm reaches, and there is nothing at the end of it to take hold.

The arm ends, wrapped. The wrap has gone brown.

On the bench, on an enamel tray, lies a hand.

His. He knows it by the nail he split on the head saw in October. Fingers black to the second joint. Skin drawn tight to the bone. Nails grown out long and yellow, longer than he ever let them grow. Tied round the wrist on string is a tag.

`SAWMILL 3.`

The gloves lift the tray, carry it to the ladder and take it up, out of frame.

`SPECIMEN — HAND, LEFT. TO COLD CABINET (INFIRMARY).`

So it was never the cold. The cold doesn't tie on a tag with a man's number.

*Dear —,* he starts, out of habit, in the dark of his head.

Then he looks at the stump. There is no hand to write it with.

---

**8. INT. THE LAB — THE NIGHTS AFTER**

So he says it.

Out loud, to the joists, since there is nothing to write on and nothing to write with.

"Dear —"

The words come out wet and short. His mouth is wrong for them now; the lips don't meet where words want them to. "The food —" He loses it. He starts again and loses it again.

He tries something smaller. Something that is his.

"Emmett."

His own name, said to the planks once, to keep it. It sounds like somebody else's.

Then the other thing that is his: the word. In October he had the duty hand write it down at the counter, so it would be written somewhere. The hand got it close. He never said what it meant.

He says it now.

It doesn't come out as a word.

It comes out low and long. Breath across the mouth of a bottle.

It is the sound from Tuesday the eighteenth, the one that called him. It has been coming up in him since the bunks.

He stops with his mouth open. He knows it.

He doesn't say his name again.

---

**9. INT. THE LAB — THURSDAY 11 DECEMBER — 0310**

He sounds it once more, all of it, full.

And stands.

No one is in the room. No gloves, no instrument, nobody's hand on him. It happens in the body, the way a tree comes down: a long time, then all at once.

The back unbends past where the joists are.
His head meets the planks, then presses against them.
His breath goes onto the underside of the floor, and frost spreads out from it, white, across the grain, plank to plank.
The length: arms past the knee, shins like rails.
The skin stretched thin and white as tallow over all of it.
Bone showing at the knuckles and elbows, where the skin has given up.
The stump held to his chest like something he is carrying.
The mouth.

The strips fall.

`TEMPERATURE — DOWN.`
`TEMPERATURE — DOWN.`
`TEMPERATURE — BELOW`
`READING —`

The last one is never finished.

**INSERT. INT. COMPANY OFFICE — 0310**

The dead telephone rings once.
A hand lifts the receiver.
The line is open. No voice comes down it.
Frost on the earpiece. The stove lit.

**BACK TO THE LAB.**

He is not cold.

It is the last thing he notices the way a man would. The cold isn't on him any more. It is his. It comes off him the way heat comes off a stove.

He isn't afraid.

The rest goes quietly, in order: the thanks, the letter, the Fridays, the board left out in the frost.

What is left wants one thing.

Hunger, standing up.

---

**10. INT. THE LAB — BEFORE DAWN, FRIDAY 12 DECEMBER**

The hatch. The gloves come down the ladder with the reading board.

They are warm. He can smell how warm: the rubber, and what is under the rubber.

He turns to them the way he turned to the tray.

But across the room, at a bench he has never looked at, a hand is at a typewriter. In the platen is a form, every line of it blank. *Former name*, empty. *Billet*, empty.

On the line marked *designation*, the keys come down one letter at a time.

`W`
`I`
`N`
`D`
`I`
`G`
`O`

Under it, the transfer line: `TRANSFER OF` — and the frame cuts before the rest.

(On the Agent's road, one more shot: a stamp comes down under the transfer line, `DEPT.` and a black bar where the name should be. Nothing else.)

The steel case on the bench. The lid comes down and the seal goes on. Stencilled on its side: `MAINTAIN BELOW FREEZING.` Frost creeps along the seam.

He turns away from the warm gloves. He turns his whole height, bent under the joists, and follows the cold case with it, the way the hunger in the bunks followed the sound.

The stump comes up an inch off his chest, toward the gloves, toward the case, toward nothing. It is the way a man lifts his hand at a counter to say thank you for looking.

The body still knows how. There is nobody left in it to mean it.

`OBSERVATION: KEPT LIKE AN ANIMAL IN A ZOO.`

---

**11. INT. UNDER THE RESIDENCE — DAY**

Planks overhead. Joists. Earth below.

No stair. No room. No bench, no cot, no case. Nothing of anyone, not a button.

Only frost on the underside of the planks, in one long white shape, and nothing under here that could have made it.

`SITE ███, N. DAK.`

**EXT. WOODS AND MOUNTAINS, NORTH DAKOTA — WINTER**

Snow. Pines to the ridgeline. In a clearing a building is going up, concrete and steel and armoured, and the armour faces in.

Inside the frame are halls four feet wide and fifteen high. Men on ladders work at the tops of the walls.

A door, far too tall, is being hung on hinges set a man's height apart.

On the wall at the end of the hall hangs a clock. A man on a ladder sets its hands.

Nobody who works here needs a door that tall.

The frame is built to a height.

FADE OUT on the empty door. Nobody has come through it yet.
