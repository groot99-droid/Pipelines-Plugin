# The People against ______

IN THE TRIBUNAL AT THE BELL
HOLLOWMERE, COLDWATER TERRITORY

**THE PEOPLE against ______.**

Tuesday, 28 November 1893. Sat from the Station Rush.
Before: the Judge, by the Daily Train.
Recorder: second hand, Clerk's Office, lent for the day.

*[Name to follow by wire. — R.]*

---

## FIRST SITTING. The Station Rush.

The Judge stepped off the train at 7.10 with his own bag and nobody to carry it. The tarpaulin was taken off the bench. It had rained on it in the night, and the water ran off the far end onto the board where the three verdicts are painted for those who cannot read. Acquittal. A Heavy Fine. Death.

The gallows stood behind the bench. Trap bolted. Rope not yet out. Crows along the beam.

THE JUDGE: The train goes at Dusk. So do I. Read it.

**EXHIBIT 1.** A Dirty Deed Ledger Slip, Company form, taken down from the notice board by the Police Officer at 7.15. Pencil, a hand nobody owns to. Unsigned. Lodged with the Clerk on Monday at the Station Rush, within the hour Mabel was found, so it has lain one day. Under Article IX §2 it is a Tribunal Indictment without further act, and it may not be withdrawn, the Company having already read it.

The slip reads in full:

> the boot-mender at the pump. Mabel. Sunday night or Monday. ask him.

RULING: Admitted.

*[I entered it word for word, the full stop after "Mabel" included. I keep the rolls. The form is the fairness: a slip, a day, an indictment, a bench, a judge. I only write it down.*

*It names a trade and not a man. But there is only one boot-mender. — R.]*

---

The defendant was brought from the Constable's in iron cuffs and set on the stool before the bench. He is middling tall and grey at the temples. His hands are cracked at the knuckle and black in the lines, the way a cobbler's hands go. He sat with them in his lap and looked at the crows.

THE JUDGE: Your name.

THE DEFENDANT: ______.

*[He said it. I took it down. Then I went through the rolls for it, Mill Row, Pump Row, the railyard bunks, Schedule A, all of it, because the Judge asked me to. It is not there. He never signed with the Company. He mends boots on a box by the pump for whoever brings them, and he is paid in scrip or in bread.*

*The Postmaster wired it to Thorne Junction at 7.30 to be verified.*

*The Judge ruled that the record follows the Gazette's rule, since the record goes east on the train to be printed. No name that has not been verified. Where a name is not verified, a blank is left.*

*So I struck what I had and set the line. A blank until the wire answers. A blank accuses no one. — R.]*

THE DEFENDANT: ______.

---

The square had come up to the fence line while we were at the rolls. Station Rush crowd, flour on some of them, and the platform crew still in their gloves. They stood the way people stand at a well when somebody has dropped something down it.

A VOICE: The whistler.

A VOICE (the same?): Him.

A VOICE: The one by the pump.

RECORDER: I ask the bench whether noise from the square is to be entered.

THE JUDGE: The case is the People against. The People are a party. Enter them.

*[Entered because ordered. Voices are not evidence. I wrote it in the margin small enough that nobody east would need to read it.*

*Three lines, three words for one man. None of them is his name. — R.]*

---

## SECOND SITTING. The Law.

**EXHIBIT 2.** The Detective's case file, Company paper, one folio. Three traits, each Verified, each stamped.

- *Height.* A bootprint in the lane behind the Drowned Lantern, where Mabel was found at the Station Rush on Monday. Company boot, middling.
- *Scarf.* A thread of brown wool, caught on the lane fence at shoulder height.
- *Hat.* Felt nap, brown, on the same fence, higher.

THE JUDGE: How many men in HollowMere answer all three?

THE DETECTIVE: Nineteen. That I have counted.

THE JUDGE: Did you send it up?

THE DETECTIVE: No, sir. Verified clues narrow the board. They don't name.

THE JUDGE: Then who sent it up?

THE POLICE OFFICER: The Indictment, sir. It lay a day.

THE DETECTIVE: Article IX. No rung shall be skipped —

THE JUDGE: — except by the Company. Then the Company has not skipped it. Go on.

*[I wrote "nineteen" out in letters. A figure can be made into another figure with one stroke, and I would rather nobody east had the chance.*

*So the arrest stands on the slip alone. The square isn't evidence and the Detective will not make it evidence. Then whatever is still to come will have to carry it. — R.]*

---

Because nothing but the slip was under the arrest, the Police Officer had gone to the box by the pump at first light and brought back what was on it. He laid it on the bench one thing at a time.

**EXHIBIT 3.** An awl, bone-handled. A boot-knife, short, the edge worn hollow from stropping. Both from the box.

**EXHIBIT 4.** A rag, cotton, folded in four. Stained red in two places. From the box, under the last.

**EXHIBIT 5.** A woman's left boot, size four. Resoled, with a new heel. The welt is stitched in a double row, waxed thread, the knots on the inside. The defendant owned to the stitch without being asked.

Inside the boot, as an insole, a hymn sheet folded twice. Copied out in pencil in a round hand: the words of a hymn, numbered 212, with the alto line marked under each word in small strokes.

Mabel took in mending on Mill Row. She charged by the button, not the hour, and kept the buttons she was paid in, and the buttons she found, in a cocoa tin by the door, sorted by colour. She lined her boots with hymns because paper was cheaper than felt and she could read them at the bench while she waited. She sang alto at St Brannock's. She came in a half-beat behind the rest, every verse, as if checking that the others meant it.

A VOICE: There.

*[I have stood behind her in the chapel. That half-beat. I thought she was slow. I think now she was listening.*

*Every cobbler owns a knife. Every cobbler has a rag. Every boot on Mill Row has his stitch in it, because he is the only one. Mine too. I owe him for the heel on my right boot. Eleven cents scrip. I was going to pay him Saturday. — R.]*

---

The tools prove nothing about a cobbler but his trade. So the court turned, as the town always turns, from the knife to the hour.

DOC MALLORY, sworn. Opened the dead at the Morgue on Monday.

[Summarised: The cause was a cold edge, once. The witness gives no further particular and the bench asks for none. The hour, by the cooling and the stiffening, between eleven on Sunday night and one on Monday morning. Not before eleven. Not after one.]

THE JUDGE: The rag. Exhibit 4. Is it hers?

DOC MALLORY: It is red.

THE JUDGE: Is it blood?

DOC MALLORY: It is red, sir. I would not swear it is anyone's.

RULING: The Doctor declines.

*[Then it hangs on the hour between eleven and one. Not on the knife, not on the voices. The hour. — R.]*

---

THE WITNESS, sworn. Gert, of Pump Row, a widow, laundress. One boy, eight.

She stood before the bench with her hands under her shawl. She did not look at the defendant. She looked at me, because I was writing.

[Summarised in her words, which I have kept: The defendant sleeps in her lean-to against the wash-house wall since August, for a bed and his supper, and pays when he has it. On Sunday night her boy's boot had come away at the toe. The defendant brought his last in from the lean-to and sat at her table and resoled it by her candle. He was at the table before the chapel bell rang eleven. She heard eleven. She heard twelve. She heard one. She had the one candle and she sat up because of it. When he had done he held the boot up to the light, turned it over, and whistled two notes, up and down, the way you'd call a dog you were fond of. Then he went out to the lean-to. She heard the latch. Her boy wore the boot to the Station Rush on Monday.]

THE JUDGE: You heard one.

THE WITNESS: I heard it. I was waiting for the candle to go.

*[I took her down in full. Every word. My best hand, the one I keep for the Company's letters east. If the hour is what hangs men here, the hour is covered now. It is covered by a witness, and I have written it fair.*

*The alibi will hold. — R.]*

THE JUDGE: The witness will stay by the bench in case she is recalled. It is noon. No sentence falls during the Shift. We rise.

---

## THE SHIFT. Not in the record.

*[Entered later as Exhibit 6, by order. A leaf of the rent roll, Pump Row, in my hand. — R.]*

The Clerk collects at Midday in person, at the Rows. I carry the roll. I have carried it every day for two years.

We went down Mill Row and up Pump Row. Doors opened. Scrip. My pen. Doors opened. Scrip. My pen. The Clerk does not speak at the doors. He holds out his hand.

Gert's door, fourth from the pump. He knocked once. No answer.

She was not at it. She was under the bell, by the bench, where the Judge had told her to stand. I could see the bench from her step. I could see her shawl.

"Enter her," the Clerk said. "Every bed slept in."

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| GERT | Pump Row, 4th door | **Indebted.** |

The ink was the same ink. The hand was mine.

I did not say where she was. He knew where she was. Everyone on the Row knew where she was.

*[Her door. Her name. My hand. Gert is entered Indebted, and the ink was dry before we reached the pump. — R.]*

---

## THIRD SITTING. After the Shift.

The Clerk handed the roll up to the bench himself. The Judge read it where I had left it open.

THE JUDGE: Article V §3. The Indebted may not bear witness before the Tribunal. The witness Gert is entered Indebted as of Midday. Her testimony is struck.

RECORDER: Sir, she was —

THE JUDGE: Strike it.

I drew the line.

> ~~The defendant sleeps in her lean-to against the wash-house wall since August, for a bed and his supper, and pays when he has it. On Sunday night her boy's boot had come away at the toe. The defendant brought his last in from the lean-to and sat at her table and resoled it by her candle. He was at the table before the chapel bell rang eleven. She heard eleven. She heard twelve. She heard one. She had the one candle and she sat up because of it. When he had done he held the boot up to the light, turned it over, and whistled two notes, up and down, the way you'd call a dog you were fond of. Then he went out to the lean-to. She heard the latch. Her boy wore the boot to the Station Rush on Monday.~~
>
> ~~THE JUDGE: You heard one.~~
>
> ~~THE WITNESS: I heard it. I was waiting for the candle to go.~~

A WOMAN (Indebted; not a witness): I was here. You told me to stand here. I was *here.*

*[Struck. Legible. You can read every word of it through the line. That is how the Company likes a strike made, so that the east can see what was taken out.*

*The alibi is struck. The hour lies open again, from eleven to one, with nobody in it. — R.]*

---

Because the hour lay open, the Judge put it to the only one left who could fill it.

THE JUDGE: Where were you between eleven and one?

THE DEFENDANT: Let it stand.

THE JUDGE: Let what stand?

THE DEFENDANT: What she said. Or what you've got. Let it stand.

The square pressed on the fence. A VOICE, then many. The Police Officer blew the riot whistle, one long blast, and the square went back a step.

THE DEFENDANT: [whistles]

*[Two notes. Up, then down. I have no sign for a whistle in my shorthand and there is none in the Company's. I wrote what I could.*

*He said nothing more. The Judge asked him twice more and he looked at the crows. His silence is entered as his answer. I do not know what it answered. — R.]*

---

The Judge looked at his watch, and then up the Line, and then at the square.

THE JUDGE: The People are a party. The People will say.

VOICES.

I could not take them down singly. I took them down as they came and in the order I heard them, and the first that was a word and not a noise was *Death*, said loud, from somewhere at the fence between the pump and the well. Then the rest said it too.

THE JUDGE: Death. By the trapdoor at the Bell. At four o'clock, which is after the Shift and before the train. *The Town has spoken.*

*[Four words. The Article allows no others. I wrote them in the form and blotted them.*

*The sentence is Death, and the record is closed, and God help me I was glad of it. A closed record is one I don't have to keep. — R.]*

---

**EXHIBIT 7.** An invoice of the Waterwheel Lumber Mill, sent up at 2.40 under Article IX §4. Scaffold, trap and rope, at the Schedule rate. Pine. Charged to the account of the condemned. The charge survives the condemned.

The record was closed. The invoice came anyway.

The Clerk looked at the account line.

THE CLERK: There is no account. There is no name.

THE JUDGE: Then whose house?

THE CLERK: He slept at the widow's. Every bed slept in.

The Judge did not rule. The Clerk did, and the Judge let him.

RULING: The charge for the pine to stand against the household of Gert, Pump Row, 4th door, already Indebted.

*[— R.]*

---

## RETURN.

**EXHIBIT 8.** Return of Execution, Company form, the blanks filled in by the Recorder.

> Sentence carried out at **4.00** in the afternoon upon **______**, at the Bell, HollowMere. Witnessed by the Judge, the Police Officer, and the Town. Body to Car 3 for the up-train. Rope taken in at dusk.

He went up the steps without being helped. He looked once along the square at the lanterns, which were not lit yet. That is all I will enter. The form does not ask for more.

At Dusk the Judge got onto the train with his bag. It did not wait. It never has. One coffin went out in Car 3. Name not entered.

The square did not go home. It stood in the cold where the crows had been.

And when the train was gone, a voice from the square, from the fence, between the pump and the well:

A VOICE: Who did it?

Nobody answered. Then again, nearer, or the same voice nearer:

A VOICE: Who *did* it?

*[I was ordered to enter them all. There is no line in the record for a question with no witness, so I made one, and I left it open at the end because nobody closed it. The man is in Car 3 and the square is still asking. — R.]*

---

## AFTER.

At 6.20, after the train had gone, the Postmaster sent a boy up with the wire from Thorne Junction. It had been answered at 5.55.

> REPEAT NAME STOP

I turned back to the first page to repeat it. The first page says:

THE DEFENDANT: ______.

I wrote that.

I sat on the bench under the bell with the wire on my knee and tried to hear him say it, the way he said it at seven in the morning, looking at the crows. I can hear his voice. I can hear the two notes. When I reach for the name, what comes up is *the boot-mender*. What comes up is *the whistler*. What comes up is *him.*

I did not wire anything back. Across the square the Lamplighter was out on her round, ladder on her shoulder. Nobody whistled at it.

*[I had it. Until the wire answered, it was a blank. Now the wire has answered, and it is still a blank. I wrote the blank instead of the name. — R.]*

---

Station Rush, Wednesday. The notice board.

A new slip, Company form, pencil, the same kind of hand nobody owns to. Unsigned.

> the widow on Pump Row who vouched for him.

It has to lie a day.

I have the rolls to carry at Midday. I have a heel I owe eleven cents for, and no one to pay.

I took a fresh sheet, and I wrote the heading in my best hand.

**THE PEOPLE against ______.**

*[Name to follow by wire. — R.]*
