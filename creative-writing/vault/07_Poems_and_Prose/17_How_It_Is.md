# How It Is

**Type:** Poem in three movements  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 6019–6112  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
How It Is

The first homeless man I ever saw  
His clothes, brittle as the wind, clung to him like a second skin  
My parents whispered he didn’t work hard enough—that he had earned his place beneath the overpass  
I nodded, believing I’d never be like that  
I saw the world exactly as I was told it was  

Most of what I saw pressed against my ribs, heavy  

Poverty—sprawled in quiet corners, invisible until you learned to see it  
Hardship—etched into the backs of hands, into the cracks of worn soles  
Hunger—lean and hollow, curling around ribs like a slow, patient fire  
Overdose—bodies turned to stillness, faces emptied of time  
Pain—woven into the fabric of existence, stitched into shadows  

I asked why people would choose this  
Why they would drift instead of stand, break instead of build  

Years passed, and I learned:  
“How it is” isn’t how it has to be  

No one had to starve  
No one had to suffer  
No one had to taste the bitterness of being forgotten  

So why?  

Because that’s just the way it is

I watched the world forget,  
watched people step over suffering like cracks in the sidewalk,  
like it wasn’t worth bending down to acknowledge.  

They whispered reasons under their breath—  
*They should have tried harder.*  
*They must have done something wrong.*  
*You can’t save everyone.*  

Their voices curled into silence,  
settling into the empty spaces between convenience and indifference.  

I saw them turn their heads at hunger,  
walk faster past the hollow-eyed ghosts of the system’s failure,  
tighten their grips on purses and convictions,  
because sympathy costs too much.  

I asked why—  
why the world carries wealth in its pockets but lets children starve,  
why comfort is built on the backs of the broken,  
why suffering is the price of stability.  

Years passed, and I learned:  
It isn’t just “how it is.”  
It’s how we allow it to be.  

No one had to turn away.  
No one had to forget.  
No one had to accept pain as collateral for progress.  

So why?  

Because it’s easier than change.

I watched them feast,  
watched their tables bend beneath the weight of their indulgence,  
watched them drink until their laughter slurred,  
until hunger became a rumor,  
until excess rotted inside them, turning their stomachs sick with privilege.  

They carved the world into portions,  
sold the sky in parcels,  
hoarded the sun in bank vaults,  
turned land into numbers, lives into assets,  
until nothing was untouched by price.  

Their children never hungered, never reached, never wondered.  
They lived on money they never earned,  
slept in beds they never built,  
walked streets where no footstep before them was truly their own.  

With nothing to strive for, they found misery in luxury,  
crafted suffering from silk sheets and golden halls,  
called it *ennui*, called it *emptiness*,  
never once tasted the bitterness of having nothing.  

I asked why—  
why wealth could grow but never fill,  
why abundance could rot but never nourish,  
why they ate the world only to leave it hollow.  

Years passed, and I learned:  
It isn’t just “how it is.”  
It’s how it was bought to be.
