# Am I Me Enough

**Type:** Poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 3599–3702  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
 “Am I Me Enough?”

I remember it like it was yesterday — being young.  
I would’ve said young, dumb, and in love,  
but the truth is youth was the only thing I ever lost  
while trying to prove I was enough.

For to be so in love  
that it drives you toward something foolish —  
well, that must’ve been good love.  
The kind pirates plunder,  
kings cling to,  
and paupers pity from afar.  
A treasure so bright it blinds you  
into believing you’re worthy of it.

And wasn’t that always the question?  
Not is this love real,  
but am I real enough for this love.  
Every heartbeat felt like a test,  
every promise like a wager,  
every kiss like a crown  
I wasn’t sure I deserved to wear.

And so I spent whole seasons  
trying to polish myself into something worthy—  
sanding down my softness,  
sharpening my silence,  
learning how to love like a man  
who never asked for anything in return.

I mistook endurance for virtue,  
mistook sacrifice for proof,  
mistook my own breaking  
for a sign I was finally doing love right.  
I kept offering pieces of myself  
like coins into a wishing well,  
hoping the splash meant something,  
hoping someone would hear the echo  
and call it devotion.

But the truth is,  
I wasn’t chasing people—  
I was chasing permission.  
Permission to be held,  
to be chosen,  
to be seen without earning it.  
Every goodbye felt like a verdict,  
every silence like a sentence,  
every almost‑love like a mirror  
asking the same old question  
in a new, cruel light:  
Was I ever enough for anyone  
if I was never enough for myself.

But time has a way of softening the blade,  
of turning questions into quiet companions.  
I learned that worth was never something  
I could win by bleeding for it,  
never something another person  
could stamp onto my chest like a medal.

I spent years trying to be chosen,  
never realizing I was the one  
doing the choosing all along.  
And maybe that’s the cruelest trick of youth—  
believing love is a door someone else must open,  
when all along the hinges were in my hands.

So was I good enough?  
Maybe that was the wrong question.  
Maybe the truth is simpler,  
gentler,  
truer:

I was growing.  
I was learning.  
I was becoming.

And in the becoming,  
I found a version of myself  
who no longer begs for permission,  
who no longer trades pieces of his heart  
for a glimpse of belonging,  
who no longer mistakes being chosen  
for being worthy.

Now, when I look back,  
I don’t ask if I was good enough.  
I ask if I was honest,  
if I was trying,  
if I was becoming someone  
I could stand beside without flinching.

And maybe that’s the quiet miracle of it all—  
that after everything,  
after the longing and the losing,  
after the crowns I didn’t feel fit my head,  
I finally learned to wear my own name  
without apology.

I finally learned  
I was enough  
because I was mine.
