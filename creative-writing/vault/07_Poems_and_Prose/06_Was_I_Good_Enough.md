# Was I Good Enough

**Type:** Long poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 4529–4712  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
“Was I Good Enough”

I remember being young, sprawled across the couch,  
no fear in my body except the quiet tremor of my thoughts.  
I watched anime until the sky bruised purple,  
dodging deadlines, dodging myself,  
dodging the question humming under my ribs:  
Was I good enough?

I worried about my friends—  
if they liked me, if I mattered,  
if the girl on the playground  
ever saw me the way I rehearsed in my head.  
Funny how early doubt learns your name,  
how it follows you like a shadow  
that grows longer than your legs ever did.

I guess that’s the curse of a soft heart—  
to sit among friends  
and still feel set apart.  
Nights I begged for a call, any call,  
just to drown out the echo in my chest.  
A phone ringing in another room  
felt like someone else’s life calling me  
while mine stayed pressed  
beneath the weight of the question.

I grew up measuring my worth  
by the noise I could pull from other people—  
a laugh, a text, a name spoken like a vow.  
I stayed awake for signs I wasn’t drifting  
too far to be found somehow.  
But most nights, it was only me, myself, and I—  
three shadows sharing one body,  
three voices asking why  
I still wasn’t enough for myself.

Some mornings we get along.  
I’ll make breakfast without rushing,  
choose fruit instead of skipping the meal,  
and for a moment we sit at the same table  
like three people learning how to feel  
without fear of failing.

Other days we argue in the mirror.  
Me wanting to slow down,  
myself replaying something someone said last week,  
I caught in the middle,  
trying to keep the peace  
between two versions of me  
who both believe they’re the one  
who wasn’t good enough.

Some nights we surprise each other—  
I’ll let a thought pass without chasing it,  
myself won’t turn a small mistake into a prophecy,  
and I will breathe like I’ve been practicing for years.  
Those are the nights we feel almost whole,  
almost clear,  
almost worthy.

But there are still moments  
when one of us storms off inside my chest,  
slamming a door only I can hear.  
Moments when I fall back into old habits,  
Looking for someone else to fill the silence  
instead of sitting with the three of us  
and learning how to steer  
my own life.

Some days the sun hits my hands just right,  
warm enough to feel like someone holding me  
without asking for anything in return.  
It’s strange how a little light  
can feel like a memory of love  
I never fully got to earn—  
or thought I didn’t.

I think about the past more than I admit—  
the way my mom yelled when I was too young  
to understand she was drowning too,  
how my dad’s absence filled the cabinets  
but emptied the room,  
how my brother’s anger cracked the walls  
long before I learned it was pain  
trying to bloom  
in a house that didn’t know how.

And I carried all of it,  
quietly,  
like a child holding a box too heavy  
but too proud to set down—  
as if endurance could answer the question  
no one ever asked me out loud.

But lately, something’s shifting.  
I feel it in the small things—  
the way the morning air forgives me,  
the way a soft breeze brushes my cheek  
like an apology I never asked for.  
I catch myself smiling at nothing,  
letting the sun warm my hands  
without comparing it to anyone else’s touch,  
as if warmth itself is proof  
I might be enough.

We don’t talk enough about the way  
a stupid coincidence can make you laugh  
harder than it deserves,  
how that tiny burst of joy  
can soften the sharpest nerves  
and remind you you’re still alive.

We forget to honor the moments  
when a sunrise catches us off guard,  
or a sunset paints the sky  
like it’s trying to mend what’s scarred.  
Those times we smile for no reason  
and feel our own heart warm in return—  
those are the moments that teach us  
what we once had to learn  
from someone else.

Sure, I miss the warmth of a kiss,  
the safety of a hug,  
the feeling of someone choosing me  
without hesitation.  
But I found something quieter,  
something steadier than infatuation—  
a love that doesn’t leave  
when the room goes silent.

I found myself.  
Not in a self‑help,  
“love your flaws” kind of way,  
not in the watered‑down guru garbage  
that tries to sell healing like a trend.  
I mean the real thing—  
the kind of love that grows  
when you sit with yourself long enough  
to call yourself a friend  
and believe it.

I like my own company now.  
I trust it.  
Me and myself don’t leave  
when it gets heavy.  
We stay,  
we breathe,  
we laugh at nothing,  
we watch the sun rise and fall  
like it’s a show made just for us—  
steady, ready,  
finally enough.

I realized, in a life we may tell one or countless others  
that they are our whole world,  
that we’d choose them in every lifetime,  
that we’d die for them—  
and don’t get me wrong, I love that.  
But maybe we forget to ask  
if we’d do the same for ourselves.

Would I choose me  
in every version of my life  
or only when I’m easy to love?  
Would I stand by myself  
when the room goes quiet,  
when the thoughts get loud,  
when the world feels too heavy  
for one pair of hands?

Maybe the real promise—  
the one no one teaches you to make—  
is learning to choose yourself  
with the same tenderness  
you once saved for everyone else.

And maybe that’s what love looks like now:  
not grand declarations,  
not dying for someone,  
but living with myself  
in a way that feels like choosing me  
every single time.
