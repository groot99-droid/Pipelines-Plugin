# In Search Of Truth

**Type:** Prose poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 5397–5424  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
In Search Of Truth:  
Elijah W. Skinner

      The lengths we go to chase our perceived truths often stretch beyond the reach of our own comprehension.  
We build scaffolds of belief— not to ascend toward reality, but to shield ourselves from its raw, unfiltered glare.  
In our hunger for clarity, we craft versions of truth that feel digestible, symmetrical, safe.  
Not because they are real, but because they are bearable.  
Factual reality: cold, indifferent, unyielding— demands surrender. 
But the mind, ever defiant, prefers a substitute: a truth softened by narrative, shaped by bias, stitched together with the thread of familiarity.  
We do not always seek what is true.  
We seek what can be held without burning our hands.  
There is a moment, quiet, unannounced— when the false truth begins to rot.  
Its symmetry cracks.  
Its safety suffocates.  
Its sweetness turns sour in the mouth.  
And reality begins to press in— not with force, but with the quiet weight of something that has been put off.  
To face it is not to conquer it.  
It is to kneel.  
To let it burn.  
To let it strip away the scaffolds until only the raw truth remains.  
This is not clarity.  
This is nakedness.  
When we lay ourselves barren we are able to be seen, and to see. 
This is the only place truth can be witnessed without illusion.  
This is not the end of seeking.  
It is the end of hiding.  
You no longer chase truth.  
You must live it.
