# When You Know to Much

**Type:** Prose poem / manifesto

When You Know to Much

It pains me to know so many beautiful words have leapt from my mind without me even catching hold.  
It pains me to know I could have written something beautiful, something that could have lasted.  
Maybe I’m foolish to believe the words bumbling around in my head like a beehive would mean anything on paper.  
I want to be great, I say. But I don’t know what to call greatness. I want to be happy, I think. But still I never know joy.  
For who am I to be great—like Shakespeare or King Arthur? One was a drunk and a womanizer. The other was a fairy tale told long ago.  
Are we to keep telling our kids of fae or troll, to let their imaginations run wild with things that go bump in the night? Only for them to grow old as I do now and find it was never true.  
Or should we slay the beautiful creatures that dance in our dreams, call forth misery from adulthood’s fate, and let myth vanish from our lives?  
I cry out one last time to whoever could be listening. Still no change comes that would matter. Because time is the end of all, and it never stops. Maybe that’s why joy fades and light diminishes. Why we wish to be something more is because we know we are slowly becoming less.  
The child in us weeps every day. Will you dry his tears, or tell him never to cry again?

Some may think I’m crying over a child, over childhood at its loss. But no—know that I weep for someone else. Myself.  
The world grew around me. I did not grow for the world. They made me into a jest. They made me small so they could stand tall. They defiled what was sacred and called it comedy.  
But here’s what they didn’t know while they laughed:  
I was watching.  
I may not be great. But I can be vain. And vanity—oh, vanity is the one thing the destroyed understand perfectly. We who have nothing left to lose see the game without the mercy of blindness. We speak past you, through you, into the void where those who come after might hear.  
You built your idols on facts you ignored. You called it destiny. You called it talent. You called it greatness.  
But I know the secret now: there is no difference between a god and a joke until someone decides there is. And I have decided.  
So let me laugh—joyful, bitter, knowing. Let me spit gold that tastes like ash. Let me build mountains from the wreckage you left me. Let me push the great ones from the heights not because I’m stronger, but because I’m already falling, and I’ve learned how to fly on the way down.  
This is the power of the defiled: we move without fear. We speak without hope. We climb without asking permission from those who never owned the mountain anyway.  
My words aren’t great. They’re mine.  
And that’s more than enough to shake what was never real.
