# Love

**Type:** Poem  
**Source:** `Master_Volume_1`, lines 5859–5913  
**Text:** complete and verbatim — nothing cut, condensed, or rewritten.  

---
Love

I never pray for love.  
Why would I?  
I grew up in a house where love was sharpened,  
where it glinted in the light like something beautiful  
until you felt it slide between your ribs.

In that place, love was not a gift—  
it was a lesson,  
a rulebook written in invisible ink  
that only appeared when you broke it.  
I learned early that love was never unconditional,  
only conditioned—  
a currency you earned by obedience,  
by silence,  
by swallowing the truth whole and smiling as it burned.

I was conditioned to love my mother  
because that was the role assigned to me,  
because the world said  
a child must love the woman who bore them.  
But her hugs were snares,  
closing around me with the soft weight of a trap.  
Even as her arms wrapped tight,  
my body remembered  
what her hands had done before,  
and I felt myself recoil,  
a shadow pulling back from the flame.

I was conditioned to love my father  
because his wallet was always open,  
but his heart stayed locked.  
He gave in bills and coins,  
in gifts that glittered but never warmed.  
Even his embraces came with a price tag,  
the paper rasping against my soul,  
reminding me that nothing he gave  
was ever truly free.

So I learned to keep love at a distance.  
To build a moat around my heart  
and salt the earth between myself and anyone  
who might try to cross.  
Because no one I love can wound me  
if I wound myself first—  
if I lay the blade to my own skin  
before they ever get the chance.

And maybe that is the truest thing I know about love:  
that for me,  
it has always been a shadow  
I watch from across the room,  
never daring to step close enough  
to see if it might be warm.
