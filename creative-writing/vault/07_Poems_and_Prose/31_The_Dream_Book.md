# The Dream Book

*HollowMere. Kept by Ernie, of the platform crew, on the back of a Company form pad Clem gave me. Begun Monday, the first of January, 1894.*

**Head note.**

Clem's book counts who comes off at Day and who gets on at Dusk. It doesn't count what you see at night. Nobody's book does. So this one will.

Mam calls them dreams, so it's a dream book. I didn't name it. I only keep it.

My rule is the kind Clem has. I only put a name in the margin if I copied it off something I could put my finger on in daylight. A dream doesn't leave you anything to put your finger on.

Every morning of my life Mam said on the step what every mother here says, while she pinned my collar: *Keep to the light, and don't miss the train.*

Since the year turned, she says, *It was a dream.*

**Monday, 1 January.**

Friday. Fog at the Rush. The line was shut, and a train came up it anyway.
Pruett in the door of the private car with his punch in his hand, not using it.
One off the private car. The fog stood off him. An iron crosier as tall as he is.
The crew took their hats off. I took mine off. Clem wrote that he couldn't say which way he meant it. Neither can I.

That's all of it. I was on the boards with the bucket. I saw what the boards saw and no more.

Mam, at the clock, this morning: *No train came Friday. The line's shut. It was a dream.*
Then she set the clock by the up-train, the way the rule says every clock in the Grant has to be set.

She set it Friday night too. It was the first time since the twenty-third.

So what did she set it by?
If Friday was a dream, why is our clock right?

I'll tell the truth here, because it's my book. I wanted it to be a dream. Then nothing would have come up the line for anybody.

*[Margin, pencil, large capitals:]* WIDOWS CUT

Copied Friday, at the Rush, off the tender, where the tank man at the Cut chalks up the water he lets go. The chalk came off on my finger. It's still on the cuff of my coat. I haven't brushed it.

**Monday, 8 January.**

Saturday, the thirtieth. Dusk. The square full.
A light came down on the chapel step, as wide as the pump.
I was too short for the step. There were backs in front of me. I saw what came down the slope.
The robes. Over the rail, down the slope, nobody in them.
I ran after them. The fog had them first.

Mam: *It was a dream.* She said it with her boots on under the blanket. That's how she sleeps now, as if she means to get up.

*[Margin, pencil:]*

You can't copy a name off a light. When Clem hasn't got a name he leaves a blank, so I've left one.

Is a thing a dream because it left you nothing to copy?
Is a blank a lie?
Then Clem's book is full of lies, and he's the most honest man on the boards.

**Monday, 15 January.**

Thursday night. From our window you can see the end of the boards.
People walking past it, one behind another. No lanterns. No sound. No hurry.
Going the way that goes to the Gallery mouth.
I counted them the way Clem counts, under my breath. When I knew one of the coats I stopped.
I'm not writing whose.

Then I saw Mam. She wasn't in her bed. She was at the other window, her shape on the glass, watching the same way I was.

Friday morning, at the clock: *It was a dream.*
Friday evening she barred the door before Curfew. She's never barred it before.

So now I know what a dream is in our house. It's a thing two of us saw at the same time and neither of us will say. It has to be called something.

I think I know why, too. Whatever you say you saw, somebody up the square writes you down for it. Mam is keeping us out of everybody's book.
Except mine.

*[Margin, pencil, large capitals:]* GALLOWS WOOD SIDING

Copied off the board at the end of the boards, the one that points down the line. Under it the frost had prints going over the rails in a line. By noon the prints were gone. The board wasn't.

**Monday, 22 January.**

Percy, at the Lantern, on a Tuesday. Three marbles. He wouldn't play for keeps. He never would.
I let him win one. He gave it back.

Mam: *It was a dream.* She said it soft. This time it was.

This one was a dream. I know the difference.
That's how I know the others aren't.

*[Margin, pencil:]*

There's nothing to copy. Percy never came on the train. He came in on Tuesdays.

Where do you put a friend in a book that only keeps what's real?
If I wrote his name in capitals, would that make him one or the other?
Percy goes in the middle of the page, with the dreams. Small. Where he'd sit.

**Monday, 29 January.**

This week Mam said it first, before I'd said anything. At the clock:
*You never went near that train. You dreamed it.*

The pump told her. The pump tells everybody everything, a week late.

So here it is, in my book, in my hand.

Saturday the twenty-third. The last train out. Arthur's brother took Arthur's ticket out of his hatband and put it in my hand. *Go on,* he said. *You always ask.*
I always ask where she goes after us. Nobody's ever told me.
I climbed to the step with the bucket still in my fist. Pruett brought the punch down to the paper.
I looked back once. I stepped down. I gave it back warm.
I said, *Who'd fill the bucket if I'm gone?*

It wasn't the bucket.

Maybe it was him, with his hand still out, and nobody on the boards to stand next to after.
Maybe it was where she goes after us, that nobody's ever told me, and I was afraid to be the first to find out.
Maybe it was Mam, who would have heard it at the pump a week late, and gone on setting the clock for one.

If it was a dream, I never gave it back.
If I never gave it back, I'm gone.
Which boy does she want at the table?

*[Margin, pencil, large capitals:]* THORNE JCT

Copied off Arthur's ticket. Arthur's brother took it down out of the band for me, stood there while I did it, and put it back. I copied the name. I didn't look to see if it was punched.

**Monday, 5 February.**

No dream this week. I slept. Nothing to call anything.

This morning Mam found the book open on the table. She looked down the margins, at the capitals going down the side of every page. She didn't say anything about the Cut, or the Siding, or the Junction.
She put her finger on the last one. I copied it this morning off the sign on the boards. It's the first word I ever copied, in October, when Clem said I could have the foot of every page.

*That one's real,* she said.

It's the only one she'll let be. The name of where we're standing.

*[Margin, pencil, large capitals:]* HOLLOWMERE

I keep the clock she set by a train that didn't come.
I keep the prints that never crossed the rails.
I keep the step I never got down from, because I was never on it.

She can call it what she likes. She's keeping me out of everybody's book, and I'm keeping her in mine.

A dream doesn't leave you the spelling. Everything in my margins did.
